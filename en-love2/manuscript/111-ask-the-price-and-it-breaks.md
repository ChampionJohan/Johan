## Up Front

Here is what this chapter has to set down. **The moment a price is asked, the
thing becomes a different thing.** It does not break so much as change, and on
the receiving side it feels like breaking.

And this chapter records that the explanation from the earlier chapters is short
in one place. It is the place the seventh and tenth chapters put off.

## What Happens When a Price Attaches

Somebody does something for you and you ask what you owe, and the nature of the
place changes. The size of the sum is irrelevant. It changes on the single fact
that a price appeared.

A neighbor watched the children. Say thank you and the thing between you stays
open. Ask what you should pay them and the thing between you is settled. Settled,
it is finished. The side that paid is relieved of a debt and made comfortable,
and in exchange for the comfort there is nothing more to come.

Why losing a debt should be a loss is the odd part. What is between people stays
open only while something is still owed. Repay it all and the relationship is
complete. A completed relationship has no next.

## Which Is Why They Will Not Take It

This looks like the reason givers refuse payment. They know that taking it closes
the thing between you. Not knowing it in words. Simply knowing.

In the country there are people who wave a hand when you try to pay for
something they gave you, and I used to think this was modesty and I see it
differently now. They are refusing to let the thing close. Money moving makes
a neighbor into a supplier.

And the ones who refuse usually set you some other task instead. Come help in my
field next time. That costs more. Choosing the more expensive one is what catches
in this chapter.

## One Explanation Short

Now to the place that was put off.

The first book explained giving as passing something through. What was received
cannot be repaid, so it goes sideways, I wrote. The seventh chapter used that to
explain the strength to bear a loss. No pipe takes a loss from the water going
through it, it said.

That explanation has a hole, and someone who received almost nothing should
have almost nothing to pass on, and **among people who received almost nothing
there are some who give the most.** People who grew up without a table and who
feed others extremely well. The place noted at the end of the tenth chapter.

What they have did not flow in from anywhere. So passing it through does not
explain it.

## I Tried to Build Another and Stopped

So I tried to build a different explanation. Two of them.

One is that a person who has gone without knows that absence. Someone who was
hungry recognizes someone hungry and feeds them. This is plausible and it does
not fit everywhere. Not everyone who went without turns out this way. Going
without makes people grip harder at least as often.

The other is that I failed to find what they received. Table or no table,
somebody somewhere may have looked at them once, and that once may have been a
seed, and this one cannot be argued against, which is not a good sign. An
explanation that cannot be argued against is usually not an explanation.

**Neither works.** I stop here. This book is looking at what happens on the
giving side, and where that strength came from is not a question this book has
standing to answer. That one explanation is missing is recorded plainly.
Pretending otherwise would be worse.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside. Inference and observation,
with the inference marked as inference.

That a gift and a trade are different in kind has a long discussion behind it in
anthropology. I confirmed from public sources that the discussion exists and did
not bring it into the text. Summarize it here and the chapter becomes a tour of
the literature, and then the neighbor who watched the children becomes a case
study. Anyone interested will find it under gift exchange.
