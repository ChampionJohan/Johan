## The First Thing Received

The first thing a person receives is food. It arrives within hours of being born.
It is received in a state of knowing nothing, and the receiving is not known
either.

And the first thing a person gives to anyone else is usually food too. Watch
children and you can see it. Before they have words they hold out their own
biscuit. Nobody taught them and they do it. It is also the only giving available
at that age.

Of all the ways of giving, feeding is the oldest. It is probably as old as
people.

## Why Is It So Strong

Feeding has something in it that other kinds of giving do not. It moves people
in a way that does not make sense.

Hear that someone is ill and people carry food to them. Go to a house where
someone has died and there too they feed you. When there is nothing to say,
people feed. Someone who cannot find the words for comfort will still make soup.

I have thought about why and I do not know. One part is guessable. Eating is
something the body does, and what the body does leaves no room for words to
get in, and it is what you do when you cannot speak, which is why it cannot be
faked.

## The Side That Lays the Table

A table has two places at it. The eating place and the laying place. And the one
who laid the table is almost never remembered for it.

Many people remember the food they ate as children. Few remember who made it and
at what hour they got up. On the receiving side the food was simply there. It is
the same place the first book described. **Seen from the receiving end, the food
has always already been put out.**

And the one who laid it usually eats later. Eats it cold, eats what is left. That
order is the same in house after house and nobody decided it.

## Why Feeding Never Gets Counted

Feeding is two or three times a day, and that goes on for decades.

Once is small. One meal is one meal, and nobody is grateful for a single meal
having been cooked. And stacked up, it grows a person, and a small thing
repeated tens of thousands of times becomes a large thing, and while it is
stacking, each one is too small to be counted.

This looks like the reason most giving is invisible. Giving something big once is
visible. Giving something small tens of thousands of times is not. And the
second is the side that keeps people alive.

## Houses Where Nobody Fed Anyone

One side has to be set down here. Writing only well of the family table injures
the people who grew up without one.

There are houses where nobody laid a table. There are people who grew up boiling
their own noodles, and there were houses where the table was frightening. The
table is not always warm. Someone who grew up in such a house reads this chapter
and confirms once more what they did not have.

I have not yet made anything I could say to them. One thing shows, though. Among
people who grew up in houses like that, some feed others extremely well. They
never received it and they are giving it. How that works is the eleventh chapter.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone made the food you ate longest. You may not know what hour they got up.
Find out if you can, and if you cannot, it is enough to call the food to mind.

## Where This Chapter Came From

The passage about children is observation. There is research on children
sharing food before they have language, and I confirmed from public sources
that such work exists and did not use it in the text, because I could not read
it properly. Summarizing what you have not read produces errors.

How widely the custom of bringing food to a house in mourning extends is
something I tried to check and could not. I wanted to write that it exists in
every culture, and I could not find the material to support that, so the sentence
came out.
