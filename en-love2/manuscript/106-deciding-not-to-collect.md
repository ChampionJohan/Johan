## The Arithmetic Is Odd

Set the word forgiveness down for a moment and look only at what happens inside
it. What happens is this. **The side with something owed to them decides not to
collect.**

By the arithmetic this is odd. There is a right to collect, the right is
legitimate, and not collecting is a loss of exactly that size. And they do not
collect. And having decided not to, the person gets lighter. Taking a loss and
getting lighter has come up again.

## It Is Not the Same as Forgetting

One thing has to be separated out first. Deciding not to collect is not the same
as forgetting.

Forgetting is the arithmetic disappearing, and the memory dims until the fact
that anything was owed no longer comes to mind, and that is the work of time
rather than the work of a person.

Deciding not to collect is done **with the arithmetic perfectly clear.** You
know the amount, you know you could collect, and you do not. The sharper the
memory the harder it is, which is why this is not something that happens by
itself as time passes.

## The Other Person Does Not Need to Know

There is one curious feature. It holds even if the other person never knows.

There is no need to inform them that you decided not to collect. Informing
them makes it stranger, in fact. *I have forgiven you* is not a good sentence
to be on the receiving end of, and there is a position of looking down built
into it, so the listener ends up in debt all over again.

So this is mostly done alone. Done alone and told to nobody. The third book
looks at things that continue even when the other person does not know, and this
belongs over there too.

## There Are Days It Does Not Work

Let me write this honestly. It does not happen in one go.

You think you have done it, and months later the thing surfaces again, and
surfacing, it makes you angry again. Then it seems you did not do it. So you
do it again. It surfaces again and you do it again, and it is not a thing that
finishes once but **a thing done many times**, and needing many times does not
look like evidence of failure.

There is a reason for writing this part down. Some people decide they are
inadequate because it did not work in one go. The nature of the thing seems to
be like that.

## Cases Where You Do Not Have To

And something must be written here. **There are cases where you do not have to.**

A piece that urges someone badly hurt not to collect becomes a form of violence.
This chapter must not be held up in front of someone still in pain, someone for
whom it is not over, someone whose other party is still doing the same thing.

This chapter is not telling anyone to do it. It is looking at the fact that such
a thing exists in the world. Blur that distinction and this becomes a bad book.

## I Do Not Know Why It Gets Lighter

Here is the stuck place.

I do not know why deciding not to collect makes a person lighter. By the
arithmetic the loss has just been locked in, so why does the weight drop.
Perhaps holding something owed is itself heavy, and then it would follow that
the more you are owed the heavier you are, which is strange in its own way.

I tried several explanations and each jammed somewhere. I am leaving it jammed.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone is owed something by you and has not collected.

You may not even know that they did not. That is the nature of the thing. If
anything does come to mind, today would be a good day to think about it once.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside.

This subject has a great deal of research behind it. There are several studies
measuring what changes in body and mind, as far as I know. **I did not use
them.** Write down the benefits and this becomes a chapter that says *so do it*,
which runs head-on into the section about the cases where you do not have to.
