## Places Where the Arithmetic Does Not Work

Gather up what this book has looked at so far and one thing catches. None of the
arithmetic works.

Attach no condition and the effect drops. Let your eye go to the one doing worse
and there is no return. Do not weigh deserving and things leak. Give to a
stranger and nothing comes back. Hide your name and even that is gone. Decline
to collect and the loss is locked in. **Six chapters, all of them stories about
losing.**

And people are doing it. Not one or two people. A very great many, and they have
been at it for thousands of years.

## The Loss Is in the Design

Let me set one thing down here. The loss does not look like an accident. It
looks like part of the design.

Design the giving well and you can make it lose nothing. Attach conditions,
weigh deserving, put your name on it, collect what is owed. Do that and
efficiency rises and the leaks shrink. Plenty of organizations really do work
this way.

And once it is built that way it has become something else. The name changes.
From then on it is called a business or a program, and nobody calls it love.
**The moment the loss is removed the name changes** is the strangest thing in
this chapter.

## Where Does the Strength to Bear It Come From

To keep going while losing, something has to be holding it up. I have thought
about what that might be.

Willpower does not last long enough. Willpower bottoms out in months and some
people do this for decades. Character does not work either, because the people
who do it are so unlike one another. Reward does not work, because as the
previous chapters showed, there is no reward.

One thing is left, which is the explanation from the first book. **Passing on
what was received.** If you are not handing over something of your own but
letting it through, then the word loss never applied. No pipe takes a loss from
the water going through it.

That explanation has a hole in it too. The eleventh chapter looks at the hole.

## They Do Not Calculate the Loss

One observation to record. Watch people who give for a long time and **they do
not calculate the loss.**

They do not know how much they have given. Ask and they say they are not sure.
It is not that they have forgotten. It looks as though they never counted. So
they have no sensation of loss, and with no sensation the point at which they
tire is different.

People who count get tired. Count and one day *after all I have done* arrives
without fail, and once that sentence is out, everything after it is a trade.

## So Am I Saying Do Not Count

Here is the stuck place.

Say do not count and it easily becomes a way of saying do not look after
yourself. I have seen people who gave everything away and collapsed. Telling them
not to count is a dangerous sentence.

Then how far should anyone count, and that line is different for every person and
I cannot draw somebody else's. *The people who do not count last longer* and
*not counting makes people collapse* are both correct, and I am leaving them
without putting them in one sentence.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone did something for you at a cost to themselves. They would not have
thought of it as a cost. Ask them and they will say no. That answer is what this
chapter is pointing at.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside. Observation and guesswork,
with the guesswork marked as guesswork.

There is probably research on people who give over long periods. I did not
look. Find it and this becomes a chapter called five traits of such people,
and then it is a list to follow, and this book started out having decided not
to make lists.
