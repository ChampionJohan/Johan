## Toward a Face You Do Not Know

Anyone who has given blood knows. You do not know where it goes.

There is no way to find out and nobody tells you. Who received it, whether they
lived, whether they were grateful, all of it ends unknown. And people do it. Not
once and done either; plenty of them keep at it for years. **There really are
kinds of giving in this world that go in a direction where nothing at all comes
back.**

## Not Even Gratitude

Giving of this kind does not even get gratitude back.

Gratitude is the small return the giver receives, and send it toward a face
you do not know and there is none of that either, and by the arithmetic it is
a total loss. Time goes in, the body takes some damage, and what returns is
zero.

They do it anyway. And having done it they do not feel bad. This is the part
that does not explain well. There are not many things in the world where you
take a loss and feel all right about it.

## The Ones Who Withhold Their Name

Some go further. They give without giving their name.

Withhold the name and the channel for gratitude is sealed off entirely. The one
who received cannot repay even if they want to. So hiding a name is also **work
done to erase the receiver's debt.** The tenth chapter of the first book said the
repayment counter is closed. Here the giving side closes it deliberately.

Why they close it would have to be asked, and it cannot be. They withheld their
name. That is the strangest part of this chapter. There is structurally no way to
learn their reasons.

## Easier Than the People Close to You

One thing should be written honestly. **Giving to a stranger is sometimes easier
than giving to someone close.**

There are no expectations of a stranger. You never have to watch whether they
are grateful, and you cannot see whether they use it well. So there is nothing
to be hurt by. All the complicated things that come up when you give to someone
close drop out here.

So if reading this chapter produces the thought *well, I am good at giving to
strangers*, that may not be entirely good news, and the hard side is usually
the near side.

## Is This Just Self-Satisfaction Then

Here is where this chapter is stuck.

If you feel all right afterward, the objection runs, is that feeling not the
return, and then it is not a total loss, and if it is not a total loss the
premise of the chapter wobbles. The objection is fairly strong.

There is one odd thing in it though. Go after that feeling and it does not come.
Give in order to get the feeling and the feeling does not arrive. So it is a
return, and a return that vanishes when it becomes the aim. What to call a
return like that I do not know.

## Someone Comes to Mind

You have received something from a person whose face you have never seen.

Blood, maybe. An organ, maybe. Maybe just money. Or maybe a road or an alphabet
that somebody laid down a long time ago. That person does not know you were
born.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside.

Blood donation and organ donation have numbers attached. How many people, how
often, which countries do more. **I did not use them.** Put a figure in and this
becomes a chapter about policy, and policy leads to how we get more people to do
it. This chapter means to stop at not knowing why they do.

It would be worth looking at those numbers once before publication and deciding
again whether leaving them out is right.
