## The Hardest Side

The fourth chapter said giving to a stranger is hard. There is a harder side.
**Someone you know, who does not say thank you.**

A stranger never had the chance to be grateful. Someone you know had the chance
and did not take it. On the receiving end that difference is very large.

## Three Different Things

There are several places where thanks does not come back, and they are not alike.

The first is not knowing. An infant is like that. So is someone whose memory is
going. This side does not hurt. Few people feel slighted by a person who could
not have known.

The second is knowing and not doing it. They take it as a given. Children are
mostly like this with parents, and old friends are like it with each other. This
side stings a little. And it is bearable.

Then there is a third. The one who received turns around and resents you, and
they say they never asked you to do it, that their life came out this way
because of it. That one hurts most. And it is more common than people expect.

## What Thank You Does

I have wondered why that sentence gets waited for so badly.

It is not money. Hearing it leaves nothing in your hand. And hearing it makes
what you did into a thing that happened. Not hearing it makes it as though
nothing occurred at all, and people want what they did to be written down
somewhere, and thank you is the sound of the writing.

When the sound does not come, it goes by unrecorded. Doing something unrecorded
for several decades is the place this chapter sits.

## Wanting It Breaks It

Something catches here. Wanting to be thanked is not a bad thing, and if you are
wanting it, the giving breaks off one day.

The order of the breaking is much the same every time. First you feel
slighted, then you start counting, and once you have counted, *after all I
have done* arrives, and once that sentence is out of your mouth it is hard to
get back, and the receiving side hears it too. Hearing it, they are in debt,
and once a debt appears, everything between you is arithmetic.

So the conclusion is do not want it, which is again not a sentence you can say
to a human being. Wanting is not a thing anyone chooses.

## So What Do You Do

Here is where this chapter is stuck. I cannot write an answer.

Watch people who have given for a long time and it is not that they stopped
wanting it. **They want it less.** Not gone, reduced. Ask how it reduced and they
say it just did. Some say it was the years. Some say it reduced after they came
to see what they themselves had received.

The second answer catches. It connects to what the first book set down, and if it
is right, there is only one road to not wanting to be thanked. Seeing first what
you were given. Whether that is a road I do not know either. I am recording only
that several people said it that way.

## Someone Comes to Mind

There is someone you have never thanked. You took it as a given. They may still
be waiting.

## Where This Chapter Came From

This chapter is observation and things I was told. I did not record who said
them, because they were talking about their families.

There is probably research on the person being cared for coming to resent the
person caring. I did not look. Put it in and the chapter becomes how to handle
such cases, and that is not what this chapter is for.
