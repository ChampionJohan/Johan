## Children Know First

The fastest people at telling conditional love from the other kind are children.
They cannot explain it. They know all of it.

*Do well and I will buy it for you* and *I bought it because I thought you
would like it* are places where the same object changes hands, and what the
child receives is nothing alike, and the child who got the first treats the
object as something they earned. The child who got the second knows they were
thought of. **The object is the same and what was received is different.**

## Conditions Raise Performance

Something has to be written fairly here. Conditions work.

Attach one and people move. Marks rise, work gets done, promises are kept.
Which is why nearly every institution in the world is built out of conditions,
and without them neither a school nor a company runs, and to call conditions
bad is to call the world bad, and a sentence like that does not land anywhere.

Conditions do have one property. The moment one is attached, that relationship
becomes a trade, and a trade is not a bad thing and most of the world is
trade, and **in a relationship built as a trade, when the condition ends the
relationship ends.**

## You Find Out at the End

So which kind it was can only be known at the end.

When the marks stop coming. When the work falls through. When illness makes
everything impossible. If the person is still there then it was not a condition,
and if they vanish then it was. It is a cruel test and not one anybody sets
deliberately. Live long enough and it administers itself.

The first book looked only at the receiving side; here we look at the giving
one. I wondered whether there is a way for the giver to tell whether they are
attaching a condition, and there is one. **Picture what you would do if the
other person became unable.** Even imagining it comes out fairly clear.

## Conditions Hide Inside Sentences

Conditions mostly hide inside sentences. And the hidden ones are stronger.

*I am proud of you* is a good sentence, and if it only comes out when there is
something to be proud of, the sentence becomes a condition. So does *what a good
girl.* Arriving only on the good days, it means that on the other days she is
not a daughter. The speaker has no intention of setting a condition and on the
listening side it stacks up as one.

So to avoid conditions **you have to speak on the days when nothing happened.**
Speak only on the days something went well and all of it turns conditional. Try
it and it is difficult, because on the days when nothing happened there is
nothing to say.

## Can There Be No Condition at All

Here is where this chapter stops.

Whether a person can be met with no condition at all I do not know. Parents are
people too, so a child doing well makes them happier, and the happiness shows on
their face. Showing on the face means the child reads it. Read, it becomes a
condition. You cannot tell someone to hide their happiness either. Hidden, it is
a different sort of lie.

So a state with no condition in it looks impossible for a human being, and what
does look possible is **having both kinds of day: days with a condition on them
and days without.** That is as far as I got.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone gave you something with no condition attached. Were you doing well at the
time, or not. If what comes to mind is something you received while you were not
doing well, that is what this chapter is pointing at.
