## Ask What They Gave

Ask people what they gave up for somebody and they start with money. Listen a
little longer and it is not about money.

A story about nights comes out. A story about years handed over, about studies
abandoned, about places never gone to. Money gets the shortest telling and is
forgotten the soonest, because money earned once can be earned again and those
few years cannot.

So looking at the amount tells you almost nothing about an act of giving. **You
have to look at the place it was cut from.**

## The Cut Does Not Fill In

Money spent can be replaced. Time spent cannot.

During the two years a child was ill, somebody went two years backward in their
own work. Those two years are never made up later. The people who started
alongside them moved forward in the meantime, and coming back, the ground has
shifted. Call that a loss and it is a loss.

Here is the odd part. Ask those people what they would do if the two years
could be returned to them, and mostly no answer comes, and they say it was
costly and they do not say they would not do it. They live in both at once.

## Sleep

The thing cut away most often is sleep. And it is the thing least counted.

Anyone who has raised an infant had their nights in pieces for years, and the
same goes for anyone who nursed someone ill, and for anyone who looked after
an aging parent. Nobody counts the hours lost. Counting changes nothing, and
once you start counting, the night gets longer.

You can recognize the face of someone who has been cutting away sleep. They
cannot see it and from the outside it shows. That face is the whole of what this
chapter is trying to say.

## Room

Of everything that gets cut away, the least sensible is room.

In a small house, when the children increase, somebody's space shrinks. The side
that shrinks is almost always the adult. The larger person moves into the smaller
spot. By the arithmetic this is backwards and it happens in house after house.

The side that shrank does not even call it giving way. They simply sit like that.
The giving with no name on it is the most common kind, and with no name on it,
it is recorded nowhere.

## How Much Can Be Cut

Here is where this chapter jams.

Writing only well of cutting away is dangerous. There are people who cut away
everything and had nothing left, and after they collapsed there was no one to
give, and the side that had been receiving had a harder time. So giving
everything is not the same as giving the most.

Then how far, and I cannot draw the line. Draw one and it becomes an excuse. Do
not draw one and somebody goes all the way. I cannot answer for either, so I am
leaving it undrawn. One sentence can be set down. **Telling someone who has
nothing left to put out more is not a sentence that belongs to love.**

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone went several years backward for you. You do not know what they might have
done with those years. If you have never asked, it is not too late.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside. There is research measuring
the hours that care work takes. I did not use it. Turn time into a number and the
number converts into money, and converted, the chapter stands on its head.

There is research on the sleep deprivation of people providing care as well. Left
out for the same reason. That both exist is worth recording.
