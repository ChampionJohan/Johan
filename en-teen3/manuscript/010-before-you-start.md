## What this book does

One line:

> **Selling big and selling for a long time are two different things,
> and there's a way to tell which is which.**

Selling big is a **bestseller**. Short and loud.
Selling for a long time is a **steady seller**. Quiet and it doesn't stop.

Neither word is only about books.
Clothes, food, games, videos — everything splits the same way.

Five parts:

| Part | What it covers |
|---|---|
| One (ch 1–3) | **They're different things** — how to tell |
| Two (ch 4–6) | **The ones that vanished** — why they ended |
| Three (ch 7–9) | **The ones that stayed** — why they didn't |
| Four (ch 10–12) | **The late arrivals** — failed, then came back |
| Five (ch 13–16) | **So what do you make** |

Every chapter ends with **Do This Today.**
Reading and closing the book means forgetting it, so do them.

## This isn't a business book

Part Five talks about making things, but nobody is telling you to open a shop.

**Whatever you make, this applies.**
Videos, drawings, a club, the way you study.

What makes a thing last is pretty much the same everywhere.

## There's a grown-up version

There's an adult book called *Why Is This Still Here?*
It says the same things across twenty chapters, in more detail.

**You don't need it.** This book stands on its own.

Where a chapter has more behind it, there's a line pointing at the adult
version.

## A lot of names are missing on purpose

You'll hit sentences like this a lot:

> "a company" "an old song" "a shop in one district"

**That's deliberate.**

Where something was confirmed, it says so. Where it wasn't, the name is gone.
**Pretending to know is the worst thing a book can do.**

Every chapter that uses a real case ends with **Where This Came From.**
It lists two things:

**One,** what was confirmed.
**Two,** what could *not* be confirmed and was therefore **left out.**

Almost no book prints the second list. It's the more useful one.

And to be honest about it: confirmed here means
**public sources cross-checked against each other.**
It does not mean anyone opened a company's filing cabinet.
That's stated too.

## Numbers and years

Treat numbers as **direction**, not as exact values. Exact values move.

There's almost no "these days" or "this year" in this book.
It should still read correctly in ten years.

**Checking things yourself is one of the habits this book is trying to teach.**
