## How old are the things in this book

Think back over what you've seen.

A shop in the same spot for over a thousand years.
A work boot over a hundred years old.
A plastic block system around seventy.
A song that reached number one thirty-seven years on.
A failure that took twelve years to become a product.

One thing in common.

**Not one of them went fast.**

And something more important.

**Not one of them set out to last.**

They all just **never went away.**

## There are two games

This whole book in two sentences.

There are two games in the world.

**One. The guessing game.**

Work out what's about to take off and get on it.
In fast, out fast.

**Two. The staying game.**

Don't disappear. Be there whenever people look.

They're completely different games. They're won differently.

> **The guessing game is won by whoever's fastest.
> The staying game is won by whoever didn't quit.**

Here's what you need to know.

**The guessing game is already decided at the starting line.**

The side with money, faster information, more contacts wins.
You're at a disadvantage there. That isn't comfort, it's fact.

**The staying game isn't like that.**

The difference between someone who started today and someone who started
three years ago is **three years.** And three years can be closed.

## The usual thought — whoever started early has the advantage

**Half right.**

In the guessing game, true.

In the staying game, false.

The reason is slightly funny, and real.

**Whoever started first is also at zero if they quit.**

Score in the staying game isn't "how early did you start."
It's **"are you still doing it."**

So in this game **people keep dropping out.**
Every year there are fewer people ahead of you.

It's a game where standing still moves you up the rankings.

## Why nobody's over here

If the staying game is such an advantage, why isn't everyone playing it?

**Because it's boring.**

Really. That's the reason.

The guessing game gives results fast. A few months and you know.
And when you win, it shows.

In the staying game **nothing happens for years.**
Nobody notices, and there's no score at halftime.

> **Competition is thin here not because it's hard to enter
> but because it's boring.**

That's good news.
**You can't enter a place that's empty because it's hard.
You can enter a place that's empty because it's dull.**

Putting up with it is free.

## Three reasons starting late is an advantage

**One. The staying game counts from today.**

Starting late isn't a loss.
And whoever started early is at zero if they quit.

**Two. Someone starting late can start small.**

Chapter 6. The lower your monthly outgoings, the longer you last.

**And having little right now is an advantage here.**

Nothing to protect, no scale to maintain, no money to pay back.
Nothing is set up big, so nothing collapses.

Remember how the people ahead of you died in Chapter 6?
**They died by growing.** You aren't big yet.

**Three. You read Part Two.**

You start **knowing** how the people ahead of you fell.

You won't mistake talk for need,
you won't sign longer than the fad,
and when it's going well you can add bridges instead of shops.

> **This is information the people ahead of you could not buy.**

When they were living it, it hadn't happened yet.

## And when people love something deeply, they talk

One more thing.

Something a few people love deeply often lasts longer
than something a lot of people bought.

Simple reason.

**Deep liking makes people talk.** Nobody has to ask them to.

Without being paid, they tell a friend, lend it, invite someone along.

That's how the rotating customers from Chapter 3 appear.
**And it costs no advertising.**

Honestly though: **depth isn't money.**
Ten people who love it deeply are ten people.

There's still an order.
**Going wide means going deep first. It rarely works the other way.**

## So one question changes

The last thing this book has to say.

The usual question is this:

> What's going to take off?

Not a bad question, but **a bad question for you.**
It's a question the fast side wins.

This book asks a different one:

> **What isn't going to go away?**

You can answer that by looking closely.
And looking closely is free.

One more thing, and it's for you specifically.

> **What's popular in your classroom right now
> will not matter in ten years.
> What you keep finding yourself thinking about will.**

Fads pass. That part is certain.

But **the thing you keep looking at, year after year,**
the thing nobody else pays much attention to and you keep noticing —

that's where the staying game starts.

## An advantage isn't the same as easy

Last honest note. Leaving it out would be a lie.

In the staying game **the prize comes late.**

**And usually it doesn't come.**

You can do ten years and have nothing happen.
That is **the normal result** of this game. Not failure. Normal.

So here's the one thing to confirm at the end.

**Why those ten years aren't wasted.**

Chapter 12 said riding a wave needs three things:
**exist, be findable, be gettable.**

And Chapter 15's four bridges:
**bought again, recognized, better with use, passable on.**

Look at those seven.

**Every one of them pays off with no wave at all.**

Being easy to find is just good.
Getting better with use is just good.
Wave or no wave.

> **Preparing for a wave and simply being in good shape are the same thing,
> so no wave means nothing was wasted.**

That's the whole of what this book can promise.

**And it's a promise that can be kept.**

## Do This Today

Write one line on paper.

> **Will I still be doing this in ten years?**

Put something you're doing now into it.

If yes, that one thing is your staying game. You don't need to look elsewhere.

If no, that's fine. It's no for most people.
**Then finding the thing you'd still be doing is the work ahead.**

How to find it is Chapter 13. Not the charts —
look at what you keep finding yourself noticing.

And remember one thing.

**You cannot start this game too late.**

It isn't a game where whoever started late loses.
**It's a game where whoever quit loses.**

So starting today is fine.
You're one day behind whoever started yesterday.
