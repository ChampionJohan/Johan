## A book nobody changed a word of starts getting borrowed

Library shelves hold books almost nobody takes out.
Borrowed once every few years. Dust on them.

Then some of them start getting borrowed more from a certain year.
A few years later they're always out and you have to reserve one.

Confirm one thing here.

**Not one word of that book changed.**

No revised edition, no rewrite by the author.
The same sentences on the same paper.

Everything that changed was outside.

Odd, isn't it?

> **If the object didn't change and the value did,
> the value wasn't inside the object.**

## The usual thought — it was ahead of its time

The most common phrase for explaining why something didn't sell.

**Half right. And it's the dangerous half.**

Dangerous because **it sounds like a compliment.**

"Ahead of its time" means you weren't wrong, the world was slow.
That's comfortable. It leaves nothing to fix.

But there's **no diagnosis** in it.

It wasn't ahead. **It was standing in a spot that didn't exist yet.**
And spots that don't exist don't appear on their own.

Follow late arrivals through time and they share one thing.

> **The world didn't come by itself. Something brought it.**

This chapter calls that something a **key.**
There are three keys.

## First key · It got cheap

Some things have the idea first and the materials later.

The design was finished long ago and what it needed was too expensive.
Electricity was expensive, or communication, or computation, or moving things.

The day that cost drops below a line, the thing suddenly becomes possible.

Note the property here.

**Cost falls slowly and possibility arrives suddenly.**

It gets a little cheaper each year, and the year it crosses the line,
the number of people making it goes from one to a hundred.

So from outside it looks like "it suddenly became popular."
It didn't. **A line got crossed.**

## Second key · The place it sits came down

Sometimes the cost doesn't change at all.

**What changes is where it sits.**

What lived only in a laboratory comes down to an office.
What lived in an office comes down to a house.
What lived in a house comes down to a pocket.

Each step down **multiplies the number of people who can try it.**

This matters because the world is full of
**good things that didn't spread because nobody could get their hands on one.**

Remember the doors from Chapter 3? Same thing.
**A narrow door keeps out good things just as well as bad ones.**

## Third key · People grew up with it as normal

The slowest and the most certain.

Some objects need explaining to anyone seeing them for the first time.
Someone who grew up in a world containing them needs no explanation.

**When explanation stops being needed, the cost collapses.**
All the money the seller spent on persuasion disappears.

The generation clock from Chapter 2 is back here.

> **The generation clock doesn't change the object.
> It changes the eyes looking at it.**

Some things **sit perfectly still and become easy one day.**

## So how do you tell ahead from wrong?

This is the most important part of the chapter.

Both look identical. Neither one sells.
So everyone says "ahead of its time" and stops there.

There's one way to tell. **Count two numbers.**

**One. How many people said it was good?**

**Two. How many people actually used it last month?**

Put them side by side and the split appears.

> **What's ahead gets used by a few. What's wrong gets praised by many.**

Read that again. It really is like that.

Lots of praise and zero users doesn't mean there's no spot.
It means **people are being polite.**

Little praise, a few real users, and that few not shrinking —
**that's a spot that hasn't arrived yet.**

At least it isn't shrinking. That's the signal.

## Waiting isn't sitting still

"Wait for the world to catch up" is also dangerous.

Money goes out while you wait. So does time.
Same problem as Chapter 9. **You can't wait without something to wait on.**

So the two things a waiter actually does are these.

**One. Stay alive small.**
Set up big and you can't wait. The money runs out before the key arrives.

**Two. Watch for the key.**
Not waiting vaguely — **deciding which key has to arrive.**

Three lines:

> **What has to get cheap for this to work?**
> **How far down does it have to come for ten times as many people to try it?**
> **Who has to grow up for this to be normal?**

If you can't write a single one of the three, it isn't ahead.

## Do This Today

Pick something you're doing that **isn't working.**

A drawing, a video, something you made, a club — anything.

Count two numbers.

**One,** how many people said it was good.

**Two,** how many people **actually used or watched it** last month.

If the second is zero and the first is large, there's no missing spot.
**People were being polite.** Admitting that fast is cheaper.

If the second is small but not zero, and **not smaller than last month,**
that's a different story. The key hasn't arrived.

Then write three lines.

> What has to get cheap for this to work?
> How far down does it have to come for ten times as many people to try it?
> Who has to grow up for this to be normal?

If you can't write a single one,
**stop using the phrase "ahead of its time" as of today.**
