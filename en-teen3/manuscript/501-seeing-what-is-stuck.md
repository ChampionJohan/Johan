## Everyone is looking at the same screen

How do you find out what's popular right now?

You check a chart, or the recommendations, or what other people are doing.

There's a problem.

**You aren't the only one looking at that. Everyone is.**

Look at the same thing and find out the same thing,
and the judgement you reach is **the same judgement everyone else reached.**

The same judgement doesn't put you in front. Obviously.

## The usual thought — read trends well and you're fine

**Half right.**

Reading is necessary. **What you read** is everything.

This chapter is two lists.
**Three not to look at**, and **three to look at.**

## Three not to look at

**One. Charts.**

A chart shows you **what sold last month.**
It isn't a tool for looking forward. It's a tool for looking back.

Plus the problem above: everyone sees the same one.

**Two. Anything that already has a name.**

Something having a name means a lot of people already noticed it.
It means it was discussed enough times for a name to stick.

> **The moment it has a name is already late.**

**Three. What somebody else already succeeded at.**

Look at a success and **you see the conditions but not the cause.**

Nobody records how many places had the same conditions and failed.
So looking only at successes teaches you the same wrong thing every time.

All three share one trait.

> **All three already happened.**

## Three to look at

**One. What people are solving badly.**

This is the most useful tool in the chapter. Genuinely.

People don't sit in discomfort. **They solve it.**
And with nothing proper available, they solve it **badly.**

Tape holding something together.
Two things used together.
A step order that's clearly been rearranged.
Someone else's object used for something it isn't for.

**Where there's a bad fix, there is always an empty seat.**

Look around a classroom. There is an enormous amount of this.

And this signal differs from a chart in the decisive way.
**It's happening in front of you right now.**

**Two. Something that just got cheap.**

The first key from Chapter 10.

**You have to be holding a list** of what has to get cheap for what to work.
No list and the price falls right past you.

**Three. Somewhere a rule changed.**

School rules, laws, terms of service —
something being allowed or banned creates or destroys a spot.

This one has a peculiar feature.
**It's announced in advance.** You're reading, not predicting.

## What reading actually means

Put together:

Reading trends is **not guessing what will take off.**

> **It's seeing what's stuck right now.**

Why that difference matters:

Guessing what takes off requires **being faster than everyone else.**
The fast side is the side with money and information. That isn't you.

Seeing what's stuck requires no speed.
**You just look closely.**

**And looking closely is free.**

That sentence runs all the way to Chapter 16.

## But not everything stuck is an opportunity

Cold water here.

Where something is stuck and nobody has fixed it,
there's usually **a reason.**

**One. It isn't worth money.** Only ten people are bothered.

**Two. Nobody can fix it.** It's genuinely hard.

**Three. Fixing it breaks something else.** Helping one side hurts another.

So once you've found an empty seat, ask one more line.

> **Why hasn't this been fixed yet?**

If the answer is "nobody noticed," it's a good seat.
If it's "nobody can," you need to check whether you can.
If it's "it doesn't pay," leaving it alone is usually right.

Skip this question and you walk straight into a place
**a hundred people already tried and failed.**

## The short version

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Charts | **last month's.** Everyone sees the same one |
| Once it has a name | **already late** |
| Success stories | you see conditions, **not causes** |
| The best signal | **what people are solving badly** |
| What reading means | not guessing — **seeing what's stuck** |
| Found an empty seat | **why hasn't this been fixed?** — one more line |

## Do This Today

Over one day, **find and write down five bad fixes.**

Home, classroom, the walk there, the canteen, the club room. Anywhere.

Something held on with tape.
Something that needs two things used together.
Something where the order has to be done strangely.
Something being used for what it isn't for.

**Five will turn up.** If they don't, you haven't looked closely yet.

Then pick one and ask:

> **Why has nobody made this properly yet?**

If the answer comes immediately, you already know the reason.
If it doesn't, **watch that one for a few more days.**

That seat is the material for the rest of Part Five.
