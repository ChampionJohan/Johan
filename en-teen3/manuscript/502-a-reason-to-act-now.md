## It's good and nobody moves

You've had this happen.

You made something properly and showed people.
They all say it's good. They seem to mean it.

**And nothing happens.**

Nobody buys it, nobody does it, nobody comes back.

It feels strange. They didn't say they disliked it.

One reason:

> **Good isn't a reason to buy.**

Good is good. Moving today is a separate problem.

## The usual thought — make it better and they'll buy

**Half right.**

If it's bad enough, obviously nobody buys. True.

But **making a good thing better does not make anyone move today.**

Because what people think is this:

*"Nice. I'll do that later."*

Later doesn't arrive. That's the whole thing.

So what you need isn't a reason it's good. It's a **reason it's now.**

## There are only five reasons to move now

Only five. They've been counted.

**One. A deadline.** You can only do it until a date.

**Two. Scarcity.** You might not get one.

**Three. A season.** Now is the time.

**Four. Being seen.** Others are looking.
Not having it now means not showing it now.

**Five. Others already did.** Not doing it leaves you behind.

Line them up and one thing is obvious.

> **All five are about time.**

**None of them** is about how good the thing is.

Which gives this chapter's sentence:

**Launching isn't showing off a product. It's manufacturing time.**

## Four of the five wear out

Criticism now, or this chapter is dangerous.

Chapter 4 said talk shrinks when used.
**These devices shrink when used too.**

A deadline every time isn't a deadline.
Scarcity every time isn't scarcity.

Use one on the same person twice and it's half as strong.
The third time it's **negative.**

They feel tricked. And feeling tricked doesn't wear off.

Of the five, **one doesn't wear out.**

**The season.**

Why is in Chapter 9. **The calendar remakes it every year.**
December arrives again whether you do anything or not.

| Device | Cost to make | Wears out? | How often usable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deadline | cheap | **fast** | a few times |
| Scarcity | medium — must be real | yes | rarely |
| **Season** | **free. The calendar gives it** | **no** | forever |
| Being seen | costs design | no | continuously |
| Others did | hardest at the start | no | continuously |

The order falls out of the table.

**Season, being seen, and others did are devices you can keep using.**
**Deadline and scarcity are cards you play once each.**

And most people do it backwards.
They keep using deadlines and scarcity, and never build a season.

**Because the easy ones are easy.**

## A warning on scarcity

Scarcity needs special care.

Its value comes from **the fact that somebody couldn't get one.**
That is **an asset that disappears if you add supply.**

Launching on scarcity and then wanting to make more is natural —
there's a queue of people wanting to buy.

Nobody is saying don't make that decision.
**Just know it's a decision to sell your own asset.**

Same place as Chapter 7.

## Write two lines before you launch

The most practical part of the chapter.

Write two lines before launching and you avoid most accidents.

**Line one.**

> **Why does this have to happen today rather than next month?**

The answer has to be one of the five.
If you answered "because it's good," you aren't ready.

**Line two.**

> **Can I use that answer again next year?**

If you can, you built a device that keeps working.
If you can't, **you played a card.**

Playing a card isn't wrong.
**You just have to be counting how many you have left.**

## And after it takes off is the real thing

Remember Chapter 6? Dying by growing.

Launching only **brings people in.**
**Keeping them is a completely different job.**

Which is why Chapter 15 sits directly after this one.

> **Do 14 without 15 and you become a Part Two story.**

Everything that went off loudly once and disappeared split right there.

## Do This Today

Think of one thing you're making now.

A video, a drawing, a club sign-up — doesn't matter.

Ask it one line.

> **Why does this have to be seen today rather than next month?**

Write the answer. Then tag which of the five it is.

Deadline · Scarcity · Season · Being seen · Others did.

If it's none of the five, you're persuading **purely on good.**

Then one more line.

> **Can I use this answer next year?**

If not, use it knowing you're spending a card.

And last:

**Which of the five have you never once used?**

For most people it's **the season.**
Which is also the cheapest and the longest-lasting.
