## The drawer full of chargers

Every house has one drawer that is only cables and chargers.

Take them out and look at them one by one.
**About half, you won't know what they plugged into.**

Here's the strange part.

**Those devices weren't thrown out because they broke.**

Most went into the drawer still working, and got binned one day later.

Different from the last chapter.
Those things got boring when the fad ended.

**These never got boring.** There was just nowhere left to use them.

## The usual thought — something better came along

The idea is they lost a competition.

**Half right.**

Some did lose. But look at what vanished and it doesn't add up.

Think of the dedicated satnav that stuck to a car windscreen.
Being dedicated, it had a bigger screen and responded faster.

Torch, voice recorder, calculator, alarm clock, camera.
**Each of those did its own job better too.**

They all lost anyway. **They didn't lose — their spot disappeared.**

There are three ways a spot disappears. They're completely different.

## One · Getting swallowed

Satnav, music player, torch, voice recorder, calculator, camera.

That whole list got sucked into one place. **The phone.**

Notice something.

**The swallower didn't win by being better.**

The dedicated satnav had a bigger screen.
The dedicated camera took better photos.
The dedicated recorder captured better sound.

They lost anyway. One reason:

> **Whatever's already in your pocket wins.**

People don't only count performance when choosing.
**They also count how many things they have to carry.**

If one thing does the job, carrying a second one requires that second
thing to be more than "a bit better."
**It has to be "can't do without it."**

So the places where dedicated devices survive are fixed.
Professional work. Situations where lives are at stake. Rough conditions.

**There, "a bit better" becomes "can't do without it."**

And this one comes with good news.

**Getting swallowed is predictable.**

Look at what people always carry.
You can mostly guess what it will swallow next.
**What can be swallowed is what can fit inside as a part.**

## Two · A house built on a bridge

Some things sit exactly between two eras.

Needed briefly, between **the old way** and **the completely different way.**

That's a house built on a bridge.

It's useful while people are crossing the river.
**Once everyone's across, nobody needs the bridge.**

These things have one trait in common.

**They solved their era's problem extremely well.**

Solving it well made them refined inside that problem,
and when the problem disappeared they went with it.

**Being well made tied them in deeper.**

The Chapter 2 sentence comes back:

> **Hold onto a method and you disappear with the method.**

That object was holding onto "how music gets stored,"
not "listening to music."

And this one is **hard to see coming.**
Whether a far bank will appear, and when, is difficult to know.
Chapter 15 comes back to this problem.

## Three · Nobody ever asked for it

The third is a different animal entirely.

Home television you watched in 3D glasses got pushed hard for several years.
Manufacturers pushed together, it got the front spot in shops,
broadcasts were prepared.

Then it quietly disappeared.

The stand-on personal transporter was similar.
It came with talk of cities being redesigned around it.

These two differ from the first two in one decisive way.

**They never had a spot to lose.**

Swallowing and bridges are spots that existed and then went.
This is **a failed attempt to create a spot.**

Which gives this chapter's most useful question:

> **Did people ask for this, or did we make it and show it to them?**

If it's the second, there's no spot yet.

Making and showing isn't bad.
Plenty of things nobody asked for changed the world.

But then **the cost of creating the spot goes into the sums too.**
That cost is usually larger than the cost of making the thing.

And here's the truly dangerous part of this route.

**Units sold is not evidence that people want it.**

The sets sold. Very few people actually switched the feature on.

That's precisely the reason manufacturers gave when they dropped it.
**No demand.**
**That's the reason given by the people who had been pushing it,
as they stopped.**

Sold and used can be different numbers. That's the most important line
in this chapter.

And this route is **the easiest one to see coming.**
That nobody asked for it is a fact you already have on day one.

## Swallowed by the same thing, and still here

Here's the counter-example, otherwise this chapter is just surrender.

**The phone swallowed the clock too.**
Purely as a way of telling the time, the wristwatch was done.

**Wristwatches did not disappear.**

The reason is the core of this whole book.

The wristwatch **stopped being a tool for telling the time.**
It became a thing you wear, and a thing you hand down.

It stepped off the field where function competes and sat somewhere else.

That's **changing clocks**, from Chapter 2.
And it used two of the four bridges. **Shape and Handoff.**

> **What gets swallowed is a function, not an object.
> Only objects that are nothing but function go down with the function.**

Turn that around and you get the work.

**Count what your thing has besides function.**
If the answer is nothing, a better function isn't the answer.

Part Three is entirely about how to count.

## The short version

| Route | What happened | Can you see it coming | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Swallowed** | the always-carried thing ate the function | **yes, easily** | build value outside function |
| **On a bridge** | that step stopped being taken | hard | hold the need, not the method |
| **Pushed** | there was never a spot | **easiest of all** | ask whether anyone asked |

All three share one thing.

**The product was fine right to the end.**

So quality stops none of them.
**Raising quality is especially dangerous in front of swallowing.**
The better you make it, the deeper it's tied to that function.

## Where This Came From

Public sources cross-checked.

1. Home 3D television: when manufacturers dropped the feature from new
   models one after another, and that the reason they gave was lack of demand.
2. The stand-on personal transporter: when production ended, and that around
   then the product was a very small share of the company's revenue.

The claim that 3D came bundled into premium models could not be confirmed,
so it was **cut from the text.** Only the manufacturers' stated reason remains.

## Do This Today

Pick one **device that does exactly one job** that you still use.

Write two things.

**One,** is there already something in your pocket that could do that job?

**Two,** if there is, why do you still use this device?

If the only answer to the second is **"it does it better,"**
that device is headed for the drawer eventually.

If the answer has something non-functional in it — it's worn to your hand,
someone gave it to you, you like the time you spend using it —
that device has already changed spots.

> **What does the thing you make have besides function?**
