## Five hundred shops in two years

There was a shop everyone queued for.

Fruit dipped in hardened sugar, on a stick.
One year it was suddenly everywhere. Every side street had one.

The company put out an announcement.
Two years in, it had opened its **five hundredth shop.**

**Seven months later the count was four hundred and ninety.**

Not up. Down.

Across three months that same year, from spring into early summer,
**a hundred and twenty-seven** shops in that trade closed nationwide.

And the closing got faster every month.
Eleven in the first month, twenty-eight two months later,
forty-nine the month after that.

**Seven months from the party for shop number five hundred.**

## One thing for a thousand years

Now a different shop.

It sits on the approach road to a shrine in Kyoto.
**It sells one thing: grilled rice cakes.**

It has reportedly been on that spot since around the year 1000.

The person running it now is the **twenty-fifth generation.**

The most striking thing about this shop isn't what it sells.
**It's what it refuses to do.**

No department store counters. No festival stalls.
No new items. No branches.

The family's explanation is one line.
**Not growing the business is the policy.**

And once you see *why* the shop is in that particular spot,
you have most of this book.

**The shrine in front of it draws people who come to pray.**

They come every year. And **every year they are different people.**
That flow has not broken in a thousand years.

## Put the two numbers side by side

**Five hundred, and a thousand years.**

The first number is big. The second is slow.
The first took two years. The second took ten centuries.

The usual question here is:

> Which one was better at business?

**That question being wrong is where this book starts.**

Put them on one line and no answer comes out.
One made a lot. One made it for a long time.
**Those aren't values you can compare.**

> **It isn't that one did well and one did badly.
> They did different work.**

One sold **what people were talking about.**
The other sold **what people keep needing.**

Talk ends. Need doesn't.

So one earned big and finished,
and the other earned small and didn't finish.

## This happens in your classroom too

You don't have to go to Kyoto.

Think of the one thing everyone in your class had last year.

Who has one now?

**That's the five hundred.**

Now think of something in the same room that's been there for years.
Nobody calls it a trend. Everybody uses it.

**That's the thousand years.**

They were in the same room at the same time.
**And their lifespans were nothing alike.**

Sixteen chapters on where that difference comes from.

## This book does not take a side

Saying it up front:

This is not a book where trends are bad and lasting is good.

Sometimes you should go loud. Sometimes that's the correct move.
Plenty of good things ended unknown because nobody went loud.

There's exactly one thing this book is against:

> **Preparing to take off
> and not preparing to land.**

Everything in Part Two split right there.

## The ending, in advance

Here's a sentence from the end of the book, now.
Knowing it makes the rest read better.

> **Most things that lasted were not planned to last.
> They just never went away.**

Why is that good news?

Planning favors people who are good at planning.
**Not going away favors people who don't quit.**

And that's a much better game for you.

Chapter 16 does that properly.

## Where this came from

This book is careful with company names and product names.
**Names age.** The rule is spelled out in the next piece.

The rule applies to this opening too, so no names appear above.
Here's what to check them against.

1. The five-hundredth-shop announcement by the sugared-fruit chain,
   and its shop count seven months later.
   Company announcements and franchise disclosure filings.
2. Closures across that whole trade and the month-by-month trend.
   Korean Ministry of the Interior and Safety local-permit data.
3. Ichiwa (一文字屋和輔), the grilled rice cake shop by Imamiya Shrine in
   Kyoto: founding period, current generation, and the no-expansion policy.
   The shop's own material and its Guinness World Records entry.

All three are **public sources cross-checked.**
No original filings were opened. That belongs in the record too.
