## "Barely used"

Search for something on a secondhand app and sometimes you get a strange screen.

**Hundreds of the same object listed.**
Even the photo angles match.

The descriptions are nearly identical.

> Barely used. Just taking up space, so clearing it out.

The sellers don't know each other.
They bought at the same time and they're selling at the same time.

**This thing wasn't thrown out because it broke.**

Not one line complains about quality. They all say it works fine.
It works fine and nobody uses it.

Strange — not long ago you couldn't get one.
**What got cut?**

## The usual thought — fad products are cheap junk, so they go away

The idea is that it's a make-it-cheap, sell-it-fast, get-out business.

**That's less than half right.**

Plenty of fad products are well made.
The dessert people queued for wasn't bad.
The gadget everybody bought wasn't unusable.

And there's a bigger problem with this idea.

If cheapness were the cause, **the answer would be easy.** Make it well.
If that answer were right, Part Two would be three pages long.

**Well-made ones vanished too. So that isn't the reason.**

Three cases.

## The food fad — the queue was the product

For a while the same sign appeared on every street.
Taiwanese castella did that, then sugar-glazed fruit skewers did.

**The chain in the opening that hit five hundred shops in two years
is the skewer one.**

Both were selling the **talk** from Chapter 1.

Careful here.
What people bought wasn't just the food.

**The queue was half the value.**

Having queued for it. Having a photo of that.
That was part of the product.

Which gives this business a strange property:

> **The moment supply grows, half the product disappears.**

Available everywhere means no queue,
and no queue means no reason for the photo.

These foods have one more thing in common.

**They aren't hard to make.**

Buy the machine, or learn it in a few days. There's no way to block it.
Trademark it and a similar name opens next door.

So supply grows much faster than demand.
**And you find out you crossed the line only after you've crossed it.**

## The owning fad — worth nothing once everyone has one

There was a palm-sized electronic device that swept through kids —
a toy where you raised a digital pet.

Different from food; you don't eat it and it's gone.
It ended faster anyway.

The reason is in Chapter 2.

**A thing on the fashion clock is no fun to own alone.**

The rest of your class has to have one for it to be a conversation.
When the conversation ends, the one in your hand loses value with it.

**The object is unchanged and its value sits in other people's hands.**

That creates an odd timetable:

**The moment everyone has one is both the peak and the end.**
The best-selling month is the most dangerous month.

## The heard fad — it shrinks as you use it

That song that played everywhere one summer.
The next year you barely hear it.

This is where the structure of the chapter is clearest.

**Listening is using it up.**

Wearing clothes doesn't reduce them; using a device leaves it there.
Talk shrinks when used.

To someone hearing the same song for the twentieth time,
it isn't the song it was the first time.
The song didn't change. **The newness ran out.**

The first two were doing the same thing.
The queue and the toy were being used up as they were used.

## What got cut first

All three in one table.

| | What was sold | What got cut first | The object |
|---|---|---|---|
| Queued-for dessert | food + **the queue** | supply grew, queue vanished | fine |
| Palm-sized toy | toy + **the rest of the class** | attention moved on | works |
| Song of that summer | song + **newness** | repetition wore it out | unchanged |

Read the middle column down and you have the answer.

> **Talk shrinks when used. And using it is the same act as selling it.**

That's the real problem with a fad product.
**The more you sell, the less you have to sell.**

Which is the opposite of most making.
Usually more volume means lower costs and a firmer position.
**Here, more volume means less remaining lifespan.**

## The real danger is somewhere else

Everyone knows the above. Nobody thinks fads last forever.

**The real danger isn't that it ends. It's when it ends.**

The curve doesn't look symmetrical.

Going up takes months. People find out one at a time.

Coming down isn't like that. **It goes flat all at once.**

The reason everyone loses interest simultaneously is simple.
The signal that kills interest is also talk.
"That's over now" spreads just as fast.

*(No data was found that shows this asymmetry in numbers.
This is an observation and it's labelled as one.)*

So in this business **the last one in loses everything.**

Someone sees that it's selling best, decides to enter,
and stands in front of a dead market a few months later
when they finally open.

> **Losses in a fad business don't come from chasing a fad.
> They come from confirming the fad and then chasing it.**

## So should you not make fad products?

No. As Chapter 1 said, a fad gives you things nothing else gives you.

The problem isn't making one. It's **how much you stake on it.**

Treat a fad product like a fad product.
Most people treat it **like something that will last.**

| | Like a fad | Like it will last (dangerous) |
|---|---|---|
| Space | rent short | sign long |
| Making | have someone else make it | buy the equipment |
| Stock | order under | order comfortably |
| Payback | **all of it within months** | across years |

The last row is the point.

**A fad product's payback period has to be shorter than the fad.**

Write that on paper at the start and you prevent most of the accidents.
There's one reason people don't write it.
**While it's going well, nobody wants to think about the end.**

## And where the money goes

Money made from a fad goes one of two places.

**One: making the same fad bigger.** Chapter 6 is what that produces.

**Two: buying a bridge.**

One of the four named in Chapter 2 — Repeat, Shape, Patina, Handoff.
**They can only be bought while the fad is still alive.**

Most of the things in Part Three made the second choice.

> **Spend fad money on the fad and it ends.
> Spend fad money on a position and it stays.**

## The short version

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What was selling | the object + **the talk** |
| Why it ended | talk shrinks when used, and selling is using |
| Was the product the problem | no — so better making doesn't stop it |
| Most dangerous moment | **the best-selling month** |
| Who loses most | the last one in, who waited for confirmation |
| What to do | payback shorter than the fad; buy a bridge with the money |

## Do This Today

Open a secondhand app.
**Search the name of one object in your house.**

See how many are listed. Read three of the descriptions.

- Lots of "broken" or "upgrading" means it's still standing in its spot
- **Lots of "barely used" means it was talk**

Then one last question.

> **When people list the thing you made, what will they write?**

If "I don't use it anymore" comes to mind, you haven't found the spot yet.
**Finding a spot can be done late. It's much cheaper while the fad is alive.**
