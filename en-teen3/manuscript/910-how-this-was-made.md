## Why print this

Books don't usually have this section.

But this one kept saying **"if it couldn't be confirmed, it isn't in here."**
You should be able to check whether that's true.

So three things go here.

**One,** what level the checking was at.
**Two,** where names were deliberately left out.
**Three,** what was cut entirely.

The third matters most. Almost no book prints it.

## One · What level the checking was at

Honestly, in one line:

> **Several public sources cross-checked against each other.**

Meaning: company announcements, disclosure filings,
statistics published by public bodies, what a shop itself stated —
found, and checked for contradictions.

**And it stopped there.**

This book **did not open trademark registries, patent documents,
original corporate filings, or weekly chart records.**

So this is **not primary-source verification. It's cross-checking.**

Those are different things, and saying so is correct.

Of sixteen chapters, **eight** carry a **Where This Came From** section:
4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12. The opening carries one too.

The other eight have none because **they contain no case to check.**
Chapters 1, 2, 3, 9 and 13 through 16 are structure throughout.

## Two · Where names were deliberately left out

This book almost never names a company or a product.

**Two reasons.**

**One. Names age.**

A name that's famous now may mean nothing in ten years,
and then the sentence stops reading.

**Two. Printing an unconfirmed name makes it a lie.**

The second matters more.

Here's every place a name was deliberately left out.

| Where | What isn't named | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | the chain; the Kyoto rice cake shop | name rule (given in the sources) |
| Chapter 5 | the 3D television makers | name rule |
| Chapter 7 | **when** the trainer moved to everyday wear | not found |
| Chapter 8 | the bottle trademark **registration years** | sources disagree |
| Chapter 10 | the **novel** that entered the curriculum | couldn't be identified |
| Chapter 11 | the military food's **country and product** | couldn't be confirmed |
| Chapter 12 | song, singer and drama | name rule |

Seven.

If you read something and thought "so what *was* it?" — it's probably here.

## Three · What was cut entirely

The most important list.

**Writing vaguely and moving on is the worst option.**
A reader takes a hedged sentence as fact.

So anything unconfirmed was **cut whole, not softened.**

**One. That 3D came bundled into premium models (Chapter 5).**
Plausible and unconfirmed. Only the manufacturers' stated reason remains.

**Two. Numbers for the fad curve being asymmetric (Chapter 4).**
It looks right as an observation and no data was found.
**The text says the data wasn't found** and leaves it as an observation.

**Three. A real example of a manufactured season (Chapter 9).**
Which company did what and when is recorded differently in different places.
**The example was cut and only the conditions remain.**

**Four. A case of a cost falling below a line (Chapter 10).**
The structure holds; no matching case was found. Structure only.

**Five. Why that particular song was rediscovered (Chapter 12).**
Not a lack of data — **it can't be said in principle.**
There's no way to count how many songs met the same conditions and weren't.
**Without the denominator there's no cause.**

**Six. That old customers left after the wave (Chapter 12).**
No case could confirm it. Structure only.

Six cut.

## And one thing that isn't a case

Chapter 6 opens with three identical signs on one street.

**That's a scene, not a real case.** The text says so.

Mixing those is not allowed, so **anything that's a scene is labelled a scene**
in this book. There's exactly one.

## What's different from the adult book

There's an adult book called *Why Is This Still Here?*
Twenty chapters. This one has sixteen.

What changed:

| Changed | How |
|---|---|
| Voice | direct address throughout |
| Chapters | twenty → **sixteen** |
| Chapter dropped | "Sold Few, Held Deep" |
| Cases swapped | things a teenager never saw → classroom equivalents |
| "One Line Today" | → **"Do This Today"** |

**Why "Sold Few, Held Deep" was dropped.**

Its conclusion is "depth gives lifespan and doesn't give a living."
An adult reads that as "so put depth first in the order."
**A teenager reads it as "so doing this doesn't pay."**

If the same sentence reads differently, don't print it.
The one usable piece went into Chapter 16 instead:
**deep liking makes people talk without being asked.**

## Finally

Having written the lists out:

**six things cut and seven names withheld.**

The book would have been more fun without the cutting.
Specific names and numbers read far better.

They were cut anyway.

> **A dull fact beats an entertaining lie.
> And writing down what you cut makes the dull part a bit more interesting.**

Whatever you make, take this one thing from here.

**Writing down what you don't know.**
It's the hardest part, and it lasts the longest.
