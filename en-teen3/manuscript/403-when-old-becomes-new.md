## A thirty-seven-year-old song went to number one

An old song was used in one season of a drama.
It played over an important scene, and that scene travelled.

Then this happened.

**The song went to number one on that country's official chart.**

The song had first been released **thirty-seven years earlier.**

And one more thing.
The same singer's previous number one had been **forty-four years earlier.**
Reportedly the longest gap between number ones in that chart's history.

One thing to confirm:

**Most of the people who put that song at number one weren't born
when it came out.**

So to them it wasn't an old song.

**It was a song they were hearing for the first time.**

## The usual thought — old and new are fixed

**Half right.**

The date it was made doesn't change. True.

But **whether it's new to the listener isn't decided by the date.**

> **The difference between old and new isn't in the object.
> It's in the age of the person looking.**

Read that once more.

You've had this happen.
**Something you loved as new turned out to be from a long time ago.**

A song, some clothes, a game.
Didn't that feel slightly strange?

That feeling is the whole chapter.

**It wasn't new to the world. It was new to you.**

## Why this keeps happening more

Old things used to be kept in old places.
You had to go and find them, so people who didn't look never knew.

Not anymore.

**Things in a recommendation feed don't have dates on them.**

Side by side on a screen, last year's and forty years ago's look the same.
Same font size, same thumbnail.

So **age is invisible.**

When age is invisible, old things **stand in the same queue as new ones.**

> **Once an old thing stands in the same queue as new ones,
> all that's left is whether it's good.**

And in that queue the old thing has an advantage.
**It survived long enough to get there, so it's already been filtered once.**

## Not everything gets rediscovered

Time to be honest.

**Being discovered is luck.** Genuinely luck.

Which scene gets which song, whether that scene travels —
nobody decides that. Not even the maker.

So "let's make it get discovered" is not a plan.

But **being in a state where you can ride it when luck arrives** can be built.
That's an entirely different thing.

Riding the wave needs three things.

**One. It has to still exist.**

The most basic and the most missed.
Gone means undiscoverable. Dead company, discontinued product, deleted video.

**Waves only come to things that are still there.**

**Two. It has to be findable.**

Searching the name has to return it.
Someone watches the scene, thinks "what's that song," and
**it has to come up in three seconds.**

If it doesn't, people move on. Everyone is busy.

**Three. It has to be gettable immediately.**

Found and unbuyable ends it there.
Cooling off takes less than a day.

One line:

> **Even when a wave comes: gone, unfindable, or unbuyable means it passes by.**

**Which is why only things that stayed a long time ride that wave.**

Staying looks dull. Staying is how you buy the ticket.

## And after the wave passes

Know this too.

Waves pass. Weeks, a few months at most.

Afterwards it's one of three things.

**One. Back to where it was.** The most common.

**Two. It settles slightly higher.** Some of the new arrivals stay.

**Three. It becomes a completely different thing.** Rare.

**Aim at the second.**

Assume everyone who came on the wave will stay
and you'll scale up to that number, and Chapter 6 happens. You die by growing.

> **A wave doesn't give you customers. It gives you a chance.
> Keeping them is what you do next.**

## Do This Today

Write down five things you like.
Songs, films, games, objects — anything.

Now look up **when each one came out.**

**How many of the five came out before you were born?**

If none did, you're only looking at what's being made now.
That means standing only on the fashion clock from Chapter 2.

If two or more did, you're already choosing **filtered** things.

And one last question.

Take the oldest one and ask:

> **Why is this still here?**

Write the answer down.
**What you build in Part Five should look like that answer.**
