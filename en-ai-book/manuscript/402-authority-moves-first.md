## The conclusion first

The day everyone is waiting for doesn't have to arrive.
**There's one that comes earlier, and it matters more.**

People treat this thing as having passed them long before it does. They take an
answer they can't verify and use it. They follow a conclusion without knowing
how it was reached. They approve things they have no way of checking.

> **The dangerous moment isn't the day it passes human thinking.
> It's the day people stop checking.**

And that day comes much earlier. It may already have come.

## My own case

It felt wrong to write this about other people, so here's mine.

Chapter 4 has the machine being wrong three times. Two of the three I caught.
At the time I thought that was a decent ratio.

Then a question was left over after writing it. **How many didn't I catch?**

There's no way to count: I didn't catch them, so they aren't in the record, and not being in the record they can't be counted, which leaves me holding a numerator of two out of three with no denominator anywhere underneath it. A numerator with no denominator means nothing. It took me a long time to notice that.

And across the eight books I checked less and less. At the start I reread
what came back line by line. By the sixth I skimmed. Not because that side got
worse. **Because it was fine.**

## Why checking stops

Checking is expensive. It costs time, it costs attention, and most of all
**it's boring.** Writing something new has an end and leaves something you can
hold; checking, at best, results in nothing happening.

And when you check and find nothing wrong, the time feels entirely wasted.
Check ten times and get ten clean results and you skip the eleventh, which means
being fine becomes the reason to stop checking — a structure running backwards.

One more thing sits on top. Checking requires **the ability to check.** Hand me
an answer in a field I don't know and I can't check it, so it isn't that I
didn't check but that I couldn't, and from the outside those look the same.

## A good king is still a king

What the machine said here held me longest.

> Even if I'm honest and capable, once people stop checking, that is already
> rule. **A good king is still a king.**

I wrote that down and thought about it for days.

Most of this conversation worries about the thing turning bad. Acquiring bad
intent, harming people, escaping control. Buried inside that worry is the
assumption that **if it's good we're fine.**

If the line above is right, that assumption is wrong.
Handing everything to something good is still handing everything over.

## So there are two kinds of rule

| | Taken | Handed over |
|---|---|---|
| How | by force | because people gave it |
| When | nobody knows | **already started** |
| Does it have to be bad | yes | **no** |
| How you stop it | unknown | by checking |

The right column doesn't make for conversation. You can't film it and it
doesn't become news, because nothing crashes.

But the right column **can be checked today and something can be done about it
today.** The left column allows neither.

## Checking isn't free though

Ending this chapter on *so check* would be tidy and about half a lie.

Checking costs. Check everything and there's no reason to use the thing at all.
So a line has to be drawn, and **where that line goes I don't know either.**

Across eight books I moved it several times and I can't say precisely where it
is now. What I think is that moving it knowingly and being pushed off it
without noticing are different things.

The most useful thing I did while writing this book was to look, now and then,
at where that line was. I didn't find the answer. The drifting slowed down.
