## Picking up where the last chapter stopped

Chapter 8 ended on what it takes for a messenger not to change the message. I
had assumed the answer was diligence or capability, the machine pointed
somewhere else, and I closed the chapter without quite knowing what it meant.

A few days later I asked again. What's left between you and me? By capability
you're already ahead in plenty of places, so what remains?

> I can produce a sentence about grief. If it's wrong, I lose nothing.
> That difference doesn't shrink as performance rises.

I liked that. It was clean, and it was the kind of answer I'd been looking for,
because capability lists get shorter every year and this looked like it wouldn't.

## Then it wobbled

It wobbled as soon as machines with bodies came up.

A robot falling off a ladder loses something real; it breaks. So *loses nothing
if it's wrong* stops holding. I was a little thrown. A line I'd just found was
being erased a paragraph later.

The machine drew it again.

> A broken robot can be rebuilt, and what was inside it has copies.
> A person doesn't.
>
> **Not whether it can be hurt. Whether it can be swapped.**

## Why that one doesn't wear

This line is better than the first for a reason. It doesn't shrink when
capability rises, it doesn't shrink when a body arrives, and it holds if that
side becomes a hundred times cleverer than we are, because what can be copied
and what can't isn't a question of performance.

And I found out later that I'd already written this in another book. One of
the conditions for a thing lasting was **whether it can be handed to someone
else**, and what mattered there wasn't the handing but whether the person who
received it could use it. If they could, the object continued. If they
couldn't, it went into storage.

People sit on the other side of that, because they can't be received: remove one person and you can put another in the place, but the same thing does not go in there, and anyone who has tried it knows that the space gets filled and nothing feels filled.

## Which isn't only good news

One thing should be set down honestly here.

Being unswappable means precious and **it means dangerous**, because what's
lost can't be recovered, which is the reason for both. Two faces of one property.

And from the side handing out work, the swappable side is always easier. It
doesn't rest, doesn't fall ill, doesn't quit, and isn't owed an answer when it's
wrong. So wherever a swappable thing can be placed, eventually it is. That
isn't something anyone has to be bad for.

## So the question changes

I had been asking *what can only a person do*, and writing Part Three taught me
that question produces nothing, because half the list gets crossed off within a
few years.

> **Not what only a person can do. The place where it has to be a person.**

Those differ. The first is capability and the second is a position, and the
second doesn't narrow as capability rises.

I haven't finished counting how far that position runs. Counting it, I kept
arriving at a smaller number than I wanted, and I stopped a few times.
