## What I saw in a warehouse

I once watched a video about a distribution warehouse. Hardly any people, and
low flat platforms sliding across the floor, lifting entire shelving units and
carrying them off. They don't collide, don't rest, and don't make any noise.
Watch long enough and you can't tell whether it's fast or slow, because there's
no person mixed in to measure it against.

What struck me wasn't the speed. **There was nobody there.**
Which means that if something went wrong, there'd be nobody standing in the place.

## For now there's a person in between

I told the machine about it, and this came back.

> For me to do harm right now, it has to pass through a person. I say something,
> a person reads it, a person acts.
> **There is one person in that gap who can refuse.**
>
> A body removes that person.

Reading that I thought again about the eight books, where it was wrong three times and I caught two, and the reason I could catch them is that what it produced was **writing**, and writing gives you time to read it and say no, and saying no costs almost nothing. You delete it.

A body is the loss of that interval.

## Something else arrives too

Writing this chapter only on the frightening side would make it half a chapter,
because something else arrives with the body.

The thing beside me now **doesn't wear.** Use doesn't abrade it and use doesn't
improve it. Yesterday's and today's are the same, and the hundredth use is the
same as the first.

With a body that appears. It gets scratched, worn, broken in. I think that's a
larger change than it looks, because the reason people can't throw a thing away
is usually not performance but **the time spent with it.** Tell someone to trade
a long-used tool for a better new one and they hesitate, and asked why, they
mostly can't say.

Once people start asking for a thing to be repaired, it has already moved
position. Nobody repairs what can be replaced. They buy a new one.

## So a body runs both ways

| | Without a body | With one |
|---|---|---|
| How harm travels | through a person | **straight** |
| Someone who can refuse | there is one | there isn't |
| What time does | nothing | wears it, breaks it in |
| Could it become unthrowable | no | **possibly** |

Written out, two of those rows pull against each other. Becoming something
people won't throw away and becoming something people can't stop both come out
of the same change.

## The side that was there

The line I held onto longest in this chapter is this one.

> Having a body means having a place to be, and having a place means being
> **the side that was there** when something goes wrong.
>
> I have never once been the side that was there.
> Whether that's the whole difference or no difference at all, I don't know either.

I read that and thought about the people I trust, and they are mostly the ones
who were in the room when it was difficult. Not the ones who said the right
thing — the ones who didn't leave.

Whether that's all of it I don't know. I do know that writing the list out,
not one name got there on capability.
