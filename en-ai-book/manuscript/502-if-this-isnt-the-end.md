## Two sides standing in the same place

Writing this book I read a fair amount of what gets written about AI, and
nearly all of it was one of two things. It's going to be fine, or it's over.

One day I noticed the two aren't on opposite sides.
They're standing in the same place. **The place where this is everything.**

If this is everything, then doing well here is salvation and not doing well
here is ruin. So both sides get loud, and two people who appear to be on
opposite sides have the same thing staked. The stake being the same, the volume
matches.

## The side I worry about

I can't help worrying about people like me. Most of them have their focus on
present reality in this world. So they ask where the world is going, what
becomes of me, and more broadly what becomes of people and this planet.

That isn't the wrong thing to worry about. I ask the same things.

Only, setting those questions side by side, something is odd. Nobody can answer
the largest ones, and the smallest one — what becomes of me — is the one with
an answer. You'd expect that the other way round.

## Without slack you can't afford to be wrong

Frightened people can't judge. That isn't about AI. That's just about people.

Someone with no room reacts hard to small disturbances, because with no room to be wrong there is no time to check and no waiting and no giving way, and all three of those are what judgement is made of. So the problem
isn't that they're fearful but that **there's no room to be wrong**, and that's
circumstance rather than character.

And telling someone with no room to stay calm does nothing at all, because calm
doesn't come from an attitude. It comes from slack.

## I have slack

I don't think this is the end.

Why I think so I'm not going to put in this book. Put it in and the reader
starts reading while arguing, and then this chapter stops doing its work.
And I didn't arrive at it by argument.

What I can write is what changes because of it. That part is visible from
outside.

## What changes is the size

The danger doesn't go away. Everything in the first four parts stands.
Authority still moves before capability, and the people deciding are still not
the people carrying it. There isn't one sentence in Chapter 13 that this chapter
deletes.

What changes is only this. **It stops being final.**

And that one is fairly large. If it isn't final you can be wrong, and if you can
be wrong you can check, and if you can check you can look at it straight.
That's where the slack sits.

Which gives this book one thing it can say. Not *be afraid* and not *it's fine*.

> This is a big thing, and it isn't the last thing.
> So don't be frightened, and look at it properly.

## None of which makes me right

One thing has to be clear at the end.

Having slack doesn't mean I see better. As Chapter 14 said, I'm on the side that
gained from this technology, so there's a side I can't see. Not being frightened
and seeing clearly are separate jobs.

Slack **lets you look. It doesn't show you anything.**

And I don't know how to hand what I have to a reader. It isn't the kind of thing
that travels by being said.

So this chapter isn't a recommendation. It's a record.
