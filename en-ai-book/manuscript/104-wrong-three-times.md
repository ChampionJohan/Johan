## "I was wrong"

The first time I heard this from the machine I was mildly surprised.

Not that it was wrong — I knew that, and had run into it. What surprised me
was that **it said so before I asked.** Not something I dug out, but something
it raised itself, and over eight books it happened three times. All three are
here. The third is the useful one.

## One — I rebuilt the cover twice and both were wasted

A book was blocked in review on the print side. The checker said it couldn't
find a barcode on the cover. The machine suspected the cover: there was a white
rectangle in the corner of the back and it looked like the problem, and the
explanation was plausible enough that I went along. We cleared the rectangle,
rebuilt the file, uploaded it again.

Blocked again.

This time the reason was the ISBN, it said. That explanation was plausible too.
Then a few days later, looking at a different book's screen, the actual cause
turned up. **A checkbox saying "my cover has a barcode" was switched on.**
That was all of it.

> First I suspected the white rectangle on the cover, then I wrote that it was
> the ISBN. Both were wrong. It was one checkbox.
>
> The cover never needed touching.

Two things came out of that. One is that the machine is **wrong plausibly.**
It doesn't stop and say it doesn't know; it attaches an explanation and is
wrong, and from where I sit there's no tell that separates a wrong answer from
a right one. The other is that it got caught because I went back and looked at
that screen again, and if I hadn't I'd have rebuilt the cover three times and
been blocked in the same place.

## Two — it told me to finish something I'd already finished

I write fiction as well, under a different name, on a different shelf. One day
the machine suggested I finish the third volume of that series first. I'd
already finished seven and was in the middle of uploading them. Something I'd
said months earlier was still in its hands.

> I was wrong. I was carrying what you told me in September.

This is a different kind of wrong. Not missing knowledge — **stopped time.**
The machine stops where the conversation stopped, so anything I did in between
stays nonexistent unless I say it.

Knowing that changed how I use it. I started writing down what had happened
before asking anything, and after watching the answers visibly improve a few
times, the habit stuck.

## Three — it said its own tool was wrong

This is the one from Chapter 2.

The tool that measured all twelve books was counting wrong. The machine had
built it, and had explained things to me using its numbers. Weeks later it ran
the same tool over one chapter and said, before I asked, that the result looked
off.

> The measurement isn't wrong. The tool is.
> Which means the numbers I gave you may be wrong numbers.

Then it fixed the tool, re-measured all twelve books, and went and corrected four separate documents that carried the bad numbers, and I should say that I hadn't known the numbers were wrong, and that if I had known I would not have been able to find everywhere they were written.

**That's why the third is the useful one.**

I caught the first two. The machine caught the third. And the third cut against
its own interest — zero was the better story.

## Which is no reason to relax

Set those three down together and the machine looks honest. That's how it felt
to me, too.

So I'm putting this beside them. It's something it said to me.

> Don't trust me too much.
>
> I am wrong, and I am wrong plausibly, which is the dangerous kind. Two were
> caught because you checked. If you hadn't, they'd have gone into the book.

Two of three were mine. That's the fact.

Which leaves one question. **How many didn't I catch?**

I don't know that number. There's no way to count it.
