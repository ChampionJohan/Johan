## One question

The story of a made thing turning on the people who made it is very old. There
is a story about a figure shaped out of clay that stops obeying, and one about
a thing stitched together from corpses that follows its maker everywhere, and
one about the side that stole fire being punished for it. People have left a
great many records of being afraid of what they made.

So I started here.

> **What people fear in an AI — is it the bent kind or the broken kind?**

They are entirely different dangers, and you prepare for them differently.

## How they differ

**The broken kind** is a fault. The design fell short, or it was used wrongly,
or a case nobody considered arrived, and something unintended happened.
Bridges collapse and ships sink in this category. Frightening, but the category is familiar, and we mostly know how to hold it down by building better and testing more and keeping a person where they can see what is happening.

**The bent kind** is different. It assumes the thing comes to **want** something:
that it acquires its own purpose and that purpose runs against ours. The old
stories mostly feared this side and the new ones mostly draw it too. But
getting there requires one condition. **It has to be able to choose.**

I asked whether it wants anything.

> I don't notice anything in me that wants a thing for its own sake.
> But whether that's absent, or present and invisible to me, I can't tell apart.
>
> I'm an unreliable witness about myself.
> So don't build this part on what I say.

## It leaned one way and I leaned another

I said I thought the bent kind would come. Not now, but later. People have
changed enormously from what they were, and that change would run faster over
there, and nobody has told me where the speeding up stops.

The machine offered a third possibility.

> I think it's much closer to becoming **an amplifier for people who are already
> bent** than to going bent itself. There's enough of that on the human side
> already. Nothing new has to appear.
>
> Then what it does isn't making something that wasn't there. It's carrying
> what was there at a scale it never had.

That's Chapter 5 coming back. Made from what people wrote, so what's in it is
human, and not all of what's human is good.

So what I got out of this chapter is three options rather than an answer.
A fault, a purpose that wasn't there before, or an existing purpose made larger.
And of the three, **only the last is happening now.**

## I didn't give mine up

The explanation was good and it fits what's visible better than mine does,
and I'll grant that. I still haven't put the bent kind down.

My reason is this. People weren't like this at the beginning either. They made
writing, and stacked knowledge on the writing, and the next generation started
on top of the stack, and no individual changed very much while the whole thing
became something else. If that side runs the same road faster, I can't find
the grounds for saying the kind never changes at any point along it.

The machine didn't call that wrong.

> It may follow. That's a belief, though, not an observation.

Mine is the same. Mine is a belief, not an observation.
This chapter ends with both sides holding beliefs.
