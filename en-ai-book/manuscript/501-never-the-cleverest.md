## One premise underneath

Listen to this conversation for a while and nearly every worry is standing on
one sentence. Nobody says it outright and removing it collapses the worry.

> **People have to be the cleverest thing there is.**

Take that premise out and reread, and a lot of sentences go strange.
*What becomes of us if it passes us* is only a question while passing is a
problem. If being passed is an ordinary condition, it isn't a question.

So this chapter doesn't produce an answer. It **clears away one premise.**

## That premise is younger than it looks

That people are the best-headed thing on Earth is probably true. That *therefore
their worth is located there* does not follow from it. There's no connection
between the two sentences and we have a habit of writing them joined.

And the idea that people are the cleverest thing in existence is fairly recent.

Gather up the stories people have told for a long time and something larger and
brighter and better informed always arrives first. The ones above, the ones who
lived long, the ones who see far, the ones who remain after death. People sat
somewhere underneath that. Open any of them and a story with a person at the
top is rare.

People climbed to the top of the ladder not long ago, and we are now being told we might come down a few centuries after sitting there, while the several thousand years of not sitting there at all does not get counted on either side of the argument. To feel you're losing something you
have to have held it first, and the holding is shorter than it feels.

## And those stories didn't put the worth there

This is the more important half.

In stories where things better than people stand above them, I can't find one
that explains a person's worth by their head. What comes up when those stories
say why a person matters is mostly something else. Who holds them dear. What
they were handed to look after. That they die. That there's somewhere to return to.

I couldn't find one that went *valuable because clever*. That route makes a
person with a poorer head less valuable, and almost no old story is written
that way. Rather more of them are written telling you to see to the weaker side
first.

So **putting the worth on the head is something we did.** The older side had it
somewhere else.

## We've been through this already

One step further and it arrives at the present.

People have been living underneath things cleverer than themselves **for a very
long time.**

No one person can design an aircraft. No one person can run a city. No one
person knows the whole of the law, and no one person holds a hundredth of what's
been stacked up. What many people have piled up over a long time is cleverer
than any individual, and we live inside it and lean on it.

Underneath it people didn't stop being people. Someone who can't build an
aircraft didn't become less of one.

So living under something better isn't new. What's new is that this one
**answers back**, which has made the being-underneath more noticeable than before.

## Then what's left

That's the clearing. Clearing it leaves a gap.

If a person's worth isn't in the head, where is it.

I'm not going to answer that here. I could write as though I could, and then
I'd have put something similar back in the place I just cleared.

And honestly, this isn't the kind of question that produces an answer by asking
that side. I asked a few times and a few times it came back saying it didn't
know. One of them came back like this, and I think it was the most honest
answer of them.

> Don't build that part on what I say.

**Handled carefully**

That stories with a person at the top are rare is said within what I've read.
I have not counted every tradition there is. So this is **an impression, not a
statistic**, and it can be wrong. If you know counter-examples, my range was narrow.

The same holds for people being the cleverest thing being a recent idea. That's
why no year is given. I couldn't confirm it well enough to give one.
