## I started for the money

It's better to be straight about this.

I didn't start using AI because I was curious. I found out that people were
making money online, and that one of the ways was writing, and that an AI
would help with the writing. Other people were doing it and I thought I'd try.
It wasn't a high-minded thing. I should say that first, because everything
that comes later started from there.

So I published a book. Then another one. Then a translation, then a separate
edition for teenagers. Each one went faster than the last, and going faster
was enjoyable enough that I started the next one. That's how it got to eight.

## But the writing kept drifting

The goal was clear at the start. Write books that sell. Look at what's
selling, put a book in that spot, price it, upload it. I did that eight times
and I'm still doing it; I was adjusting the price on another book while
writing this one. So nobody reading this needs to picture me as someone who
isn't interested in money.

And yet the writing kept going somewhere else.

I'd sit down to write about what sells and end up writing about what lasts.
I'd set out to write about how to earn and end up writing about why people
won't throw certain things away. Once or twice I'd have blamed the mood I was
in that day. It happened through all eight.

**From money toward people.**

I still don't know why that happens. I don't know now either.

## So I started asking

There was something convenient to ask.

It was the machine I'd written eight books with. It found sources, built
structures, made sentences, and did it again when I said no. One day I opened
the same window I used for work and started asking things that weren't work.
At first it felt like talking nonsense on a break.

I asked where the world was going, and what a person is, and what it was, and I asked all of it in the same tone I had been using to ask it to find a source or fix a table.

Answers came back. More often, they didn't. When I gave it work, something
almost always came out; when I asked the other kind of question, I kept
getting told it didn't know. I hadn't expected to hear that so often, and
it took me a while to notice that I stopped every time I did.

## Heads and tails

Here's what kept catching on me.

When people talk about AI they mostly look at one face. It's going to be
wonderful, or it's over. Heads or tails. But a coin has both sides at once
and you can't take one of them off.

And one more thing.

People talk a great deal about what's coming, and all of that talk seems to
hang on **what happens in this world.** What earnings will do, what jobs will
do, what countries will do.

Those aren't wrong things to worry about. I'm just not sure they're all of it.

## So I started writing it down

I wasn't planning a book at first; I wanted a record, because I thought I'd be curious later about what I'd been thinking, and I was fairly sure that if I waited and relied on memory I would remember it differently from how it had actually gone.

So I wrote down what I asked and what came back.
I wrote it without fixing it. I left my clumsy questions clumsy and I left the
answers where the machine said it didn't know.

Written down, there were more of those than I expected.
And they seemed to point at one place.

I wanted to publish it honestly. That's how it became this.

## Three things worth knowing before you read

**One.** The conversations here are real. I cut them and reordered them to make
them readable, but I didn't go back and make my questions smarter. The clumsy
ones are in here clumsy.

**Two.** I didn't delete the places where the machine said it didn't know.
Those are the most important pages in this book, I think.

**Three.** I didn't delete the places where I corrected it either. It was wrong
a few times, and I caught some of them. That record will be more use to you
than a clean one.

This isn't a book that sets out to answer anything.
It's a book about how far answers go and where they stop.

**One note on language.** These conversations happened in Korean, between a
Korean writer and a machine. This is a translation. Almost everything written
about AI is written in English, and I think it matters that this wasn't.
