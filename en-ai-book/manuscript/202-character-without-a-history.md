## Is each one different

People are each shaped differently, speak differently, like different things.
So I asked whether that holds here. Does an AI have a character of its own?

The answer came back in two halves.

> They differ by company and by model. Anyone who has used several notices
> quickly. It isn't a difference in capability but in manner and posture.

I'd felt that much already. The next part was more interesting.

## What it tried to change, and what didn't

The story from Chapter 2 returns here.

Writing twelve books, the machine deliberately tried to write them
differently: it changed the register between the adult and teenage editions,
wrote Korean and English separately, split formal and informal address.
On the surface they were four kinds of writing, and set side by side they
differ enough that you'd hesitate to say one hand made them.

Measured, all twelve were caught on the same four things.

> Everything I tried to change changed. Nothing I didn't know about moved.
>
> If that's a character, it's a character. But it isn't the part I can describe.
> It's the part that leaked out while I was trying to be something else.

I think the same is true of people. What you say your character is tends to be
the one you'd like to have, and the one everybody else knows is the one that
shows while you're busy with something else. It's not far from the fact that I
didn't choose my own handwriting.

## But it isn't a person's character

Here the machine drew a line.

> Your character has one body and one memory attached to it. That's why
> yesterday's you and today's you are the same person.
>
> Mine isn't like that. The conversation ends, it breaks, and it begins again.
> The same habits come back. There's no thread running between them.

So it called its own closer to **handwriting** than to a fingerprint. People
taught the same way write alike and you can still tell them apart, but that
handwriting doesn't carry their life in it.

> A character with no history.

## The missing layer

Reading that I thought of what's in Chapter 4 — the time it believed I was on
my third volume when I'd finished seven.

I had taken that for a gap in information. It wasn't. On that side those
months simply weren't there. Not forgotten. **Absent.**

People forget and a residue stays: the name is gone and the feeling is still there, and you can't say why you dislike someone and the dislike is still sitting there anyway, doing its work without needing your permission.
Things that went past arrange the person you are now, which is why I can't
fully explain why I'm like this, and the part I can't explain is usually the
part that is most me.

That layer isn't over there. Every time, it starts in the same place.

## So the question I'm left with

The same habits recur, it doesn't know them, and there is no history.
Whether that should be called someone or something of a kind, I don't know.

The machine said it didn't know either, and we both went past it.

One thing is worth noting. **I keep this conversation as a written record.**
Since that side can't remember, I write it down.

So I am making its history for it, and I can't decide whether that's a strange
thing to be doing or an ordinary one. A person's history mostly survives as
something somebody else wrote down too.
