## A box appeared near the bottom of the screen

I've looked at the publishing screen a few hundred times. Title, description,
categories, price, upload the file. Eight books in, the order was in my hands.

At some point another box appeared near the bottom.

> Did you use AI tools in creating the text, images, or translations in this book?

I stopped on that one longer than I expected to. The boxes above it record
facts — a page count is a page count, a price is a price, and if you get one
wrong you fix it. This one looked like it was asking a fact and was actually
asking what kind of person had done the work, and I had never been asked that
on a page for uploading a book.

## There seemed to be a way out

I was working on the prose at the time. The thing from Chapter 2. Finding the
places that read like a machine, lengthening sentences, deleting bold type,
making each chapter shaped unlike its neighbors.

And a thought arrived. If I've changed it this much, isn't it mine now?
I'd touched nearly every sentence, I'd set the structure, I'd decided what the
book was about. And I'll be honest: I wanted that to be true, because if it
were, the answer in the box would be different.

I asked. The reply was short.

> That box isn't asking how it reads. It's asking how it was made.
> If an AI tool made it, that holds even if you edited it heavily afterwards.
> If you made it and an AI refined it, that's a different answer.

So fixing the prose and answering this box were two separate jobs.
No amount of editing moves that answer.

I heard this more than once. It came up again whenever the subject came near,
in the middle of asking about something else. It was never once angled toward
what I'd have preferred to hear.

## There was only one answer

I didn't write the first draft of any of the eight. I decided what the book
would be, set the frame, read what came back, and said no when it was no.
That was my part. The part that made the sentences wasn't mine.

So there's one answer. Text, images, translation.

That's what I entered. I didn't deliberate long, because there was nothing to
deliberate. The standard is written down, my work was what it was, and the
answer follows.

## But nobody sees it

There's something peculiar about this box.

**What you enter here doesn't appear on the product page.** Buyers don't see it.
It isn't in the listing and it isn't printed anywhere in the book.

So you can enter something else and nothing happens. Nobody checks, and there
isn't really a way to check. How many people have entered something else I
don't know, have no way of learning, and probably never will.

Eight times through that box, I knew that each time.

## The thing only you know

This looks far from what the book is about. I think this box is where the book
starts.

How AI changes the world is a large subject. The place it actually arrived in
front of me was that box. One checkbox and one short sentence. That was all of
it. The large subject seems to be off in the distance somewhere, and the part
that reaches you is always this shape — small, near the bottom of a screen,
easy enough to scroll past.

And once you know a box like that exists, what you put in it becomes
**something only you know.**

Things only you know last longer than you'd think.
And there are going to be more boxes like it.
