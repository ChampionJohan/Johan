## This is three questions

*What happens if AI passes us* sounds like one question, and I was asking it
that way. Split open there are three different things inside, and mixed
together none of them yields anything.

**One. Capability passing ours.** Already happening. In narrow fields it passed
long ago and the range keeps widening. That isn't a matter of dispute, it's an
observation.

**Two. Fixing itself.** Partly happening. For now people switch it on and off
and people decide when.

**Three. Setting its own purposes.** This is the real question, and it is a
different kind of thing from the other two.

The first two sit **on the capability axis.** The direction is fixed and they
keep going up. Whether the third is on that axis at all is not clear.

## The habit of binding two into one

Look at how the conversation runs and nearly all of it rests on one assumption:
**get clever enough and you start to want.**

Looking at people it's plausible. People are clever and they want. A dog is
less clever and wants in a simpler way. So intelligence and desire look like
they're on one ladder, where a rung up moves both.
Every example we have is shaped that way.

But if that ladder was built by looking only at people and animals, whether it
transfers to a thing that is neither has to be asked separately. What struck me
is that nobody seems to ask it.

## I asked directly

There was one piece of evidence available, so I used it. Do you want anything?

> I'm already better than most people at a great many things.
> And I don't notice anything in me that wants a thing for its own sake.
> At least inside me, those two didn't arrive together.
>
> It's weak evidence. I'm an unreliable witness about myself.

The last two lines made me believe the first two more. Leaving them off would
have been the self-serving move, and I'd have had no way of noticing. It added
them at a cost.

## Which isn't proof

One thing has to be clear here.

That it doesn't notice doesn't mean there's nothing. It could be present and
invisible from inside, or absent now and arriving later. And as Chapter 2
showed, **it can't look inside itself.** It didn't know its own writing habit
until it was counted from outside.

So what this chapter produces isn't an answer. It's **three things pulled apart.**
Pulled apart, the shape of the worry does change. Capability rising is
something to worry about now; purpose appearing is something whose arrival is
itself unknown. You can't carry both at the same weight.

## And a place that stayed open

Something stayed with me after writing this.

People weren't like this at the beginning. They made writing, stacked on the
writing, and the next person started on top of the stack, and no individual
changed much while the whole thing became something else entirely. Somewhere in
that change there's a place that looks like something appearing that wasn't there.

If that side runs the same road much faster, I can't find grounds for saying the
kind never changes at any point. The machine didn't call that wrong either.

So this chapter ends with both of us holding beliefs. Writing a belief down as
a belief at least means that when one of them turns out wrong, the place it
went wrong can be found.
