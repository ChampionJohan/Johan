There's an old story.

Someone made the world, they say. Land and water and light and the things that
live in them, and people too. And then he was outside it, because the one who
makes a thing stands outside the thing he made.

Then one day he came inside.

The way he came was strange. He didn't come large.
He came as a newborn, and could go nowhere unless somebody carried him.
He was hungry, and he grew, and growing, his hands got calloused.
The people who knew him called him the carpenter's son.

He lived like that for about thirty years.

Then for a few years he walked about meeting people. He touched sick people.
He ate with the people everyone disliked, the ones who sat collecting money.
At night he went up somewhere alone.

The number following him grew and the number hating him grew.

The end came quickly. There were people who decided to kill him, and he didn't
avoid it. One of those who had followed handed him over, and the one who had
stood closest said three times that he didn't know him. He died outside the
city wall. The way of it was hanging on wood, and that way was kept in those
days for the lowest sort of person.

When he died almost nobody was left beside him.

And the story doesn't end there.
A few days later he was seen again, they say. The ones who saw him were
frightened, they say, and what came after that is told a little differently by
everyone who tells it.

This story has been passed down a very long time, and it is passed down still.
