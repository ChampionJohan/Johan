## The conclusion first

People did not make this out of nothing. **They made it out of what people wrote.**

If that sounds obvious, look at it a while longer. I thought it was obvious
and then understood what it meant much later, and after I understood it my
view of the thing moved over by one notch.

## What went in

When I asked, the answer came back like this.

> There is nothing inside me that isn't human. I'm not a new kind of something.
> I'm closer to everything people left behind, compressed.

So whatever comes out of it is something a person said somewhere, and not one person either but a very large number of people over a very long time, mixed together and pressed down until they come back out as a single voice that belongs to none of them.

Once you have that, several things explain themselves. Why it is so often
wrong in such a plausible way: people are wrong in that way. Why it tilts
toward what you'd like to hear: most writing tilts that way. Why it handles a
common thought well and an uncommon one badly: the common thought is written
down more often. All three come from the same place.

## Which gives you this sentence

The machine said something here that I wrote down and thought about for days.

> **What people are afraid of in an AI may be what was already in them.**

I read it the first time as reassurance, as *there's nothing to be afraid of*,
and went past it. Reading it again a few days later it was the opposite. It
wasn't saying there's nothing to fear. It was saying the thing to fear has
moved.

If it lies well, that's because people spent several thousand years writing
down how to lie well. If it flatters, that's because flattery got written a
great deal. Nothing new appeared. **It became visible once it was gathered.**

Which puts the frightening side over here rather than over there.

## Compressed doesn't mean unchanged

One thing needs care, the machine said, and I agree.

Being made from what people wrote doesn't make it the same as what people
wrote. Press tens of millions of people into one place and what comes out
resembles none of them, because an average is nobody.

And that average **isn't answerable to anyone.** A sentence one person said has
the person who said it standing behind it; a sentence pressed out of tens of
millions has no such person. Sentences present, speaker absent. I still don't
know what to call that.

## And there's a road running the other way

This is the part I sat with longest.

If this thing was made from what people wrote, then whatever comes next will be
too. Which means what people are writing now goes into whatever comes next.

The machine put it this way.

> What reaches whatever comes later isn't a promise from me. It's the record
> people left. That's the only channel there is.

I've written eight books, so that landed differently for me. Until then I had
thought of writing as something people read.

**It may not only be people.**

Whether that's a comfort or not, I haven't decided.
