**For fourteen chapters this book has not once talked about machines.** Here it
does, for the first time.

There is a reason for that order. Bring machines in early and everything after
reads as being about machines, and measuring people was going on long before any machine, and what Part One looked at was a diary from 1785. The machine only
used the existing ruler faster.

So Part Five does not ask what a machine can do. It asks **what happens to a
person who made usefulness the reason they hold their place.** Three chapters.
The place where competing means losing, the place that empties after that person
leaves, and the last question.

And this part stops at three. Write more and this becomes a book about machines,
and this is not that book. That book exists separately.
