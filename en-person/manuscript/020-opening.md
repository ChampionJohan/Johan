## Opening

I started to write that a person has worth, and stopped.

I had the sentence down on paper and sat looking at it for a long time. It is
not a false sentence. But it cannot be argued with, and a person stops thinking
in front of a sentence that cannot be argued with. Nice, they think, and turn
the page. Fill a book with sentences like that and it reads well and leaves
nothing behind.

So I changed roads. I decided not to argue.

Instead I went to **the places where usefulness really does fall to zero.**
Someone who cannot do anything yet. Someone stopped for a while. Someone handing
back, one at a time, what they used to be able to do. Someone skilled with
nowhere left to use it. In those four places there is nothing to weigh and
nothing to count. And yet people there do something.

That is what I decided to watch. What people do cannot be argued with. It is
simply there.

Watching turned up something stronger. **What people do in those four places
resembles itself.** Nobody taught them and they did not agree among themselves,
and still it resembles itself, and write that resemblance down and you have things
to do. Part Three of this book is that.

It would seem, then, that we could start at Part Two. There is one thing before
it. If you do not know why we came to measure people so constantly, Part Two
reads as a collection of sad stories. So Part One goes first. Three chapters,
and the first one opens with a man writing in his diary in 1785.
