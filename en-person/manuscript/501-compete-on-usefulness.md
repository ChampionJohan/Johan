## The Loser Is Decided in Advance

The conclusion first. A person who has made usefulness the reason they hold their
place gives that place up on the day something more useful appears, and it is not the kind of contest you survive by trying harder. The moment you agree to compete, the
loser is decided.

For fourteen chapters this book has not once talked about machines. Here it does
for the first time. But what this chapter looks at is less what the machine did
than **the person who was standing there.**

## It Was Not a Small Job

You picked up a telephone and a person answered. An operator pushed a plug in and
joined two houses.

In the 1920s the largest employer in America was the telephone company, and more
than half its workforce were operators. And a calculation from 1930 survives.

> If the present rate of growth continues, within a few years we will need most of
> that population.

*That population* is the young women of American cities. To connect every call you
would have to sit nearly all of them at a switchboard. That is how large the job
was.

## Twenty Years

Between 1920 and 1940 the company converted more than half of the American network to dial, and when a city converted, the number of the next cohort entering as operators
in that city fell immediately, and stayed down. Fifty to eighty percent.

A job the whole country nearly needed was gone in twenty years.

The scope of the figures should be narrowed here. What the study counted was young,
white, American-born women. Write it as simply *women* and I am the one who widened
it.

## The Statistics Are Fine and the People Are Not

Then comes the part that is this chapter. Split what happened after the job
disappeared into two rows and the result reads in opposite directions.

| Who | What happened |
|---|---|
| The people doing the job at the time | Less likely to be in that job ten years later, more likely not working at all, and lower paid when working |
| The next cohort | Total employment did not fall. They went into clerical and service work, and into jobs that had not existed |

The top row is people and the bottom row is statistics.

When someone says it turned out fine in the end, they are reading the bottom row,
and the person who was in the top row is invisible inside that statistic. The
bottom row is also true. That it was of no use whatever to the person in the top
row is also true.

## Size Is Not Protection

One thing can be set down here. **That the job was large did not protect the job.**

It was the job the most people in the country did. Without it the telephone did not
work. And it went. So *this work employs so many people that it will be fine* is
not grounds for anything. Size is not protection.

And not one of the people doing it was pushed out for being bad at it. The
operators were good at it. Which returns us to saying that it was not a contest
being good could win.

## Chapter Seven Is Here

Part Two had four people, and the last of them was someone skilled with nowhere to
use it. That chapter contains no case material from outside. It is not that none
could be found. That chapter has to stand on what the author saw.

The case is here.

Think of an operator in a city converted to dial. Her hands are the same. She can
still take a number and push a plug, probably faster than anyone else in that city.
What disappeared is not the skill. It is the place to use it.

In chapter seven this was called having nowhere to use it. *Nowhere to use it*
sounds like the person shrank, and nothing on the person's side shrank at all. A
hole closed on the world's side. The same person was indispensable yesterday and
unnecessary today, having done nothing in between.

## Whose Story Are We Telling

Here is the knot I could not tie. I tried several times to put the top row and the
bottom row into one sentence, and each time one of them got erased.

Read only the top row and it becomes writing in which the world only gets worse.
Read only the bottom row and it becomes writing in which there is nothing to worry
about. Both are false. It seems that people having this conversation would need to
settle first whose story they are telling, and I could not find a way to settle it.

I should put down my own case, which sits slightly askew to this chapter. I will
leave it askew.

I cannot easily think of a place I held because I was better at it than others.
There is one thing I have done for a long time: speaking to people. Greeting them
and paying small attention. **That work has still not disappeared.**

Thinking about why it has not, the answer is a little deflating. **Because it was
never a contest.** There is no ranking in it, and if someone better at greeting people arrives, the greeting I was doing does not become unnecessary, because the
receiving side is different.

This chapter is about losing when you compete on usefulness, and here is its
reverse side. **Work that cannot be competed over cannot be taken away either.**
That kind of work pays badly, and the reason it pays badly is the same as the
reason it cannot be taken.

## In Short

| What people say | Which row it reads |
|---|---|
| Just as many new jobs appeared | The bottom row — the next cohort |
| What became of those people | The top row — the ones doing it |
| A job that big will not disappear | Neither row supports it |

## Today

Write one line on paper. One place you are currently holding **because you are
better at it than others.** A job, a task at home, a share in some group.

Then ask once. If something better than me arrives, what happens to this place?
It is normal for no answer to come. The answer is the next two chapters.

## Where This Chapter Comes From

Feigenbaum and Gross, "Answering the Call of Automation: How the Labor Market
Adjusted to Mechanizing Telephone Operation," *Quarterly Journal of Economics*
139, no. 3 (2024), 1879–1939. It uses data from three thousand American cities.

I could not open the original. This is public sources cross-read against one
another, and the figures and citation are confirmed at that level.

One quotation is flagged. The 1930 calculation is something the paper itself drew
from a contemporary source. That makes it a quotation of a quotation, and since it
carries quotation marks, someone should go to where it was first printed before
publication.

people went; the next looks at what remains where the people were.
