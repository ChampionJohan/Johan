## Where It Fails Hardest

The four things in Part Three were all doable. Say a name, listen to the end, do
not ask about work first, swallow the solution. They work in front of a stranger
and they work well enough with people you have known a long time.

There is one place where they fail hardest. In front of the person you live with.

That is backwards. It is the person you have known longest, know most about, care
most for, and that is where it collapses first. It collapses harder in proportion
to the caring.

## Saying the Word Here

There is a word this book has been holding back. Dignity. In eleven chapters it
has not been used once, because using it empties the room of anything further to
say. There is no sentence that follows *a person has dignity*, and in front of a
sentence that cannot be argued with the reader stops thinking.

In this chapter it gets used. Because this is the place where it is shaved down
first.

Outside, the word has force. It is written into law and into regulations, and
when a place stops treating people as people there is somewhere to take it. The
review form in chapter thirteen was eventually changed. That is how the outside
runs — slowly, late, and running.

At home there is none of that. No written rules, nowhere to take it, and above
all **the one shaving it down is the person who cares most.** What someone who
cares does is not called shaving by anybody. So at home the thing goes on for
decades with no name on it.

> Dignity is not shaved down far away. It is shaved down first at the closest
> range.

That is this chapter. So what follows is not a set of small domestic habits. It is
looking at where the word actually gives way.

## The Questions Are Always the Same

Think about what gets asked of a child at home. What did you get today. When is
the exam. Are you done. What gets asked of a husband or wife has something of the
same. How did that thing go. Did you get it this time. What gets asked of a parent
is a little different and still a measuring question. Did you take your medicine.
Did you get to the hospital.

Not one of them is wrong. All of them come out of worry, and without worry nobody
asks. But worry always worries about **one box.** The score box, the results box,
the body box. So while worrying you keep asking about that box, and after some
years the questions asked about that person have narrowed to the one.

## That Sentence Is a Way of Saying I Love You

I had that written down and then sat still a long time. Something in it is wrong.

*Mother, have you eaten?* is not a measuring question. It is a way of saying I
love you. In households using my language it is rare for anyone to tell their
mother they love her, and that sentence is what goes in the place where it would
go. Did you take your medicine. Have you eaten. Aren't you cold. The one saying
it knows and the one hearing it knows.

So was the section before it wrong? Half wrong. Corrected, it reads like this.

> It is also true that the sentence means I love you. Only, **there is one
> translation.**

When there is one thing to say and only one vessel to carry it, the speaker does
not notice they are short and the receiver has to take everything through that
one. And then on a day when the body is fine there is nothing to say. Having
nothing to say, you do not call.

This is nobody's fault. The language is shaped that way. But a person who knows
there is only the one is different from a person who does not. The one who knows
goes looking for a second sentence.

## Expectation Brings a Ruler

Why do we measure harder the closer we are? Expectation.

There is no expectation toward a stranger. Whatever the person in the next seat
does for a living, my day does not change. So I do not measure them; I simply see
them. With someone close, my day changes according to them, and if it changes I
stand to lose or gain, and where loss and gain are involved, measuring becomes
natural. Expectation is evidence of love and it arrives carrying a ruler.

So the difficulty of this chapter does not come from a shortage of feeling. It
comes from having the feeling. The four people in Part Two are the same. The place
where someone who cannot do anything yet, or who is stopped for a while, gets
measured most in the world is probably inside the house. The world passes by not
knowing. The house knows. The side that knows is the side that counts.

Our youngest is three. The oldest is seventeen and the second is fifteen. The
space between is empty. We lost the third and the fourth. My wife was hurt far
worse than I was. The child who came fifth is the youngest. In our house that
child is recovery, and a blessing.

On the child's birthday I said it. My beloved youngest. I did not hesitate. There
was not one reason to hesitate.

And something should be written down. I do not ask my children about scores. I
think process matters more than result, that you do what is in front of you with
everything you have and take what comes out of it.

But writing this chapter something caught. Process is a ruler too. *Did you try
hard* is gentler than asking for a score, and gentle in proportion to how little room it leaves: you can say the score was bad and be done; the sentence *you did
not try* attaches to the whole person. It may be that instead of a score I was
holding a different ruler.

To a three-year-old that ruler does not get applied. There is nothing to apply it
to. It is the same room as chapter four. What happens to that room as the child
grows? That is what this chapter is asking me.

## Only the Part You Can Repeat

One more thing. Among the things a close person does, we ask first about **what
can be carried to other people.**

If a child wins something, that story can be used at the next gathering. The story
that the child drew alone for a long time has nowhere to be used. So the prize gets
asked about and the drawing does not. Nobody decided not to ask. The usable side
simply comes out of the mouth first.

This is the quietest thing that happens inside a house. Nobody intended anything
bad, and one person's day is divided into pieces that can be carried elsewhere and
pieces that cannot, and the pieces that cannot are asked about by nobody until
they never happened.

## At Home the Name Is Least Said

In chapter eight I wrote that a name is the only form of address unconnected to
usefulness. Carry that sentence home and something strange appears.

At home names are barely used. Mom, dad, dear, older brother, older sister. All of
them are position words. Going downward it is the same, with one more layer: the
big one, the second, the youngest. Called by order. The word used for the person
you cherish most in the world is the order in which they were born, and nobody
finds that odd.

So the place where a name is least said is the closest place. A person addressed
politely by name outside walks in the door and becomes the second one. When the
years of being called only by a position word run long, a household can be the
last to learn what that person was besides the position, and that is why it is common to hear, for the first time after a parent dies, a different story about them.

This is not a call to change it. No household calls its father by his name, and
that would not be better. Only: the person inside that position word is larger
than the position, and the larger part is what the word written above points at.
A house is the likeliest place to forget it.

## Can Expectation Be Subtracted

Here is a question I never finished. Is love without expectation possible?

To stop measuring it seems you would have to set expectation down, and I do not
know whether a state with all expectation set down is love. Wanting nothing has
the same shape as indifference. It is hard to call a parent loving who says it
does not matter what becomes of their child. So expectation has to be there, and
with expectation a ruler follows, and with a ruler there is measuring. I have not
yet met anyone who knows where to cut it. I am as far as holding the question.

One guess can be set down. Subtracting expectation is hard, and **asking about
something besides expectation** looks worth trying. Not *do not ask about the
score.* Do not ask only about the score. That much can be attempted.

And the word used in this chapter only reaches so far. With it you can point at
where the shaving happens; what to do from there does not come out of it. So the
section below is written without it.

## Today

Go home today and **do not ask one family member what they did today.** Do not ask
how the day was, do not ask whether they finished. Ask something entirely useless
instead. What was funniest today. What they keep thinking about lately.

Try it and it is strangely awkward. The awkwardness tells you what kind of talk
has been going through that house.

## Where This Chapter Comes From

There are no outside facts in this chapter. Only what the author saw and thought
while standing there, and the scene in the middle is its only case.

One thing to record. This book set out to use the word *dignity* sparingly, and
this is the only chapter that uses it. It is used here to show one thing: that the
word has force outside and nowhere to be applied inside a house. The question that
follows — then where does the worth come from — is not handled here. That belongs
to the last chapter, and there the word is not used again.

There is one place in this chapter that was corrected while writing. I had *did
you take your medicine* down as an example of a measuring question, and on its own
that was wrong, because it left out that the sentence is also a way of saying I
love you. Correcting it deepened the chapter by a layer, and the reason I did not
write it that way to begin with is that I was looking at the sentence from
outside.

I went looking for research on conversation inside families. Figures circulate
along the lines of what percentage of what parents say to children concerns
grades, and where and how it was measured never came out. One number would have made this chapter look solid, and a number that cannot be supported was not used.

This chapter was inside the house. The next one is where the same thing has become
an institution outside it. There, the person measuring does not feel bad. It is
the job.
