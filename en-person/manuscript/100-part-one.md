Measuring people is not something that started yesterday. That has to be looked
at first.

Three chapters. The first looks at the place where a number got attached to a
person. The second looks at why it was that number and not another, and the third looks at what gets folded inward when the number is written onto a single sheet
of paper.

This part was not written to blame counting. Counting exists in order to get
work done and it mostly does its job extremely well. It is also true that the
people nobody counts are the ones who live hardest.

So what is being looked for here is not a culprit. It is **when, and what, began
to be counted.** Knowing that is different from not knowing it. Not knowing it,
a person starts to think the numbers attached to them are them.
