## What Was Asked Seventeen Chapters Ago

This book began with one question.

> If a person's worth comes from usefulness, does a person with no usefulness left
> have no worth?

Seventeen chapters have gone by. Part One looked at when we began counting people,
Part Two at four people standing where usefulness had been taken away, Part Three
set down four things to do, Part Four looked at how we measure at home and at work
and on the street, and Part Five at the place where competing means losing and the
place that empties out.

So now the question is turned over.

> If it does not come from usefulness, then where does the worth come from?

## No Answer Is Written Here

If I could write an answer in this spot, this book would have been much shorter.
One page would have done. I cannot produce the grounds. And I think not being able
to is correct. Writing down the answers that come to mind, there are three, and all
three have a hole at the end of them.

There is the side that says a person has worth because they are a person, and then you have to ask again why being a person constitutes worth, and from there it circles.

There is the side that says a person has worth because someone gave it, and then the giver has to be accepted first, and for a person who has accepted that, this
question is already finished.

There is the side that says a person has worth because we have agreed to treat each
other that way. Then the worth disappears on the day we agree otherwise. What Part
One looked at is a place where that happened.

I wrote all three down and looked at them a long while and could not take one more
step in any direction.

It was the day my wife had surgery.

I stayed up through the night on a chair in the corridor outside the operating
room. It must have been about seven hours.

There was nothing in my hands. Nothing to hold and nothing I could do. I prayed
there for the lives of my wife and the child. There was nothing else I knew how to
do.

In that place I was of no use at all. It was a place where the surgery did not go
better for my being there and nothing would have been different had I not been, and
still I did not leave. The thought of leaving did not arise.

I think I was standing in front of this book's question that night. I did not know
it at the time.

## There Is No Answer in This Book

But it is not as though seventeen chapters did nothing.

Each chapter had one *Today* at the end of it. Saying a name once more. Counting
the places you wanted to cut in. Having a conversation without asking about work.
Swallowing the solution. Not asking a family member what they did today. Finding
something a coworker's review form has no box for and saying it. Talking about a
person with the adjectives left out. Saying one more thing to someone you meet
only as a role. Writing down three things besides money.

All seventeen are small. Not one of them is an answer. And a person who has done
all seventeen comes to see the person in front of them differently. It happens without
the grounds ever being produced.

This is all I know. I do not know where the worth comes from, and **even not
knowing, it is possible to treat someone that way.** It may be that the order of
the two is the reverse of what I assumed. Knowing and then treating people
accordingly looks like the sequence, and it may be that the person who has treated
people that way is the one who comes to know later.

This is the end of the book, and I think stopping here is right. Write more and
from the next line on it is a sermon.

## Where This Chapter Comes From

There are no outside facts in this chapter. No case material either. It stands on
what the seventeen chapters brought. This is the only chapter with no *Today.*
Seventeen have one and only this one does not. It is not a space left blank for lack
of anything to put there. The question below takes that place.

## Last

There is a person beside you right now. There will be days when that person is of
no use to you at all.

> Where does that person's worth come from?
