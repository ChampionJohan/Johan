## Nothing Was Handed Back

The last chapter was about handing things back one at a time. Driving handed
back, stairs handed back, names handed back. The more that is handed back the
less there is you can do, and the less there is, the more it gets counted. At
least that has a certain internal consistency.

This chapter is the case where nothing was handed back.

I have stood in that place. What I am best at is saying what I honestly mean,
plainly. I could do it then and I can do it now. It never shrank and it never
rusted. There was simply nowhere to use it.

What changed was the surroundings. Nobody told me I could no longer do anything.
The way people spoke was the same and what they called me was the same. **The
atmosphere changed.**

Atmosphere is the only word I can find. I cannot put my finger on what changed and write it down, and not being able to point at it, I cannot dispute it, and not
being able to dispute it, I just sit in it. That is the hardest part of this
place.

## Unchanged and It Still Falls

There is one way this person differs from the three before.

The baby cannot do it yet. The sick person stopped for a while. The old person
shrinks one thing at a time. In all three, what they could do really was absent
or reduced, and so if worth went down alongside it there was at least a reason to
shrug at. Even if we want to believe that people do not count, the counting was
not flatly wrong.

This person is different. What they could do yesterday they can do today. The
hands and the head are the same, and the twenty years stacked up are still there.
The only thing that changed is where it could be used.

And the worth falls.

**So that worth was not hanging on ability.**

In the previous three chapters I could not say this. Ability really had decreased,
so the drop in worth could be charged to ability. In this chapter that excuse is
gone. One side was held fixed and only the other moved, and it moved, so the fact
that the two were not tied together simply shows.

That is why Part Two ends here.

## How Long the Word "Unchanged" Stays True

This chapter stands on the word *unchanged.* Then it is only fair to write down
how long that word stays true.

Unused, it rusts. Hands do and speech does. With no place to use it for several
years a person really does get duller, and once they are duller it becomes
impossible to sort out whether the original drop in worth was about ability or
not, because by then the two are mixed together.

The person starts to doubt it too. Nobody is using me, so have I actually lost it
— and once that doubt arrives, trying becomes frightening. Not trying means no
confirmation, and with no confirmation the doubt is the only thing that grows.

So the *unchanged* in this chapter is true only for a while at the start. **The
worth falls first, inside that while.** Later the ability comes down after it
until the gap is no longer visible, and from that point the fall in worth can be
charged to ability.

To see it you have to look early.

## They Start Explaining Without Being Asked

A habit develops in a person standing there.

They start explaining. Without being asked, they say what they used to do. Where
they worked and for how many years and what they were responsible for, and they do it with people they have just met and they do it again with people who already know.
The sheet of paper listing their history gets longer every year.

In chapter six I wrote about asking an old person what they used to do, and
called it a question that borrows usefulness from the past.

Here the person does it to themselves. Nobody borrows it for them, so they borrow
it alone.

It looks pitiable, and that feeling of pity is exactly the place this chapter is
pointing at. Why pitiable? Because they look like someone who has to borrow in
order to stand.

## "Even So"

There is a thing people say to someone in this position.

> You are a person of worth even so.

It is said with good intent, and it overlaps with what this book has been trying to say throughout, but there is one place where it catches for the person
receiving it.

It is the "even so."

*Even so* means something in front of it has fallen away. It is continuing a
sentence with both parties knowing what fell. So that one phrase carries comfort
and verdict together. The listener hears both.

Should it then not be said? That does not seem right either. Anyone who has tried
knows that saying nothing is worse.

This chapter stops here too. Whether that sentence is comfort or insult, I cannot
yet sort out. The same words hold a person up on one day and push them away on
another. I have looked several times at what separates the two, at what is
different in the person saying it, and it has not come into my hands.

## In Short

The four places Part Two looked at.

| Who | What they can do | Who counts | What shows there |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cannot do anything yet | Nothing | Nobody counts | We know how to not count |
| Cannot for a while | Stopped | The surroundings count | What was attached to me |
| Stops being able to | Shrinking | They count themselves | Comfort uses the same ruler |
| Skilled, nowhere to use it | Unchanged | Everyone counts | Worth was not hanging on ability |

The bottom row holds up the three above it from behind.

Read the four rows downward and one more thing shows. **The receiving side hands
something over.** The ill person hands over an apology, the old person hands over
concealment, the person out of work hands over an explanation, and all three are
things a body that will not move can still produce. The only one of the four who
hands nothing over is the baby, and the baby is the only one nobody asks it of.

## Today

Ask someone who has lost their work about something other than work.

Do not ask what they are up to these days either. That is work talk too. Any
question about what they are accomplishing right now is the same ruler.

Weather is fine. Food is fine. Something you saw yesterday is better. Making a
ten-minute space **where that person has to explain nothing** is close to
everything this chapter can offer.

## Where This Chapter Comes From

There are no outside facts in this chapter either. That is true of all four
chapters in Part Two.

What actually happens in a place where work disappears has been studied with
numbers, and that is used in chapter seventeen, and it is not used here because Part
Two is the place for looking at one person at a time. Also it would mean telling
the same story twice.

And the passage about *even so* is one phrase taken apart, not research. I could
not find material asking people who received that sentence how it actually felt.
