## Seven Households in Ten

There is a record from 1933 of a village in Austria. The village factory closed
and most of the village lost its work. Three researchers moved in and lived there
and wrote it down.

In that record there is a number. **Seventy percent of the village's households
were in a state the study called drifting along.**

Drifting is the study's own word. It does not mean despair. It means being there
with no plan and no expectation, doing only barely what has to be done.

## Time Collapsed Before the Money Ran Out

What catches first in that record is not the money.
**The sense of time collapsed first.** With the frame of a day and a week gone,
people could not build time for themselves. If there is no reason to get up in the
morning the hour of getting up scatters, and once it scatters the day mashes into one lump, and then what you can do shrinks. It is unlike not being able to do something
for lack of money. It is not being able to start.

And what they wanted shrank. Even the few chances that remained went unused. That
is the most frightening line. The willingness to use a chance disappears after the
chances do.

## There Is No Money Among the Five

One of those researchers set out a summary forty-nine years later: five things
work had been quietly giving people.

| | |
|---|---|
| One | The frame of a day and a week |
| Two | Activity that makes you act |
| Three | People outside the family |
| Four | A shared purpose — the sense of being useful to someone |
| Five | A position and a name |

There is no money among the five.

That losing work means losing money is known to everyone. What nobody counts as
lost is those five. And while the work is going on you do not even know you are
receiving them. Receiving without knowing, when it is cut you are left empty
without knowing what was cut.

Look at the fourth line again. The sense of being useful to someone. This book has
spent fourteen chapters saying not to measure people by usefulness, and there it is inside what a person out of work misses, and the contradiction is written down rather than cleared away.

I was out of work once. Six months.

On weekday afternoons I was at home or at the library. I went to the library partly
because there were books to read and partly because I needed somewhere to be at that hour, and leave in the morning and come back in the evening and the day has a
shape. There were days I went in order to make the shape.

If I had to write down one thing that disappeared in those six months, it is not
money. **My own self-importance disappeared.** The stubbornness went with it. That
is not a loss to regret. It was better gone.

Then what was it that I missed? Which of the five it was, I have still not managed
to name. There was definitely one, and it would not come into focus then and it does
not now.

## Looking Into That Contradiction

Having decided not to clear it away, look once more. The word usefulness is being
used for two things.

The usefulness used for measuring and the sense of being used are different
things. The first makes a ranking: it produces where you come in the order. The
second makes no ranking. There is no ranking in putting a meal on a table, and
none in the feeling that somebody is waiting for you.

So what this book was trying to say cannot be *throw usefulness away.* Throw it
away and the fourth of those five goes with it. **Only the ranking-making kind is
being set down.**

Whether the two can be cleanly separated, though, I do not know. The sense of
being needed turns into a ranking as soon as you start counting it, and the
moment it becomes how many people need you it is the same ruler again, and where
that crossing happens is something I could not put my finger on.

## Ninety Years Later, Another Country

There is a study from 2019. It looked at American regions where manufacturing
declined between 1990 and 2014 and recorded what moved together on the side of
young men.

Employment and income fell. The share doing nothing rose. Early deaths rose.
Marriage fell and children fell.

Care is needed here. What the study said reaches as far as these things moving
together. Writing it so that it reads as *no work means no marriage* is me going a
step further. And this list is from looking at young men.

## The Two Places Are Not Joined

In one village in 1933 and one region in 2014, the same place is empty. Writing it
that far is what can be done.

Writing that nothing changed in ninety years is not allowed. The countries differ,
the times differ, the methods differ. Moving into a village and writing it down and
cross-reading regional statistics are not the same kind of material. Chapters nine
and fourteen passed the same place, and this is the third.

The same shape was seen separately in different places. That far. That is heavy
enough on its own.

## If You Make Up the Income, Is the Rest Made Up

The unresolved part of this chapter is made by that list of five.

If you make up the lost income for someone out of work, is the rest made up? Does
money bring back the frame of a day? Does it bring back people outside the family?
A position and a name? It does not seem so, and I also have no material saying it
does not. Reading this as *do not give money* would be worse. Money should be
given, and whether money alone fills those five I do not know. I leave the question
here and go to the next chapter.

## Today

Write down **three things besides money** that you get from your work. Choose from
the five above or write something not on the list. Three lines on one sheet.

Written out, those three are what you are currently receiving. Counting what you
were receiving without knowing is today's job, and a person who has counted them
can recognize their absence in someone else.

## Where This Chapter Comes From

The 1933 record is the Marienthal study by Jahoda, Lazarsfeld and Zeisel. The list
of five is Jahoda's own summary from 1982. The 2019 study is Autor, Dorn and Hanson
in *American Economic Review: Insights* 1, no. 2, pages 161–178.

I could open none of the three originals. This is public sources cross-read against
one another. Someone should obtain a copy of the Marienthal book before publication,
and check at the same time whether a translation exists.

Three things were cut. They are commonly quoted alongside that village — that
people's walking speed slowed, that library borrowing fell, that organizations
disappeared. None of the three could be supported. Putting them in would make this
chapter far more vivid, which is exactly why they are more dangerous. They were cut
whole.

Only the three above were used. The sense of time, the shrinking of expectation, and
the seventy percent.
