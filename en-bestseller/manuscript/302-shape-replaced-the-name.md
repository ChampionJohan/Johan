## Take every label off and lay them out

Put ten objects on a table.
Then peel off every label.

Most of them become unidentifiable.
You can't tell whose it is, or whether it was expensive.

**A few you still recognize.**

The curve of a bottle, the tread pattern on a sole,
one colour on a wrapper, the angle of a handle.

The name came off and the name is still there.

Odd. The name was erased. Why is it still there?

## The obvious reading — a brand is a logo

Make a good logo, spread it widely, and you have a brand.

**Half right.**

What's missing is the order in which a person recognizes an object.

**A logo has to be read. A shape is known before reading.**

Recognizing letters requires stopping the eye and focusing.
Shape is processed before that. From a distance, small, blurred — still known.

The consequences are large.

| | Logo | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| To recognize it | Takes reading time | **Known before reading** |
| If you can't read | Not recognized | Recognized |
| In another country | Needs translating | **Works as is** |
| Far away · small | Invisible | Visible |
| On a shelf | You have to pick it up | **You know it walking past** |

The last row is what this chapter is worth.

> **Shape works while you're spending nothing on advertising.**

Sitting on the shelf is itself the advertisement.
And that advertisement has no monthly cost.

The property from Chapter 1 — that a steady seller's cost barely grows
with revenue — has shape as one of its actual mechanisms.

## Three conditions for a shape becoming a name

Not every form becomes a name. Three things are needed.

**One. It has to be drawable.**

This is a test you can actually run.

Hand people paper and ask them to draw it from memory.
**If it comes out roughly right there's a shape. If it doesn't, there isn't.**

Drawable shapes have something in common. They're simple.
Describing one takes no more than a sentence.

**Two. It has to not change.**

Shape is built by accumulation.
The same form has to be seen for a long time before it becomes a name.

Change it every year and nothing accumulates.
And the urge to change comes from inside.
**The maker sees their own object far more often than anyone else.**

Seeing it often makes you tired of it. Being tired makes you want to change it.
The buyer sees it a few times a year. The maker sees it daily.

> **The one who got bored is the maker, not the buyer.**

**Three. It has to come from function.**

The most important and the most often missed.

The shapes that lasted mostly became that shape for a reason.

A tread pattern exists so it doesn't slip.
A curve in a bottle exists so a wet hand can hold it.
The angle of a handle exists so less force is needed.

A shape that came from function has a large advantage.

**It needs no explanation.** Look at it and you know why.
And **it doesn't age.** As long as the function holds, the shape doesn't get strange.

A shape made as decoration ages with its era.

So there's a test.

> **Did this shape come from function, or from the taste of its time?**

Fix a shape that came from taste, and when that taste passes, you pass with it.
**The shape stops being an asset and starts looking dated.**

## Shape isn't only form

The scope has to widen here.
What matters about shape isn't form. It's that **it doesn't go through language.**

Five other things do the same job.

**Sound.** The sound of opening, of closing, the short note at startup.

**A single colour.** Recognizing something by one colour.
Colour is processed even faster than form.

**Proportion.** The ratio of width to height. The form can change; the ratio stays.

**Texture.** How it feels in the hand. This one doesn't need the eye at all.

**Smell.** The longest-lasting in memory and the least used.

All five are recognized without reading.
And all five **can't be peeled off the way a logo can.**

A logo can be removed, covered, forged.
A sound and a texture can't.

**To copy them you have to change the object itself.**
And then it isn't a cheap imitation any more.

## What you lose by having a shape

The passage in this chapter that has to be honest.

**Shape is an asset and a shackle.**

Fix a form and the form starts blocking things.

You could make it lighter but the silhouette would change, so you don't.
You could make it cheaper but that texture wouldn't survive, so you don't.
You found a better structure but it wouldn't be recognizable, so you don't.

That list gets longer with time.

So a company with a shape is **late to technical change.**
Late not from incompetence but from defending an asset.

**There's no way to have a shape without paying this.**

And there's one especially dangerous case.

**When the form was on the fashion clock to begin with.**

Fix a form made from the taste of its era
and the object passes when the taste passes.
Except you've already fixed it, so you can't change it.

**The shape ties the object to its own decade.**

## So how do you change it — erasure

The time comes when you have to change while holding a shape.
There's a method, and it's the one actually used.

**Keep the skeleton and change the flesh.**

First you have to know **what the skeleton is.**
Most people don't know which element of their object people actually recognize.
They guess.

The way to find out is simple. **Erase one thing at a time.**

Show a version with the colour removed. Still recognized?
Keep the form, remove the pattern. Still recognized?
Keep only the proportion, remove the rest. Still recognized?

**The one thing still recognized after everything is erased is the skeleton.**

Hold that one and everything else can change.

This work costs almost nothing. Paper and ten people.
**Not many companies have done it.**

Most do the opposite. They change everything a little
without knowing what the skeleton is.

The recognizable element slowly disappears, and
**one day nobody recognizes it and nobody can explain why.**

## The short version

| Question | This chapter's answer |
|---|---|
| Why is shape strong | **It doesn't go through language.** Known before reading |
| What does it save | Advertising. Sitting on the shelf is the advertisement |
| Three conditions | Drawable · doesn't change · **came from function** |
| Is shape only form | Sound · colour · proportion · texture · smell do the same job |
| What do you lose | You can't change. **You're late to technical change** |
| Most dangerous case | When the form came from **the taste of its time** |
| How do you change it | **Erasure** finds the skeleton. Hold only that one |

> **Shape isn't an asset you get for free.
> It's an asset bought by selling your freedom to change.**

## One Line Today

Get paper and a pencil. Then don't look at the objects.

**From memory**, draw three things you like.
They don't have to be good drawings. Recognizable is enough.

Then compare them with the real objects.

- If one came out roughly right, that object **has a shape**
- If nothing surfaces when you try, you know that object only by its logo

And one more.

> **If you asked people to draw what you make, from memory,
> what would come out?**

If the answer is nothing, that's the work in front of you.
**Making a shape doesn't cost much.
Not changing it once it's made is the hard part.**
