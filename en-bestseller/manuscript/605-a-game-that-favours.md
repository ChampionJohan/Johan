## The ages of the things in this book

Having read this far, there's something you can count.

**How old the things in this book were.**

The ones that vanished in Part Two were mostly young.
They reached a peak in a few years and were gone in a few more.

The ones that stayed in Parts Three, Four and Five were mostly old.
And looking at why they stayed, **it wasn't because they were fast.**

Almost none of them stayed by arriving first.
**They stayed by not disappearing.**

Odd.

We're taught we live in a world where the fast side wins.
And in the list of things that lasted, speed barely appears.

## There are two games

What looks like one market is two games running at once.

**The speed game.** Whoever arrives first takes it.
**The stay game.** Whoever has been there longest takes it.

| | The speed game | The stay game |
|---|---|---|
| What's contested | Who is first | **Who is still here** |
| What it takes to win | Capital · information · people · luck | **Not quitting** |
| Can you have it in advance | **Yes** | No |
| When does the prize come | Soon | Late. Maybe never |
| Is it crowded | Crowded | **Empty** |

The third row is the whole chapter.

What the speed game needs **can already be in your hands before you start.**
Someone with money starts with money; someone with information starts with it.

What the stay game needs cannot be held in advance.
**It only accumulates from today.**

> **The speed game is already decided at the starting line.
> In the stay game everyone starts level.**

And one more thing attaches.

**Whoever started first also goes to zero if they quit.**

Twenty years ends and those twenty years are gone.
In the stay game you can't put your accumulation in storage.
**It's only still there while you're still there.**

## Why is this side empty?

If the stay game is that favourable, why is it empty?

This book has answered three times.

**Chapter 3.** Renewing demand doesn't grow. No growth rate, nothing to announce.
**Chapter 13.** Density gives lifespan and doesn't give a living.
**Chapter 15.** Even with all four conditions, most never get a wave.

All three are true. And all three drive people away.

> **This position isn't uncontested because the barrier is high.
> It's uncontested because it looks unappealing.**

That sentence, from Chapter 3.

And it actually matters to someone starting late.

**Large capital doesn't enter a position that doesn't grow.**
Its reason for staying out is rational, so it will keep staying out.

## Not planned. Just never gone

The most important observation in this book.

Of everything in Parts Three through Five,
**almost none of it started out trying to make something that lasts.**

They were all built to solve a problem of their moment.
The game shoe was for the game; the work boot was for the work.

And they didn't disappear.
And so they became old.

> **Most steady sellers were not planned. They just never disappeared.**

If that sentence is true, the goal has to be reset.

"Make something that lasts" can only be scored ten years from now.
Nobody can sustain a goal they can't check for ten years.

So what's the goal?

## One question to ask

Reduce this whole book to one question and it's this.

> **Not what will rise,
> but will I still be doing this in ten years.**

That question can be answered today. No ten-year wait.

And work you can answer yes to has a character.

**It isn't fun work.**

Fun wears out. Same as the talk in Chapter 5. It decreases when used.
Work begun for fun gets abandoned the day it stops being fun.

Work you say yes about is a different kind.

**It's work that keeps nagging at you.**

Work you keep coming back to after it's settled.
Work where seeing someone else do it carelessly makes you angry.
Work you do even when there's no money in it.
Work other people ask why you bother about.

Staying a long time in work like that isn't difficult.
**It isn't endurance. You just find yourself still doing it.**

Chapter 15 redefined holding on.
Not waiting for a wave but being in a state that doesn't require one.

**That state has another name, and this is it.**

## Three reasons late is better

Three.

**One. The stay game counts from today.**

Starting late is not a loss.
And whoever started first goes to zero if they quit.

**Two. Someone starting late can start small.**

Bring back the number from Chapter 15.
What leaves every month when revenue is zero.

The lower that number the longer you last.
**And someone starting late finds it easy to keep that number low.**

Little to protect, no scale to maintain, no money owed back.
**Having little right now is, here, an advantage.**

**Three. Someone starting late has read Part Two.**

They start knowing how the ones ahead collapsed.

They don't mistake talk demand for need demand,
they don't sign a lease longer than the fad,
and when things go well they can add bridges instead of locations.

**This is information the ones ahead could not buy.**
When they were living through it, it hadn't happened yet.

## Favourable doesn't mean easy

The honest ending.

In the stay game the prize comes late.
And as Chapter 15 said, **usually it doesn't come.**

You can do ten years and have nothing happen.
That is the normal result of this game.

So this book closes by confirming why those ten years aren't wasted.

The four from Chapter 15.

Being there and findable. Being there and recognizable.
Having the story. Having a body that fits.

**All four pay without a wave.**

And so do the four bridges.

Bought again when the time comes. Recognized with the label off.
Better with use. Passable to someone else.

**An object with those four is a good object even if it never becomes famous.**

> **Because preparing for a wave and simply being in good shape
> are the same thing, it isn't wasted when no wave comes.**

That's the whole of what this book can promise.
**And it's a promise that can be kept.**

## One Line Today

Last one. Write two things.

**First,** write down one thing you have **kept doing for more than ten years.**

It doesn't have to be work. A habit, a relationship, an interest.
**Better if it's something people ask why you bother with.**

If there's nothing, that's an answer too. Move on.

**Second,** write down one thing you're doing now that you can answer this about.

> **Will I still be doing this in ten years?**

If you wrote even one yes,
**you are already inside the stay game.**

And the other nineteen chapters of this book were about how to make that one thing
still be there in ten years.

If there's nothing to write yes about, that's the work in front of you.
**Decide what you'll do for a long time before deciding what to make.**

**Reverse that order and every method in this book stops working.**
