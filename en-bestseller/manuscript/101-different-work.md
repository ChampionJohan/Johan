## Two clocks run inside one bookshop

Walk into a bookshop and there's a table by the entrance.
Books lying face up, covers showing.

Most of what's on that table is gone in three months.

Twenty steps further in there are shelves.
Books standing upright, only their spines showing.

Some of those have been in the same slot for ten years.

Twenty steps between the two places.
And the books in those two places are **two different businesses.**

The shop **earns** from the entrance. It **survives** on the shelves.

Odd. Same building, same product. Why does it split in two?

## The obvious reading — a steady seller is a bestseller that lasted

The usual assumption: the especially good ones among the big sellers
survive and turn into the long sellers.

**Not even half right.**

Some do survive. The proportion is low.
And there's a more important fact underneath.

> **A large share of things that sell for a long time didn't sell at first.**

The moment that sentence holds, you can no longer place the two on one line.

They are not the top and bottom of one ranking. **They are different exams.**

The two exams are marked differently, prepared for differently,
and they pay out differently when you do well.

## What a bestseller sells — talk

Look closely at something selling in large numbers
and it isn't only the object moving.

People are talking about it.
To be in that conversation you have to have it.

**What a bestseller sells is admission to the conversation.**

This produces an unusual property.

> **The sales figure itself creates the demand.**

Selling a lot becomes something to talk about,
and that talk produces more sales.
Past a certain point, a bestseller runs by itself.

It ends for the same reason.

**The value of talk falls when it repeats.**
To someone hearing the same story for the third time, the story is worth nothing.

And when the conversation ends, the reason to buy ends.
The object is unchanged. The reason is gone.

**A bestseller doesn't end because the product got old.
It ends because the story got old.**

## What a steady seller sells — the answer

Look at something that has sold for a long time and it usually isn't
a conversation piece. Almost nobody talks about it.

It has a different property.

**There is a fixed moment when not having it becomes a problem.**

Winter arrives. A baby is born. Someone lives alone for the first time.
A parent starts getting ill. Somebody manages people for the first time.

Those moments don't pick their people.
**Every year the same moment arrives for someone new.**

A steady seller is standing in that moment.

It sells not because the object is being discussed,
but because the moment came and the object was there.

> **A bestseller bets on what people are talking about.
> A steady seller bets on when people get stuck.**

## The curves are different

Put the two sales curves side by side and the shapes are nothing alike.

**A bestseller is a peak.** Steep up, steep down.
Impressive height, narrow width.

**A steady seller is a plain.** You can't see it rising.
Low, wide, and you can't tell when it started.

There's a miscalculation that happens here often.

Peaks are visible and plains are not.
So the peak looks bigger.

**The area is often larger on the plain.**

The trouble is that you can only know that **ten years later.**
So at the moment of deciding, the peak always looks bigger.

## The costs move in opposite directions

More important than the curves.

**On a bestseller, cost grows with revenue.**

Talk is the fuel. Cut the fuel and the fire goes out immediately.
So you cannot stop promoting while it sells.
You spend the most exactly when you sell the most.

**On a steady seller, cost barely grows with revenue.**

The buyer comes looking. You only have to be sitting
in the place where the question gets asked, as the answer.

Stop promoting for a year and sales barely move.
**This one sentence separates the profit margins of the two businesses.**

The same revenue leaves a different amount in your hand.
And the gap widens with time.

## So is a bestseller a bad thing?

No. This book doesn't say that.

There are three things a bestseller gives you that nothing else does.

**One, speed.** It builds in three months what a plain takes ten years to build.

**Two, visibility.** People learn that the thing exists.
However good it is, nothing happens while nobody knows it's there.

**Three, it's also the entrance to becoming a steady seller.**

Plenty of things that sell steadily now were once enormous topics of conversation.
They didn't come down off the peak. **They moved across to the plain.**

All of Part Three is that story.

So the problem isn't aiming for a bestseller.

> **The problem is preparing only to finish at the front table,
> and not preparing to move to the shelves.**

## The short version

| | Bestseller | Steady seller |
|---|---|---|
| **What it sells** | Talk — admission to the conversation | The answer — in that moment |
| **What creates demand** | The sales figure itself | A situation that repeats |
| **The customer** | Whoever knows the story now | Whoever reached that situation this year |
| **The more it sells** | The talk wears out | The name hardens |
| **Stop promoting** | It goes out immediately | Very little changes |
| **The curve** | A tall narrow peak | A low wide plain |
| **Why it ends** | The conversation ended | **The situation disappeared** |

Look at the last line once more.

A steady seller usually doesn't end because a better competitor arrived.
**It ends when the situation it was standing in disappears.**

Part Two uses this distinction again.
This sentence is the tool that sorts the vanished things into groups.

## One Line Today

Pick up three objects within arm's reach.
Not the expensive ones — the near ones.

Write one line for each on why you bought it. Then mark it one of two ways.

- **Talk** — everyone was discussing it at the time
- **Answer** — something came up that required it

Then one last question.

> **Of the three, which ones do you still actually use?**

The answers usually pile onto one side.
**The side they pile onto is the side you should be building.**
