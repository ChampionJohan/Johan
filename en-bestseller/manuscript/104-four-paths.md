## Two lists that barely overlap

Take a sheet of paper and make two lists.

**The first list.** Ten things that sold enormously ten years ago.

Most of them won't come to mind.
At the time everybody knew them, and now the names won't surface.

**The second list.** Ten things you actually use in your home right now.

That one's easy. You just have to look around.

Put the two lists side by side.

**Almost nothing overlaps.**

Odd. Why are "what sold" and "what stayed" two different lists?

## The obvious reading — the good ones stay

**Half right.**

Some of the good ones stay. But look closely at what stayed and a problem appears.

**A fair number of the survivors were judged badly at first.**
Some didn't sell and looked like they'd soon be gone.

Meanwhile, of the things called the best of their year,
only a handful are still here.

So what's needed to stay isn't quality.

> **What's needed to stay is a route.**

Count the routes and you get the table of contents of this book.

## Two questions, four boxes

Take one object and ask only two questions.

**Did it break out at the start?**
**Is it still here?**

Two questions, yes or no each, so four boxes.

| | **Still here** | **Gone** |
|---|---|---|
| **It broke out** | ② Broke out and stayed | ① Broke out and vanished |
| **It didn't** | ③ Arrived late | — Never recorded |

Three boxes become stories. One doesn't.

## The box with no name has to come first

The bottom right box has no label.

Things that didn't sell at the start and aren't here now.
**By count this box is overwhelmingly larger than the other three combined.**

And nobody records it.

Nothing gets written about an object that failed to sell and disappeared.

So this book has a limit.

> **This book was written by looking only at what survived and what got noticed.**

Far more things may have used the same methods and vanished anyway.
There's no way to check.

Stating that limit first has a purpose.

This book is not saying "do this and it will last."
**It's saying "the things that lasted had these in common."**

Those are different sentences.
The first is a guarantee. The second is an observation.

An observation raises the odds. It doesn't guarantee.
**A book that hides that difference ages fast.**

## The remaining boxes are Parts Two through Four

Limit stated. Now the map.

**① Broke out and vanished. Part Two.**

The most common route. And the one with the most to learn.
The reasons for dying split three ways, and those three ways are Part Two's three chapters.

**② Broke out and stayed. Part Three.**

The most important part of this book.
① and ② started from the same place. Where they split is the core.

**③ Arrived late. Part Four.**

Things that didn't sell and then stayed.
Here time appears to have run backwards.

The reading order means something.

**First how things die, then how they live, then how they live late.**
① and ② have to sit next to each other for the split to be visible.

## There is one more route

There's a route that doesn't fit a four-box table.

**Something that sat quietly for a long time and suddenly broke out.**

A steady seller becoming a bestseller.

You could call it a kind of ③. Its nature is different.

In ③ the original maker is usually no longer there.
By the time the world came back, the company was gone or the person had died.

**On the fourth path the maker is alive.**
So this is the only route you can actually collect on.

Part Five is that story. Two chapters only,
and the center of gravity of this book is in them.

A warning belongs here immediately.

**Holding on does not mean breaking out.**

Far more things hold on and then quietly disappear with nothing happening.
Not admitting that turns Part Five into comfort, and comfort ages a book.

So Chapter 15 opens with **"why most of them can't catch it."**
The hope in front of it is only worth something after the conditions are stated honestly.

## The words this book uses

That's Part One. All the tools are out.
Collected on one page. Everything after this is said in these words.

**Three clocks — where you are now.**

| Clock | Cycle and nature |
|---|---|
| The fashion clock | Months. Runs on what others are doing |
| The generation clock | Ten to thirty years. Runs on the years you grew up in |
| The need clock | Doesn't turn. Hooked to the body and to life events |

**Four bridges — where you can go.**

| Bridge | The question |
|---|---|
| Repeat | When it's used up, do you buy it again? |
| Shape | Do people recognize it without being told? |
| Patina | Does time add value? |
| Handoff | Does it pass to the next person? |

**Four paths — what happened so far.**

| Path | The route | Where it's covered |
|---|---|---|
| ① | Broke out and vanished | Part Two |
| ② | Broke out and stayed | Part Three |
| ③ | Arrived late | Part Four |
| ④ | Sat, then suddenly broke out | Part Five |

Part Six turns these three sets of tools into a maker's checklist.

## Three things this book does not promise

Narrowing the promise makes a book last.

**One, no guaranteed method for making a bestseller.**
Luck matters a great deal. Chapter 17 only covers raising the odds.

**Two, no claim that holding on eventually pays off.**
Chapter 15 proves the opposite first.

**Three, the survivor problem gets repeated, not buried.**
Each part notes where the opposite cases might be.

> **A book that overstates is old in three years.
> Overstating while claiming to write something durable doesn't hold together.**

## One Line Today

Draw four boxes. Across the top, still here and gone.
Down the side, broke out and didn't.

**First,** sort ten things you know into the four boxes.
If they pile into one box, your range is narrow. Notice which box it is.

**Second,** put whatever you're making now onto the table.
If it hasn't broken out and it's still here, you're in the bottom left box.

Then one last question.

> **Which box are you trying to get to?
> And do you know the route?**

Answering "no" is normal.
**Knowing that route is what the remaining sixteen chapters do.**
