## These were fads too

Three chapters. And the most important part of this book.

One thing has to be clear first.

**The things in Part Three started from the same place as the things in Part Two.**

They were enormous topics of conversation. People talked, people queued,
not having one felt like being behind.

They were indistinguishable from what's in Part Two.

**At the time nobody knew which ones would stay.**

So Part Three has one question.

> **They started from the same place. Where did they split?**

## One rule — what Part Two also had doesn't count

Look closely at survivors and a problem appears.
**Too many things look like the reason.**

Good product. People loved it. Luck held. The maker meant it.
All true and all useless.

**Everything in Part Two was all of those things.**

So this part has a rule.

> **A trait that Part Two also had is not counted as a reason.**

Keep that rule and what remains shrinks hard.
And what's left is the real answer.

## The four bridges get used here

The four named in Chapter 2 come out now.

| Chapter | Bridge | The question |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | **Patina · Handoff** | Does time add value? Does it pass to the next person? |
| 9 | **Shape** | Do people recognize it without being told? |
| 10 | **Repeat** | When the time comes, do you buy it again? |

Two are grouped into Chapter 8 for a reason.

Patina and handoff are **the landing bridges.**
They decide where an object comes down to when it steps off a fad.

Shape and repeat are **the holding bridges.**
They keep it in the place it landed.

Hence the order: 8, then 9, then 10.

## And a limit

Part Three looks only at survivors.

Things may have laid the same bridges and vanished anyway.
**They probably did. There's just no record.**

As Chapter 4 said, this book is an observation, not a guarantee.

Not "lay a bridge and it will stay,"
but **"the ones that stayed had bridges laid."**

Read it with that difference in mind.
