## Good, and not selling

Sometimes a good object doesn't sell.

Everyone who has tried it says it's good.
Everyone nods when they hear the explanation.

**And nobody buys it.**

Look closely at objects like this and they have one thing in common.

**"Why buy this" has an answer.**
**"Why buy this now" doesn't.**

People buy later what they can buy later.
And later mostly doesn't arrive.

**What a bestseller does is answer the second question.**

## Five reasons to buy today

There are only five.

**One. A deadline.** You can only get it until a date.

**Two. Scarcity.** You might not be able to get one.

**Three. A season.** Now is the time.

**Four. Visibility.** Other people see it. Not having it now means not showing it now.

**Five. Others already bought.** Not buying leaves you in the position of behind.

The five have something in common.

> **All five are about time.**

Not one of them is about the object being good.
**Breaking out isn't boasting about the object. It's manufacturing time.**

## But four of them wear out

The criticism goes here immediately.

Chapter 5 said talk decreases when used.
**These devices decrease when used too.**

A deadline every time isn't a deadline.
A shortage every time isn't a shortage.

Used on the same person a second time it's half as strong,
and the third time it works backwards, because they feel handled.

**One of the five doesn't wear out.**

**The season.**

The reason is in Chapter 10. The calendar rebuilds it every year.
December returns whether the maker does anything or not.

Lay the five out by cost and lifespan and they separate.

| Device | Cost to build | Wears out? | How many times |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deadline | Low | **Wears fast** | A few |
| Scarcity | Medium. It has to be genuinely short | Wears out | Few |
| **Season** | **None. The calendar provides it** | **Doesn't wear** | Unlimited |
| Visibility | Costs design | Doesn't wear | Continuous |
| Others already bought | Hardest at the start | Doesn't wear | Continuous |

The table gives an order.

**Season, visibility and others-already-bought are reusable devices.**
**Deadline and scarcity are cards you play once.**

Most people do the reverse.
They keep using deadlines and shortages and never build a season.

**Because they start with whichever is easiest to use.**

## A warning attached to scarcity

Scarcity is especially dangerous. Chapter 7 covered it.

The value of scarcity comes from **the fact that somebody went without.**
And that is an asset that disappears when supply increases.

Wanting to scale up after breaking out on scarcity is natural.
**You only have to know that the decision is a decision to spend your own asset.**

## Two things to write before breaking out

The actual work of this chapter is these two.

**One. Write down the ending date.**

The second time this appears, after Chapters 5 and 7.

Not wanting to write a date is normal.
**Don't write it and the default becomes "indefinitely," and that default is what ruins people.**

**Two. Decide now which of the four bridges you'll lay.**

Deciding after it breaks is too late.

Chapter 7 put it plainly.
**The real value of a fad isn't the revenue. It's that for a while, people are looking.**
Carve a shape while nobody is looking and nobody learns it.

So 17 and 18 aren't a sequence. They're simultaneous.

## The checklist

```
1. Is there a reason to buy today?
   If not, pick one of the five

2. Write whether what you picked wears out
   If it does, decide in advance how many times you'll use it

3. Write the ending date
   Then set the payback period shorter than that

4. Decide now which of the four bridges you'll lay
   → go to Chapter 18
```

## One Line Today

Stand in front of what you're making and say one sentence out loud.

> **Why should someone buy this today rather than next month?**

If no answer comes immediately, it isn't ready to break out.
**Not because the object is bad, but because there's no time in it.**

If an answer came, check one more thing.

> **Can you use that same answer next year?**

If you can, you built a reusable device.
If you can't, you played a one-time card — **and that's fine.
You only have to know there aren't many of them.**
