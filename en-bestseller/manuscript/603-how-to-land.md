## There is an order

Chapter 2 named the four bridges.
Part Three showed how each one actually gets laid.

Here, only **the order.**

You can't lay all four at once. And in the wrong order they don't hold.

```
Repeat  →  Shape  →  Patina  →  Handoff
```

Each position has a reason.

## Why Repeat is first

**An object nobody buys twice has no use for the other three.**

Carve a shape into something sold once
and people get one chance to see that shape.

Chapter 9 said shape is built by accumulation.
Accumulating means seeing it many times, and seeing it many times means buying it many times.

And Repeat is **the cheapest.**

Pick one of the four in Chapter 10.
Attach to a date that already exists, design the consumption rate,
let the object announce its replacement time, or make something in yearly units.

**All four are far cheaper than making a season.**

## Why Shape is second

Once Repeat exists, people see the thing many times.
**That's when a shape starts to accumulate.**

Lay Shape first and what happens?

A shape on something nobody looks at twice doesn't accumulate.
You spend and nothing stays.

The three conditions from Chapter 9 apply directly.
**Drawable, unchanging, from function.**

And there's a cheapest way to lay Shape.

**Pick one thing and don't change it.**

One colour, one form, one sound.
**Deciding costs nothing. Not changing it takes effort.**

## Why Patina is third

There's a reason not to lay Patina first.

**Patina reduces repeat purchases.**

Things used for a long time sell less often. Chapter 8 said so.

So laying Patina first cuts revenue first.
You can't survive in that state.

**Only once Repeat is secured can Patina be afforded.**

There's a cheapest way here too.

**Set up somewhere to get it repaired.**

No redesign needed.
Leave the object as it is and simply accept repairs.

Chapter 8 said repair requests are a signal, not a cost.
**Repairing gets you two things at once: patina, and information about whether
the object is changing lanes.**

## Why Handoff is fourth

Handoff needs all three of the others.

To pass it on it has to **not break.** Patina comes first.
Whatever was passed on has to **work with what's current.** Shape and compatibility come first.
Whatever was passed on has to **have a use.** Repeat comes first.

Missing any one of the three and the inherited object goes to storage.

And the cheapest way to lay Handoff:

**Make a place to write a name and a date.**

Chapter 8 said what gets handed down isn't the object but the time spent with it.
**Time attaches where memory can attach.**

## When they have to be laid

**Before it breaks out.**

Laying them after costs more, for three reasons.

**One. People have already decided what the object is.**
Change it later and it's a different object.

**Two. When revenue is large you can't decide to reduce it.**
All four bridges reduce current revenue.
Reducing a small number and reducing a large number are different weights of decision.

**Three. The organisation has grown and can't change.**
The more that's been settled, the more changing costs.

So 17 and 18 have to happen at the same time.

## Why doesn't everyone do it?

Repeating Chapter 8.

All four bridges **reduce current revenue.**

The price can fall, handing down replaces buying,
keeping compatibility blocks changes, long use means fewer sales.

**Judged by the quarter, not laying them is always right.**

> **Bridges aren't expensive.
> They're just hard to decide on, because they reduce revenue now.**

That sentence is where Part Two and Part Three separate.
And it's the only genuinely hard thing in this book.

As Chapter 2 said. **Two or more of the four and it tends to last.**

## The checklist

```
1. Count how many yes you have now

2. If it's 0 or 1, start with Repeat
   Attaching to an existing date is the cheapest

3. Once Repeat holds, pick one element of Shape
   Then don't change it for at least three years

4. Once Repeat and Shape have settled, start accepting repairs
   You get patina and information at the same time

5. When all three exist, make a place to write a name
```

## The short version

| Bridge | Why that position | The cheapest way to lay it |
|---|---|---|
| **Repeat** | Without it the other three are useless | Attach to a date that exists |
| **Shape** | It has to be seen many times to accumulate | Pick one and don't change it |
| **Patina** | It cuts repeat sales, so Repeat comes first | **Set up repairs** |
| **Handoff** | Needs all three of the others | Make a place to write a name |

Look at what laying them costs and there's a pattern.

**None of them costs much money.**

## One Line Today

Answer the check above. Four lines and it's done.

**If you got 0 or 1 yes, that's normal.**
Most objects are like that, and everything in Part Two was too.

Then decide one thing.

> **Which of the four will you lay first?**

Once decided, write down **one thing you can do this month** for that one.

Attaching to an existing date. Fixing one colour.
Announcing that you'll repair it. Adding a place to write a name.

**All four are the size of something you can start this month.**
The hard part isn't starting. **It's not changing it for three years.**
