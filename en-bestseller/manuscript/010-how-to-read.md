## This book uses three sets of words, and that's all

Everything you need before starting is on this page.

**Three clocks — they tell you where you are now.**

| Clock | Cycle | What sits on it |
|---|---|---|
| The fashion clock | Months | Whatever is being talked about |
| The generation clock | Ten to thirty years | The taste of one generation |
| The need clock | It doesn't move | Cold. Hungry. Sleep. A gift to give |

**Four bridges — they tell you where you can go.**

| Bridge | The question |
|---|---|
| Repeat | When it's used up, do you buy it again? |
| Shape | Do people recognize it without being told? |
| Patina | Does time add value or take it away? |
| Handoff | Does it pass to the next person? |

**Four paths — they tell you what happened so far.**

|  | Still here | Gone |
|---|---|---|
| **It broke out** | ② Broke out and stayed | ① Broke out and vanished |
| **It didn't** | ③ Arrived late | — Never recorded |

There is one more path that doesn't fit the table.

**④ Sat quietly for a long time, then suddenly broke out.**

Three sets of words. That's the whole apparatus. Part One builds them one at a time.

## The shape of the book

| Part | What it covers | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Part One (1–4) | The two successes are different work | Concepts |
| Part Two (5–7) | Broke out and vanished | **Autopsy** |
| Part Three (8–10) | Broke out and stayed | Autopsy |
| Part Four (11–13) | Arrived late | **Chronicle** |
| Part Five (14–15) | Sat, then broke out | Chronicle |
| Part Six (16–20) | So what do you make? | **Checklists** |

The format changes from part to part for a reason.

**When the same format repeats twenty times, the reader stops reading
the content and starts reading the procedure.**

## Where the names are, and where they aren't

This book has one rule worth stating up front.

> **Part One and Part Six contain no company names and no product names.**

Names age.

A name everybody knows today will need a footnote in ten years,
and an example that needs a footnote slows the argument instead of helping it.

Part One builds the tools. Part Six lists the work.
**Both need to age more slowly than anything else here.**

Names appear from Part Two through Part Five — **only where evidence is needed.**

And the names of things that were popular recently appear **only in Part Two**.
That part is about things that vanished, so a name going stale there
helps the argument rather than hurting it.

If someone opens this book years from now, reads Chapter 5,
and asks "what was that?" — **the question itself is the proof of that part.**

The opening and closing follow the same rule.
The two shops in them are not named in the text; **they are named in the sources
at the end of the opening.**

## Numbers and sources

One rule.

> **A number that couldn't be verified doesn't go in.**

Where a number is needed, this book first records which source would settle it.
Anything that couldn't be confirmed was **cut whole** rather than softened.

**A hedged sentence gets read as a fact.**

So a few passages give a direction rather than a figure.
And Chapters 13 and 15 contain no names at all.
In both, putting a name in would have meant writing
something unverifiable as if it were verified.

## Three things this book does not promise

**One, it does not promise a method for making a bestseller.**
Luck matters a great deal. Only the odds are covered here.

**Two, it does not say that if you hold on, you eventually break out.**
Chapter 15 proves the opposite first.

**Three, it keeps saying that only survivors were studied.**
No list exists of the things that never sold and quietly disappeared.

**A book that overstates is old in three years.**
Overstating while claiming to write something durable doesn't hold together.

## How to read it

In order, or one part at a time. Each chapter stands on its own.

If you're in a hurry, the **short version table** at the end of each chapter
is enough. Twenty of those tables laid end to end make the map of this book.

Two requests.

**One, read both chapters of Part Five in one sitting.**
Chapter 14 alone turns that part into comfort.
Comfort makes a person feel better and changes nothing.

**Two, write down at least one line at each chapter's end.**

Every chapter closes with **One Line Today**.
Read it and shut the book and nothing stays.

**All of them are sized to be done today.**
