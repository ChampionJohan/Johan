## A book that didn't change a word, and started circulating

There are books in a library's stacks that almost never go out.
Borrowed once every few years.

Then for some of them, one year, the loans start climbing.
A few years later it's always out.

Something needs checking.

**Not one word of that book changed.**

No revised edition, no rewrite.
The same sentences on the same paper.

Everything that changed was outside.

Odd.
**If an object didn't change and its value did, the value was never inside the object.**

## The obvious reading — it was ahead of its time

The most common explanation for not selling.

**Half right. And it's the dangerous half.**

"Ahead of its time" sounds like praise.
So hearing it is reassuring.

But there's no diagnosis in it.

It wasn't ahead. **It was standing in a slot that didn't exist yet.**
And a slot that doesn't exist doesn't appear by itself.

Follow the late arrivals in time order and they share one thing.

> **The world didn't come on its own. Something brought it.**

This chapter calls that something a **carrier.**
There are three.

## First carrier · The price fell

Some things have the idea first and the materials later.

The design was finished long ago and what it needed was too expensive.
Electricity was expensive, or communication, or computation, or moving things.

The day that price drops below some line, the thing suddenly becomes possible.

An important property here.

**The price falls gradually. The becoming-possible arrives all at once.**

Below the line nothing works; across the line everything does.
So from outside it looks like it appeared out of nowhere.

This is **the only carrier you can predict.**
Because you can write down in advance which price has to fall.

## Second carrier · It moved to a different place

A film that failed in cinemas sometimes becomes a classic later.¹

One prison film did this.
In cinemas it didn't earn back what it cost to make. It won no awards.¹

Then the distributor pressed a large run of rental tapes the following year
and pushed them out.¹
It went to number one on the rental charts that year.¹

A few years later a cable channel took the broadcast rights and ran it repeatedly.¹
It became a fixture of that channel's schedule.

**The film didn't change. It's the same print.**
What changed is **where it's watched.**

A cinema is a hard place.
A fixed time, money paid in advance, two hours of being held there.
So the audience picks the safe thing.

Watching at home is different.
It costs far less, you can stop halfway, you can follow a recommendation.

**The same object, and the hurdle differs by place.**

That film lost in cinemas not because it was bad.
**The other films opening the same week looked safer.**

In a cinema the safe thing wins. In a living room that criterion disappears.

This structure isn't only in film.

Some things live only in expensive places before coming down to cheap ones.
Specialist shops before general shops. Big cities before small ones.

**Each step down multiplies the number of people who might try it.**

Failing in the first place does not mean failing in the next.

## Third carrier · The people changed

A novel that didn't sell in its own time sometimes ends up in classrooms
a generation later.²

This carrier is far slower than the other two. Ten years, twenty.
And when it works, not only is the object unchanged —
**the world's prices are unchanged too.**

What changed is the reader.

The same sentences sounded strange in that era and obvious in the next.
The generation clock from Chapter 2 runs backwards here.

Something on the generation clock goes down when the generation goes.
**But something that was out of step with its generation comes into step
when the generation passes.**

The same clock is a loss for one object and a gain for another.
Which one depends on **whether the thing agrees with the majority now
or disagrees with it.**

## Ahead, or wrong?

The most useful passage in this chapter.

Someone who isn't selling right now doesn't know which one they are.

**Ahead doesn't sell, and wrong doesn't sell.**
From the outside they're identical.

And most people believe they're ahead.
It's far easier to bear.

There's one way to tell.

> **Are there many people saying it's good,
> or are there people actually using it?**

These are completely different signals.

**Something ahead is used by a few.**
The number is small, and those people keep using it. They're inconvenienced without it.

**Something wrong is praised by many.**
"Nice." "That would be useful." "Hope it goes well."
And nobody uses it.

Praise is free and use costs something.
**Only the thing that costs something is a signal.**

So while it isn't selling, the number to count isn't revenue.

**How many people keep using it.**
If that number is small but not falling, the slot is missing — not the thing.

## If you arrived early, what do you do?

Say you've confirmed you're ahead. Four things.

**One. Surviving is the strategy.**

Chapter 7's arithmetic applies again.
There, the payback had to be shorter than the fad. Here it has to be longer.

**Because you don't know how long the wait is, make the monthly outflow minimal.**
That means enduring a state with no glamour, no growth and nothing to announce.

**Two. Keep selling, even a little.**

Stop and you're gone. Gone, and there's nobody to receive the world when it comes.
Among the things in Part Four, the maker was rarely still there.

**Three. Leave a record.**

Things discovered late have something in common.
**The original still existed.**

The print was in storage, the book was on a shelf, someone kept a box.
If it hadn't survived there'd be nothing to rediscover.

**Keeping what you made findable costs almost nothing.**
And not doing it makes the later part impossible.

**Four. Don't wait for the carrier — write it down.**

The thing you can do today.

On paper, three lines.

> **What has to get cheaper for this to work?**
> **Which place, one step down, would multiply the people who'd try it by ten?**
> **Which people, once grown, would find this obvious?**

Write the three and you'll recognize the signal when it arrives.
Don't write them and the signal passes unseen.

**Waiting and watching are not the same thing.**

## But most of them weren't there

To close this chapter honestly.

For a large share of the late arrivals, **the maker was not there.**

The company was gone, the person had died, or they were doing something else.
When the world came, somebody else collected.

So this isn't a chapter about waiting.

**It's a chapter about building a state in which you can wait.**

How that state gets built, and what happens to the ones who were still alive,
is Part Five.

## The short version

| Carrier | What changes | How long | Predictable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The price fell** | Materials · power · communication · computation | A few years | **Yes** |
| **It moved place** | Where it's sold, where it's seen | A few years | Partly |
| **The people changed** | The generation reading or watching | Ten years or more | Hard |

> **Something ahead is used by a few. Something wrong is praised by many.**

## One Line Today

Pick one thing you're doing that **isn't going well.**

Count two things.

**First,** how many people have told you it's good?

**Second,** how many people **actually used it** last month?

If the second is zero, the problem may not be a missing slot.
If the second is small but not zero and not falling, the slot is missing.

Then write three lines.

> **What has to get cheaper for this to work?**
> **Which place, one step down, would multiply the people who'd try it by ten?**
> **Which people, once grown, would find this obvious?**

If all three are blank, there's no reason to wait.
**If even one fills in, that line is what you're watching.**
