## Why the dead ones come first

Three chapters. And the first part of this book where names appear.

Starting with the survivors feels better.
Ordering it that way leaves less to learn.

Line up only what survived and **too many things look like the reason.**
It was a good product. Luck was with it. People loved it.
All true, and all useless.

Start with what vanished and it changes.

Those were good products too, and people loved them at the time.
**So those aren't the reason they split.**

> **A trait the dead ones also had cannot be the reason the others lived.**

Part Two does the filtering.
When Part Three looks at the survivors, the already-filtered traits don't get counted again.

## The autopsy format

The three chapters here follow the same order.

```
when it broke out → what was actually being sold →
when it turned → what got cut first
```

The last box matters.

When something collapses, several things collapse at once and the cause disappears.
**Finding the one thing that got cut first is the autopsy.**

## Why recent examples appear only in this part

This book has a rule.
**The names of recently popular things are used only in Part Two.**

Part One and Part Six contain none. Those places have to last.

Part Two is different.
**Here a name going stale helps the argument rather than hurting it.**

If someone opens this book years from now, sees a name in Chapter 5,
and asks "what was that?" — the question itself proves the part.

**The reader confirms for themselves that what was called vanished did vanish.**

## Three ways of dying

| Chapter | What got cut first | The product was |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | **The talk** — the conversation ended | Fine |
| 6 | **The slot** — the category disappeared | Actually good |
| 7 | **The scarcity** — they gave it away themselves | Still selling well |

In all three, nothing was wrong with the product.

**So making a better product stops none of them.**
Stopping them means looking somewhere else, and where that is, is Part Two's answer.
