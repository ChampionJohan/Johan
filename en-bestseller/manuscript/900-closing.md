## Back to five hundred and a thousand

Return to the two shops from the opening.

The one that opened five hundred in two years and had fewer seven months later,
and the one that has sold one thing for a thousand years.

Both can now be described in this book's words.

**The five hundred was on the fashion clock.**

It sold talk. Talk decreases when used, and using it was selling it.
And talk demand is a single nationwide quantity, divided by five hundred locations.

The leases outlasted the fad.

Not one bridge was laid. No reason to buy again, no shape to recognize,
no property that improves with use, nothing to hand on.

**The thousand years is sitting on the need clock.**

There's a shrine in front, and people come to pray there.
That flow doesn't grow and doesn't shrink. **It just changes people every year.**

The shop is standing at a door where demand renews.

And bridges are laid.
The praying repeats, one item makes it easy to recognize,
and twenty-five generations of making the same thing have accumulated a story.

**Above all, the size was set.**

Not entering department stores and not appearing at events
isn't modesty. It's the calculation from Chapter 15.

> **Small enough to sit through a quiet thousand years,
> large enough to receive the days the worshippers come.**

After twenty chapters you can write those two descriptions yourself.
**That was the whole point.**

## What this book never said

One thing was left unsaid on purpose.

**This book doesn't decide which one you want to be.**

It didn't say earning a lot and finishing is bad.
It didn't say earning a little for a long time is right.

Both hold up. Both are paid for.

What this book did was **show the price in advance.**

- Break out big and it's fast, and you don't know when it ends
- Go long and it doesn't go out, and the prize comes late or never

Which you pick is decided by your circumstances and your temperament.
**Choosing without knowing and choosing knowing are different.**

Most of the people who opened those five hundred stores were probably diligent.
**The missing piece wasn't diligence. It was a judgment about which clock they were on.**

## Twenty chapters in one sentence

Reduced:

> **Don't try to guess what will rise.
> Find what won't go, sit on it,
> and spend your time on not disappearing.**

None of the three requires a special talent.

Guessing requires faster information than others.
**Finding what won't go requires looking closely.**
And not disappearing requires only not quitting.

Which is why the last chapter is called
"A Game That Favours Whoever Started Late."

## And the long one is hardest

Honestly: the third is the hardest of the three.

Told to wait ten years, nobody can.
Waiting shaves a person down daily.

Chapter 15 redefined the problem.

> **Not holding on while waiting for a wave,
> but holding on in a state that doesn't require one.**

That thousand-year-old shop wasn't waiting a thousand years.
**It grilled rice cakes, and each day turned on its own.**

Build a state that turns by itself, however small,
and holding on stops being endurance. **It becomes just living.**

How to build it is in Parts Three and Six.
And in that state, no wave is not a loss.

Findable. Recognizable.
The story surviving. A body that can receive.

**All four are simply a good state, wave or no wave.**

## One last thing to do

Before closing the book, do exactly one thing.

**Find the oldest object in your home.**

Not the most expensive — the oldest.
Over ten years is good; the oldest thing you have will do.

Hold it and write one line.

> **Why is this still here?**

The answer will catch on one of four.

**Because the time keeps coming around** is Repeat.
**Because it never occurred to you to change it** is Shape.
**Because it fits your hand better now** is Patina.
**Because somebody gave it to you** is Handoff.

It might be none of the four, and simply still here
because it never got thrown away.

**That's an answer too.**
And it's actually the answer closest to this book's conclusion.

## And one more

Ask the same question about your own work.

> **What will still be in my hands in ten years?**

It's all right if no answer comes. Usually none does.

**Knowing there's no answer is the first step to finding one.**

And as Chapter 20 said,
work you can answer yes about is not fun work.

**It's work that keeps nagging at you.**

The thing other people ask why you bother with,
and you keep looking at anyway.

What that is differs for everyone, and nobody can decide it for you.

## What writing this book confirmed

One last thing.

Gather all the old things in this book and they have one trait in common.

**Every one of them was new once.**

The sign that looks worn now was a new sign at the start,
and what gets called a classic was just a new release when it appeared.

That thousand-year-old shop had a first day too.
The person who grilled the rice cakes that day didn't know about
twenty-five generations.

> **Something old wasn't born special.
> It's something that didn't disappear while it was new.**

The same is true of whatever you're making now.
There's no way to know yet whether it will last.

**But how to keep it from disappearing can be known.**
And that method can start being used today.

That's all this book has to give,
**and I think it's enough.**
