# Chapter 37. Asked Again

> _Previously_ — Speon had run the channel toward the valley. When I asked
> why down instead of up, he said, "Up there isn't dry." I turned that
> over the whole way back into the city.

In the morning I helped Speon work on the ditch.

The channel now ran all the way to the edge of the city. From there the
ground turned steep, and the water kept slipping sideways. I gathered
stones and built up the banks. My hands knew the work.

"…Going again today." Speon asked it. He wasn't a man who asked me
things much, so the question stood out.

"…Yes."

"…What for."

I couldn't answer him right away. Speon didn't wait for an answer. He
picked the shovel back up and dug.

"…Take some water with you." he said. "Don't go empty-handed."

I didn't ask what he meant by that. I filled the flask.

---

Walking toward the inner part of the Preserve, I heard the sound of
water.

The first time I'd come into this city, what had struck me strangest was
the absence of sound. There had been no sound of water. Now there was.
Along every alley, faint under everything else, ran the sound of the
channel.

A few of the city people passed by, hearing it. No one stopped. They
didn't need to. But I saw one of them slow down. Just for a moment. Then
that person went back to their ordinary pace.

*I didn't take much notice of that brief slowing, at the time. Looking
back now, that was the first tremor I ever saw in this city. A very
small one. One step's worth of a tremor.*

---

I carried the visitation form with me, and at the counter I folded it
back up.

*I didn't know, right there, why I folded it. The form always had the
same box on it. Purpose of visit. I stood there a long time holding the
pen, not knowing what to write. Persuasion. I couldn't write that. It
was too honest to count as a purpose.*

The clerk looked at me.

"…The purpose box is empty."

"…Just a visit."

"…That's what everyone puts." The clerk smiled. "There are sub-items
listed over there."

I looked at the sub-items. Well-being, counsel, notification, other. I
marked other.

*It took me that long to fill in one small box. Looking back now it
seems absurd. But at the time I truly didn't know. What I was going
there to do, that day.*

---

There was no one else in the waiting room besides me.

I was the only one in this city who ever applied for a visit. It had
been that way the first time I came, and it was that way on this, my
thirty-second time. City people had no need of visits. If you wanted to
see a preserved family member, you simply walked over. You didn't get
startled, and you didn't miss them.

I started counting the empty chairs, then stopped. There were always
twelve. No need to count them.

Instead I called up my past visits. The nineteenth, the day I'd tried to
ask why and couldn't. The twentieth, the day I'd learned that I missed
you wasn't an answer. The twenty-first, the day I'd cancelled the
application myself, in front of my brother.

*Writing those numbers down here again, I realize all over how faithfully
I'd been counting those visits, all that time. Counting was how I
endured things. But that day, even sitting in the waiting room, I
didn't count. I just sat.*

The clerk called my name. I stood up.

---

My mother welcomed me as she always did.

"…Anemos."

"…Yes."

"…You've come again."

*You've come again. I could count that phrase by now. I didn't count
which visit this made. I'd started to, and stopped. At some point I'd
understood that the number told me nothing. To my mother, the first
visit and the tenth were exactly the same.*

I sat down across from her. I had no words prepared. For the first time.
For seven years I had always come with something ready.

*I counted back through what that preparation had been. At the
nineteenth visit I'd come prepared to ask why. At the twentieth I'd come
to confirm that I missed you wasn't an answer. I always came to confirm
something. Today there was nothing to confirm.*

"…I don't have anything to say today." I said.

My mother looked at me. Her expression didn't change much. This person's
expression never changes much. I know that now.

"…Then why did you come."

I hadn't prepared an answer to that question.

"…No reason."

---

I just talked about the valley.

Not words I'd prepared. I just told her what I'd seen that day. How
Speon had run the water channel downward, how when I'd asked why down
and not up, he'd answered, "Up there isn't dry."

"…A water channel?" my mother asked.

"…Yes. From the well."

"…There's a well in this city?"

I caught the way she said it. Not the same polite form she used when she
spoke about herself. She'd said in this city. As if this city weren't
the place she lived.

*That was the first time I noticed that my mother never spoke of this
city as her own. In this city. Over there. My mother always spoke of
this city as though standing one step outside it. I didn't know, that
day, what that meant.*

"…Yes. Newly dug. Someone named Speon."

"…Speon." My mother repeated the name. "That's an unfamiliar name."

"…He's an unfamiliar person. Nobody asked him to come, but he came."

My mother smiled, a little, for the first time. Very small.

"…It takes someone like that, for a well to come to be."

---

I told her about the valley too.

I told her about going down, about seeing traces there dry as bone,
about meeting a man. I didn't say what he'd asked me. That wasn't
something to tell my mother, not yet.

"…Why did you go there." my mother asked.

"…Speon told me to go see it."

That wasn't a lie. But it wasn't the whole of it either.

"…Were you afraid?"

I stopped for a moment at that question. It had been a long time since
my mother had asked me what I was afraid of. No — it was the first
time. In seven years of visits.

*I didn't count it, right then. It didn't even occur to me to count it.
I counted it later. Seven years, thirty-two visits, and this question,
once. I still remember that ratio exactly.*

"…No." I said. "It was strange."

"…How."

"…The water hadn't reached there. The channel hasn't gotten that far
yet. But even so, something there felt alive."

My mother didn't answer that. She just looked at me for a long time.

"…What kind of person was he, the one you met there." she asked, after
a while.

"…Someone whose age I couldn't guess. He was just walking among the
bones."

"…What did he say."

I stopped there for a moment. The question he had asked me — whether I
thought these could live again — was something I hadn't yet passed on
to anyone.

"…He didn't say much." I said. "We just walked together a little."

That was a lie. A very small one. My mother didn't press.

---

I took out the water flask.

The one given to me when I left my father's relatives' house, the one
I'd carried for seven years. It was half full inside.

"…This." I said. "It's not this city's water. It's water flowing down
to the valley. I got a little from Speon."

My mother looked at it. She didn't hold out her hand.

"…May I drink it?"

That question startled me. My mother had never needed anything, all
this time. Not water, not food, not sleep. And now she was looking at
the flask and asking a question like that.

*At the time I thought it was just a politeness. Now I know. It wasn't
politeness. It was the first time she'd been curious about something.
I didn't catch the difference, that day. In the next chapter, I come
back to what I did that day, not even knowing why myself.*

"…No." I said. "Not yet."

My mother nodded. She didn't seem hurt. She simply accepted it, as it
was.

"…I see."

---

I brought up my brother too, briefly.

"…My brother's doing well." I said. "He couldn't come today. He came
first, earlier."

"…Does he still do that." my mother asked. "Ask if his brother's come."

"…Yes. Every time."

My mother laughed at that. Just for a moment.

"…He's been that way since he was small. He always asked, when you
weren't there."

*Listening to that, I remembered again the path where my brother used
to follow me, the spot in front of the third stone wall where he always
stopped. I didn't tell my mother that. Not yet.*

---

Near the end of visiting hours, my mother asked,

"…Weren't you here to persuade me?"

I stopped at that.

"…No."

"…Then what did you come for."

*I couldn't answer that question. To be precise, it wasn't that I had
no words for an answer — I didn't know myself what the answer was. That
night I would go looking for it for the first time. It's one of the
most important nights in this book.*

"…I don't know." I said honestly. "I just wanted to talk."

My mother looked at me. Something in her eyes was a little different
from usual. I couldn't put my finger on what, at the time.

"…All right." my mother said. "I was glad you talked to me."

---

I gathered the flask back up.

Standing, I turned back for a moment. My mother was still sitting there.
In the same posture as seven years ago.

"…I'll come again." I said.

"…All right."

It was the same farewell as always. But that day something was
different. I thought about what it was, walking out the door. No answer
came.

*I only understood it later. That I'd written other in the purpose box
on the form, that I'd gone without any words prepared, that I'd just
talked about the valley — those had all been the same thing. That was
the first day I hadn't gone there to persuade her. What I had gone
there to do, I would only find out that night.*

---

I ran into the Curator in the hallway.

"…You kept it short today." he said. He always recorded the length of
visits.

"…Yes."

"…Are you all right?"

I raised the flask instead of answering.

"…Will there be a day I can give this to my mother?"

The Curator looked at the flask for a moment. Something crossed his
eyes. I couldn't read what it was, at the time.

"…After release." he said. "Not before then. The body doesn't need it."

"…I know."

"…But you didn't bring that up today."

I stopped at that. I didn't ask how he knew. This man couldn't hear
everything that happened inside the visiting room, but he always read
the face of the person who came out.

"…No." I said. "I didn't, today."

The Curator nodded. He didn't ask further. He just walked past me.

A few steps on, he turned back.

"…Your mother's expression was a little different today." he said.
"I've been recording this for years now, and I've never seen this
before."

"…Different how?"

"…It's hard to explain." the Curator said. "Just — different."

He left it at that and walked on.

*I watched his back for a moment. This man always knows something and
doesn't ask. I used to think that was consideration. Now I know. It was
that, and it also meant this man already knew the answer to that
question himself.*

---

I walked to the edge of the city. I could hear the channel running.
Along the ditch Speon had dug, water was flowing down, toward the
valley.

I sat there for a long while.

Eikon passed by, saw me, and came over.

"…Coming back from a visit?" she asked.

"…Yeah."

"…Did you try to persuade her again today?"

I couldn't answer that right away. Eikon knew exactly what I went to my
mother to do. She didn't mock it. She was just curious.

"…No." I said. "Not today."

"…Why not?"

"…I don't know."

Eikon looked at me for a moment. There was no judgment in her eyes. She
doesn't judge people. She lacks nothing, so she has no interest in
measuring others either.

"…That's strange." she said. "After preparing so long."

"…Yeah."

She didn't ask further and went her own way. I watched her go, and ran
back through everything that had happened that day, in order. The
folded form, the empty preparation, my mother's then why did you come,
my I don't know.

*That night I realized something strange. In that whole visit, not once
had I said let's go together now. Five words I'd been preparing for
seven years. I hadn't forgotten them. I'd just felt, that day, that the
words weren't needed.*

*Why I felt they weren't needed, I still didn't know. I would find out
the next day.*

The sound of water kept going. Listening to it, for the first time in a
long while, I counted nothing at all.

*That night I slept with my head on the flask. It was half full again
inside. The water Speon had told me to take. I didn't know, even then,
exactly why I'd brought it. It was water I couldn't even give my mother.
And yet I hadn't emptied it out. I'd filled it back up.*

*Before I fell asleep that night I asked myself one thing, once. Had I
really gone today wanting nothing at all. I wanted to answer yes to
that. But the answer wouldn't come.*
