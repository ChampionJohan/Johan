# Chapter 12. I Couldn't Apply

> _Previously_ — The second visit hadn't differed from the first by a single word. The same greeting, the same pause, the same farewell. That night, though he was already dreading the next visit, Anemos decided not to give up. Not yet.

I let several days after that simply slip by.

*I didn't count exactly how many days. Numbers show up this often in this record, and yet those days alone I couldn't count. It felt like counting them would mean deciding something, so I deliberately didn't.*

I spent more time lying in the lodging house, staring at the ceiling. I hung the flask on the wall. Those days had the longest stretch, in all this time, of it not being in my hand.

The person next door knocked a few times. I only answered, and didn't go out.

"…Are you all right?" the person next door asked.

"…Yes. Just a little tired, is all."

*It wasn't a lie. But it wasn't a complete answer either. I've written several times now that no one in this city tells lies. Over those days, I learned for the first time that you could go without lying and still not be honest.*

The person next door came once more after that.

"…Have you eaten?" they asked.

"…Yes."

*That was a complete lie. I hadn't eaten anything that day. In that moment I realized, for the first time since coming to this city, that I had told a complete lie. Trying to imitate the people of this city, I found I wasn't, after all, a person of this city.*

In those few days, I went as far as that building's entrance twice.

The first was on the second day. I made it to the door, and then, seeing someone come out from inside, turned right back around. It wasn't anyone I knew. Just a stranger. I was afraid to see that person's face.

*Even now I don't know exactly what that fear was. Maybe I was afraid of seeing what expression that person had just come out wearing. Something like the face of that older man from Chapter 7.*

The second was on the fourth day. This time I got as far as taking hold of the doorknob. And let go.

*I remember the feel of that doorknob. Cold, much like the one I'd felt at my mother's door. I took my hand off that cold.*

I told no one about those two failures. Not the person next door, not the officer.

Over those days I thought of that older man from Chapter 7 many times. Three times in three years. I recalculated that number. I'd only gone twice so far. And already I was standing here, stuck in front of a third. By that math, I was faster than he was.

*That calculation frightened me. The first time I saw that man, I'd thought he was a different kind of person from me. Someone who'd become that way from being worn down, from age, from years gone by. Over those days I came to see that thought had been wrong. He, too, must have started out exactly like me.*

---

One morning I got up and packed the flask again.

*I'd like to write that decision as some great resolve. In truth it wasn't. I got up simply because I couldn't lie there any longer. It felt like if I kept lying there, I would never go again.*

I decided to apply for a third visit.

*Three. I said that number to myself, once, in my head. One and two I had already counted. I thought it only natural to carry that number forward. I didn't know, then, how far from natural that was.*

---

The application counter was in a different building from the waiting room. I'd passed that counter once, back when I first arrived in this city. At the time I'd thought it was just part of the information office.

A tree stood in front of the building. Much like the one I'd seen through the waiting-room window.

That day was unusually clear. I had never once seen bad weather in this city. No rain, no overcast sky, no strong wind. Every day, a sky of about the same brightness.

*I thought even the weather was true to this city's nature. A place where nothing changed suddenly. Even the weather was like that.*

I stopped for a moment in front of that tree. I thought about counting the leaves and gave it up. That day, counting felt meaningless. I didn't know, then, why that feeling came over me.

I stood a little longer in front of the door. Not going in, not leaving either.

*Those few minutes are the ones I've gone back over more than any other, since. Standing in front of that door for those few minutes was, I think now, the real substance of this whole chapter. Everything else was only the result of those few minutes.*

Standing at that door, I thought of the old man I'd met on the road to this city. The old man I wrote about in Chapter 1. The one who'd said his own family had someone here too.

"…Do you visit?"

"…I did, at first."

"…And now?"

"…I don't go."

*I've already written that I didn't understand that answer when I heard it on the four-day road here. Standing in front of that door, in that moment, I understood that answer in my body. I had thought not going and not being able to go were different things. That day I learned how close the two really are.*

---

I pushed the door open and went in.

There was no line at the counter. A clerk was sorting paperwork.

*Seeing that empty line, I finally noticed. People applying for something new in this city, at all, were rare. Everyone here already lived according to what was fixed. Filling out an application meant you meant to change something. In this city, there wasn't much left to change.*

"…Here to apply?" the clerk asked.

"…Yes. For a visit."

"…Which visit is this?"

"…The third."

The clerk nodded and took a sheet of paper from a drawer.

"…Write your name here, and the name of the person you're visiting. Simple."

*Simple. I laughed a little inside at that word. I had never seen a procedure in this city that wasn't simple. Visits were simple. Rations were simple. Simple, here, wasn't praise — it was just a word describing a fact.*

At the next window, someone was handing over another form. Something like a ration change request. That person handed it over and turned to leave in under thirty seconds.

*I envied those thirty seconds. I think, by then, I already knew my own paper wasn't going to finish inside thirty seconds.*

---

I took the paper and went to a small desk off to the side. There was one chair. I sat down.

A pen lay there. I picked it up.

There was nothing else on the desk. One inkwell, a few sheets of paper. I thought that plainness resembled the visiting room I'd described in Chapter 8. This city put in each room only exactly what was needed.

*I had thought that restraint was consideration — meant to make deciding easier for a person. I see it differently now. It wasn't consideration. It was this city's nature. Here, there simply wasn't much that was needed to begin with.*

The space for a name was at the top. I set the tip of the pen there.

*Up to that moment I still thought there was no problem at all. Write my name, write the name of the person I was visiting, submit it, and that was that. I had already done that exact sequence twice. Nothing hard about it.*

But the pen wouldn't move.

I tried, right there, to find the reason. My hand wasn't hurt. There was nothing wrong with the paper. I simply couldn't draw the next stroke.

I clenched and unclenched my hand a few times. My fingers moved just fine. The problem wasn't my fingers.

*I've since put it this way to myself. My hand was fine. Some nameless part, one I can't even quite call the heart, wouldn't move. I still haven't found a name for that part.*

---

*Right then, a great many things passed through my head all at once. In no order.*

*My mother's "you've grown so much." Those words I'd heard again after only three days.*

*My brother's "Brother, you got taller." The line I've tried and failed, more times than any other in this record, to write again.*

*"…Bye now," heard at the end of the second visit. The two words that hadn't differed in the slightest from seven years before.*

*"…Because you're the kind of child who'd come." My mother had said those words identically, both times.*

*And my brother, touching the flask, asking, "…Brother, can I have this?"*

*"…Exactly the same." The words I'd said to myself in the waiting room came back to me too. From Chapter 9.*

*And the five words I had prepared for seven years. Let's go together now. I had never once managed to bring that phrase fully out into the open air.*

I looked at the blank space on the paper. Anemos. I knew exactly how long it would take to write those letters. About twenty strokes, and it would be done.

I couldn't make those twenty strokes.

I took one deep breath. The same breath I'd taken before opening the door in Chapter 7.

*Back then, that breath had opened the next door. This time it didn't. I didn't know the difference in that moment. The breath had the same shape, but the place it carried me to was the exact opposite.*

---

I began the first letter.

I drew one circle.

And stopped there.

*I looked at that circle for a long time. It was an unfinished letter. Not the start of my name, not the end — just one circle. Right then I thought that circle looked exactly like me. A shape that pretends to be the start of something, when really it leads nowhere at all.*

I sat there for a long while, still holding the pen.

*I tried to count that stretch of time. I couldn't. It was the second moment I failed to count. The first had been in front of my brother. This time I was alone.*

---

I set the pen down.

I folded the paper again and slid it back toward the counter.

The clerk took it. Asked nothing. Just took it and put it in the drawer.

*I've said, many times since, that that moment was the most frightening one of that day. If someone had asked why, I would have made up some answer or other. Because no one asked, I didn't have to give myself an answer either.*

"…Come back another time." the clerk said. Not an angry voice, not a sympathetic one. Just a voice explaining procedure.

"…Yes."

I barely managed that one word.

"…Is there any way to know ahead of time, next time?" I asked, mumbling it out. It was a question with no meaning behind it. I just felt like I had to say something.

"…It is possible at any time." the clerk said.

*That line. I had already written it down at the end of Chapter 1. It is possible at any time. I had read that line in the bulletin for seven years. I wrote then that it was a kind and a frightening thing at once. Hearing it spoken directly at that counter that day, I felt both of those meanings again, all at once.*

---

I couldn't explain, right there, why I had done that.

Even writing this record now, I still can't, completely.

*Since that day I've been asked this question many times again. By myself. Each time the answer came out a little differently, and each time it ended up back at the same place in the end. I don't know. That was the most honest answer.*

*It wasn't that I was afraid of my mother. It wasn't that I was afraid of my brother either. I still loved those two people. I love them now. But apart from that love, my hand simply would not move on that paper.*

*I didn't know then that these two things might be the same. Loving someone, and confirming that love once more by repeating it in exactly, precisely the same shape. I would only come to understand the difference between the two much later in this record.*

---

I turned around.

Leaving the counter, I didn't look back.

*I still sometimes imagine what that retreating back must have looked like. A sixteen-year-old with a flask on his shoulder, walking out and leaving the form behind. I've never seen that back with my own eyes. But what I did that day was, exactly, one thing.*

*I did nothing.*

I pushed the door open and stepped outside. The tree was still standing right there. I never did count how many leaves it had.

I stood under that tree for a moment. I had no idea where to go.

*Right then, for the first time, I truly felt that I had nowhere at all to go in this city. Not the lodging house, not my mother's room, not my brother's room — none of them, right then, was where I wanted to go. I just stood there and breathed.*

A few people came and went from that building. Every one of them went in faster and came out faster than I had. Some came out without any paper in hand. Probably they'd come for some other business, not to apply, the way I had.

*Watching them, I tried to figure out how long I'd been inside that building. I couldn't. I had no sense of how the time had passed. That was the first day I lost track of time. In a way different from counting.*

*Part One of this book ends here. I know that's a strange place to end it. Nothing happened, after all. No one was hurt, nothing was broken. I simply turned away from a counter.*

*And yet I think that turning away was, of everything I had done in this city up to then, the most honest act. I didn't know, in that moment, what I should do. And I didn't hide the fact that I didn't know. At least for that moment.*

That night I didn't open the flask's cap. Even though my throat was dry, I didn't open it.

*I decided to just let that thirst be. Not doing something was, that day, the only choice left to me.*

*That night, before I fell asleep, I thought of just one thing. I hadn't accomplished a single one of the reasons I'd come to this city. And yet strangely, that night, it didn't feel so heavy.*

*That very lack of heaviness was the strangest part of all. And I fell asleep holding onto that strangeness.*

*I didn't know, then, that this lack of heaviness would take on another name in the part to come. I won't say that name here first.*
