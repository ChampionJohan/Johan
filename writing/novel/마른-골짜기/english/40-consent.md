# Chapter 40. Consent

> _Previously_ — My mother asked me, for the first time, "How have you
> been?" For the first time in seven years she was curious about
> something. I decided to hold that question, quietly.

I only held onto that question for three days.

I didn't apply for a visit. My resolve not to persuade her held me back
this time in a different direction. I didn't want to rush the question
into something.

*I still think, now, that those three days were the right thing. Some
things disappear if you check them too quickly. I was afraid that
question would disappear.*

---

Over those three days I kept helping Speon dig the ditch.

The channel had now passed the halfway point of the city. Speon dug
alongside me without saying much. He glanced sideways at me sometimes,
but never asked what I was doing.

Only on the evening of the second day did he ask.

"…You still haven't gone."

"…No."

"…Why."

"…I'm not ready."

Speon set down his shovel.

"…Even after water changes direction, it takes time before it comes
out. If you keep tearing at the ground in the meantime, you only block
it. You have to know how to wait, too."

Hearing that, I felt a little relieved. It meant what I was putting off
might not be running away, but waiting. I fell asleep that night holding
onto those words.

---

On the fourth day, my brother pushed me himself.

"…Brother, when are you going again."

"…Why."

"…I miss Mom."

Hearing that, I knew I couldn't put it off any longer. I had been
spending those three days for myself. My brother hadn't.

"…All right. Let's go today."

---

We went together.

It had been a long time since my brother had walked beside me. As we
walked, he kept glancing sideways at the channel. I didn't ask whether
the sound of the water scared him. Instead I watched. He wasn't scared.

*That day, for the first time, I wondered when my brother had stopped
being afraid of the sound of water. There was no way to find out.
Asking him wouldn't have told me either. Seven years changes a person
that way. Without the person even knowing it themselves.*

Walking along, my brother suddenly asked,

"…Brother, what are you going for today?"

"…To see Mom."

"…Not that. Your face isn't the face of someone just going to see
someone."

That surprised me. I hadn't expected to hear something like that from
an eight-year-old.

"…I'm going to ask Mom something."

"…What?"

"…You'll find out when we get there."

My brother didn't press further. He just kept walking beside me.

---

At the front desk I hesitated a moment over what to write in the
purpose box on the form.

I wrote counsel.

*I looked at that word a long while. Counsel is words exchanged to
decide something. For the first time, I had written the exact word.*

My brother wrote his own name beside mine. His handwriting was
crooked.

---

My mother was glad to see the two of us.

"…You came together."

"…Yes." My brother answered first. "Brother brought me."

"…I was the one who said let's go." my brother added. "He was about to
go without me."

My mother laughed at that.

"…That sounds like you."

It had been a long time since I'd seen the two of them talk back and
forth so easily. I stayed a little back from it. Today I had something
else to say.

---

My brother talked about himself for a long while first.

"…The water's reached the big tree down there now." my brother said.
"I go every day to see how far it's gotten."

"…Is it fun?" my mother asked.

"…Yeah. Yesterday there was a little bug in the water. Mr. Speon says
that's a good thing. It means the water's alive."

"…Living water." my mother repeated the phrase. "That's an interesting
way to put it."

My brother kept going, excited. The nameless weeds blooming by the
ditch, the dry bread Speon sometimes gave him, how the sound of water
was like a lullaby at night.

My mother listened to all of it. Her eyes never left my brother.

*Watching that, I felt all over again that this was how she had always
responded to my brother. Listening to a child's words all the way
through. I knew that wasn't a response born of lack. It was just my
mother.*

---

When my brother's story wound down, I looked at my mother.

"…You asked me, last time." I said. "How I'd been."

My mother nodded.

"…I want to answer that properly, today."

My brother went quiet, reading the room. Eight years old, but he knew
when to do that.

---

This time I didn't summarize.

The winters at the settlement, the seventeen funerals, the well where I
drew water, the old man who taught me to dig. I told it in order. I
left some things out, but I didn't rush.

"…My uncle raised me." I said. "He wasn't a bad person. But since I
wasn't his own child, he only looked after me up to a point. I thought
that was just how it was."

"…It isn't how it should be." my mother said quietly.

"…I know that, now."

I went on — learning to dig wells at fourteen, the old man who'd been a
man of few words, that the ninth funeral had been his. My brother sat
quietly through all of it, just listening.

My mother never once interrupted. Only after hearing it all did she
ask,

"…So now."

"…Now I'm here." I said. "Because I want to see you and my brother
with living bodies again."

---

Even saying that, I tried to keep last month's self and this month's
self separate.

"…Before, I only thought it was for your sake." I said. "Now I know.
My own share is mixed into it too. I came here because I wanted to not
be alone again."

My mother wasn't surprised at that. Her face was one that had known for
a long time.

"…Is that a bad thing?" she asked.

"…I don't know. But I didn't want to hide it."

---

My mother looked out the window for a moment. Toward where the sound of
water was.

"…I've been thinking about it too." she said. "After you left, last
time, and the time after that, and the time after that. I kept
thinking."

"…About what."

"…This thing called release."

I stopped breathing at that. I had never brought that word up first.
Not this time either.

*I still remember that moment exactly. I heard that word from my
mother's mouth for the first time. Not me — my mother said it first.*

---

"…How did you know." I asked.

"…The Curator told me. That you'd asked, many times."

Hearing that, I thought of the Curator. He had never told me how my
mother was taking the idea. But apparently he had told her side of it.

"…Are you afraid?" I asked.

My mother thought about that for a moment.

"…I'm not even sure what afraid means, anymore." she said. "That's
probably the strange part."

---

In the meantime, my brother asked quietly,

"…What's release?"

"…It means becoming a living body again." I said. "Then you can get
sick again, and get hurt again."

"…So can you die again too?"

I couldn't answer that right away.

"…Yeah." I managed to say. "You could."

My brother listened to that quietly. He didn't ask anything further.

---

My mother looked at me again.

"…You came to ask me that, knowing all that?"

"…No." I said. "I didn't come to ask, today. I just came to answer
your question from last time."

My mother nodded at that.

"…But you ended up here anyway."

"…Yes."

---

The room was quiet for a while. Only the sound of water.

"…I don't." my mother finally spoke. "I don't really know what being
able to die again means. But watching you stand here talking about it,
it doesn't seem like a bad thing."

"…Is that the reason?" I asked.

"…Of course not." my mother laughed. "That's not a reason. It's just a
thought that came to me."

---

"…Then what's the reason?" I asked.

My mother looked at me for a long time. Then at my brother. Then at me
again.

"…I want to see the two of you standing here, side by side."

That was all. It wasn't a long speech. It wasn't an argument. The
moment I heard that sentence, I knew there was nothing left to ask.

*I've copied that sentence out many times since. I tried to change it
into something better. I couldn't. That sentence was right exactly as
it was.*

---

"…Are you consenting?" I asked. My voice shook.

"…Yes." my mother said. "I'll be released."

In that moment I didn't know what to say. I must have had words
prepared, but not one of them came to me.

In that silence I called up all seven years of visits at once. The
faces that had answered why with why. The answers that never changed.
And now, this one sentence, this moment. I understood that all of it
had been pointed toward this moment. But now that I had arrived, I
didn't even know whether I was supposed to feel glad.

My brother asked in my place.

"…So what happens now?"

"…There's a procedure." I managed to say. "They say it's simple."

I remembered what the Curator had told me. That one signature was
enough. That simplicity felt strange, somehow. That undoing seven years
could end with one signature.

"…So will Mom get sick again?" my brother asked.

I couldn't answer that right away.

"…I might." my mother answered in my place. "But that's all right
too."

"…Why is it all right?"

My mother stroked my brother's head.

"…Because being sick is being alive too."

My brother stayed quiet, his face unreadable — whether he'd understood
or not. I carved that answer into myself too.

---

We left the room and walked for a while without talking.

My brother took my hand. It had been a long time. He used to do it all
the time when he was little, but not these last few days.

"…Brother." my brother said. "Is Mom really going to live with us now?"

"…Yeah. That's how it'll be."

"…Then I want to do that too, later."

I stopped walking at that. My brother didn't seem to know yet what he
meant by it. I decided not to explain it, today.

"…Let's talk about that later." I said.

My brother nodded obediently.

---

Leaving the room that day, I looked for the Curator.

He was standing at the end of the hallway. It looked like he'd been
waiting.

"…My mother consented." I said.

The Curator nodded at that. Not a surprised face.

"…I knew." he said. "I've seen a few people come out with that face
before."

"…How many times?"

"…Not many." he said. "Truly not many."

"…What happened to them?" I asked.

The Curator hesitated a moment.

"…Most of them were released. After that, it's not our concern
anymore. Once they become living bodies again, they may leave this
city, or they may stay. That belongs to them."

"…Have some left?"

"…There have." he said. "I don't know how they went on to live. I want
to know, and at the same time I feel it might be better not to."

In those words I felt this man's old weariness again.

---

Walking back to my room with the procedure papers, I met Eikon.

"…Your face is different again today." she said.

"…Yeah. My mother consented."

Eikon stopped walking.

"…Really?"

"…Yeah."

She said nothing for a long while. I tried to read something in her
face. It wasn't envy. It wasn't fear either. Just a face weighing
something.

"…Wasn't she afraid?" she asked.

"…She said she wasn't sure what afraid even means."

"…Same with me." Eikon said. "Is that the same thing, that and this?"

I couldn't answer that right away.

"…I don't know." I said honestly.

Eikon nodded at that answer and went on her way.

---

That evening I told Speon the news too.

He was sitting by the ditch, watching the water flow.

"…She consented." I said.

Speon's face took a moment to take that in. Then he stood up and put
his hand into the channel.

"…I won't say congratulations." he said.

"…Why not?"

"…Because it's just the beginning. A well isn't finished just because
water comes out. That's when the upkeep starts."

I turned that over. It seemed right.

"…Still, rest a little today." he added. "Days like this don't come
often."

---

*That night I thought back on that sentence again. I want to see the
two of you standing here, side by side. I know that isn't an argument.
If you ask why choose to become able to die again, this sentence isn't
an answer. And yet this sentence was enough.*

*Of everything in this book, this is the scene I rewrote the most,
while writing it. I wanted to put in a more plausible reason. Every
time, I deleted it again. My mother didn't argue that day. She simply
wanted.*

*Before I fell asleep that night I thought back, too, on what my
brother had said. I want to do that too, later. I hadn't made any
promise to that yet. I only knew that it was the thing I would have to
face next.*
