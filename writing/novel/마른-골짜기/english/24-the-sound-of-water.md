# Chapter 24. The Sound of Water

> _Previously_ — I learned what had become of two of the eleven who'd
> come with us from the settlement. The traces of those whose
> registration was never finished go down into the Dry Valley. I
> didn't go down that day. But the landscape was no longer strange to
> me.

The days after that passed without much happening.

More precisely, nothing much seemed to happen, from the outside. I
didn't go to see my mother, or my brother. I ran into Eikon twice or
so, but we didn't talk long. I counted those days. Four of them.

*I felt the temptation to skip these four days in this account.
Nothing had happened, after all. But the fact that nothing happened
was itself a rare thing in this book. Up until then I'd run into
something new every day. Why, I'll do it, the ones it failed. For
four days I ran into nothing at all. I only walked, went to the
ration station, and slept.*

During those four days I went to the valley's edge twice more. No
one was there. I stood alone, looked down, and came back. I wasn't
trying to find anything. It only seemed that the landscape had found
its place inside my days by then.

I didn't take out the application again during those days, either.
Four of my five fingers had broken, and one I'd hidden away myself.
My brother's application I'd withdrawn with my own hand. I wanted to
set it all down for a while. Setting it down didn't mean giving up.
I just wanted to breathe a little.

*Looking back on those four days, I think they were the quietest
stretch in this whole book. Quiet didn't mean peaceful. I spent those
days still waiting for something. I didn't know exactly what.*

On the third day the attendant at the ration station greeted me a
little differently.

"…You've been quiet lately," he said.

"…Yeah. I guess so."

"…Are you all right?"

I couldn't answer that question right away. It wasn't common for
someone in this city to ask after me first.

"…I don't know. I think I'm waiting for something."

"…For what?"

"…I don't know either."

The attendant didn't add anything more to that answer. He just
filled the flask and handed it back. Taking the water, I felt again
how strange the word "waiting" sounded in this city. People here
rarely had anything to wait for. They could get what they wanted when
they wanted it.

On the fourth day I ran into Eikon in the garden. She spoke first.

"…You haven't been asking lately," Eikon said.

"…Asking what?"

"…You used to ask all the time. Doesn't it frustrate you, isn't it
sad, that kind of thing."

I smiled a little at that. She was right.

"…I think I understand now. Even without asking."

"…Understand what?"

"…That you really are all right."

Eikon heard that and stared at me for a moment. Not a surprised
face. Just a face confirming something.

"…Finally figured it out."

I laughed again at that answer. This time she laughed too. A short
laugh. And yet it was the lightest moment of those few days. I wanted
to hold on to that lightness for a long while, but it wasn't the kind
of thing that stretched the longer you held it.

---

On the evening of the fifth day, I was sitting by the window in my
room.

It was not long past sunset. The city's lights were coming on one by
one. I followed the order with my eyes for a while, then stopped. It
was an order I'd already counted more than once.

The flask sat beside me. Nearly empty. I fiddled with its cap,
thinking I'd have to fill it the next morning.

*I still remember that evening clearly. I thought it would be an
evening with nothing to it. It had been that way four days running,
so I assumed the fifth would be the same.*

A little wind came in through the gap in the window. Not a common
wind, in this city. I leaned into it slightly.

---

That was when I heard the sound.

At first I didn't know what it was. Very faint. Mixed in with the
city's other sounds, I couldn't be sure whether it was really a
sound at all, or just my own imagining.

I held my breath and listened.

It came again. A little clearer this time. Something being dragged.
Then something unspooling. Long, at a steady pace.

In that moment I felt my body react before my mind did. Before I
could think, my hand had already gone to the windowsill. I knew this
sound before I'd even realized I knew it.

---

It was the sound of a well rope unspooling.

I'd heard that sound since I was born. At our settlement's well,
several times a day. The particular friction of the rope paying out,
and the creak of the wooden pulley taking it up. Then a short
stillness. And at the end, far off and small, a splash — water
touched.

I knew the order of that sound exactly. The drag, the unspooling, the
stillness, the splash. I'd grown up counting those four beats. The
same beats my brother had once been afraid of.

*I already wrote down this sound in Chapter 1. The sound my brother
had been afraid of, until one day, out of nowhere, he said he liked
it. Because it meant his brother was there, and that was good. I had
never once forgotten that sound, in seven years.*

I closed my eyes and gave myself over entirely to the sound. The
drag — I knew that was the rope going over the pulley. The unspool —
the bucket falling, the rope paying out behind it. The stillness in
between was the time the bucket spent crossing empty air. Its length
told you the depth of the well. I counted that stillness. One, two,
three, four. Only after four did the splash come.

*Four beats. I remembered that number as though it had been carved
into my fingers. Our settlement's well had been three. This one was
deeper than that. I don't know why that difference stayed with me so
strongly. Probably because that single number told me this wasn't the
well I knew — it was an entirely new well.*

I thought, too, of the water jar my mother had filled the night
before she left. The water we'd left three days undrunk, gone bad on
the fourth day and thrown out. I still remembered the weight of that
jar exactly, its smell, the moment I'd cried for the first time,
pouring that water away. I couldn't say why that memory rose up now,
of all times. But the sound kept calling it back.

*And that sound, I was hearing again, in this city without water.*

---

I sprang up from my seat.

I looked out the window. The sound seemed to be coming from the
east side of the city. I couldn't judge the exact direction. But
from the nature of the sound, it wasn't close by. And yet, in this
city's stillness, it carried through with perfect clarity.

I opened the window a little wider. The sound grew louder. The drag,
the unspooling, the stillness, and this time, a little louder — the
splash.

*In that moment I realized how long I'd been waiting for this sound.
Realizing it startled me. It was strange, to have been waiting for
something without even knowing I was waiting for it.*

I looked around the room. I wondered whether anyone else had heard
this sound. I looked out over the city through the window. Everyone
was moving about as usual. No one stopped walking. No one turned
toward the sound.

*That day I confirmed, with my own eyes, that this city truly did
not hear sound. It wasn't that they couldn't hear it. That sound was
clearly loud enough to hear. It was only that no one had any reason
to respond to it. However loud a sound might be, if it was a sound
no one needed, it wasn't a sound at all, in this city.*

Footsteps sounded on the floor below. Another lodger. I threw the
window open wide and called down.

"…Do you hear that?" I called down.

The person stopped walking and looked up at me.

"…Hear what?"

"…That sound, over there. Sounds like something dragging."

The person made a show of listening for a moment. Then shook their
head.

"…Hm. Can't say I do."

For a moment that answer made me doubt whether I'd misheard. But the
sound was still there, clear as ever. The drag, the unspooling, the
stillness, the splash. I couldn't tell whether that person truly
couldn't hear it, or heard it and simply let it pass.

"…Right. Sorry to bother you," I said.

The person nodded once and walked on. I watched their back and left
the window open instead of closing it.

---

I stood there and listened to nothing but that sound for a long
while.

The drag. The unspooling. The stillness. The splash.

I counted the beats with my fingers, pressing down on each one.
Once, twice, three times. Someone was drawing water again and again.
It wasn't a sound that ended after one round.

*I think now that the repetition was, more than anything else, the
proof. If it had happened only once, it could have been chance. Could
have been the sound of something dropped by accident. But the sound
kept going. It was the rhythm of someone at work. I know that
rhythm. Different from the rhythm I learned at the old man's side,
and yet the same kind of rhythm. Not hurried, and yet never
stopping.*

This city has no well. I'd known that fact from my very first day in
the city. The ration station handed out water. The preserved needed
none, and the living received only their allotted share and drank it.
I'd been the only person in this city who carried a flask around with
him.

And yet right now, somewhere out there, someone was drawing water
from a well.

---

I stepped back from the window and stood in the middle of the room.

I ran through a few possibilities. Had the ration station brought in
some new equipment? No — that sound wasn't coming from the ration
station's direction. The direction was wrong. Was someone digging
something, on their own? But no one in this city dug into the
ground. There was no reason to.

*Crossing out those possibilities one by one, I found only one left
standing. Someone was digging a well. Something no one had done since
this city was built, someone was doing right now.*

I tried to imagine who that person might be. It didn't seem like
anyone from this city. People here always said there was no reason
to dig a well. That meant it would have to be someone from outside,
like me — and yet I'd never once seen another outsider in this city
besides myself.

*In that moment, for the first time, I found myself curious. That
there might be another person from outside, here in this city besides
me. I felt that curiosity outweigh any fear. A strange thing. A
stranger was doing a strange thing, and I wasn't afraid. If anything,
I was glad.*

I tried to count how much time had passed since the sound began. I
couldn't count it exactly. While I was listening to the sound, I
couldn't count anything else.

---

I picked up the flask. Nearly empty. I stood there a moment, feeling
that lightness in my fingertips.

In that moment I ran back, one by one, through everything that had
happened to me in this city. My mother's "Why?" My brother's "I'll
do it." The withdrawn application. The trace of two people whose
names I didn't know. For the first time I strung all of it together
into a single line. This city was a place without thirst. Without
thirst, there was no wanting; without wanting, there was no consent.
I couldn't lay out that connection in exact words, in that moment.
But I felt it in my body.

*I would unpack that evening's thought in much greater detail, much
later. Here I set down only the thread of it. That the sound of
water had returned to a city without water. I believed then, and I
believe now, that it wasn't a coincidence.*

*Writing this chapter, I've thought many times about why I didn't
run straight out that night. It wouldn't have been strange if I had
bolted the moment I heard the sound. But I didn't. I stood there and
wanted to listen a little longer. Before confirming anything, I just
wanted to listen.*

The drag. The unspooling. The stillness. The splash.

I heard those beats again and again. Outside, the city's lights began
going out, one by one. This city's night slept on a fixed schedule.
But that sound alone never went out. It continued late into the
night.

I lay down without closing the window. I wanted to fall asleep to
that sound.

Closing my eyes, I saw the well at the settlement again. The path my
brother used to walk behind me on. My mother's words, patting his
back. Why make something scarier when he's already scared. I hadn't
thought of those words in a long while. This city was a place afraid
of nothing. Because there was nothing here to be afraid of — I'd
already written that, back in Chapter 1. But the sound I was hearing
now seemed to be waking something this city had forgotten long ago.

*That night I didn't yet know what this sound was waking. If I had
known, I wouldn't have fallen asleep so easily. Looking back now, I
think that ignorance was, if anything, the last peace I was allowed
that night.*

The sound of water went on past midnight. The drag, the unspooling,
the stillness, the splash. Instead of counting those four beats, I
simply let my body go. It had been a long time since I'd heard a
sound without counting it — only listening.

*I slept deeply that night, for the first time in a long while. It
was the most restful sleep I'd had since coming to this city. The
next morning, I decided to follow that sound. That is what happens
in the next part of this account.*

*Ever since I came into this city, I'd felt as though I were
standing in front of a door. In front of the people who do not
disappear, in front of the words *it can be reversed*, in front of
the question *why*. This time was different. This time, for the
first time, someone on the other side of the door was already
making a sound of their own. All I had to do was follow it.*
