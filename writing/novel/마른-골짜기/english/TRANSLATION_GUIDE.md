# The Dry Valley — Translation Guide

Book 5 of the **Loop Chronicles**. Korean source: 『마른 골짜기』.
This guide is authoritative. Where it conflicts with a translator's
instinct, the guide wins — consistency across 48 chapters matters more than
any single elegant line.

Books 1–4 use the same conventions; see their guides under
`../../루프-연대기/english/`, `../../울음-장부/english/`,
`../../사십일-조항/english/`, and `../../이삭-줍는-자리/english/`.

## 4. Register and voice

- **First-person retrospective.** Anemos narrates after the fact and knows
  how it ends. Past tense throughout.
- **Italic passages** marked `*...*` are his later commentary — the
  "지금은 안다" voice. Keep them italic in English. They are the series'
  signature device; never merge them into surrounding narration.
- **Dialogue opens with an ellipsis**: `"…네."` → `"…Yes."` Keep the
  ellipsis exactly. Do not convert to "Well—" or drop it.
- **Anemos counts things.** Steps, days, the number of times someone says
  the same sentence. Keep every number. Do not paraphrase a count into
  "several" or "a few." The counting is characterization: it is how a boy
  holds on to what he cannot keep.
- **Short paragraphs, spare prose.** Resist smoothing Korean's clipped
  sentences into flowing English periods.
- Anemos does not plead or dramatize. His feeling shows in what he notices
  and what he declines to write down.
- **No Scripture epigraphs** per chapter. Each chapter opens with a
  `> *이전 이야기*` recap block only → render as `> _Previously_ — ...`

## 5. Scripture

Use the **King James Version** only (public domain). Book 5's seed text is
Ezekiel 37, and its diction should be audible under the prose:

**Ezekiel 37:3** — *"And he said unto me, Son of man, can these bones
live? And I answered, O Lord GOD, thou knowest."*

**Ezekiel 37:9** — *"Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe upon
these slain, that they may live."*

Two consequences for the translation:

1. Ch.31's question is the book's second axis. The Korean is deliberately
   **plain, not liturgical** — he is a man standing among bones asking a
   boy a question. Render it **"…Do you think these can live again?"**
   and keep that exact wording every time it returns (ch.31 ×3, ch.32 ×3,
   ch.37, ch.48). Do **not** import the KJV's "can these bones live" into
   the dialogue itself; the chapter title carries the KJV echo instead.
2. Anemos's answer is **"…I don't know."** — three times in ch.31, and it
   is **received as the right answer**. Never soften it into "I'm not
   sure," never let the prose hint that he is failing the question.
   *Thou knowest* is the model; he simply lacks the last two words.

## 9. Rules that must not be broken

1. **Perpetuity never breaks its promise.** Not once in this book. The
   preserved really do not disappear, really do not suffer, really are
   themselves. There is no twist in which the preservation turns out to be
   fake, a copy, or a lie. Never translate in a way that hedges this or
   winks at the reader. The horror is that the promise is kept.
2. **Perpetuity makes no judgment at all.** Unlike the Scales (Bk 3) and the
   Roll (Bk 4), it does not weigh, rank, or qualify anyone — and the result
   is the same anyway. Never give it an opinion, a preference, or a motive.
3. **Eikon is satisfied, not pitiable.** She is not suffering and she is not
   in denial. Her argument is hard to answer and the book does not hurry to
   answer it. Never add a tremor, a crack, or a hint of secret longing that
   the Korean does not have. She does not convert, and ch.45 ends with her
   asking to be asked again later — leave it there.
4. **The Curator is sad.** He knows exactly what his work is and continues.
   He is the most human official in the series. Never render him as cold,
   smug, or evil, and never let him defend himself.
5. **He never gets angry.** The walking man, as in Books 1–4. He was in the
   valley before Anemos came down, and the book never says how long. Do not
   let any line imply an answer.
6. **Never gloss the names before chapter 44.** Not in a footnote, not in a
   chapter title, not in a recap block. In ch.44: **Anemos = ἄνεμος,
   "wind" — the thing that cannot be kept.** **Eikon = εἰκών, "image,"
   also "likeness."** Eikon has no reply to hers; preserve that silence.
7. **Anemos is not a hero.** He came out of love, and mixed into that love
   is something for himself — he wants to go back to when he was not alone.
   He sees this himself in ch.38–39. Never translate his motives as purer
   than the Korean makes them.
8. **Death is not glorified.** The ending is not "dying is good." It is
   "being able to die again means being able to live again," and the Korean
   never states that sentence — it only shows it. Do not supply it.
9. **No declared faith.** No character says the English equivalent of
   "이제 믿습니다." The ending stays open: a single finger-width sprig of
   green in wet soil, and nothing said about it.
