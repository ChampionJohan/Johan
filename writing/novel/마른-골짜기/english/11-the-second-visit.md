# Chapter 11. The Second Visit

> _Previously_ — At his first visit with his brother, Anemos was undone by one line: "Brother, you got taller." That night, in the lodging house, he cried out loud for the first time. A few days passed. He decided to apply for another visit.

I waited three days, then applied for a second visit.

*Three days. I still remember that number exactly. The first day simply passed. On the second, I held the application in my hand but couldn't make it to the counter. It was only on the morning of the third day that I finally submitted it.*

The officer was a young woman again. I couldn't be sure it was the same one. I didn't really look at her face that day.

"…How long will it take?" I asked.

*I had asked that same question, word for word, back in Chapter 7. This time, I felt like I knew the answer before I even heard it.*

"…About half an hour."

It was exactly the same answer.

*Right then I thought of the older man from Chapter 7. That face with every expression used up. I know exactly now what had caused that face. He must have heard this same answer any number of times. The same half hour, every time.*

---

I sat in the waiting room. I didn't take out the paper. This time, I had no prepared words.

*To be precise, I did have one. Just one. Let's go together now. I still carried those words. Only, I had no wish to write them on paper again and set them in order.*

Through the window I could see that tree. The one whose leaves barely stirred even in the wind.

*Confirming the tree was exactly the same, I laughed a little. I felt like I could count the leaves one by one. In fact I did. Just as before, the officer came when I reached twenty.*

"…Ready now."

I stood up. My hands didn't tremble this time.

*I thought, at the time, it was because I'd grown used to it. I think differently now. Trembling comes from expectation. That morning I already knew what I was about to see.*

---

The corridor was exactly the same. Three doors, the middle one.

The officer said, from behind me,

"…Take your time."

*That line was exactly the same too. I wrote, in Chapter 8, how strangely kind that line had felt. Hearing it a second time, something else came before the kindness. This line was part of the procedure. Words said at this exact spot, every time.*

I pushed the door.

---

The room was small. There was one window, and sunlight was coming in. Two chairs stood facing each other.

My mother was sitting in one of them.

*I wrote that same sentence in Chapter 8. There was no reason to write it differently. What I saw that day was exactly that.*

She saw me and stood up.

"…Anemos."

"…Mom."

Right there I noticed that even the order those two words came out in was the same as last time. She said my name first, and I said Mom after.

---

She came to me and cupped my face in both hands.

"…You've grown so much."

I froze a little at that.

*I had come back after only three days. There was no way I could have grown taller in three days. I did the math right there. There wasn't. There really wasn't.*

"…Yes." I answered, exactly as before.

"…Have you been eating well?"

"…Yes, I've been eating well."

She looked me over for a long time. Up and down, from my face to my feet.

"…Your voice has deepened too."

*There was no way my voice could have deepened in three days either. A chill ran down my spine the moment I heard that. My mother was looking at me. Looking at me exactly. But that looking wasn't directed at the me standing there now. Words meant for a son she'd parted with seven years ago were coming out again, unchanged.*

She noticed the flask slung over my shoulder and tapped it lightly with her fingertips.

"…What's this?"

"…A water flask. My uncle gave it to me."

"…Did you think you couldn't get here without water?"

"…Just habit, I guess."

*Even as I gave that answer, I silently said the next line to myself in advance. Same as ever, you. Even as a child you always carried everything you needed. I already knew it before it was even said.*

She smiled a little at my answer.

"…Same as ever, you." she said. "Even as a child you always carried everything you needed."

It was exact.

---

"…Sit." she said.

I sat in the chair across from her. She took my hand. It was warm.

"…Are you all right?" I asked. "Does it hurt?"

"…No, it doesn't hurt at all."

"…Is anything hard?"

"…No. Everything's fine."

*I wrote these four lines in Chapter 8 without changing a single word. Copying them out again now, I still can't find a place to change. That day, these four lines really did come out exactly the same.*

"…Weren't you scared?" I asked. "Back then, when the fever got bad and you had to lie down."

She thought for a moment, then said,

"…I think I was scared. But I don't remember it well."

"…You don't?"

"…The feeling from back then is blurry. Because now I'm not scared at all."

Listening to that answer, I pressed my fingernails into my palm.

*I wanted to confirm, right then, whether this was really the same, or whether I was forcing something similar to overlap in my mind. The pain in my palm was real. And yet my mother's words, as vivid as that pain, were words I'd heard somewhere before. From that moment on, I was certain.*

---

"…Did you know I was coming all the way here?" I asked.

"…No, I didn't know. But it's not a surprise."

"…Why?"

"…Because you're the kind of child who'd come."

*Even that answer. I hadn't expected even that answer to be the same. This was a line that came out even when the question changed. I had thought I'd asked a slightly different question from three days ago. And yet the answer wasn't different in the least.*

"…Did you miss me?" I asked.

She paused a moment at that question.

"…Yes. I missed you."

I even counted the length of that pause.

*One, two. In Chapter 8 too, I had felt that pause. I hadn't counted how long it was, then. This time I counted. It was two. Exactly two.*

---

We talked a little more. About Gaon, about the lodging house, about the person next door. Talking about those things, I tried guessing her answers ahead of time, one by one.

*Every time my guess was right, I felt a strange feeling. It wasn't joy. It wasn't relief either. It was closer, if anything, to fear. I knew what would come out of my mother's mouth before she said it.*

Once, I deliberately asked something different.

"…Mom, I've been thinking about something lately."

*That was the first time I'd said that. It was something I hadn't said last time. I was curious what kind of reaction it would draw.*

"…What kind of thing?" she asked.

I couldn't answer. Since it wasn't a question I'd prepared for, the words stuck on my end.

"…Never mind."

She nodded. She didn't press.

*That part was exactly like Chapter 8. That day I learned that even throwing in something new only brought us back to the same old place. The problem wasn't my mother. She was always standing in the same place. It was I who threw in something new, and yet that newness kept getting absorbed back into place.*

I tried another question.

"…How is it, staying here?"

"…Good. Everything's good."

"…Isn't it boring?"

"…What is there to be bored of."

*I had already known that answer too. She had said those exact words in Chapter 8. Hearing it again, I fully accepted that it made no difference how I changed the question. It wasn't the question that was fixed — it was the answer.*

I wondered whether the subject of my father would come up again.

"…You must have thought of your father a lot too." she said first.

*I had known that line was coming. It was the exact line she had raised first, back in Chapter 8. I deliberately said nothing and waited. I wanted to see whether she'd really bring it up first, on her own.*

"…Do you remember his face?" she asked.

"…It's a little blurry."

"…That can happen."

*All three lines were exactly the same. I had thought, in Chapter 8, that calm was because she was strong. I know it differently now. It wasn't strength — it was a fixed place. In front of that question, she always stood in the exact same spot.*

---

The officer opened the door a little and said,

"…Time's up."

She didn't let go of my hand, and said,

"…Come again."

"…Yes."

"…Let's talk more next time."

*Next time. I had heard that word before too. Next time was always pushed off to a further next time. I started to count how many "next times" this would end up being, and gave it up.*

At the door she held me once. Her embrace was exactly as I remembered. Same warmth, same strength.

"…Bye now."

I stopped in my tracks at those two words.

*In Chapter 8 I wrote that I hadn't thought, at the time, how strange that farewell was — how it was exactly what you'd say to someone you'd met yesterday, even though it had been seven years. This time, I really hadn't met her yesterday. Only three days had passed. And yet the farewell wasn't different in the least. Only then did I understand. That farewell had nothing to do with time. Whether seven years or three days, to my mother it was the same distance, no different at all.*

---

I came back out to the waiting room and sat down.

*Sitting there, I tried, for the first time, to put into words what I'd just seen. The same greeting, the same order, the same pause, the same smile. I counted through that list in my head, up to ten, and stopped. Not because there was nothing more to count, but because I felt that counting further would be frightening.*

The officer came over and said,

"…Shall we go see your brother too?"

I nodded.

The officer spoke again as we walked.

"…Your brother likes to draw."

*I had heard that same line before. Not a single particle different. Right then I realized this line was something like a script. Not words the officer was saying to me now, but words attached to the procedure itself.*

---

The way to my brother's room was short. I stopped for a moment in front of the door.

*By then I half-knew what would happen when I opened that door. And still, I opened it. I learned, for the first time that day, that opening a door knowing and opening it not knowing are two different things.*

The door opened. My brother was standing at the window.

His eyes went wide at the sight of me. He came toward me. I didn't count his steps this time. There was no need. I already knew it would be six.

He stopped in front of me and looked up at me.

"…Brother, you got taller."

---

I couldn't breathe at those words.

*The exact same words as three days ago. There was no way I could have grown taller in three days. My brother didn't know that. Or rather, it wasn't that he didn't know — it didn't matter to him. This line came from the same place, always, whether three days had passed or seven years.*

*This one line shook me more than my mother's "you've grown so much" had. Her words had sounded like inertia. My brother's were different. He was really looking up at me, really feeling that way, and really saying it because of that feeling. And yet the exact same feeling as three days ago was being manufactured, fresh, right there.*

*I understood, then. This wasn't repetition. It was, every time, the first time. To my brother, I really did look, every time, like I was being seen for the first time. Three days apart or seven years apart weighed exactly the same inside him — new. And that newness carried no memory.*

My brother took my hand.

"…Brother, you came again."

"…Yeah."

"…That was fast."

"…Yeah." was all I answered.

*I couldn't add anything after that short answer. There was nothing to add. I wanted to say that I'd come back after only three days, but I had no idea what that would even mean to him.*

---

The visit was shorter than last time. I didn't say much. My brother didn't say much either. We just sat there holding hands.

I heard that time was up.

"…You'll come again, right?" my brother asked.

"…Yes. I'll come again."

*Giving that answer, I doubted the weight of those words for the first time. I'll come again. How many times had I said that now. I started to count and gave it up.*

Leaving through the door, I turned to look at my brother once more. He was waving. The same gesture as last time.

*I even felt the angle of that wave was the same as last time. The slight bend of the wrist, the speed his fingers opened. I knew that was a leap, and I still couldn't stop myself from feeling it.*

---

Outside, I walked the road back to the lodging house.

*Walking that road, I kept thinking the same thought. Everything I had seen today was exactly what I had seen three days ago. The order, the words, even the length of the pauses. I started to calculate the odds of that being coincidence, and gave it up. I already knew it wasn't coincidence.*

Only when I reached the lodging house did I stop. I opened the flask and took a mouthful of water.

*That night before I fell asleep, I thought this. If I go next time, it will be exactly the same again. And the time after that, exactly the same again. I couldn't tell whether that thought was sad or frightening. But one thing was clear. I was already dreading the next visit.*

*That night I thought again of something my uncle had said. If you go, you have to see it, don't you. I hadn't understood those words, walking the four days here. Now I thought I understood a little. My uncle had already known about this repetition. What it meant to see the same thing, every single time.*

*Even so, I decided not to give up on the next visit. Not yet.*

I added that day's visit to the count. One, two.

*I didn't know, then, when that number would stop.*
