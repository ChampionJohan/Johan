# Chapter 30. The One Already There

> _Previously_ — Far off, deeper in the valley, I saw something moving.
> It was too far to make out what it was. I started walking that way.
> Until that moment I had believed no one else was in this valley besides
> me.

The closer I got, the more that moving shape was a person.

I was sure of it by the time we were about a hundred steps apart. I
recognized it not by the walk itself but by the rhythm of it. An animal
doesn't walk that way. Only a person walks like that — stopping, then
going again, lowering the body, then standing back up.

I started counting my steps. Ninety-nine, a hundred. Counting helped
press the fear down. Eighty, seventy. The number shrank as the distance
shrank.

He didn't seem to have seen me. Or — he'd seen me, but it didn't seem to
concern him. He walked slowly among the scattered things. Every so often
he stopped and looked down at something. Then he walked on again.

*I understood then that his was not the walk of someone searching. A
person searching looks around. He didn't look around. He walked the way
someone walks who already knows exactly what is where.*

I tried to count his steps. Fifty, sixty. But at some point I lost the
count. The timing of when he stopped wasn't regular. In some spots he
stood a long time; some he simply passed by. I tried to start counting
again, and gave it up.

*That was the first time in my life I gave up counting something. It
wasn't that I couldn't count it. I felt, in my body, that counting simply
didn't fit this person. In the city, everything could be counted. The
number of visits. The benches in the square. The number of times my
mother said the same sentence. This man's footsteps were unlike any of
those.*

Looking down, I saw my own footprints falling in a row alongside his.
Mine were clear and distinct. His pressed in and then blurred almost at
once. I couldn't tell whether that was the wind, or simply the way he
set his feet down. I'd learned the day before that this valley didn't
hold even a new trace for long, but his footprints seemed to vanish
faster than that, faster than mine.

---

About thirty steps off, I stopped walking.

I wasn't sure it was right to go any closer. The valley had none of the
city's rules. No one here told you who anyone was, or what you were
allowed to do.

He was the one who stopped first.

He didn't turn all the way around. Only his head tilted slightly this
way. In the dimming light, I couldn't make out his face well. I couldn't
guess his age. He seemed old, and at the same time not so old.

His clothes weren't the city's clothes. Nor were they like the outside
clothes Speon wore. The color was almost indistinguishable from the
soil. I couldn't tell whether that was from long wear, or whether that
had always been their color.

"…Hello," I said first.

He said nothing back. Only he turned his head a little further this way.
I could tell he was looking at me, but there was no surprise in that
look.

*I would go back to this many times afterward. That he wasn't surprised,
that day. A person had appeared in a valley no one ever comes to, and he
showed no surprise at all. As if he'd already known, even if not today,
that someone would come sometime.*

---

I took a few more steps closer.

The sound of dry things crumbling came from underfoot. That sound rang
unusually loud to me. It was probably because of the different kind of
breathing I was hearing for the first time since I'd come to this
valley. Not my own breath. Someone else's breath.

"…Were you already here?" I asked.

"…Yes."

His voice was low. Not angry, not glad to see me. Just a voice giving an
answer.

"…I'm Anemos."

At that he nodded once. He didn't give his own name in return. I thought
that was strange, but I didn't ask again. I seemed to have learned
somewhere, without knowing where, that this was the courteous thing to
do.

*In this record I keep calling this person "he." I can't explain exactly
why. Only, I felt for the first time that day that there are people it
isn't right to give a name to.*

---

He started walking again. It wasn't a walk meant to shake me off. It was
simply the walk of someone continuing what he'd already been doing.

I followed a few steps behind.

Every place he stopped seemed to have a reason. In some spots he
lowered himself and looked a long time at something on the ground. In
others he reached out and touched a dry branch, very lightly. He didn't
break it. It looked like a gesture only meant to confirm that it was
still there.

"…What are you doing?" I asked.

"…Walking."

"…Just that?"

"…Yes."

That answer was so simple it made me want to ask more, rather than less.

"…Do you walk like this every day?"

"…Nearly."

"…Why?"

He stopped walking and looked at me. The first time our eyes properly
met.

"…Why would I need a reason not to?"

I had no answer to that. He was right. Walking didn't need a reason. In
the city it would have needed one. Here, apparently, it didn't.

---

*I thought, at the time, that this man resembled Speon. A man who kept
going without needing a reason. I learned later that this wasn't quite
right. Speon was making something. A well, a channel for water. This man
made nothing. He only walked. I didn't understand then how large that
difference was.*

I walked alongside him. He didn't stop me from coming up beside him. Nor
did he welcome it. He simply let it be.

We walked a while without speaking. It struck me as strange that this
silence didn't feel uncomfortable. Walking quietly like this beside
someone I'd only just met wasn't a common thing, even outside.

He stopped again in one spot. In front of a small heap of bones. I
couldn't remember whether I'd passed this spot before. Inside the
valley, every heap looked alike, and then looked entirely different from
every other.

He crouched down in front of it. He reached out and touched the soil,
lightly. He didn't dig into it. It was only a gesture of stroking the
surface.

"…Someone you knew?" I asked.

"…No."

"…Then why touch it?"

He drew his hand back and stood up.

"…Does touching have to be the same as knowing?"

Again I had no answer to that. This was a man who answered a question
with a question. I couldn't tell whether that was evasion, or whether he
was genuinely asking out of curiosity.

---

The sun went fully down.

Stars began coming out above the valley wall. The same stars I'd seen
from the city's edge. That familiarity was, again, a small comfort.

"…How long have you been here?" I asked again.

This time I asked it a little differently. The first time it had been
"were you already here." This time it was "how long." I felt that the
difference mattered.

He said nothing for a moment.

It wasn't an angry expression. I studied his face closely. There really
was no emotion in it at all. No irritation, no displeasure, and no trace
of amusement either. Just empty — but not an emptiness that was cold.
An emptiness that was quiet.

"…I don't know," he said.

"…You don't know?"

"…I stopped counting a long time ago."

I stopped short at that answer. It was something I'd heard somewhere
before. The Curator had said something like it. That it had been a long
time since he'd stopped counting. At the time I'd thought it was a way
of avoiding grief.

This time something was different. This man didn't seem to not count
because he was avoiding grief. He seemed not to count simply because
there was no need to.

"…Longer than seven years?" I asked. I thought if I gave him a specific
number, he might answer.

"…Maybe."

"…Ten years?"

"…I don't know."

*I realized then that this conversation was strange. In the city, when
you asked with a number, people answered with a number. Exactly. This
man, asked with a number, didn't answer with a number. And yet he wasn't
lying, either. He really seemed not to know.*

---

"…Then what can you tell me?" I asked.

For the first time he tilted his head slightly. It wasn't a gesture of
amusement. It was only the gesture of someone hearing the question
again.

"…I was here first," he said. "Is that enough?"

"…First?"

"…Before you came."

I thought that wasn't really an answer. And yet, strangely, I didn't
want to press further. I felt, in my body, before I thought it, that he
wouldn't say anything more than that.

*I return to that moment often, afterward. He wasn't angry. He turned the
question aside, and there wasn't a trace of irritation in it. He didn't
laugh either. I'd seen plenty of people who deflect while laughing. This
man didn't run away into laughter either. He simply turned it aside, as
it was. I felt that was a far more honest way to do it. It's a strange
thing to say — honest, while turning something aside — but that is
exactly how it felt to me.*

---

I undid my flask.

"…Would you like some water?" I asked.

He looked at me a moment. Then he shook his head.

"…You don't need any?" I asked again.

"…I'm fine."

It was a phrase I was used to hearing in this city. I don't need it. I'm
fine. But the way this man said it was somehow different from the way
the city's people said it. In the city, people's "I'm fine" meant they
really were filled up, and so they were fine. This man's "I'm fine" — I
didn't know how to put it. It wasn't that he was full and so fine. It
was more that he was a person who simply had very little need at all.

I drank a mouthful of water by myself. My throat was dry.

"…And food?" I asked. "You don't eat?"

"…Sometimes."

"…Only sometimes?"

"…Yes."

I noticed that he didn't look hungry. His body was thin, but it didn't
look weak. I thought that was strange, but this whole valley was
strange, so I let it pass as one more strangeness among the rest.

---

Night settled fully in.

I thought briefly about whether I should find the way back to the city.
But my body pushed that thought aside. Climbing a dark slope was
dangerous. And more than that, I still wanted to be here a while longer.
I didn't know the reason for that, even then.

"…Can I stay here tonight?" I asked.

He looked at me. Neither permission nor refusal in the look.

"…That's for you to decide."

"…Isn't it dangerous?"

"…What is?"

"…Sleeping here."

"…I sleep every day."

That surprised me a little. It was the first time I learned that this
man slept here.

"…Where?"

He pointed a hand toward the distance. I looked that way. It was too
dark to see anything.

"…There's a hollow over there."

*I thought of the shallow hollow I'd seen in Chapter 29 — the one too
dark inside to make anything out, the one I hadn't gone into. Knowing now
that he slept there, I was glad I hadn't looked inside it. I would have
felt as if I'd peered into someone's room without asking.*

"…Aren't you cold, there?" I asked.

"…I'm used to it."

"…Is being used to it the same as not being cold?"

Only after I'd said it did I realize it was something my mother had once
said to me. When my brother had been afraid of the bucket's sound at
the well, my mother had asked me: is being used to it the same as not
being afraid?

At that question he looked at me a little longer than before, for the
first time.

"…No," he said. "It isn't the same."

That answer surprised me. In the city no one would have answered that
way. Everyone said that once you got used to something, it was fine. This
man kept the two things apart.

*I held onto that short answer for a long time afterward. This man never
got angry and never laughed, and yet somewhere in him he was exact,
precisely exact. I felt, dimly, even then, that this exactness was
different from the city's exactness. The city's exactness came from
knowing everything. This man's exactness seemed to come from having
lived through something, for a long time.*

---

I settled myself down on the bare ground. He sat a few steps off. Not
side by side, but not far either.

"…Will you walk like this again tomorrow?" I asked.

"…Yes."

"…Can I come with you?"

This time he looked at me a little longer.

"…If you want to."

I liked that answer. It neither stopped me nor led me on.

More stars came out. I tried to count them, then gave it up. It wasn't a
number that could be counted.

"…Won't you tell me your name?" I asked, last.

He was silent for a moment. There was no anger in that silence either.

"…Why would I need a name?"

I had no answer to that. He closed his eyes after that. I couldn't tell
whether he was sleeping or simply had his eyes shut.

*That night, before I fell asleep, I had a strange thought. This man was
unlike anyone I'd ever met. The city's people gave short answers because
they wanted nothing. This man gave short answers for some other reason.
I didn't know what it was, then. The next day, he would ask me a question
first. Standing in front of that question, I would come to see part of
that reason.*

The wind flowed low along the valley wall. Dry things rustled. I closed
my eyes listening to that sound.

I woke once in the night. I didn't know how long I'd slept. Opening my
eyes, I found he was still sitting in the same spot. His eyes were
closed. He might have been asleep; he might only have been sitting
there.

I looked at his back for a moment. The angle of his shoulders, the way
he sat. There wasn't a single tremor in it. I wondered whether a living
person could stay that unmoving for that long.

*Every time I recall that night since, it's that stillness that comes
back first. I had lived believing that every living person trembles a
little. Everyone outside was like that. Breathing shakes you; cold shakes
you. That night, for the first time, I fell asleep beside someone who
did not tremble at all.*

I closed my eyes again. When I next opened them, it was morning.
