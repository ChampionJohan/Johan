# Chapter 6. The Curator

> _Previously_ — Anemos read the notice titled "Concerning Perpetuity." It does not judge, it does not ask qualifications, a visit may be made at any time. He felt this system was kinder than anything he had heard of before. The next day he was to meet the Curator.

The Curator's office was a building at the very center of the city.

The building itself was a little larger than the others. But it wasn't ornate. There was almost nothing you could call decoration. I found that, if anything, dignified.

There were six steps. I counted them as I climbed. One, two, three. My hand trembled and I lost count a few times. I counted twice more to be sure it really was six.

*I noticed later that this was the only building in this city that looked old. The stone was darker than the other buildings'. No one ever told me why.*

---

The reception officer walked me there and said they would wait outside.

There were a few long benches in front of the building. People sat waiting their turn. I looked over their faces one by one.

One person looked anxious. Their leg was shaking. Another had no expression at all. Their eyes simply faced forward. I wondered why those two faces were so different.

*I would learn the answer later. The anxious one was a first-timer. The blank one had come many times before. I didn't even know, that day, which I was closer to. I know now. That day, I was the anxious one.*

When my turn came, the reception officer called my name. I adjusted the flask on my shoulder and went into the building.

I stood a moment before the door. There was no marking on the door at all. No name, no title. I thought that strange. Every other place had had a marking.

*I would learn later that this door hadn't originally been unmarked. I won't tell that story here.*

I knocked.

"…Come in."

The voice was gentle. Age was hard to guess. I pushed the door and went in.

---

The room wasn't large. One desk, two chairs. Nothing hung on the walls.

One person sat behind the desk. They looked to be around forty. Their clothing was tidy. There was no particular expression on their face. Only their eyes looked a little heavy.

A stack of papers sat piled on one side of the desk. Fairly tall. I wondered what it was but didn't ask.

*I would learn later that it was the day's paperwork to be processed. I wanted to count that height, but it wasn't the place for it. Looking back now, that pile of paper was the most honest object showing this person's day.*

"…You're Anemos, I take it," the Curator said first. "I heard from reception. Sit, please."

I sat. The chair was hard.

"…You've come to see your mother and your brother, I understand."

"…Yes."

"…It's been seven years."

"…Yes."

The Curator turned over a sheet of paper. It seemed to be my file.

*I didn't see, then, what was written on that paper. I never saw it, later either. The Curator never once showed me that document.*

---

"…The road here must have been hard," the Curator said.

"…It took four days."

"…On foot?"

"…Yes."

"…Did you have enough water?"

That question surprised me a little. I hadn't expected to be asked that.

"…Yes, it was fine."

The Curator looked at my flask. I was still wearing it on my shoulder.

"…You seem to carry that around with you."

"…Yes. It's become a habit."

The Curator smiled slightly at that. A small smile.

"…A good habit. Though it's an uncommon object, here."

*I still remember that smile. It was the first real smile I saw after coming to this city. The reception officer had smiled too, but that had been an exact smile. This was a little different. I couldn't explain where the difference lay, then. I can't say it exactly even now.*

The Curator pressed the corner of an eye briefly with a fingertip. A very short motion. I let it pass, assuming it was fatigue.

"…Are you all right?" I asked.

"…Yes, I'm fine. Don't mind it."

The Curator straightened again. The voice remained just as calm.

*I didn't recall that brief motion again until that night. And after that I found myself looking for it every time I saw this person. Sometimes it was there, sometimes it wasn't. I got in the habit of guessing, from that, what kind of day it had been.*

The Curator lowered a hand and, arranging the papers again, said,

"…Sorry. Where was I."

"…The application."

"…Ah, right."

In that brief gap I sensed something strange. This person had, until a moment ago, been explaining the procedure with perfect precision, like flowing water. And yet, for just an instant, they had lost their place.

*I let that gap pass that day. I thought, people can be like that. But meeting this person many more times later, I came to see that gap arise, each time, in roughly the same spot. While explaining a procedure, for just an instant, looking somewhere else.*

---

"…Let me explain the procedure," the Curator said. "A visit takes just one application form. If you apply today, you may see them today."

"…Do you meet with everyone like this? Every applicant?"

"…Yes. I personally walk you through the procedure for the first visit. From the second on, reception handles it."

"…Why only the first, in person?"

"…Because the first is the most important."

I liked that answer. It didn't sound like a formal answer but one with a real reason behind it.

"…How many people do you see in a day?"

The Curator thought a moment.

"…It differs day to day. Some days many, some days few."

"…Today?"

"…Today's on the busier side."

Hearing that answer, I looked again at the pile of paper on the desk. And I felt a little sorry. I was one of that many.

"…I'm sorry, you must be busy."

The Curator shook their head.

"…No. This is my work. And…"

The Curator paused a moment.

"…This work — it's not the kind of work that can be let fall behind."

I felt something heavy in that tone. It didn't sound like ordinary talk about work.

*I only heard it as a sense of duty at the time. Later I would learn it wasn't duty so much as something close to a vow. This person was pressing themselves not to let this work fall behind.*

"…Today?"

"…If you'd like."

Something in me swelled right there. Seven years of waiting, and now it would be a matter of minutes.

"…Can I ask something, though?" I said.

"…Yes."

"…I heard at reception yesterday that you've been busy lately. They said it wasn't because there are many applicants, so why—"

The Curator looked at me a moment. Choosing an answer, it seemed.

"…There's some other matter."

"…What matter?"

"…It's a little early to say."

I asked no further. But in that brief silence, I saw something. The Curator's eyes turned, for just an instant, somewhere else. Somewhere in the corner of the desk.

*I couldn't read that gaze at the time. I know now. That gaze was directed outside the city — to be exact, below the city. Toward the valley. I wouldn't confirm that until much, much later.*

---

"…Will you write the application?" The Curator held out a sheet of paper.

I took it. There were boxes for name, relation, and the subject's name.

In the name box I wrote my own name. In the relation box I wrote "Son / Elder brother." At the box for the subject's name, my hand stopped.

*I won't disclose it here either. I didn't in Chapter 1. I don't write my mother's and my brother's names anywhere in this record. I can't explain the reason exactly myself. I only want these names to be mine alone to say.*

I wrote the names. My hand trembled a little.

The Curator saw this and said nothing. Only waited.

*I liked that waiting. Someone who doesn't rush you. I hadn't met many people like that.*

Pushing the form forward, I asked,

"…Have you been doing this work long?"

The Curator tapped the desk lightly with a fingertip.

"…A long time."

"…How long?"

"…It's been a long time since I stopped counting."

That answer surprised me. I was a person who counted. The words "stopped counting" sounded foreign to me.

"…Why did you stop?"

"…When you keep counting, eventually all that's left is the counting."

I didn't fully understand that, right there.

*I would think over those words again and again, later. I have carried numbers through every part of this book. I wrote in Chapter 2 that counting is a way of avoiding grief. The Curator had used that method far longer, far more, than I had. And knew that even that method wears out, in time.*

---

Once the application was finished, the Curator took it up.

"…Are you ready?"

"…Yes."

"…Let me say one thing beforehand."

"…Yes."

"…After you see them, it may be different from what you expected. Don't be alarmed."

I tensed at that.

"…Is she sick?"

"…No. It's not that."

"…Then what?"

The Curator paused a moment before saying,

"…It only means the feelings you've prepared may not all get used."

I didn't understand that, then.

"…I'm not sure what that means," I said.

The Curator looked at me a moment. A face weighing whether to explain further.

"…There's a lot you've wanted to say, all this time, isn't there."

"…Yes. Really a lot."

"…Have you prepared all those words?"

"…Yes. I even decided the order."

The Curator gave the smallest nod at that. A nod that seemed both regretful and resigned.

"…That order — you may not get through all of it today."

"…Why?"

"…You'll know once you go."

Hearing that, I nearly laughed to myself. I nearly counted how many times I'd now heard "you'll know once you go" since coming to this city.

*In that moment, for the first time, I faintly sensed that this answer wasn't evasion. Everyone truly could not find a way to explain it. I found that more frightening. It wasn't that there was no explanation — it was that it couldn't be explained.*

"…Can I still ask one thing?" I said.

"…Yes."

"…Could it be different in a good way? Not just a bad way."

At that question, the Curator looked at me a while longer for the first time.

"…That, I don't know either. It's different for everyone."

"…What do you think, yourself?"

"…This isn't a place for me to give my opinion."

That answer was firm. I couldn't tell whether it was because of a rule or this person's own choice.

*I would learn later that the distinction hardly mattered. Rule or choice, the result was the same. This person never once told me their own opinion, to the end.*

*I have called this sentence back to mind hundreds of times since that day. The feelings you've prepared may not all get used. It was a warning. A very precise warning. I would live out that warning myself, two hours later.*

---

Gathering the application, the Curator said,

"…It's been received. If you follow the procedure, you should be able to see them within today."

"…How long do I have to wait?"

"…Not long. It just takes some time to prepare."

"…Prepare?"

"…There's a procedure for bringing them in. An officer will guide you shortly."

I nodded at that. As I rose and turned toward the door, the Curator called me back once more.

"…Anemos."

"…Yes."

"…Are you nervous?"

"…Yes."

"…That's all right. Everyone is."

There was something laid under that tone. I couldn't find it strange, at the time.

*I know now. That wasn't comfort. It was a confirmation of fact. I didn't have the nerve to guess how many times the Curator had said those same words in that room.*

I looked once more around the room. A room with nothing on the walls. One window. Through that window, the roofs were visible.

*I would come to that room again, later. Many times. Each time, the room hadn't changed in the least. I thought that was this person's taste. Later I learned it wasn't taste. There was simply no reason to change it.*

Before leaving through the door, I bowed once more.

"…Thank you."

The Curator nodded.

"…Go well."

That farewell, too, was short. I faintly sensed, even then, that it wasn't from a lack of sincerity. This person wasn't sparing words — they were choosing only the words that were truly necessary.

*Coming out of that room, I thought, for the first time, that this person was to be pitied. I couldn't explain the reason. The thought simply came. Not a grieving person, not an angry person, and yet someone who made me think, this person is to be pitied. I had never met anyone like that until then.*

Coming out into the corridor, the reception officer was waiting for me.

"…All finished? The officer will guide you through preparations for the visit now."

I adjusted the flask on my shoulder and followed that person. My heart began to race again.
