# Chapter 42. Breathing

> _Previously_ — My mother was released. One signature, one switch. No
> one celebrated and no one cried. Speon offered her a mouthful of
> water. My mother stood holding the flask in both hands, not drinking.

My mother blinked.

Once. Twice. Three times.

I saw that too.

---

My mother moved her fingers, one by one.

Thumb, index, middle, ring, little.

They all moved.

"…They all work." she said.

---

The wind blew.

My mother drew her shoulders in.

"…Cold." she said.

That word startled me. I hadn't heard it once in seven years.

---

My brother took off his own coat and put it over my mother's shoulders.

It was too small to cover her fully. She left it on anyway.

"…Thank you." she said.

---

My mother raised the flask.

Brought it to her mouth.

Drank.

---

One mouthful.

Her throat moved. I watched that movement. A movement I hadn't seen in
seven years.

My mother took the flask away.

---

She coughed.

Once, twice, three times. Bending forward each time.

My brother, startled, patted her back.

"…Mom, are you okay?"

---

My mother raised a hand to say she was fine. She couldn't speak until
the coughing stopped.

Speon said quietly,

"…It's been so long, her throat's startled."

Hearing that, I looked at the flask again. It was half full.

---

The coughing stopped.

My mother steadied her breath. Breathed in deep, breathed out.

"…The taste of the water." she managed to say.

"…How is it?" I asked.

Instead of answering, my mother drank another mouthful.

---

This time she didn't cough.

Her throat moved again. A little bigger, a little easier.

My brother stared at that.

"…There's a sound in Mom's throat."

"…Her throat's remembering." Speon said.

---

My mother touched her throat.

"…It still feels strange." she said.

"…Does it hurt?" my brother asked.

"…No. Just unfamiliar."

---

One more mouthful.

This time no sound at all. Her throat just moved.

My mother felt that and opened her eyes wide.

"…There. Now it's working." she said.

---

My mother set the flask down.

Wiped her mouth with the back of her hand. A completely ordinary
motion. I was struck, rather, by that ordinariness.

*I still remember that motion exactly. Wiping her mouth with the back
of her hand. A motion more ordinary than anything else, and it was the
biggest event of that day.*

---

We sat down by the ditch.

Sitting, my mother swayed slightly. I reached out and caught her.

"…Are you all right?"

"…Yeah. My legs feel a little strange."

"…Strange how?"

"…Heavy." she said. "Having a body that's all mine — was it always
this heavy?"

---

My mother dipped her hand into the water.

"…Cold." she said.

"…Do you like the cold?" I asked.

"…I don't know. It's just cold."

---

My brother dipped his hand in too.

"…It's cold for me too."

"…Your hand already knew that." my mother said.

"…Didn't Mom's hand know?"

My mother paused for a moment at that. She pulled her hand out of the
water, then dipped it back in. Pulled it out, then dipped it back in.

---

"…I don't think I knew." she said.

The sound of water kept flowing. We sat for a while with our hands in
it, listening to that sound.

*I didn't count how long that lasted. I didn't want to.*

---

The sun fell across my mother's face.

She narrowed her eyes.

"…Bright." she said.

She raised a hand to shield the sun.

---

Under that shade of her hand, my mother closed her eyes for a moment.

Stayed still.

"…What are you doing?" my brother asked.

"…Just feeling it."

"…Feeling what?"

"…The sun."

---

The wind blew again.

This time my mother didn't flinch.

Her hair scattered in it. She swept it back with her hand.

---

Speon brought out a piece of dry bread.

"…You must be hungry." he handed it to my mother.

My mother took it and looked at it. Smelled it.

"…Was this what bread smelled like." she said.

---

She took a bite.

Chewed.

Chewed slowly.

My brother watched. No one rushed her.

She swallowed.

---

My mother stayed still there for a moment. A face checking something.

"…It's good." she said.

Her voice shook.

---

My brother laughed at that.

"…Mom's voice sounds weird."

"…It's not weird." Speon said. "It's shaking."

My brother's face didn't quite understand the difference.

---

My mother finished chewing the bread. Something glinted in her eyes.
Not tears. It might just have been the light catching them that way.

*I still can't be sure whether it was tears or not. My mother didn't
cry that day. Her eyes were only a little wet. That's a different
thing.*

---

Finishing the bread, my mother brushed off her hands.

"…Was this what being full felt like." she said.

"…Don't you remember?" I asked.

"…I remember. But feeling it is different."

---

There was a small scratch on the back of my mother's hand. A mark from
brushing against something on the way. She noticed it and pressed a
finger to it.

"…That hurts." she said quietly. Half surprise, half wonder, in her
voice.

My brother blew a breath on the scratch. A habit he'd had since he was
small.

"…It'll heal." my brother said.

---

My brother kept laughing beside her.

A laugh with no reason. A laugh that came just because things were
good.

My mother watched that laugh and laughed along with it.

---

A very small laugh.

Almost no sound at all. The corners of her mouth rose slightly, then
came back down.

I didn't miss it.

---

In that moment I found myself counting a number. I hadn't meant to. My
body counted before I could stop it.

Seven years.

It was the first laugh in seven years.

I couldn't say anything, right there. Not because my throat had closed
up. I simply had no word to call it.

My brother, not knowing what it was, kept laughing. The sound of that
laugh overlapped with the sound of the water. I heard both sounds at
once.

---

*I write down that laugh the most carefully of anything in this book.
It wasn't a big laugh. There was almost no sound to it. The distance
her mouth moved was probably less than a finger's width. But I knew,
right there, that this one small movement was the whole of what I'd
been waiting seven years for.*

*I didn't cry. My mother didn't cry either. We just sat by the channel,
drank water, coughed, ate bread, and laughed. That was all. And because
that was all it was, that's the day I still go back to, more often than
any other.*

---

My brother asked,

"…Laugh again, Mom."

My mother shook her head at that.

"…It doesn't work that way." she said. "You need something funny to
laugh."

My brother looked around, trying to find something funny at that. He
couldn't find anything. But the sight of him looking was funny enough
that my mother laughed a little again, once more.

---

The second laugh.

I counted that one too.

---

My mother yawned.

Covered her mouth with her hand. Then looked surprised.

"…That was a yawn, wasn't it." she asked.

"…Yes."

"…So this is what being tired is."

---

My mother rubbed her eyes.

"…I'm sleepy." she said.

That was another word I was hearing for the first time from her.

---

Standing up, my mother swayed again. This time my brother caught her
hand.

"…I'll help." my brother said.

It was as much help as an eight-year-old could give. Still, my mother
took that hand and stood.

---

The sun began to set. The sound of water carried more clearly in the
dark.

My mother raised the flask again and drank one more mouthful. This
time she didn't cough.

"…I think I'm getting used to it now." she said.

---

Walking back toward the room, my mother stopped a few more times.

Once she braced a hand against the wall.

Once she just stood and looked down at her own feet.

"…What are you looking at?" I asked.

"…It feels strange, my feet touching the ground."

---

Crossing the Preserve's yard, her foot caught on a stone.

My mother staggered slightly.

"…Ah." she made a small sound.

"…Are you all right?"

"…That hurt." my mother said. A surprised face. "So this is what it
means to hurt."

I couldn't answer that at all.

---

At the door of the room, my mother touched the doorframe with her
hand.

Ran her fingers along the wood grain.

"…Rough." she said.

---

Inside the room, my mother touched the blanket.

Pressed on it.

"…Soft."

My brother pressed the blanket the same way beside her.

"…I want to touch it too." my brother said. It was a blanket he'd
always touched. But today his face wanted to touch it together with
his mother.

---

That night the three of us slept in one room for the first time.

A room the Preserve had lent us temporarily. Cramped. But no one
complained about it.

My brother fell asleep first. My mother lay beside him, listening to
his breathing for a long while.

---

"…Anemos." my mother called my name.

"…Yes."

"…I didn't know a breathing sound could be this loud."

"…It was always like that."

"…But today it sounds different."

---

I didn't answer for a moment. My mother's breathing and my brother's
breathing sounded, in turns, through the room.

I felt I understood what she meant.

*When the listener changes, the same sound is heard differently. I
learned that, that day. My mother had had no need, all those seven
years, to listen to my brother's breathing. A person with no need
doesn't listen. That night, for the first time, my mother needed that
breathing sound.*

---

We laid out the bedding.

My mother was a little awkward about how to lie down. Not sure where to
put her arms, she shifted position a few times.

"…It wasn't like this before." she said.

"…Before, you didn't need to lie down."

"…That's true."

---

My mother wiggled her feet under the blanket.

"…My feet are cold." she said.

My brother pressed his own feet against hers.

"…My feet are warm."

"…So they are."

---

My mother put a hand to her chest.

Stayed still.

"…This is it beating, isn't it." she asked.

"…Yes."

"…It's fast."

"…Maybe it's surprised."

"…No." my mother said. "I think it's because it's glad."

---

My brother fell asleep first. His breathing evened out.

My mother listened to that breathing for a long while.

She reached out and touched my brother's forehead.

"…Warm." she said.

---

I lay down beside them too.

Three breathing sounds filled the room. I didn't count them.

*That night, for the first time, I set the habit of counting down
completely. It was all right not to count. Not because there was no
need to count, but because I simply didn't want to turn that moment
into a number.*

The sound of water kept running outside the window. I listened to it
last, and fell asleep.

---

In the morning I woke to find my mother already awake.

Sitting up, looking down at her own hands.

"…Did you sleep well?" I asked.

"…I don't know. I saw something strange."

"…What was it?"

"…A dream."

---

I sat up at that.

"…What kind of dream was it?"

"…I don't remember." my mother said. "I just know I saw something."

*Hearing that, I was quietly astonished. The preserved don't dream.
They have no need to. That morning, for the first time in seven years,
my mother had dreamed. And she couldn't remember the dream. I thought
even that was enough.*

My mother looked at her own hand again. Ran a finger along the lines of
her palm.

"…It's like I'm seeing this for the first time too." she said.

"…It was always there." I said.

"…I know. But it feels like I'm seeing it for the first time, today."

---

My mother stretched.

Something in her body cracked.

My brother opened his eyes at the sound.

"…What was that sound?"

"…It came from Mom's body." my mother said. A wondering face.

---

Her stomach made a sound.

My mother pressed a hand to it.

"…Is this something new too?" I asked.

"…Yes." she said. "Was being hungry always something that made a sound
too?"

---

We got up and opened the door to the room.

The morning sun came in.

My mother walked out into that light. One step, one step, a little
lighter than yesterday.

At the edge of the yard, my mother stopped and looked toward the
channel. The sound of water had kept flowing through the night without
stopping.

"…I guess it ran all night." I said.

"…I suppose it did." my mother said. "Without even sleeping."

"…Water never sleeps."

My mother nodded at that. Then she took a deep breath. It looked as
though I could see the morning air going into her lungs.

I watched her back.
