# Chapter 47. How Long Have You Been Here

> _Previously_ — My mother fell ill. I didn't leave her side for four
> days. While she was preserved there had been no reason to watch over
> her. Now there was. Hearing that, my mother smiled quietly. "So
> that's part of it too."

After my mother recovered, I spent a few more days at home.

Only once I'd confirmed her body had fully come back did I find room
to think about work outside again. My brother had started going to
something like a school. A new place had opened in the city for living
children, they said. My brother was delighted at the news.

Then, one morning, I went back down into the valley.

---

There was nothing special about that morning. I woke as usual, my
mother made breakfast as usual, my brother put on his clothes to go to
the new place.

I was packing the water flask when my hand stopped, without my
meaning it to. Up until that instant I'd had no plan to go back to the
valley. But with the flask already in my hand, I realized my heart had
already leaned that way.

"…Not going to the ditch today?" my mother asked.

"…I'm going to go somewhere for a while."

"…Where."

I hesitated for a moment at that question.

"…The valley."

My mother's hand stopped at that. She didn't ask anything more.

"…Be back before evening." she said.

"…Okay."

---

It's hard to say exactly why.

In the four days my mother was sick, I had come close to the word
dying again. Eikon had left me waiting at a door half open. My brother
kept drawing his lines on the wall. Carrying all of that, I found
myself, without quite meaning to, thinking of that place again.

*The place where the bones had been. The place where he was. I'd
thought, once I left that valley, that I would never go back. But one
morning, I simply found myself walking that way.*

---

I went without telling Speon.

On the way I passed the channel. The water was pushing past the edge
of the city now. I followed the flow with my eyes and confirmed again
that it was headed toward the valley. It hadn't reached there yet. But
the direction was clear.

The path down the cliff was the same as before. My body remembered
where to set its feet. I found that strange — I'd only walked it once.

Reaching the bottom, the dry smell came. The same smell as before.
Earth, old wood, and another smell hard to name. I stopped walking for
a moment, taking it in. The scattered shoes, the faded scraps of
cloth, the small bones — all of it was still there. Nothing had been
moved.

*I've decided not to describe this valley again in detail. I already
did that once. Writing it again, I felt, would blur the weight of the
first time. But I'll set down one thing. Walking among those remains,
I wasn't afraid, unlike before. Before, this valley had been a strange
land of death. This time it felt like walking to meet a familiar
face.*

---

He was there.

Not in the same spot as before. A little further in, among the dry
trees. He was crouched beside the bones. I couldn't see what he was
doing from a distance.

I walked toward him. Dry things crumbled underfoot with every step.

---

Up close, he was simply sitting there.

He held nothing in his hands. He wasn't touching the bones either. He
was just sitting beside them, watching the wind carry the soil, little
by little.

"…Back again." he said. Without turning to look at me.

"…Yes."

---

I sat down beside him.

We said nothing for a while. I went back over everything that had
happened, in order. My mother's release, her laugh, and then the
fever. Eikon's name, and her silence. The lines on my brother's wall.

He didn't hurry that silence.

The wind blew once more. I thought of how that wind was the same word
as my name. The thing that cannot be kept. I thought that wind must
have been carrying this valley's soil, little by little, for a very
long time. And beside it, this man must have been sitting, unmoved,
through all that time.

"…The bones here." I spoke first. "There seem to be fewer than
before."

"…Is that so."

"…Yes. Or am I imagining it."

"…You're not imagining it." he said. "When the water gets closer, the
soil moves. When it moves, the traces move with it."

I thought of the channel again at that. It hadn't reached here yet,
and already something was changing.

---

"…You asked me something before." I said, speaking first. "Whether you
think these can live again."

"…I did."

"…I said I didn't know."

"…That's right. That was the right answer."

"…Now." I said. "I still don't know. But I think I know something."

He looked at me for the first time, hearing that.

---

"…What do you know." he asked.

"…That being alive means being afraid." I said. "My mother was sick.
For four days. Those four days were the most afraid I've ever been.
But those four days were more time spent beside my mother than all
the seven years I spent visiting her."

He listened to that quietly.

"…I still don't know what that means." I went on. "But I have the
feeling of knowing something."

"…That's enough." he said. "You don't have to know everything to
know something."

---

I decided to bring out a question I'd been holding onto for a long
time.

I didn't know exactly when I'd started carrying it. Probably from the
day I first came down into this valley.

"…Can I ask you something." I said.

"…Go ahead."

"…How long have you been here?"

---

He looked at me.

And laughed.

*I'll set down that laugh only briefly. I already knew he was a man
who never got angry. But even so, that laugh caught me by surprise. I
felt something very old inside it. I didn't know what.*

"…That's an interesting question." he said.

"…I didn't ask it to be interesting."

"…I know."

---

"…You're not going to answer?" I asked.

"…No." he said. Briefly.

"…Why not?"

"…Some answers." he said, "outlast the one who hears them. I think
this one would be too heavy for you."

I started to argue with that, and stopped. I couldn't be sure he was
wrong.

---

"…Then just one thing." I said. "Were you here before I came?"

"…That's the same question." he said. "Just dressed in different
clothes."

I laughed at that. Oddly, I wasn't angry.

"…You're right." I admitted. "But I'm really asking because I'm
curious."

"…I know you're curious." he said. "But being curious doesn't mean
you get an answer."

---

"…Then how about this." I asked again. "Has anyone else come down
here and asked you something like this? Besides me?"

He looked steadily at me for a moment at that question.

"…There's always been someone asking something like it." he said.

"…What did you tell them?"

"…The same thing I'm telling you now."

I laughed at that answer. I felt like I should be angry, but oddly, I
wasn't.

"…That seems a little unfair." I said.

"…What does."

"…That I'm not the only one getting a special answer."

He laughed out loud at that, for the first time. A short laugh.

"…Wouldn't it be more unfair." he said, "to answer everyone the same
way?"

I turned that over for a long while. It sounded right, and it sounded
wrong, both at once.

---

We fell quiet again.

The wind blew. Dry soil swirled low across the ground. Watching that
swirl, I thought it was exactly the same sight I'd once seen here
before.

*Strangely, I was comfortable inside that silence that day. I'd always
felt frustrated, before, not getting an answer. The Curator, Speon,
this man — all of them, at some point, stopped answering. But that
day, it didn't frustrate me.*

---

"…Can I ask one more thing." I said.

"…Go ahead."

"…Are you going to stay here? Even after the water reaches this far?"

He seemed to think a little longer over that question.

"…I stay where I need to be." he said. "That doesn't change, whether
the water comes or not."

---

"…And that's here?"

"…For now." he said.

I felt that answer was different from the others. It wasn't a
complete evasion. But it wasn't a complete answer either.

*I couldn't pin down that difference precisely, then. I still can't,
even now. But I had the sense that he'd given a little more of himself
away in that question than in the others, that day.*

---

The sun was sinking little by little. The shadows against the valley
wall were growing long.

"…I should get going." I said.

"…All right."

I stood, then looked back at him once more.

"…Can I come again?"

"…No need to ask." he said. "You're going to come again anyway."

---

I stopped at that.

"…How do you know that?"

He didn't answer. He just laughed. The same laugh as before.

*I thought about that laugh the whole way up. I hadn't gotten an
answer that day either. When he'd started being here, whether he'd
still be here — I left the valley without knowing any of it for
certain.*

*But writing this question down for the third time in this book, I
came to know one thing. Some questions aren't meant to get an answer.
The act of asking itself carries the person holding onto the question
somewhere. That day, in that valley, I climbed out having received not
an answer, but that carrying.*

---

Climbing the cliff, I looked back once.

He was still there. Crouched beside the bones, watching the wind carry
the soil. The same posture as before I'd come.

I held that sight in my eyes for a long while. Then I started walking
again.

---

On the way back into the city I ran into Speon.

He was adjusting the direction of the channel at its far end. Seeing
me, he set down his shovel.

"…Where'd you go." he asked.

"…The valley."

Speon's hand paused at that.

"…Did you meet him?"

I was surprised by that question. I had never told Speon much of
anything about him.

"…Do you know him?"

"…Know is a stretch." Speon said. "I've crossed paths with him a few
times. Well-diggers run into someone like that, oddly often."

"…What did you two talk about?"

"…Not much." Speon said. "Just a few nods of the head."

---

"…I asked him today." I said. "How long he'd been there."

"…What did he say?"

"…He just laughed and didn't answer."

Speon nodded at that. His face wasn't surprised.

"…Figured as much." he said.

"…How did you know?"

"…Because I asked him too, once." he said. "A very long time ago."

My eyes went wide at that.

"…What did he say?"

Speon swung the shovel back onto his shoulder.

"…He laughed." he said. "The same as he did with you."

---

*I couldn't say anything for a while, hearing that answer. I had
thought, up till then, that I was the only person to have ever met him
in that valley. I wasn't. I was only the most recent one.*

"…Weren't you angry?" I asked. "That he wouldn't answer?"

"…At first, yeah." Speon said. "But I know now. For some questions,
it matters more to keep asking than to get an answer."

I held on to that for a long time.

He picked the shovel back up and went back to the channel. I watched
his back for a moment, then turned my steps toward home. The sun
hadn't set yet. I had kept my promise to be back before evening.

---

I came home to find my mother making dinner. My brother was excitedly
listing off everything he'd learned at the new place.

"…Where'd you go today, Brother?" my brother asked.

"…The valley."

"…What's there?"

I thought about that question for a moment. Bones, soil, and him. I
didn't know where to begin.

"…There's a man there." I said. "A strange man."

"…Strange how?"

"…He doesn't really answer when you ask him things."

My brother laughed at that.

"…You do that too. To me."

I had no comeback to that. My mother was laughing quietly beside him.

---

After dinner, I sat alone in my room and went back over what had
happened that day.

*How long have you been here. I'm writing that question down for the
third time now. There is still no answer. But I know something now. I
wasn't the only one who ever carried this question down into that
valley. Speon had, and maybe someone else too, carried the same
question and sat in that same place, and come back up. And none of
them, I think, ever got an answer either.*

*Strangely, that fact comforted me. That not receiving an answer
wasn't something that belonged to me alone. That night I fell asleep
thinking of that laugh again.*

*I still don't know how long he's been there. Even now, finishing this
book, I don't know. I've stopped finding that strange. Some things can
be kept close without being known. I learned that, that day, and
walked out of the valley.*
