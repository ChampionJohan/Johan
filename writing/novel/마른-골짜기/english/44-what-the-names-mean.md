# Chapter 44. What the Names Mean

> _Previously_ — My brother wanted release, but I judged that an
> eight-year-old couldn't truly give that consent, and put it off. We
> made a pinky promise that I would ask him again every year. The
> Curator told me it was the best decision I could make.

Around that time, it had become a habit for us to gather by the
channel each evening.

Me and my brother, sometimes my mother, and Eikon too. Speon always
sat there watching the water run for a while after he finished work,
before he left. That evening was no different. My brother had already
gone in to the room, and my mother, still tired, had gone to rest
early. Only Eikon, Speon, and I were left.

---

One evening, sitting by the channel, Speon said out of nowhere.

"…Do you two know what your names mean."

Eikon and I looked at him at the same time. We had been sitting there
together.

"…They mean something?" Eikon asked.

"…They do."

---

I had wondered about that question for a long time. But I had never
asked. I didn't know why myself.

Not from the settlement's paperwork, not from any of the grown-ups
there — I had never once heard what my own name meant. I had just
assumed my parents picked it because it sounded nice. It was the same
with Eikon's name. It had never even occurred to either of us to ask
the other.

*I know now. I was afraid to hear the answer. If a name has a meaning,
it means someone made me for that meaning. I didn't know who that
someone was. Not knowing was what frightened me.*

"…Tell us." I said.

---

Speon looked out at the channel for a moment. His face was choosing
something.

"…Anemos." he said. "In an old, old language, it means wind."

"…Wind?"

"…Yes. Not just wind. The thing that cannot be kept. The thing that
can't be held in a container. That meaning comes bundled in with it."

---

I said nothing at all for a long while after hearing that.

*The thing that cannot be kept. In that moment I knew I had lived that
phrase across this entire book. I was a counter. Counting is an
attempt to hold on. I had spent my whole life trying to hold on to
something. And my name had been telling me, from the very start, that
I was a person who could not be held.*

---

"…You don't know who gave it to me?" I asked.

"…I don't know that either." Speon said. "That's how names are. They
outlast the one who gave them."

I didn't ask anything further after that answer. It felt like asking
wouldn't help.

I looked down at the channel. Water and wind shared that much, at
least — neither could be held in a container. In all my time beside
Speon learning to dig for water, I had heard, more than once, that
standing water rots. That it has to flow to stay alive. I thought my
own name might have come from that same place. Something that must
not be kept. Something that has to flow.

*In that moment, for the first time, I stopped being ashamed of my
name. I had blamed myself, more than once, for being the kind of
person who couldn't stay close to his family for long. I had even
wondered whether leaving the settlement and coming all this way was
just some restlessness in my nature that couldn't stay in one place.
Hearing the name, I found myself thinking it wasn't a flaw — I had
simply been born with a name like that, and that was all.*

---

"…What about me?" Eikon asked.

Her voice sounded exactly the same as always. I didn't even notice,
then, that it sounded exactly the same as always.

Speon looked at her. He paused for a moment.

"…Eikon." he said. "It means image. Or likeness."

---

"…What's that mean?" Eikon asked.

"…A form made in the shape of something else." Speon said. "Something
copied over exactly from a thing that already exists."

Eikon's face turned that over.

"…A copy of what?"

Speon didn't answer that question.

---

I felt something unfamiliar in that silence.

Eikon seemed to feel it too. She asked again.

"…I'm a likeness of something?"

"…That's just what the name says." Speon said carefully. "A name
doesn't necessarily explain the whole of a person."

---

"…Then why would someone give a name like that?"

Eikon asked it that way. For the first time, I heard something shake a
little in her voice, in that question.

*I heard that shake and, even then, didn't know exactly what it was.
It was the first time I'd ever heard Eikon's voice shake.*

---

Speon didn't answer.

Eikon started to ask something more into that silence, and stopped.

She opened her mouth, then closed it again.

---

I watched that beside her.

It was the first time I had ever seen Eikon fail to answer something.
She always had an answer. I don't lack anything. What do you mean. Why
would I need more. Her answers had always come fast and clear.

That day was not like that.

---

*I write this moment more carefully than any other in this book. I
don't want to attach any explanation to that silence. Eikon heard the
meaning of her own name, and had no reply to it. That's all there is.*

The sound of water filled the silence. No one tried to break it.

---

After a long while, Eikon finally spoke.

"…I just." she said. "I feel kind of strange."

"…Strange how?" I asked.

"…I don't know."

That answer was unfamiliar too. Eikon was a girl who almost never said
I don't know.

---

Speon quietly got to his feet.

"…Let's leave it here for tonight." he said. "This kind of thing
doesn't get taken in all at once."

He gathered his shovel and left. Only Eikon and I remained.

---

We sat without speaking for a while.

The sound of water kept running. I didn't know what to say. Ordinarily
Eikon would have been the one to ask something light first, but that
night she was quiet too.

---

"…Anemos." she said, after a long while.

"…Yeah."

"…How did you feel, hearing what your name meant?"

I thought about that question for a moment.

"…Strange. But the strangeness wasn't bad."

---

"…For me." Eikon said. "It's strange, but I don't even know if this
is a bad thing or not."

"…Is this the first time?"

"…First time for what?"

"…This feeling of not knowing."

Eikon went quiet again for a long while at that question.

---

"…I don't know." she said, finally. And she laughed a little at her
own words. Something like a bitter laugh. "I don't even know how many
times I've said I don't know, by now."

I said nothing back to that. Not because I had nothing to say, but
because it felt like silence was the right thing for this moment.

---

Eikon leaned a little toward the channel.

She looked down at her own face reflected on the water. The current
kept the reflection shaking.

"…Is this me too?" she asked.

"…What is?"

"…The one in the water."

I didn't know how to answer that.

"…Probably close." I said. "But it's water, so it keeps shaking. Your
real face doesn't shake."

Eikon heard that and touched the water lightly with a fingertip. The
reflected face shook harder for a moment, then settled again.

"…Is that what a likeness is?" she asked. "Something that shakes."

"…I don't know." I said. "You'd have to ask Speon."

"…Speon didn't answer either."

I had no answer to that. She was right.

---

"…You know." Eikon said again, after a long while. "I've never once,
in my whole life, felt like I lacked something. You know that,
Anemos. You've heard me say it, always."

"…Yeah. I know."

"…But right now." she said. "I don't even know if this is lacking
something or not. Whether it's because the name is strange, or
whether something that was already there is only showing itself now."

That distinction was foreign to me. I had always thought lack was just
something you knew. When you're hungry, you know you're hungry. But
Eikon, right now, wasn't even sure what it was that she felt.

*In that moment, for the first time, I thought that lack might not
arrive all at once. Speon had once told me it takes time even for
water to change direction underground. I remembered that, right then.
Maybe a person's heart worked the same way.*

"…So will I change too, someday?" Eikon asked. Not looking at me — at
the water.

I couldn't answer that right away.

"…I don't know." I said, honestly. "But the fact that you're asking a
question like this tonight — that wasn't true even yesterday."

Eikon said nothing back to that. She just kept looking down at the
water.

---

*Writing this chapter, I felt the temptation more than once to attach
some great meaning to Eikon's silence here. That this was her first
lack. That this was the beginning of her changing. I decided not to
write it that way.*

*I only set down what I saw, in that place, that day. Eikon heard the
meaning of her own name. And for the first time, had no reply to it.
What that meant, neither she nor I knew.*

---

The sun went down. Eikon stood.

"…I'll go on ahead." she said.

"…Okay."

She walked a few steps, then turned back.

"…Anemos."

"…Yeah."

"…Say my name's meaning once more? Exactly what it was."

---

"…Image." I said. "Likeness."

Eikon mouthed the words after me. No sound, just the shape of it on
her lips.

Then she walked on.

*I watched her back for a long while. I knew, that day, that I had
witnessed the quietest crack in this whole book. Where that crack led,
I didn't know yet, then.*

---

I went back to the room and told my mother about it.

She was sitting on the bedding, mending my brother's clothes. Her hand
was still a little unpracticed at moving the needle.

"…Speon told me what my name meant today." I said.

"…What did he say?"

"…Wind. The thing that can't be kept."

My mother's hand stopped.

---

"…Your father gave you that name." my mother said.

I was startled by that. It had been a very long time since my mother
had brought up my father.

"…My father did?"

"…Yes. I never knew the meaning myself. He just always said it was a
good name."

"…Why that name of all names, do you think."

My mother thought for a moment, then smiled.

"…Your father was another one who could never stay in one place for
long. That was probably why."

---

Hearing that, I felt, for the first time, that I knew my father a
little better as a person. I knew almost nothing about him. All I had
ever known was that he'd disappeared when I was five.

"…Did you ask about Eikon's name too?" my mother asked.

"…Yes. It means image. Likeness."

My mother's hand stopped again.

"…That's a different kind of meaning."

"…Yes. She had no answer at all when she heard it."

---

My mother stopped her sewing for a long while at that.

"…So that's the first time she's ever said she didn't know
something."

I was startled by that. My mother had barely even met Eikon, and yet
she'd named it exactly.

"…How did you know?"

"…Because I was the same." my mother said. "When you asked me that
question, I couldn't say anything either. At first."

---

Hearing that, something opened up in me all at once.

*In that moment I set my mother and Eikon side by side. Both of them
had been women who lacked nothing. Both of them had, at some moment,
met the first time they had no answer to give. My mother changed after
that. Eikon does not know yet.*

*That day, for the first time, I saw that these two stories held the
same shape. Only my mother's story had already turned the page, and
Eikon's story had only just turned its first.*

---

The next morning, I went to find the Curator and told him about it.

He stopped, mid-paperwork.

"…The meanings of your names." he said. "Who told you?"

"…Speon."

The Curator nodded. There was no surprise on his face.

"…I already knew the meanings of both your names." he said. "They're
written in the registry from the start. I simply didn't think it was
my place to be the one to tell you first."

"…Why not?"

"…The meaning of a name belongs to the one who gave it, not to the one
who keeps the record." he said. "I'm only the one who preserves it."

---

Something in that answer caught at me.

"…Eikon had nothing to say, when she heard hers." I said.

The Curator set down his pen at that.

"…I thought that might happen." he said quietly.

"…You knew?"

"…I guessed." he said. "I've been reading her file for seven years.
In that whole record, there was never once a moment when she had no
answer to something. Today will be the first."

"…What does that mean?"

The Curator looked out the window, toward the channel.

"…I don't know exactly, myself." he said. "But I've learned one thing,
doing this work. Sometimes, not being able to answer isn't because
there's no answer. It's because the answers have started becoming too
many."

I held on to that for a long time.

---

That evening I saw Eikon again by the channel.

She looked much like her usual self. She smiled, said hello, asked me
what I'd done that day. I was a little reassured by that ordinariness.

But before she left, she asked.

"…Anemos."

"…Yeah."

"…I said my name over a few more times, when I was alone. Image.
Likeness."

"…And?"

"…It's still strange." she said. "But less strange than yesterday."

I couldn't tell whether that was a good sign or a bad one.

"…Is that it getting better?" I asked.

"…I don't know." Eikon said. "But I was glad you asked me about it.
Better than holding it by myself."

She left those words and walked on. I watched her back. The same
walk as yesterday. But I knew now that something was following behind
that walk.

*That night, I thought again, side by side, about my name and hers.
Wind is wind because it cannot be held. Image is image because it
carries over something that already exists. I was born a person who
cannot be held, and she was born a person carrying something within
her. Why those two names came, of all names, to the two of us — that
night, I still didn't know.*
