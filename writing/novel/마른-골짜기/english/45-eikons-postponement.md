# Chapter 45. Eikon's Postponement

> _Previously_ — Speon told us what our two names meant. Anemos: wind,
> the thing that cannot be kept. Eikon: image, likeness. Hearing the
> meaning of her own name, Eikon had no answer for the first time. That
> night I thought about our two names, side by side.

For several days after that, Eikon went on exactly as she always had.

She came to the channel at the same time, sat in the same spot. Her
laugh was the same. I found that, oddly, stranger than if something
had changed. I had expected something big to shift. Nothing shifted
much at all.

*I understood something for the first time, then. A person doesn't
change all at once. I had been thinking of this story as one racing
toward a turning point. It wasn't. Some changes arrive at a speed too
slow to see.*

---

The channel kept growing, those same days.

It had crossed half the city and was nearing the edge now. I helped
Speon dig, and came back each evening to the place where the water
sounded. Day to day it all looked the same. But I knew the color of
the soil changed a little each day. To the eye it was still the same
brown, but under the hand there was more moisture in it, little by
little. Speon had taught me how to measure that moisture — by
fingertip, by smell, by weight.

*I think Eikon was measuring something the same way. A moisture
invisible to the eye, by fingertip. I didn't understand that clearly
at the time. I only had the sense that something was changing.*

My brother went on drawing one line on the wall each of those days
too. I counted the lines every time I passed. Twelve, thirteen,
fourteen. I set that number beside the length of the channel and
looked at the two together. Both were growing a little every day, too
slowly to see.

---

Still, there were small things.

One day Eikon asked me.

"…What did you do yesterday, Anemos?"

I stopped short at that question. Eikon rarely asked things like that.
She'd always said there was little she was curious about.

"…Dug the ditch. Why?"

"…No reason." she said. "I just wanted to ask."

This time I couldn't let that no reason go by unremarked, the way I
once would have.

---

Another day she asked this.

"…If I don't lack anything, how can something start making me
curious?"

I didn't know the answer to that.

"…I think you'd know that better than me."

"…I don't know either." Eikon said. "That's why I'm asking."

*I turned that answer over for a long time. I had failed to answer
Eikon's questions plenty of times before. But that day was different.
That day, she was asking a question knowing that even she didn't know
its answer.*

---

There was another moment, too.

Eikon didn't come one evening. I worked on the channel longer than
usual, waiting for her. She didn't appear until nearly an hour had
passed.

"…You're late." I said.

"…Yeah." she said. "I was just sitting in my room."

"…Doing what?"

"…Nothing." she said. "I just sat there. I wanted to."

That answer was unfamiliar to me. Eikon had never spent time that way
before. If there was something to do, she did it; if not, she simply
moved on to the next thing without a second thought. Wanting to do
nothing, and doing nothing because of it — I had always thought that
wasn't a thing that happened to someone who lacked nothing.

*That day, for the first time, I noticed she had begun spending more
time alone. A person seeks solitude when there's something to sort
through. I'd learned that outside. Someone with nothing to sort
through has no reason to be alone either.*

---

The Curator called me aside around that time.

"…It's about Eikon." he said.

"…What is it?"

"…Nothing's wrong." he said. "But I've been watching her file, and in
the last few days her rate of questions has gone up. We keep records
of that sort of thing."

"…What does that mean?"

The Curator thought for a moment.

"…I've done this work a long time." he said. "Lack usually arrives
this way. Not as one big event, but as questions accumulating one by
one. I call it the way water seeps in. Not pooling all at once, but
drop by drop."

I thought of Speon, hearing that phrase. Two people I'd met in this
city, saying the same thing in different places.

---

"…So what do we do now?" I asked.

"…Nothing." the Curator said. "We never ask first. Not until the
person wants it themselves. That's the one principle of this whole
procedure. Never rush."

"…Why be so careful about it?"

"…Because once you ask, it can't be undone." he said. "Being asked, in
itself, becomes pressure on a person. I don't want to put that
pressure on her. Waiting is our work too."

"…Then why are you telling me this?"

"…Because you're family, Anemos." he said. "I thought family should
know first. What comes after that is hers alone."

I thought, hearing that, about whether Eikon was someone I could
really be called family to. No answer came. But I didn't dislike the
word.

---

That evening I passed the news on to my mother.

She had been moving her body a little more easily these days. She
could walk out to the yard on her own now.

"…Eikon's been asking a lot more questions lately." I said. "The
Curator says that's how lack arrives."

My mother's hand stopped at that. She was still mending my brother's
clothes these days.

"…Do you want to help her?" she asked.

"…I don't know. I don't even really know what helping would mean."

"…For me." my mother said. "When you came to me, the hardest thing was
doing nothing at all. You wanted to do something, anything. But I
wasn't ready, then."

Something clicked in me at that.

"…So should I do nothing with Eikon?"

"…Being there beside someone and doing nothing aren't the same thing."
my mother said. "That's something I learned."

---

A few evenings later, Eikon spoke first.

"…Anemos." she said. "Can I be released too?"

I stopped digging.

"…You can. You're fifteen. It's not like my brother."

"…Then all I need is to consent?"

"…Yes."

Eikon didn't say anything right away, hearing that. She looked down at
the channel for a long while.

---

"…Are you scared?" I asked.

"…I don't really know what scared is, yet." she said. "But it's
different from before. Before, that word had nothing to do with me at
all. Now it's doing something to me."

I started to ask exactly what that difference was, and stopped
myself. She didn't seem able to explain it yet either.

---

"…Why did Anemos's mother do it?" Eikon asked.

"…She said, she wanted to see the two of us standing side by side."

"…That's all?"

"…Yes. That was all."

Eikon turned that answer over.

"…I don't have a reason like that." she said. "I have people who want
to see me. My parents. But I can see them even now, without release.
They're here too."

*I couldn't argue with that. It was true. My mother's reason was my
mother's own. Eikon needed a different one. I felt the temptation to
make one up for her, in her place. I held back.*

---

"…Then why are you telling me all this now?" I asked.

Eikon was quiet for a long while at that question.

"…I don't know." she finally said. "But lately this thought keeps
coming back to me. A thought I never used to have. I don't even know
why it started. Whether it's because of the name, or whether it was
always there and I'm only seeing it now."

"…Is that why you started wanting to be alone too?"

Eikon looked at me at that. Her face was a little surprised.

"…How did you know?"

"…You came late, a few days ago. You said you just sat there."

"…I guess that's what it was." she said. "I can't explain what it is,
either."

---

The sound of water filled the space between us.

I put nothing on top of that silence. I didn't want to be the one
persuading her, this time. I already knew that would be the moment my
own wanting got mixed into hers.

*That night I thought of something Speon had said a few days before.
Water changing direction doesn't guarantee it will reach here. But if
the direction never changes, it will never come at all. I was
confirming that, again, in front of Eikon.*

---

"…Do you want to be released?" I finally asked. Very carefully.

Eikon looked at me. For a long time.

"…I don't know." she said. "I can't even tell whether I want it, or
whether I'm just curious."

"…Are those different?"

"…Do you know, Anemos?"

I had no answer to that question. I didn't know either.

---

"…For me." Eikon said. "This is fine, just as it is. Really. You think
I'm pitiable, don't you, Anemos."

"…No." I said. Honestly. "Maybe I did once. Not anymore."

"…Then why?"

"…Because you saying you're satisfied doesn't sound like a lie to me
anymore."

Eikon laughed a little at that. Her usual laugh, the kind I hadn't
seen in a while.

---

"…But." she said. "Being satisfied and this being everything — I
guess those turned out to be different things. I thought they were
the same."

I stopped breathing at that sentence.

*I set this sentence down more quietly than anything else in this
book. I don't want to attach any explanation to it. Eikon had begun to
tell satisfied and everything apart. What that meant, I didn't know
that day. I still don't fully know now.*

---

"…So are you deciding today?" I asked.

Eikon shook her head.

"…No."

"…Why not?"

"…Not yet." she said. "I want to hold on to this question a little
longer. If I answer too fast, I don't think I'll know if it was really
my own answer."

Hearing that, my brother's voice overlapped in my mind. What's the
difference between not yet and no? My mother had answered him. Not yet
changes, when enough time passes.

---

"…So is this the end of this conversation?" I asked.

Eikon looked straight at me.

"…No." she said.

She paused a moment.

"…Ask me again later."

---

I heard that sentence several times over, right there, though it had
only been spoken once. It rang again and again inside me.

*I know that sentence isn't an answer. It isn't a conclusion either. Of
everything in this book, I rewrote this moment the most. At first I
wanted to make her say something more definite. But that was my own
greed. Eikon, that day, did not close the door. She didn't open it
either. She simply left it standing ajar.*

---

"…I'll ask." I said. "Whenever."

"…Promise?"

I thought of the finger my brother had hooked with mine. I didn't hold
out a finger this time. I said it in words instead.

"…Promise."

Eikon nodded at that. Not so much a relieved face as a face that had
set something down.

---

"…One thing." she added. "Don't ask too often."

"…Why?"

"…If you keep asking, I'm afraid it'll stop being my answer and start
being the answer Anemos wants."

I had no reply to that. She understood that boundary more precisely
than I did.

"…Then how often should I ask?" I asked.

"…I don't know." she said. "Don't decide that either, Anemos."

I laughed at that. A strange kind of laugh. I knew, in that moment,
that she was handling this postponement far more exactly than I was.

---

The sun went fully down.

We sat listening to the sound of water for a while without speaking.
Stars came out one by one. The city's lights and the valley's darkness
divided the space between us.

*That night, for the first time, I understood what this book was
trying to say — not through words, but through where we sat. I didn't
persuade Eikon. She didn't persuade me either. We just sat in the same
place, with one unanswered question between us.*

---

Eikon rose first.

"…See you tomorrow." she said. The same goodbye as always.

"…See you tomorrow."

She walked off. I watched her back. Her walk was the same as
yesterday's. But I knew now that the walk wasn't the same as
yesterday's at all.

*That night I opened the water flask and drank, thinking. I'd spent
this whole book waiting for the moment someone changed for certain.
The way my mother had. But Eikon didn't come that way. She only left a
door open a crack behind her. That crack looked smaller to me than a
closed door. But I know now: a door left open, however little, is a
completely different thing from a door that is shut.*

*Writing this chapter, I rewrote the ending more than once. I tried
writing an ending where Eikon chooses release in the end. I didn't
like it. I tried an ending where she refuses to the very last. That
wasn't it either. In the end I decided to leave it exactly as it
happened that day. What this book is trying to say was never a
conclusion — it was simply the fact of a door left open.*
