# Chapter 21. My Brother's Answer

> _Previously_ — The five words I'd prepared for my mother broke, one
> by one, or were hidden away by my own hand. I decided to go and see
> my brother next, carrying no prepared words at all.

Before I went to my brother, I went first to see the Curator.

There was something I needed to ask. Whether an eight-year-old could
give consent at all. Saying that question out loud, I felt how odd
it was, even to myself. Why had I assumed that something not even an
adult could do might somehow be possible for a child?

The Curator didn't answer right away. He turned over a few pages of
paper.

"…It's not a procedure that stops at age," he finally said. "Consent
is consent. Old or young."

"…But?"

"…There's no but. Not procedurally."

I felt I'd heard that phrase, "not procedurally," somewhere before.
I'd already heard it once, in Chapter 17. Then as now, there was
always something more behind those words.

"…Anemos," the Curator said. "What an eight-year-old understands
isn't mine to judge. That isn't my place."

"…Then whose place is it?"

"…Yours. You're his brother."

That weighed heavily on me. It was a sentence that handed the weight
over to me. I wasn't ready to take it up yet.

"…What if," I asked, "my brother says no?"

"…Then it stops there. You can ask again later, but there's no way
to force it forward."

"…What if he says yes?"

The Curator thought a little longer about that question.

"…If he says yes, the procedure moves forward. Except."

"…Except what?"

"…We confirm it once more, on our end. Directly with the subject.
Not through the family."

Hearing that, I felt some relief. It sounded like the judgment
wouldn't rest on me alone.

"…How do you confirm it?"

"…We simply ask. Whether they truly want it. But Anemos," the
Curator looked straight into my eyes, "that confirmation isn't
everything. It confirms the form of it. It doesn't confirm the
content."

I didn't fully understand what that meant, at the time. I would
understand it a few hours later.

---

The corridor to my brother's room was the same one I'd walked in
Chapter 10. I'd already counted its steps once before. This time I
didn't count. I had no room left over for counting.

The door opened. My brother was standing by the window. He ran to me
when he saw me.

"…Brother!"

"…Been doing well?"

"…Yeah. Did you get taller again?"

I smiled at that. There was no way I'd grown taller in a few weeks.
But he asked every time, in complete earnest. I knew it was this
child's only way of measuring time. My height was his clock.

My brother took my hand. That small hand. Still the same size.

"…Want to see this?" he said. The stone. The one he'd picked up by
the well.

"…Still have it, huh."

"…I carry it all the time."

I took the stone in my hand for a moment. Then I sat down. I had
something to say today.

---

"…Listen," I began. "There's something I want to ask you."

"…What?"

I'd thought all night about how to explain it, but I never found the
right words. In the end I started with the easiest thing.

"…Would you like to be able to grow again?"

My brother's eyes went wide.

"…I can grow?"

"…Yeah. There's supposed to be a way."

"…Can I get tall like you?"

"…Yeah."

My brother's face lit up. Watching that face, I felt my heart drop.
I hadn't expected he'd be glad about it so easily.

"…But," I went on, "if you do that, some other things come with it."

"…Like what?"

I stopped for a moment. I never did find, all the way through, how
to say this part.

"…You could get sick. If you got hurt. And someday…" I couldn't
finish the sentence.

"…Stop?" my brother asked.

I was startled by that word. It was the word I'd written back in
Chapter 1. In the settlement, that's what they called it — being ill
a long while and then going. My brother still remembered the word.

"…Yeah. Someday that could happen too."

My brother heard that and got a thinking look on his face for a
moment. The face of an eight-year-old working hard to weigh
something.

"…Did Mom stop?" my brother asked.

"…No. Mom's still here, just as she is."

"…So if I stay the same too, I won't stop?"

I couldn't answer that question. It was exactly right. My brother
was already stating, on his own, something close to the conclusion.

---

"…What do you think, brother?" he asked.

I hadn't prepared for that question. I'd come here resolved to carry
no prepared words, so I had no answer for this one either.

"…I don't think I should say what I think," I said. "This is your
own matter."

"…But do you want it?"

I went a long while without answering that one, too. I wanted it. Of
course I wanted it. But it felt wrong to say so. I'd already learned
this once, in front of my mother. What I want isn't a reason.

"…I," I finally managed, "want what you want."

My brother heard that and stared at me for a moment. Then he smiled.

"…Then it's simple."

"…What is?"

"…I just want what you want."

My breath caught at that, all at once.

"…What do you mean?"

My brother said it like it was obvious.

"…If you'll do it, I'll do it."

---

I heard that single line and, at first, I was glad.

Truly glad. My mother had said, "I'll think about it." I'd heard
Eikon's "What's that got to do with it?" more times than I could
count. Here, for the first time, I heard "I'll do it." I nearly rose
from my seat.

*I remember those few seconds even now, exactly. Something inside me
cheered without a sound. There it is. My brother wants this. I
repeated that sentence to myself, over and over.*

In that moment I was already picturing the next scene. Myself
running to the Curator's office. My hand writing my brother's name on
the application. Even the scene of holding my brother's hand and
leaving a mark for him on the signature line. All of it passed
through my head in a few seconds. I was startled myself at the
speed. What I'd imagined in those few seconds felt more vivid than
anything I'd prepared over seven years.

But that cheer didn't last.

"…Why did you answer that way?" I asked. My voice shook strangely.

"…Because it's what you want."

"…No, I asked what you want."

My brother tilted his head.

"…If you want it, I want it too."

I heard that and looked down at the stone still in my hand. I'd been
gripping it this whole time without noticing. Its shape had pressed
into my palm.

"…Not that," I asked again. "Without me — just thinking about it on
your own — how do you feel?"

My brother put on a serious, thinking face at that question. He
thought a long while.

"…I don't know. I just never thought about it, because you were
here."

---

I had no words for a long while, in front of that answer.

*I've rewritten this scene more times than any other in this
account. In my first draft, I wrote that I withdrew the application
right there on the spot. That was a lie. In reality, it wasn't so. In
that moment, briefly, very briefly, I thought about simply accepting
that answer as it was.*

*I set that thought down here honestly. I thought about taking my
brother's "I'll do it" straight to the Curator. Procedurally, there
was nothing wrong with it. My brother had answered. If you'll do it,
I'll do it. All I had to do was write that answer on the form,
exactly as it was. Probably no one would have stopped me. Even if the
Curator confirmed it himself, my brother would clearly give the same
answer in front of him too. If you'll do it, I'll do it. That
confirmation confirms the form, not the content — those words, only
then, did I finally understand completely.*

*I'll also note that during those few seconds, my hand was already
moving toward the application in my pocket. My fingertips touched
the edge of the paper. I pulled my hand back right there. I didn't
know exactly why, at the time. I know now. The moment I pulled that
paper out, I would have become someone who kept, on the surface, what
this city calls respect, while betraying it completely underneath.*

I took my brother's hand again. That small hand.

"…I'll ask you again. Later," I said.

"…Can't we do it now?"

"…No, not now."

My brother's face fell. Watching that face, my chest tore. This
child had been trying to give me what I wanted. And I was refusing
the gift.

---

On the way back to the lodging I thought again of the Curator's
words. What I'd heard in Chapter 18. "A preserved person lacks
nothing." And why consent was a problem in the first place. I'd never
once applied those words to my brother. My brother wasn't someone who
had never wanted anything — he was someone still learning, from me,
how to want.

*That night I saw that difference clearly for the first time. Eikon's
"What's that got to do with it?" came out of her having no lack.
My brother's "I'll do it" came out of having no direction of his own.
Neither one was consent. And yet I had to admit that my brother's
answer was, by far, the more tempting of the two. Because at least it
had been a yes.*

That night I took out the application again. I looked a long time at
the space where my brother's name would go. My hand moved over it
several times. I didn't write.

*That night I thought again, for the first time, about the word
"respect." The Curator had said once that the consent requirement
had probably come out of respect. A respect meant to keep anyone from
turning a person back carelessly. That night I also saw the other
side of that word. To take my brother's "I'll do it" and carry it
forward would have been, at the same time, both keeping respect and
completely destroying it. The procedure would have been followed. But
the thing the procedure was meant to protect would not have been.*

---

The next morning, I went to the Curator's office.

"…I asked my brother yesterday," I said.

"…What did he say?"

"…He said, 'If you'll do it, I'll do it.'"

The Curator heard that and showed no expression at all. Not a face
that was judging. Just a face that was waiting.

"…Does that count as consent?" I asked.

"…Procedurally, yes."

There it was again. Procedurally.

"…But what I want to ask," I went on, "is whether that's real
consent."

For the first time, the Curator smiled, faintly. A sad smile.

"…It sounds like you already know the answer to that, Anemos."

I had no answer to that. He was right. I already knew the answer.

---

"…I'd like to withdraw the application," I said.

"…Your brother's?"

"…Yes. It hadn't even been filed yet, but I wanted to make it
certain."

The Curator took something out of a folder and set it in front of
me. A form confirming the withdrawal. I wrote my name on it. My hand
didn't shake. This time, I was truly glad that it didn't.

"…You did well," the Curator said.

"…At what?"

"…There have been people, sometimes. People who, when the child
answers that way, go ahead and carry it through as it stands."

I looked up, startled at that.

"…There have been people like that?"

The Curator nodded. He said nothing more. I asked nothing more. It
was a weight I could understand without asking.

*Leaving that room that day, I felt for the first time that I had
chosen something myself. Up until then, everything had been
happening to me. I'd been asked, "Why?" I'd been given, "I'll think
about it." This time was different. I could have taken my brother's
"I'll do it," and I set it down myself instead. It was the first
choice I had made in this book.*

I didn't go to my brother that day. Instead I left him a letter. I
asked the attendant to pass it along.

*"I'll ask you again later. Next time, think it over by yourself,
without me there. You can take as long as you need."*

Writing that letter, I felt that this line, of all things, was more
honest than any word I'd prepared over seven years. It was honest
because I hadn't prepared it. I caught that irony that very day.

---

Passing through the garden, I ran into Eikon. Not on the bench —
we met in the middle of the path.

"…Your face is different again," Eikon said. "Different from
yesterday's face."

"…How's it different?"

"…Yesterday's was a broken face. Today's is…" she chose her words
for a moment. "…a face that put something down."

I thought that phrase was exactly right. I'd picked something up
today and set it back down. The feel of it was still in my hand.

"…What did you put down?" Eikon asked.

I told her briefly about my brother. The answer, "I'll do it," and
withdrawing it.

Eikon heard the whole thing and said nothing for a long while. Then
she said this.

"…I honestly don't really see why that's a problem. Your brother
said he wanted to."

"…He said he wanted to because of me. Not because he wanted to,
himself."

"…Is that different?"

I couldn't answer that question right away. In this city, that
distinction seemed to be a foreign one. For Eikon, wanting and having
a reason always came together. If you want something, there's a
reason; if there's no reason, you don't really want it either. But
with my brother, the two had come apart.

"…It's different," I finally said. "I only saw that difference
clearly myself, for the first time, today."

Eikon heard that answer and tilted her head. Not a face that
understood, but a face that simply accepted not understanding.

"…You've been picking out nothing but hard things to do lately,
Anemos."

I smiled a little at that.

"…Yeah. I have."

*I remember that short conversation for a long time after. Eikon
didn't understand my choice. And yet she didn't argue against it. It
was the first time I felt what it was like to be respected without
being understood.*
