# Chapter 33. Eikon's Logic

> _Previously_ — Back up from the valley, the city looked different
> though nothing about it had changed. I carried the question over to
> Eikon. "Do you think these can live again." She'd said no without a
> moment's hesitation.

The next morning, I went to find Eikon again.

It wasn't that there was anything left unsaid from the day before. To be
exact, I didn't know myself why I'd gone looking for her. Her certainty
just kept sitting on my mind. That face saying no, without hesitation.

She was on the bench she always sat on. She saw me and nodded slightly.
No surprise. In keeping with this city's people.

"…Back again," she said.

"…Yes."

"…Because of what we talked about yesterday?"

I was a little surprised she'd guessed that.

"…Yes. That's right."

---

On the way there I'd passed Speon's pit. The sound of running water came
from it. Listening to it, I'd thought — everyone always said it was a
good thing when water appeared where there had been none. But that
reasoning didn't work on Eikon. For her, everything was already there.

I sat down beside her. I set the flask on my knees. My hand fiddled with
the cap.

"…There's something I wanted to ask yesterday, and didn't," I said.

"…What is it?"

"…Is there really nothing you wish for?"

Eikon's face took on a thinking look at that question. Not an angry
expression. A face that was really thinking it through.

"…What do you mean, wish for?" she asked back.

"…Wanting something more, because something's lacking."

"…I don't lack anything."

I'd heard that answer from her many times before. But that day it sounded
a little different. I wanted to ask her more.

"…Really?" I asked. "Not one thing?"

Eikon looked straight at me.

"…Yes. Really."

"…For example?" I asked. "Something you'd like to eat, something you'd
like to wear — nothing at all?"

"…There's nothing I'd like to eat," she said. "What the distribution
station gives is enough. I've never wished for anything more
delicious. It's the same with clothes."

I lost words in front of that answer. Outside, wanting something more
delicious, something warmer to wear, was simply a given. Only then did
it really sink in that here, that given didn't hold at all.

---

"…Can you explain it to me?" I asked. "Why you don't lack anything."

Eikon was quiet a moment. Not a silence for choosing her words — a
silence more like rethinking the question itself.

"…I've never been hungry," she said. "Never been sick. Never been
lonely. My parents are here, and I'm still me. What more would I need?"

"…But isn't there something you wish for anyway? Anything at all."

"…No," she said. It wasn't a firm tone. Just a tone stating a fact.
"Wishing means wanting something you don't have right now. There's
nothing I don't have right now."

I tried to find something to argue against that. I couldn't find it.

"…Is there nothing you're curious about?" I asked again. "Something new,
something you've never seen."

"…There are things I'm curious about. I'm curious about that person you
say you met, yesterday. But curious and lacking are different things."

"…How are they different?"

"…When you're curious, it ends once you know. When you're lacking,
filling it just makes more come up. From what you've said, that's how it
sounds for you."

Something in me flinched at that. It was an exact hit. Outside, filling
it had always just made more come up. In this city, there was nothing
like that.

"…So you've never once gone through that? Filling something and having
more come up."

"…No. Not since I came here."

"…And before?"

Eikon closed her eyes a moment, then opened them.

"…My memory's hazy. It was seven years ago. But I probably did. Everyone
does."

I turned that over for a long time. It was the first time it occurred to
me that she might once have been like me.

---

"…So why would I need more?" she asked, first this time.

I stopped at that question.

"…What do you mean?"

"…I know you keep looking at us thinking it's a pity. You try not to
show it on the outside, but it shows anyway."

I felt ashamed at that. I wanted to deny it, but I couldn't.

"…But me," Eikon said. "I don't lack anything. Why would I need more?"

I read that sentence over to myself several times. I don't lack anything.
Why would I need more. It had the shape of a question, but it wasn't
really a question at all. It was the speech of someone who already had
the answer.

---

*I hold onto this sentence longer than any other in this record. Writing
this book, I tried, over and over, to argue against it. I failed every
time.*

"…If you had more," I tried to go on, "you'd learn more, and grow more,
and—"

"…More what?" Eikon asked. It wasn't a taunting tone.

I stopped at that question. Grow more. But why should you grow. Why is
growing better than not growing. I tried to find the answer and realized
it had always been so obvious to me that I'd never once had to put it
into words.

"…I don't know," I said, honestly.

Eikon smiled a little at that answer. Not a mocking smile.

"…You say I don't know a lot lately too."

"…I guess I do."

---

"…I'm not sad," she said again. "I'm not suffering. I'm me. The same as
I was seven years ago. Why is that bad?"

I had no answer to that question either.

"…I didn't say it was bad," I said.

"…But you think it. You look at me and feel sorry for me."

I started to deny it, then stopped. It was true.

"…I'm sorry," I said.

"…There's nothing to be sorry for," Eikon said. "I just think it's
strange. I'm fine, so why does everyone look at me feeling sorry?"

*In front of that question, for the first time, I saw this whole city
differently. Maybe it wasn't this city that was to be pitied. Maybe it
was me, looking at this city and finding it pitiable. I tried to push
that thought away for a long time.*

---

"…If you're really all right," I asked, carefully, "why did that person
apply for release, back then? You told me about it before. The one you
called sick."

Eikon's expression shifted slightly, for the first time.

"…That person was sick."

"…Sick with what?"

"…I don't know. She just turned strange. She started wanting things,
over and over. She hadn't been like that before."

"…What did she want?"

"…I don't remember. Anyway, then she applied, and after that she was
gone."

That word — gone — stuck with me.

"…How can you be sure it was a sickness?" I asked.

Eikon looked at me. For the first time something other than certainty
flickered a little in her eyes.

"…I can't be sure," she said. "But there's no other word for it. If
someone who was fine suddenly starts wanting something, what else would
you call it?"

---

I had no answer to that either.

"…Have you ever been afraid?" I asked. A question that came to me
suddenly. "Seven years ago, before you came here."

Eikon paused a moment at that question.

"…Maybe. I don't remember well."

"…Not even what you were afraid of?"

"…The dark, maybe. Doesn't everyone get afraid of that?"

"…And now?"

"…Now there's no reason to go anywhere dark. I sleep at the set time,
at night."

Something bitter came over me at that answer. It wasn't that the fear
had gone away — it was that there was nothing left to be afraid of at
all. I remembered feeling this same distinction with my mother and my
brother. This city hadn't overcome fear. It had simply removed everything
there was to fear.

*In that moment I understood how honest that word "sickness" really was.
Eikon wasn't using it out of malice. In her world, it truly was a word
with no other name to give it. When a lack appears in a world with no
lack in it, there's no word for it but sickness.*

"…I don't think it's a sickness," I said, carefully.

"…Then what do you think it is?"

I stopped a long time at that question.

"…I don't know," I said finally. "But I don't think 'sickness' is the
right word."

Eikon showed no particular reaction to that. Not angry, not agreeing
either. Just a face that seemed to set the words aside somewhere inside
her.

---

"…You know," Eikon said, after a long while, "I don't understand why you
keep trying to shake me."

I was startled by that.

"…I wasn't trying to."

"…You are. By asking so many questions."

I didn't know what to say to that.

"…I'm sorry," I said again.

"…I told you there's nothing to be sorry for," Eikon said. "But know
this much — I'm not shaking. No matter how much you ask."

For the first time, I felt something else behind her certainty. It
sounded almost like a defense. And yet the certainty was still perfectly
clear. It was strange that the two could sit together.

*I didn't know, then, how to hold those two things together. I still
don't fully know now. Only, having finished this whole book, I've come to
think this was one of the most honest moments in it.*

"…But," Eikon said again, "why do you keep trying to shake me? I don't
shake you."

I thought about that question for a long time.

"…I guess it's because I'm worried about you," I said.

"…There's nothing to worry about, I keep telling you."

"…I'm still worried."

At that, for the first time, Eikon's face softened, just a little.

"…You're a strange person. Worrying about someone with nothing to worry
about."

"…I guess I am."

---

"…I don't lack anything. Why would I need more?"

Eikon said it once more. This time it wasn't asked of me — it sounded
more like something she was confirming to herself.

I tried again to argue against that sentence. I thought about bringing
up love. That love doesn't need a lack. But before I could say it, I knew
it wouldn't persuade her. She was loved. Her parents were still beside
her. Even love, for her, was already something fully filled.

*In that moment, for the first time, I admitted I had no words that
could beat this logic. She wasn't wrong. She wasn't suffering. No
argument had any power in front of her satisfaction. I think this is the
most frightening fact in this whole book. I still think so now.*

In that moment I thought of my brother. Preserved at eight, he stood in
the same place as Eikon. An eight-year-old who lacked nothing. I
remembered the answer I'd gotten when I first brought up release to him.
"I'll do it if you do it." I'd withdrawn my own application because I
couldn't accept that as consent. Standing in front of Eikon now, I felt I
understood that reason a little more clearly. My brother hadn't answered
because he had no lack to answer from. It was the same with Eikon. Both
of them weren't refusing to want something — for both of them, the very
motion of wanting had become unnecessary.

*I saw that difference clearly for the first time, in that moment.
Refusal and having-no-need are different things. My brother and Eikon
had never refused. They simply had no reason to want. I had confused the
two for a long time.*

"…What about longing?" I asked, last. "Missing someone who's gone. Isn't
there any of that either?"

Eikon looked at me for a moment.

"…There's no one for me to miss," she said. "My parents are here. I'm
here. There's no one who's gone, so there's no one to miss."

I was completely out of words at that. For longing to exist at all,
something had to be lost first. In her world, nothing had been lost.

"…Yeah," I said finally. "I think you're right."

Eikon looked a little surprised at that. She didn't seem to have
expected I would concede so easily.

"…And yet something's still left over?" she asked. For the first time
she turned the question back on me.

"…Yeah," I said. "Something's left."

"…What is it?"

"…I don't know. I'd feel better if I knew what it was, too."

---

The sun was going down. Eikon stood up.

"…I hope you find that answer," she said. An unexpected thing to say.

"…Why?"

"…So you'll stop shaking me."

I laughed at that. She smiled, just barely, too.

"…I'll come again," I said.

"…All right. But I'm not going to change."

She walked off. I watched her back and turned that sentence over again.
I'm not going to change. I understood that it wasn't a threat, and it
wasn't a warning either. It was just her stating a fact.

I stayed sitting there a long while after. I could feel the bench
cooling. I watched the people passing by. All of them wore the same face
as Eikon. A face that lacked nothing. Those faces looked a little
different to me now. Before, I'd thought of them as empty faces. That
day I understood they weren't. Not empty — full. Only what filled them
held none of the things I knew.

*Since I'd come to this city, I'd kept looking at these people with
pity. That day, for the first time, I thought that the pity might be a
lack on my side. Was it not because something was missing in me that I
looked at them and found them pitiable? I held onto that question a long
time, and set it down again without an answer.*

*That night, I tried to write Eikon's logic down on paper. I lack
nothing. Therefore I have no reason to want more. There was no flaw
anywhere between those two sentences. I sat a long time in front of that
perfection.*
