Nobody dies in the Preserve. The system called Perpetuity holds a person's mind and memory entire, and it keeps that promise exactly. The preserved do not vanish. They do not fall ill. They are not forgotten. They also never laugh again, and never weep.

Seven years ago Anemos was outside fetching water when his mother and his younger brother were preserved. He has come back to wake them. It takes him only a few days in the city to learn that preservation can in fact be undone — the procedure is simple, and it has never been forbidden. Undoing it returns a person to a living body. From that moment, they can die again.

To get them back is to make them losable.

Eikon was preserved at fifteen and is fifteen still. She is not suffering and she is not deceived. She lacks nothing, and she thinks wanting more is a kind of sickness. Her argument is harder to answer than grief, and this book does not hurry to answer it.

The Dry Valley is Book Five of the Loop Chronicles, a Christian fantasy series for readers who would rather be asked an honest question than handed an answer. Here the machine passes no judgment on anyone at all — and the outcome is no different.

A story about a boy who counts things because he cannot keep them, an official who knows exactly what his work is and does it anyway, a man digging a well in a city that has no use for water, and one question asked in a valley full of bones: do you think these can live again?
