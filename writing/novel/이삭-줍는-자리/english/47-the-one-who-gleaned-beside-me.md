# Chapter 47. The One Who Gleaned Beside Me

> _Previously_ — Lytron pulled his own line back out, for the first
> time, as a question. "I hadn't done anything wrong. So why wasn't that
> enough?" No one gave an answer. Not me, not Pikra, not "him."

The day before Mena left, "he" disappeared with her.

I thought of the day I first met him. He'd been just another quiet
hired hand. I hadn't singled him out. I'd only thought him a man of few
words.

Then the day he began gleaning beside me. I hadn't thought much of it,
then, either. Just someone working the same hour, the same corner.

And then the day I noticed there was always a little more, in the spot
where he worked. From that day I began to see him differently. Exactly
how, I could never quite say, even to myself.

*Looking back over these months, I keep forgetting exactly when he
began standing beside me. It feels as though he had always been there,
and at the same time it feels as though he appeared, one day, out of
nowhere. I think both feel true. I still cannot say for certain, even
now, whether he had been there from the start and I was only slow to
notice him, or whether he truly did begin standing at my side one day.*

I didn't notice, at first. I went out to the field that morning and he
wasn't there. I thought he was only late. He still hadn't come by
midday. He hadn't come by evening.

---

I asked another of the hired hands.

"…Have you seen him today?"

"…Who?"

"…The one who always worked at that far end. The quiet one."

The man thought for a moment, then shook his head.

"…Oh, him. He wasn't here today, now that you mention it."

*Something cold went through me in that moment. The field without him
felt unfamiliar. His usual spot, empty now, suddenly looked enormous.
Even the sound of the wind passing through that empty place sounded
different.*

---

He didn't come the next day, either.

I went to the field office and asked. I didn't even know his name. I'd
only ever called him "he." I wasn't the only one. No one else seemed to
know exactly what to call him either.

"…The quiet one who worked at the far end," I said to the clerk. "He
hasn't come today either."

The clerk checked the roster.

"…He's off the hired-hand list," the clerk said. "A request to leave the
work came in two days ago."

"…Where's he going?"

"…That's not recorded here," the clerk said. "Hired hands aren't
obligated to state that."

---

I sat alone at the corner that evening.

The spot he always sat in was empty. I looked at that empty spot for a
long time.

*That was the first time I understood he had always been someone free
to leave. I had come to think of him as part of this field. Someone
always there. But he had always, in truth, been someone passing
through. I had forgotten that.*

---

That night I told Lytron he was gone.

"…That quiet one?" he asked. "The one at the end of the field."

"…Yes."

Lytron thought for a moment.

"…That's strange," he said. "I hardly knew him, and hearing he's gone
still leaves something strange in me."

"…What way strange?"

"…Like I've missed something important," he said. "That I never got to
say a proper goodbye."

I felt the same way.

"…He said something to me, once," Lytron said. "That day I reset the
loss rate and stayed alone in the field afterward. He passed by and said
one thing."

"…What did he say?"

"…'You did well,'" he said. "That was all. But it was the most support
I felt that whole day. Not my father, not the administrator — no one
else said anything like that to me."

*Hearing that, I understood for the first time that he had quietly
passed through more lives than just mine. I had thought of him only as
part of my own story. He had been much wider than that.*

---

Pikra stopped her hands too, hearing the news.

"…He's gone?"

"…Yes."

"…I saw him once too," Pikra said. "Around the time you first started
going out to the corner. The quiet one working at the end. I just
thought, then — there's a man who doesn't talk."

"…And now?"

Pikra looked out the window a moment.

"…Now I'm not sure," Pikra said. "But it's strange — hearing he's gone
leaves something empty in me. And I hardly ever saw him at all."

*I saw, in that moment, that this regret wasn't only mine. No one had
really known him well. And still no one heard the news of his leaving
without some regret.*

---

On the third day, I saw him one last time.

Near the gate. He was carrying a small pack. Not much. He was, as
always, a man traveling light.

"…Are you leaving?" I asked, coming closer.

He looked at me and smiled.

"…Yes," he said. "Today."

I didn't know what to say, in that moment. I had gleaned beside this man
for months in this city, and never once prepared myself for a goodbye.

---

"…Where are you going?" I asked.

"…Somewhere else," he said. The same vague answer as always.

"…Another field?"

"…Probably," he said. "Wherever there are people who need to glean."

The words sounded strange to me. He'd never once named a place. I used
to think that was just his manner. Now it felt like something else.

"…Can I ask your name?" I asked. The first time I'd asked, in all these
months.

He looked at me and smiled.

"…Why ask now?" he asked back.

"…I thought you wouldn't tell me, if I asked," I said, honestly.

"…That's right," he said. Gently. "I won't."

I wasn't hurt by that answer. It felt, if anything, exactly like him.

"…Then what should I call you? Keep calling you 'he'?"

"…That'd be best," he said. "That suits me exactly."

---

"…Pikra and Lytron say hello," I said. "They were sorry they couldn't
see you themselves."

"…Give them my regards," he said. "I'm sorry too."

"…Lytron told me — the day he reset the loss rate and stayed alone in
the field, you passed by and said 'you did well,' just that one thing."

He smiled a little.

"…I remember," he said. "His face looked hard, that day."

"…Did you go by others too, the same way?"

"…Sometimes," he said. "There isn't much I can do. But I can say one
thing."

I felt something old in that answer, some habit worn deep. He seemed a
man who had always gone about this way. Not only in this city. For much
longer, through many more places. Through several seasons, several
cities, moving in the same manner.

We sat a moment on a bench near the gate.

"…I wanted to ask you something," I said. A question I'd carried a long
while.

"…What is it?"

I hesitated. A question I hadn't managed to ask, all these months.

"…At your spot," I said, "there was always a little more. Beside me. I
don't think that was chance."

He said nothing. He only smiled.

"…Did you do it on purpose?" I asked. "Did you drop grain there, so I
could gather it?"

---

He looked at me for a long time. Something warm crossed his face.

"…Why do you think that?" he asked back.

"…At first I thought it was just chance," I said. "But it happened too
many times. Always at my spot."

He laughed. He didn't answer.

"…You're not going to answer?" I asked.

"…Well," he said. "If I said yes, what would change?"

I had no answer to that.

---

"…I'm just curious," I finally managed. "I just wanted to know."

He looked up at the sky for a moment. Evening was coming on.

"…Some people are the kind who spill," he said. "That's just their way.
But a person who spills doesn't say they spilled it. The moment you say
it, it stops being spilled, and becomes given."

"…What's the difference?"

"…What's spilled is something the gleaner discovers," he said. "What's
given is something the receiver owes. I didn't want you to owe
anything."

*I didn't fully understand that answer then. I understand a little of
it now. Even in that moment, he never answered my question directly.
And yet the answer was already inside the refusal. It took me reading
it back many times before I saw it.*

---

"…One more thing," I said. "You've never once been angry. Not when
Medon refused, not when people only ever saw you as a hired hand. Why?"

He thought for a moment.

"…There was no reason to be," he said.

"…Not even when Medon refused? I was angry, that day."

"…You had every right to be angry," he said. "I was watching from a
different place."

"…What place?"

He smiled again, and didn't answer.

*I remember that smile exactly, even now. It wasn't the smile of a man
holding back anger. It was the smile of a man whose anger had already,
long ago, turned into something else entirely. I didn't understand the
difference then. I understand a little of it now.*

---

"…I have to go now," he said, standing.

I wanted to hold him back. I had no words to hold him back with.

"…Will I see you again?" I asked.

"…I don't know," he said. "But I think this. The ones who once gleaned
together go on gleaning together, somewhere. Even if it isn't the same
field."

I wanted to ask what he meant, and didn't. As always.

---

"…Thank you," I said. No other words came.

"…And you," he said.

"…What is there to thank me for? I only ever received."

He looked at me quietly.

"…Receiving is doing something too," he said. "Not everyone knows how
to receive. I liked watching you receive."

I nearly wept at that. I held it back.

---

He walked toward the gate. A few steps on, he turned back once.

"…Xene."

"…Yes."

"…Keep gleaning," he said. "Even after you no longer need to."

"…Why?"

"…Because if there's someone gleaning, then the one who spills has
meaning too."

And he passed through the gate. I stood there until he was out of
sight.

The road stretched straight out past the gate. Dust rose at its far end
— other travelers walking, a few passing carts. He walked into that
dust, and before long, I could no longer tell him apart from anyone
else.

I felt that moment was strangely familiar. I had once walked the
opposite way down that same road — into a strange city, with no
standing at all. He was walking out of it now. The direction was
opposite. The lightness of the step looked much the same.

*I couldn't leave the gate that evening. I kept feeling, more than
once, that he might reappear. He never did. I finally went home once it
had grown dark.*

---

*I never saw him again after that day. Not within this book. I never
learned his name. I never learned where he went. I never did find out
whether he'd dropped the grain on purpose. He deflected, to the last,
with a smile.*

*I've read that conversation over many times, writing this chapter. I
don't think it's that he never answered me, that day. He answered in a
different way. What's spilled is discovered by the gleaner; what's given
is owed by the receiver. I've decided to take that as the method of
this whole book. I cannot prove, exactly, what he did for me. I only
know this. Where he stood, there was always a little more. That's
enough, I think now.*

The next day, I went out to the field again.

His spot was still empty. I passed it and went to my own usual place. I
bent and gleaned. My hands moved by their own habit.

*I felt something strange, looking at that spot, that day. Too quiet to
call grief; too deep to call nothing at all. I still can't put an exact
name to the feeling somewhere between those two.*

That evening, filling my sack with what I'd gathered at the corner, I
found myself remembering what he'd left me with. Keep gleaning. Even
after you no longer need to. Because if there's someone gleaning, then
the one who spills has meaning too.

*I think that was the day I finally understood that fully. Not knowing
whether he would ever come back, in a field where he no longer stood, I
kept gleaning, exactly as he'd told me. It was the only goodbye I knew
how to give him.*
