# Chapter 42. Pikra's Answer

> _Previously_ — My name went into the Roll. Not zero, anymore. For the
> first time I collected a ration in my own name, went out to the field
> in my own name. And in every one of those moments, the weight of what
> Lytron had given followed after me.

It was about ten days after my registration.

In that time I had wanted, more than once, to call that name again. I
never let it out. I felt, somehow, that I had no right to. While I was
still zero, I hadn't felt I was in any position to give anything back to
this household.

*Looking back now, it was a strange logic. What did a name have to do
with rights? And yet that was truly how I felt, at the time. That a
person without standing couldn't demand anything, and couldn't give
anything back, either. No one had taught me to think that way, and
still, somehow, I'd already learned to think the way the Roll thinks.*

Once I was registered, I thought it would finally be all right. I had a
number now. I was someone who could give something now, too.

I brought it up with Pikra. Something I'd wanted to say for a long time.
Something I hadn't felt I had the right to say, before I was registered.

"…I'm going to call you Glyke again."

Pikra set down what was in her hands. We were cleaning vegetables that
evening. The sound of the knife against the board stopped.

"…What did you say?"

"…I have standing now too," I said. "I have a number. You don't have to
be Pikra anymore. Not anymore."

*I thought, saying it, that my heart would grow lighter. I thought it
would feel like paying off a debt I'd carried a long time. I was wrong.*

---

Pikra looked at me for a long time.

"…Who decided you get to decide that," Pikra said.

I didn't know what to say to that.

"…What do you mean?"

"…When I asked to be renamed," Pikra said, "it wasn't because of your
number. It was because of mine."

I had no words.

"…Your being registered," Pikra said, "doesn't give me back what I
lost."

*I felt something ashamed at that. I had thought my registration was
this whole household's recovery. It wasn't. It was mine. Pikra's place
hadn't moved at all.*

---

"…I'm sorry," I said. "I only meant—"

"…There's nothing to be sorry for," Pikra said. Her voice softened. "I
know what you meant. I'm grateful, even. But there's something you
still don't understand about this."

"…What."

Pikra picked up the knife again, then set it down again.

"…Do you know the last time I used the name Glyke," Pikra asked.

"…Before your husband passed."

"…That's right," Pikra said. "In those days I was someone who had
things to give. I had a husband, two sons, two daughters-in-law who came
to me. I was someone who could share something every day — food,
clothes, a place at the table. Glyke was that person's name."

I listened quietly.

"…I'm not that person anymore," Pikra said. "I still don't have much to
give. You being registered doesn't make me someone with something to
give again. I'm still someone who has lost."

---

"…But," I said, carefully. "That hurts me."

"…What does."

"…Mother," I said, "you still make me see what's lost, every time I look
at you. I don't want to see you only that way."

Pikra looked at me a long time.

"…Xene," Pikra said. "There's something you've misunderstood."

"…What."

"…I don't use this name to show what's lost," Pikra said. "It's the
opposite."

I didn't understand what she meant.

---

Pikra went on, slowly.

"…If I were called Glyke again," Pikra said, "people would look at me
and think — she's all right now. She's become a good person again. A
smiling person again."

"…Why is that bad?"

"…It isn't bad," Pikra said. "But it isn't true. I'm not all right. I
still have no husband, no sons, either of them. That fact doesn't
change. Your being registered doesn't bring my husband back to life."

Something went cold in me at that.

"…The moment I'm called Glyke again," Pikra said, "people will forget
that grief. That's how everyone thinks, once a name changes. Ah, it's
over now. It's passed. I don't want that."

---

I sat quiet for a long while, then said,

"…So you'll go on being Pikra?"

"…Yes," Pikra said. Quite plainly.

"…For the rest of your life?"

"…Probably," Pikra said.

I thought that would be a sad answer. Pikra's face wasn't sad. It was,
if anything, a face that had settled something.

"…The bitter didn't go away," Pikra said. "I just learned I could live
with it still there."

---

I said nothing to that. I couldn't.

*I thought, in that moment, of the day we left the outer settlement, long
before. The day Pikra — she was still Glyke, then — first asked to be
renamed. "Don't call me Glyke. Call me Pikra." I couldn't say the name
at first. My tongue wouldn't shape it. It took weeks before it came
naturally.*

*I had thought, at the time, that changing her name was only a mark of
grief. A name changed to flee what she couldn't bear. Two years later, I
know now that it wasn't flight. It was honesty. She had decided she
would not pretend to still be someone who smiled. And now, even after
things had gotten better, she had decided not to give that honesty up.*

*I turned that sentence over all night. I had always thought recovery
meant returning to how things were. A sick person healing. A lost thing
found again. What was bitter turning sweet again. Pikra wasn't talking
about that kind of recovery.*

"…So how does Mother," I asked, carefully, "live now, still bitter?"

Pikra smiled a little. A smile I hadn't seen in a while. It resembled
the old Glyke's smile, and wasn't quite the same.

"…I live knowing it's bitter," Pikra said. "Before, I didn't know it was
bitter. I thought having a husband and sons meant everything was sweet.
Now I know. Sweet turns bitter, too, in time. But bitter isn't only
bitter, either."

"…What do you mean?"

"…You," Pikra said. Just that.

---

Something caught in my throat at that one word.

"…Me?"

"…You followed me," Pikra said. "I told you three times to go back. You
didn't go. I think that's a sweet thing. One sweet thing inside a bitter
life. Not bitter turning sweet. A sweet thing, inside the bitter."

*I didn't fully understand what that meant until much later. Pikra had
decided, by not changing her name, to neither reduce nor enlarge her
grief, but to simply carry it as it was. She was telling me, that day,
that other things could live alongside that grief.*

---

I looked down at the vegetables on the board. My hands started moving on
their own again. My mind was still on that sentence.

"…So," I asked again, "I'm not supposed to call you Glyke."

"…No," Pikra said. Firm.

"…I should keep calling you Pikra?"

"…Yes," Pikra said. And picked up the knife again.

"…Doesn't it bother you?" I asked.

Pikra spoke as she trimmed the vegetables.

"…Bothering and being wrong are different things," Pikra said. "Your
being registered is right. My staying Pikra is right, too. Both are
right. One doesn't cancel the other."

---

---

A few days later, I got the name wrong, in the market.

A stall owner had asked Pikra's name. I said, out of habit, "Glyke—" and
stopped. The owner gave me a strange look.

"…Pikra," I corrected quickly.

Pikra said nothing. But I saw her shoulders stiffen for a moment.

I apologized on the way home.

"…I'm sorry. It just came out."

"…It's fine," Pikra said. Her voice sat lower than usual.

"…Are you angry?"

"…No," Pikra said. After a moment, she added, "But it frightens me a
little, that the name still comes that easily."

"…Why?"

"…As much as you want to call me that name," Pikra said, "there are
times I want to live under it too. But I shouldn't. I hold that back
every time. You slipped once. I hold it back every single day."

I had no answer for that.

*That was the first time I saw that the decision hadn't been easy for
Pikra, either. I had thought she'd finished making that decision, long
before. I was wrong. She was making it again, every single day.*

---

A neighbor woman stopped by that evening.

Someone she'd known a long time, she said. Seeing Pikra, she spoke
warmly.

"…You've been looking well lately, Pikra. Your daughter-in-law's
registered too, I hear. Couldn't you go back to your old name now?"

Pikra smiled a little.

"…Why should I?" Pikra asked back.

"…Something good's finally happened," the neighbor said. "No need to
keep hold of a bad name."

"…It isn't a bad name," Pikra said. "It's the name I chose."

The neighbor looked a little taken aback at that. She didn't press
further, and turned to other things — a rumor the ration schedule was
changing, someone's leaking roof — and Pikra's name scattered off into
the air like that.

*I've kept that brief exchange a long time. People saw the name Pikra
as a mark of misfortune. A garment to be shed, they thought. Pikra
herself wore it not as a garment but as her own face. Not many people in
this city knew the difference.*

---

That evening, I asked something I'd been curious about.

"…What does my name mean?" I asked. "Does Mother know?"

Pikra stopped her hands.

"…I don't know," Pikra said. "Your name was given by your own family,
not by me."

"…And Mother's name? Glyke?"

Pikra thought for a moment.

"…I heard it's an old word. I don't know exactly, myself," Pikra said.
"Mena would probably know. She knows things like that."

"…Mena?"

"…She's a recorder," Pikra said. "People like that know the roots of
words. I never asked her. I didn't want to know."

"…Why?"

Pikra looked at me.

"…Once you know the meaning," Pikra said, "you hear the name
differently. I wanted to hear Glyke just as my own name. I was afraid
that once I knew the meaning, the name would start sounding like it was
asking something of me."

I thought I understood what she meant, and didn't fully.

*I didn't know then that my name had a meaning too. And Lytron's. Nor
that the moment those meanings would be revealed was still a few
chapters away. Pikra's words, that day, were only something passing by.
Later, they sounded like prophecy.*

---

That night I lay down and took the notice out again.

Name, number, date, standing. I folded the paper away and thought. I was
registered now. Not zero. And yet there was still someone in this house
who had chosen to remain zero. By their own will.

*That was the first time I dimly felt that standing wasn't only the
number the city assigned me. Pikra carried a number inside her that was
different from the one the Roll gave her. A number the Roll couldn't
see. It was probably something beyond price.*

*I lay a long time that night, beside Pikra, without sleeping. She
didn't seem to be sleeping either. We lay there like that, in the dark,
each holding our own number. One was the number the Roll had given. The
other was a number no one could ever assign.*

In the dark, Pikra spoke once more. I'd thought she was asleep. She
wasn't.

"…Xene."

"…Yes."

"…Thank you," Pikra said, "isn't for your being registered."

"…Then what is it for?"

"…For your going on calling me Pikra," Pikra said. "Except for just now.
That one thing, I'm more grateful for than anything else."

I felt tears come at that. I tried not to make a sound.

"…Why is that the thing you're grateful for?" I finally managed.

"…Everyone in this city tells me to become Glyke again," Pikra said.
"They all mean well by it. But no one ever asks why I don't. You did.
And then you just accepted it."

*I began, that night, listening to those words, to understand a little of
what my place in this household was. I hadn't come here to save anyone.
I was only someone who asked, and then accepted. That was all. And yet I
felt, for the first time that night, that it hadn't only been that.*
