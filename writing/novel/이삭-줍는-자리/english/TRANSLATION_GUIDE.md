# The Gleaning Ground — Translation Guide

Book 4 of the **Loop Chronicles**. Korean source: 『이삭 줍는 자리』.
This guide is authoritative. Where it conflicts with a translator's
instinct, the guide wins — consistency across 48 chapters matters more than
any single elegant line.

Books 1–3 use the same conventions; see their guides under
`../../루프-연대기/english/`, `../../울음-장부/english/`, and
`../../사십일-조항/english/`.

## 4. Register and voice

- **First-person retrospective.** Xene narrates after the fact and knows how
  it ends. Past tense throughout.
- **Italic passages** marked `*...*` are her later commentary — the
  "지금은 안다" voice. Keep them italic in English. They are the series'
  signature device; never merge them into surrounding narration.
- **Dialogue opens with an ellipsis**: `"…네."` → `"…Yes."` Keep the
  ellipsis exactly. Do not convert to "Well—" or drop it.
- **Short paragraphs, spare prose.** Resist smoothing Korean's clipped
  sentences into flowing English periods.
- Xene does not plead or dramatize. Her feeling shows in what she notices
  and what she declines to write down. **Never add pathos the Korean lacks
  — she is the one choosing, not the one being rescued.**
- **No Scripture epigraphs** per chapter. Each chapter opens with a
  `> *이전 이야기*` recap block only → render as `> _Previously_ — ...`
- A few chapters use the device "이 장은 내가 겪지 않은 이야기다" — Xene
  narrating events she was not present for, reconstructed from what she was
  told. Translate the frame plainly; do not soften it into omniscience.

## 5. Scripture

Use the **King James Version** only (public domain). Book 4's seed texts
are woven into the prose rather than quoted as epigraphs, but these
underlie the book and their diction should be audible:

**Ruth 1:16** — *"…whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I
will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God."*

**Ruth 1:20** — *"call me not Naomi, call me Mara: for the Almighty hath
dealt very bitterly with me."*

**Leviticus 19:9** — *"And when ye reap the harvest of your land, thou
shalt not wholly reap the corners of thy field, neither shalt thou gather
the gleanings of thy harvest."*

Xene's answer in chapter 5 is the Ruth 1:16 position, but the Korean
deliberately keeps it **short and dry, not declamatory.** Do NOT inflate it
into KJV cadence. See the phrase table below.

## 9. Rules that must not be broken

1. **The Roll never breaks the law.** Not once in this book. Never
   translate in a way that hints at illegality or hedges the
   administrator's claim that the law is being kept. The horror is that he
   is telling the truth.
2. **He never gets angry.** Never render the walking man's lines with
   irritation, sternness, or reproach — and note that in ch.32 he deflects
   *without* a smile, while in ch.47 he deflects *with* one. Preserve that
   difference exactly.
3. **Never gloss the names before chapter 44.** Not in a footnote, not in a
   chapter title, not in a recap block. In ch.44: **Xene = ξένη, "stranger"
   — and at the same time "guest."** **Lytron = λύτρον, "the price paid to
   get someone back."**
4. **Glyke and Pikra stay proper nouns.** Never "Sweet" and "Bitter."
5. **Xene is never pitiable; Lytron is never villainous.** What this book
   indicts is not malice but the harmlessness of a harmless person's
   indifference.
6. **No declared faith.** No character says the English equivalent of
   "이제 믿습니다." The ending stays open.
7. **Chapter 24's rain is not mercy** and the book says so outright. Do not
   soften that into ambiguity.
