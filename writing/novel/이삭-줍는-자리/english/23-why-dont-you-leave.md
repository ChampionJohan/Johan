# Chapter 23. Why Don't You Leave

> _Previously_ — Xene went to the office for Pikra's renewal papers.
> Somewhere in the administrator's explanation of the procedure was a
> brief line about the possibility of transferring standing to someone
> else. No one lingered on it. Xene soon forgot it too.

Around that time, Lytron began coming to the corner even on days he wasn't scheduled to inspect it.

At first I didn't notice. I assumed the inspection schedule had simply shifted. Then one day, Damia mentioned it in passing.

"…He's not on rounds today." Damia said, tipping her chin toward Lytron. "Wasn't last week either."

"…How do you know?"

"…The round roster's posted, by the storehouse." Damia said. "He came on a day his name wasn't on it."

I gave the remark little thought. I'd never counted the days he came against the days he didn't. To me, he was just someone who occasionally stopped by in passing.

---

After mentioning that, Damia asked me something else.

"…Why don't you leave, anyway, Xene?"

I'd heard that question before. But coming from Damia, it sounded a little different.

"…Leave where?"

"…Here. This city." Damia said. "I'd have left long ago, in your place. No standing out there, right? Better to stretch your legs out there than sit here like this every day."

"…Why don't you leave, then?" I asked back.

Damia laughed.

"…I've got standing." she said. "Low, but I've got it. If I left, even that would be gone. Staying here, at least there's something."

I thought her answer was exact. Damia had something in this city worth holding onto. I had nothing. And yet I still didn't leave. Damia didn't seem to notice that difference and turned back to her own work.

*I couldn't give Damia a proper answer that day. She didn't seem to be waiting for one either — just a remark thrown out in passing. I didn't know, then, that the same question would come back to me, far heavier, from someone else's mouth just a few days later.*

---

And that day was one of those.

The light was overcast. No wind, so the dust hung low. I was sifting the dirt in my usual spot. He wasn't at the ditch that day — assigned elsewhere, I'd heard.

Lytron came over and stood beside me. At first he said nothing. Just stood there. I found that odd. Lytron was always someone who came with something to say. Not that day.

"…Did you have something to say?" I asked first.

"…No." he said. "Just passing through."

I didn't believe him. It wasn't a day for inspection; there was no reason to be passing through here at all. But I didn't press. I'd already learned, by then, that pressing rarely led anywhere good.

---

He stood a long while, watching me sift.

"…You lay your fingers flat for that." he said.

I stopped my hand.

"…Yes."

"…You used to keep them up."

I was a little startled. I hadn't expected him to remember a detail that small.

"…I learned it." I said. "It's faster this way."

"…From whom?"

I hesitated over the answer. I didn't want to bring up the quiet man in front of Lytron. I couldn't quite explain why, even to myself.

"…Just picked it up." I said.

Lytron looked at that answer a moment, as if turning it over. He didn't seem to believe it. But he didn't press further either.

*I still remember that small lie. It wasn't that I was trying to protect him. It was only that something felt like it would break if I brought him up in front of Lytron. By then, I already understood — some good things only last if you don't describe them too closely.*

---

"…Xene." Lytron said, abruptly.

I stopped my hand and looked up at him. That was the first time he'd used my name alone, without any title attached.

"…Yes."

Lytron chose his next words carefully. An unfamiliar hesitation, for him. Usually his questions came right out.

"…Could you not go somewhere else, instead of here?"

I didn't understand the question at first.

"…The corner? Somewhere with a higher loss rate, like you mentioned before?"

"…No." he shook his head. "Not that either. I mean the city."

---

I let my dirt-covered hand rest still on my knee.

"…What do you mean?"

"…Outside." Lytron said. "You came from outside originally, I heard. There's no such thing as standing out there."

"…That's right."

"…Then you wouldn't have to sit here like this every day, out there." he said. "Here, however long you stay, your standing never rises. I know that calculation — I run it. If your origin is outside, once and for all, that outside never changes."

I couldn't say anything for a moment, hearing that.

"…Are you telling me to go back?"

"…That's not what I mean." Lytron said, a little flustered. "I'm just curious. Whether it's actually better for you, being here."

"…Better how?" I asked.

That question stopped him.

"…Full, and safe." he finally managed. "That kind of thing."

"…You can be full outside too." I said. "As long as it's not a bad year. Safe's about the same too. There's no Roll out there, so no one judges me by standing, either."

"…Then it's better, isn't it." Lytron said. "So why are you here?"

I thought, for a moment, of that settlement. Twenty-two houses, once. How many of them were still standing now, I didn't know. Since my husband died, and then his brother too, I hadn't heard a single word from that settlement.

"…Going back wouldn't be the same as before." I said. "No husband. Neither of that family's sons left."

"…Even so, you could live without standing."

"…Yes, I could live." I said. "But that's not why I'm here."

---

*I turned that question over a long time, that night. Even now I don't think Lytron meant it unkindly. He really would have seen it as a matter of numbers. Outside, no standing, none needed. Inside, no standing, and it's a problem. The same nothing, and yet it's not a problem out there, and it is in here. To him, it must have been a perfectly simple calculation.*

*I can't say that calculation was wrong. It wasn't. Only, there was something that calculation didn't include. It took me a long while, that day, to put it into words.*

Lytron waited for my answer. He didn't press. As he always did.

"…I'm not here because I have nowhere else to go." I finally said.

"…Then why?"

I set my hand back down on the dirt. As if preparing the answer in my hand first.

"…Pikra is here."

---

Lytron looked, for a moment, like he didn't quite understand the answer.

"…Your mother-in-law?"

"…Yes."

"…Couldn't she come with you?" he said. "You both came from outside, didn't you?"

"…Pikra's not going."

"…Why not?"

I didn't answer that right away. Not because I didn't know the answer. Because I didn't know how to say it, in front of Lytron.

"…It's where she'll be buried." I said. "Her whole family's in this city's graveyard. Husband, both sons. Pikra wants to be laid beside them."

Lytron was quiet a moment.

"…That's the reason." he finally said, just that much.

"…Yes."

---

"…Then what about you?" Lytron asked again. "You're not someone who belongs buried outside. Couldn't you leave on your own?"

I thought the question was exact. Exact enough that it was harder to answer.

"…That's right." I said. "I could leave, on my own."

"…But you don't."

"…No."

"…Why?"

I held onto that *why* for a long while. I had several answers ready for it, at the time. All of them true, and all of them incomplete. *I can't leave her alone* was true but wasn't everything. *There's nowhere particularly better to go to* was also true, and also not everything.

*I've rewritten this passage several times. At first I tried to explain it at greater length — laying out reasons one by one. But what I actually said that day was much shorter. I decided to leave that shortness as it was. Long reasons are usually invented afterward.*

---

"…I go where you go." I said.

Lytron looked at me a moment, hearing that.

"…That's it?"

"…Yes."

"…I don't quite understand." he said, honestly. "You gave up standing, and you sit here every day, and that's all because of that one thing?"

"…Yes."

Lytron looked like a man who couldn't find any words to turn that answer around. I recognized that look. It was close to the look I'd worn myself, in front of the administrator. The look of someone who can't find where to object, standing before something true.

*I understood, in that moment, for the first time, that I could put that same look on someone else's face. Until then it had always been my turn to wear it — before the administrator, before this city. That day, for the first time, the seats changed.*

---

"…Isn't it a loss?" Lytron asked. He looked a little surprised at himself for saying it.

"…What is?"

"…You're not gaining anything. Being here."

I turned that over.

"…I'm not here to gain anything." I said.

"…Then what?"

I had no answer. Truly none. There was no right word in me for that question, then. If I wasn't there to gain something, why was I there — I couldn't find the words for that either.

Lytron didn't ask further. We stood, for a while, without speaking. A little wind stirred, and the dust drifted low along the ground.

---

"…I don't understand it." Lytron said, after a long while. His tone was sincere. "But not understanding it doesn't mean I think it's strange."

I was grateful for that distinction.

"…It's just how it is, that's all." he went on. "I've lived thinking it was natural, not to take a loss. Whether that's strange or not — today was the first time I've ever really thought about it."

I didn't know what to say to that.

*I heard this conversation again, years later, from Lytron himself. He added, that time: "That day, I thought you were the strange one. I don't think that anymore. Now I think it was me who was strange. That not taking a loss isn't just naturally right — that was the first time I ever heard it." I set that down here. That was something I hadn't heard, that day.*

---

Lytron got to his feet.

"…I'll head off." he said.

"…All right."

A few steps away, he turned back.

"…Xene." he called.

"…Yes."

"…How's your mother-in-law's health?"

The question surprised me. I hadn't expected him to ask that.

"…Not so good, lately." I said. "Not badly, though."

"…I see."

He didn't ask anything further, and went. Watching his back, I only later understood that the question hadn't been mere pleasantry.

---

That evening, Pikra sat down by the hearth first and asked.

"…Late today."

"…Lytron came by." I said. I mentioned Lytron; I left out what we'd said.

"…What did he want?"

"…Nothing much." I said. "Just asked this and that."

Pikra looked at me for a moment. She seemed to sense there was more to it. But she didn't press.

"…Things are hard for you here, aren't they." Pikra said instead. Not a question — a confirmation.

"…No, they're not."

"…Liar." Pikra said. There was no anger in her voice. "I know. You're here because of me."

I couldn't say anything to that.

"…Not only because of you." I managed at last.

"…Not only, sure." Pikra said. "But I'm one of the reasons, aren't I."

I didn't deny it. I couldn't have.

*I don't think it was chance that Pikra brought that up first, that night. She must have known I'd heard something that day. She may not have known exactly what, but she likely guessed that someone had asked me a question along those lines. Pikra was someone who could guess things like that.*

---

I didn't repeat to Pikra the exact words Lytron had used. *That's it?* — if she'd heard that, I had no idea how she'd have reacted. Pikra would surely be angry, if she believed I'd given up my standing because of her. So for the rest of that evening, I kept to the usual talk — sifting dirt, the weather.

Pikra went to bed early that night. She coughed a little. I lay awake a long while, listening to it.

*That night I went back over Lytron's question. "If you're not here to gain something, then what?" I wrote that I couldn't find the answer, then. I know a little of it now. I wasn't there to gain something. I was there because I couldn't leave her behind. Those are two different sentences. I didn't know, then, how to put that difference into words.*

*I didn't know the book of Ruth yet, at the time. I first heard that name from Mena, months later. And learned that the answer the woman in that book gave was, almost word for word, the same answer I'd given Lytron at the corner. I don't think that coincidence is a coincidence at all. Some answers cross centuries in exactly the same shape. The gleanings at the corner of the field don't fall, by design. But some things don't fall even though no one ever designed them to.*
