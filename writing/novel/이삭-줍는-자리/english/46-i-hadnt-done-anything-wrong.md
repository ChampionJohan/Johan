# Chapter 46. I Hadn't Done Anything Wrong

> _Previously_ — My case became a precedent in the Roll. The first
> recorded use of the standing-transfer procedure. The office added it
> to their information sheet. A name that had never been in the Roll had
> become the Roll's standard.

As autumn deepened, Lytron and I began walking together often.

Not for any particular purpose. From the field to the office, from the
office to the market. Our errands simply started overlapping more.

That day was the same. We were walking along the edge of a field the
harvest had already left. Dry stubble crunched underfoot at every step.
The air was turning cool.

---

It was that time when the morning and evening air had grown noticeably
different. The field lay empty, the harvest done, and on some days a
thin frost sat on the bare stubble.

A few days before, he'd told me he had finally had a long conversation
with his father. Not what you'd call a reconciliation. Only a few words
had passed between them.

"…My father asked me something," he'd said, then. "Whether I regretted
it."

"…What did you say?"

"…I said I didn't know," he said. "I didn't want to lie."

"…And your father?"

"…He didn't say anything," he said. "But we ate dinner together that
evening. The first time, since it happened."

I felt I understood how large that small change was for him.

---

"…You seem better lately," I said.

"…I've gotten used to it," he said. "A few months ago, every day felt
unfamiliar. Now it's just my life."

"…Standing in line, too?"

"…That too," he said, smiling a little. "It'd feel stranger now, not to
stand in it."

I found that both bitter and, in its way, fortunate.

---

We walked a while without speaking.

"…There's something I keep thinking about," Lytron said, suddenly.

"…What?"

He stopped walking. I stopped too.

"…Do you remember," he said, "the first time we met, something I said?"

I searched my memory.

"…You asked why I kept coming out to a place with nothing here."

"…Not that," he said. "After that. Something I said, not to you — to
myself."

---

I thought a moment, and remembered.

"…I haven't done anything wrong," I said. "That's what you said."

Lytron nodded.

"…Yes," he said. "I meant it, truly, when I said it then. And I still
don't think those words were wrong. I really hadn't done anything
wrong."

I watched him quietly.

"…And?" I asked.

He looked off toward the field. The corner was faintly visible, far
off.

"…I hadn't done anything wrong," he said again. A different voice, this
time.

"…So why wasn't that enough?"

The silence stretched. Wind swept through the stubble. He wasn't waiting
for an answer. It was the face of a man who had simply set the question
up in the open air. I felt an urge to say something, anything —
something comforting, some plausible reason. I held it back. I knew,
somehow, that such words had no place here.

---

I had no answer to that question.

*I understood, in that moment, that he had pulled his own sentence back
out and set it before himself for the first time. I remembered the day
I'd first heard those words. Then, he'd stood surrounded by his own
house, his own room, the things he'd had since birth. His world was
complete, then. Those words had been the door that closed to protect
that complete world. Now he was asking the same words as a question. The
words were identical. The direction had changed entirely. Then, they
closed a door. Now, they knocked on one.*

"…What do you mean, asking that?" I said, carefully.

"…I hadn't done anything wrong," he said, slowly. "From the day I was
born until now, I kept every rule the Roll set. I paid my taxes, I never
harmed anyone, I tried to give back as much as I received. And yet—"

He paused.

"…And yet something was lacking," he said. "I didn't know what, for a
long time. I thought not having done anything wrong and being enough
were the same thing."

---

I felt I ought to say something back. Nothing came.

*I set this moment down more carefully than anything else in this book.
Lytron wasn't asking me for an answer that day. That's how it felt. He
was only speaking aloud, for the first time, a question that had circled
inside him a long while.*

"…I don't know," I finally managed. "I don't know the answer either."

He looked at me. He didn't look disappointed.

"…It's fine," he said. "I wasn't hoping for an answer, either."

---

"…Then why did you say it to me?" I asked.

Lytron thought for a moment.

"…Because you're the only person I could say it to," he said.

"…What do you mean?"

"…You never once asked anything of me," he said. "That surprised me,
at first. People usually want something, especially from someone with
what I have. You never did. You just came out here, every day."

I didn't know what to say to that.

"…I thought that was a strangely honest way to be," he said. "So I
think I can speak honestly to you, too. Even without an answer."

---

We started walking again.

"…I keep thinking about that question lately," he said. "Why wasn't
that enough, when I hadn't done anything wrong. The more I try to find
an answer, the more certain I become of one thing only — that it's a
question with no answer."

"…Doesn't that make it harder?" I asked.

"…It's hard," he admitted, plainly. "But it's a different kind of hard
than before, strangely."

"…Different how?"

He stopped walking again to think.

"…Before, when something felt wrong, it felt like there was something
to fix," he said. "Try harder, keep more rules, and it would resolve.
This time it isn't that. I don't need to do anything better. I just—"

He stopped again.

---

"…Just what?" I asked.

"…There's some way to be all right, being not enough," he said. "I
think that's what I had to learn. But I still don't know exactly what
that way is."

Something went cold in me at that.

*I thought, that day, that Lytron was searching for an answer. Looking
back now, he wasn't searching for an answer. He was learning to bear a
question. Those are two entirely different things.*

"…I can't give you an answer," I said again. "Really."

"…I know," he said. "But I wanted to ask, anyway. Anyone."

---

We started walking again.

"…I see the Roll differently, these days," he said, suddenly.

"…How?"

"…I used to think the Roll was fair," he said. "Cut for wrongdoing,
raised for doing right. I believed it was exact. And I believed exact
was the same as good."

"…And now?"

"…I still don't think the Roll is inexact," he said. "It really is
exact. It's never once been wrong. But now I know. Exact and enough are
different things."

I thought that summed up this entire city.

"…What do you mean?" I asked.

"…The Roll knew, precisely, that I hadn't done anything wrong," he
said. "That was my score. An exact score. And that exact score wasn't
enough to register you. The Roll calculated that lack precisely too. The
Roll was never wrong about anything. There was simply a place where
exact wasn't enough."

*I thought a long time about that. I felt, for the first time that day,
that Lytron was looking more deeply into this city's rules than I had.
He had lived his whole life inside those rules, and now, from within
them, he was seeing their limit.*

---

The evening light stretched long across the field.

"…There's one thing I can say," I offered, carefully.

"…What?"

"…Hearing that question," I said, "I like you better now than the day
you first said those words."

"…Why?"

"…Then, those words closed a door," I said. "Now they sound like they're
opening one. I don't know which door. But something that was shut is
open now. That much I think is true."

Lytron said nothing to that. He only looked out over the field for a
long while.

We parted that day with no answer to that question.

---

I told Pikra about it that night.

Pikra stopped trimming vegetables and sat with it a while.

"…He's finally asked it," Pikra said.

"…You knew he was carrying that question?"

"…I guessed," Pikra said. "Everyone who's given up something of their
own meets that question, once. I met it too."

I was surprised.

"…You did too?"

"…I asked it differently than he did," Pikra said. "I asked, what did I
do wrong. After I lost my husband and my sons. He asks, I did nothing
wrong, so why. The direction's opposite. But it's the same place the
question comes from."

"…Same place?"

"…The place where you've met something you can't understand," Pikra
said. "I lost without doing wrong. He was lacking without doing wrong.
Neither one resolves with reason."

*I saw, hearing that, that the two questions faced each other. Pikra had
been wrongly deprived; Lytron had rightfully possessed. And yet both of
them, in the end, stood before the same wall. The wall where the
question *why* gives no answer.*

"…Did you find an answer to that question?" I asked.

Pikra shook her head.

"…No," Pikra said. "I never found it. I found something else,
instead."

"…What?"

"…Someone to sit with," Pikra said. "The answer never came. But a
person came. Like you."

---

A few days later, I told "him" about it too.

He was moving his hands at the corner of the field when I told him. He
stopped.

"…Lytron asked something like that," I said. "He hadn't done anything
wrong, and asked why that wasn't enough."

He said nothing for a long while. He only looked down at the few
stalks in his hand.

"…You didn't give him an answer?" he finally asked. I couldn't tell if
he was asking me, or asking himself.

"…No. I didn't know one either."

"…Good," he said.

I found that strange, hearing it.

"…Why good? I didn't help him at all."

He looked at me. Something was different in his face from usual. I
didn't want to call it sorrow. It was something older than sorrow.
Something settled deeper down.

"…Some questions," he said, "are better shared than answered."

He said nothing more after that. I asked nothing more either.

*I remembered his face a long time, that day. I didn't know it then.
That the question may not have been unfamiliar to him, either. I only
watched him, quietly moving his hands, as he always did.*

---

*I felt the temptation, more than once, writing this chapter, to write
in an answer. Anyone reading this book will want to know it, too. Why
wasn't it enough, when he'd done nothing wrong. I could have written
that I know. I decided not to. Lytron didn't find that answer that day,
and neither did I. This record isn't only a record of what we found. It
is also, honestly, a record of what we never did.*

I told Mena about it, the next day. She had nearly finished packing to
leave.

"…Lytron asked something strange," I said. "That he'd done nothing
wrong, so why wasn't it enough."

Mena stopped and looked at me. It was the face of someone hearing a
question she'd known a long time.

"…What did you say back?" she asked.

"…Nothing. I had no answer."

"…I actually wanted to make something up," I added. "A few plausible
things came to mind. Comforting words. But that would have been me
pretending to know what I didn't. So I held it back."

Mena nodded.

"…Good," she said. He had said the same thing.

"…Why does everyone say that?" I asked. A little frustrated. "Why is not
answering the good thing to do?"

Mena went on sorting her things as she answered.

"…I've traveled through many cities," she said. "I've seen many people
ask that question. And I've seen many people rush to answer it. A hasty
answer is always wrong. Because that isn't what the person actually
wanted to hear."

"…Then what was it?"

"…Confirmation that their question wasn't strange," Mena said. "You
gave him that. That was enough."

*I felt some relief, hearing that. I had believed, until that day, that
I'd given him nothing at all. Mena told me that wasn't true. I wanted to
believe her.*

---

*I know only one thing for certain. The day he first spoke that question
aloud, his face looked at ease. Not the face of a man who'd found an
answer. The face of a man who no longer had to carry the question
alone.*
