# Chapter 44. What the Names Mean

> _Previously_ — After the loss rate changed to 3 percent, grain began,
> for the first time, to be left at the corner. Those without standing
> gathered there. I no longer needed to glean, and kept going anyway.
> Pikra said she didn't know what my name meant, either. "Mena would
> probably know."

It was a few days before Mena left.

I went to see her that day. Not for any particular reason. I knew she
would soon be leaving this city, and I wanted to see her once more
before she went.

She was in her room, sorting through papers. Months of records had
piled up. I looked at that stack of paper and felt freshly surprised. I
hadn't known she'd written so much.

The room was quiet. Late afternoon light came through the window. Only
the sound of pages turning, now and then a sound of her setting one
aside. I liked that sound. I hadn't sat in a room that quiet often,
since coming to this city.

"…Getting ready to leave?" I asked.

"…Yes," Mena said. Her hands kept moving.

I sat beside her. For a long while I said nothing, just watched her
sort the papers.

---

"…I wanted to ask you something," I said finally.

"…What is it."

"…My name," I said. "Pikra said you'd know the meaning."

Mena stopped her hands. She looked at me. There was something in that
look. I couldn't have named it, then.

"…I know it," Mena said. "Why are you asking now?"

"…No reason," I said. "I was just curious."

Mena looked at me a moment longer, then set the papers aside.

"…Sit down," she said. "This is something to hear properly seated."

---

I settled myself.

"…Xene," Mena said. "This name comes from Greek. ξένη. *Xenē.*"

"…What does it mean?"

"…Stranger," Mena said. "A foreigner. Someone who doesn't belong to
this city."

I nodded, hearing that. It didn't surprise me. I really had been a
stranger. I'd come from the outer settlement; I had never belonged to
this city.

"…That sounds right," I said. "I really was a stranger."

Mena looked at me steadily.

"…But that isn't all of it," she said.

---

"…What do you mean, not all of it?"

"…The same word," Mena said, "carries another meaning, at the same
time. Guest. That's what it means, too."

I didn't understand at first.

"…Stranger and guest at once? How can that be the same word?"

"…To people of the old world, it was the same word," Mena said, slowly.
"When a stranger came to your door, there was an obligation to take them
into your house without knowing who they were. They called it *xenia.*
The taking-in of a traveler. It was a sacred obligation. You could not
turn a stranger away, because—"

Mena paused for a moment.

"…Because a god might come to you in the shape of a traveler."

I sat a long while without a word, after hearing that. Mena didn't
press. She seemed to know exactly how to hold a silence like that.

"…So those people," I finally managed, "weren't afraid of strangers,
but careful with them? Was that because—"

"…Yes," Mena said. "Because you never knew. The one standing at your
door might be nothing but a passing traveler. Or he might not be. There
was no way to be sure. So they treated everyone the same way."

"…Wouldn't it have been easier to simply turn away anyone you couldn't
be sure of?"

"…It would have," Mena said. "But those people chose not to live that
way. Since there was no way to be sure, they chose to take everyone in
instead. That's xenia."

---

I said nothing for a long while after hearing that.

*I remember that moment exactly, even now. The air in the room, the
smell of the paper, the way Mena's voice dropped a little lower. I
couldn't move from where I sat.*

"…So," Mena went on, "your name says two things at once. You were a
stranger. Someone who didn't belong to this city. Someone with no
standing. And at the same time—"

"…A guest," I managed. Barely.

"…Yes," Mena said. "Someone who must be received. Someone the receiver
is obligated toward."

I couldn't speak for a long time.

---

*I had lived sixteen years knowing this name only as my own name. I
never knew it held the meaning of stranger. And once I knew, something
struck me strangely. I had been a stranger from the very first day I
arrived in this city. From the moment I was classified as zero standing
at the gate. And that strangeness had, at the same time, been an
obligation someone else owed — to receive me. I only learned that, that
day.*

"…Then," I asked, "the people who took me in — Pikra, Lytron, the
administrator — was it obligation, for them?"

Mena shook her head.

"…For some it was obligation. For some it wasn't," Mena said. "Pikra
was more than obligation. She took you in as family. Lytron —"

Mena thought for a moment.

"…Lytron is a different matter," she said.

---

"…What do you mean?"

Mena looked at me.

"…I'll tell you that name too, now," she said. "You'll need to know it
eventually, and I think now is the time."

I held my breath.

"…Lytron," Mena said. "λύτρον. Greek."

"…What does it mean?"

"…The price paid to get someone back," Mena said. "Ransom, is another
way it's translated. The price paid to buy back someone, or something."

---

I couldn't breathe, hearing that.

*I couldn't say a single word, in that moment. Truly, not one. Mena
didn't press me. She simply sat and waited.*

I thought of how many times I had said his name. In the field, outside
the office, on sleepless nights. Every time I said it, I'd thought I was
simply saying a person's name. A sound. I never knew an answer had
already been sitting inside that sound.

*His name had already been saying, from the moment he was born, what he
would go on to do. The price paid to get someone back. He had lived out
his own name. Without ever knowing it himself.*

---

"…Isn't that strange?" I finally managed. My voice cracked. "How can a
name know something like that, in advance?"

Mena shook her head.

"…I don't know either," she said. "I'm not someone who explains things
like that. I'm only someone who writes them down. But I can say this. I
knew these names before I ever came to this city. And over these past
months, I watched them line up, exactly, with what actually happened
here."

"…Is it a coincidence?"

Mena looked at me for a moment. She didn't answer.

*I remember that silence exactly, even now. Mena wasn't someone who
judged. She never told me it was a coincidence, and never told me it
wasn't. She only left the fact where it was. The rest, I think, she left
for me to see for myself.*

---

"…Then why tell me now?" I asked. "You could have told me sooner."

Mena went back to sorting her papers as she spoke.

"…If you know the meaning first," she said, "you already know the
ending before the thing happens. If you'd known, ahead of time, that
Lytron would give up his standing, you wouldn't have been able to see
that moment the way you saw it."

"…How do you mean?"

"…You'd have watched it as someone living up to their name," Mena said.
"Like watching fate play itself out. I didn't want that for you. I
wanted you to see that moment as a person actually choosing something.
Not because a name told him to."

I had to sit a long while, turning that over.

*I know it now. Mena hadn't hidden a prophecy from me. She had given me
the chance to see a real choice. When Lytron signed that day, it wasn't
his name leading him. He was living up to what his name meant. The order
matters. I understand that order now, precisely.*

---

I said nothing for a long while.

Mena didn't rush me. She just kept sorting papers. The room held only
the sound of turning pages.

After a long while, I finally managed to ask.

"…My name too — did I live up to what it means?"

Mena stopped and looked at me.

"…Do you think you did?" she asked back.

I had no answer to that.

*I still haven't fully answered that question, even these many years
later. I was a stranger. And I was received. Both of those had been
inside my name from the start. I don't know, even now, whether I made
that happen, or whether I simply lived out what my name had always
meant.*

*I know only one thing. I never heard my own name the same way again,
after leaving Mena's room that day. Xene. A name that means stranger and
guest, both at once. Every time I say it now, I hear both, at the same
time.*

---

I left Mena's room and went straight to the field.

The whole way there, I wondered whether to tell Lytron. It felt like it
would be wrong to tell him. It also felt like it would be wrong not to.

He was tidying something near the corner. He looked up and saw me.

"…Something wrong?" he asked. He seemed to read it in my face at once.

"…I heard the meaning of my name, from Mena," I said. "My name, and—"

I stopped there.

"…Mine too?" he asked.

I nodded.

---

He was quiet for a moment.

"…What did she say?" he asked. His voice was quieter than usual.

"…The price paid to get someone back," I said. "Ransom."

Lytron stood a long time after hearing that. He didn't even set down
what was in his hands. He just stayed frozen in that posture.

"…Did you know?" I asked.

"…No," he said. "I had no idea."

I studied his face. It was surprised, but not the kind of surprise I'd
expected. Not fear, not the weight of a burden. Something quieter than
that.

---

"…That's strange," he said, finally.

"…What is?"

"…I told you, that day I signed," he said, "that I'd tell you the
reason later. Remember?"

"…Yes."

"…I still can't say the reason exactly, even now," he said. "I just
wanted to, in that moment. That was all of it. But hearing that my name
meant this, from the start—"

He stopped.

"…What is it," I asked.

"…I don't feel like I invented anything," he said. "I thought, that
day, that I'd made some great decision. Now I wonder whether I just did,
late, what my name had always meant to do."

---

I wanted to argue with that. I wanted to tell him his choice had been
truly his, not just his name playing itself out.

"…That was still you," I said. "The name didn't sign for you."

Lytron looked at me. And smiled, a little, for the first time.

"…I suppose so," he said. "Is that what Mena said? That the name didn't
make me do it?"

"…Yes. Something like that."

"…I think she's right," he said. "I didn't know my name. So the name
couldn't have led me. I just lived the way I lived, and later on, it
turned out the name had already been standing there."

I thought that was exactly right.

---

We stood together a while without speaking. An evening wind passed
through the field.

"…Aren't you curious about my name's meaning too?" I asked. Meant as a
joke.

"…I am," he said. Seriously.

I told him. Stranger and guest, at once.

He listened, and looked at me for a long time.

"…It suits you," he said.

"…What does?"

"…That you're a person living both those things at once," he said. "The
first time I saw you, I just thought you were a stranger. I wondered why
you kept coming out to a place with nothing in it. But now—"

He paused.

"…And now?"

"…Now you feel like the least strange person in this field," he said.

I laughed at that. It had been a long time since I'd laughed.

---

That night, back home, I told Pikra everything as well.

Pikra listened quietly. She was silent a long while, even after I'd
finished.

"…I should have asked about Mother's name too," I said. "While I was
with Mena."

"…Didn't you ask?"

"…You said you didn't want to know," I said.

Pikra smiled a little.

"…That's right, I did say that," Pikra said. "And I still feel the same
way. I want to hear my own name just as my own name. I don't need to
know what it means."

"…I was glad to know mine," I said. "Strange as that is."

"…You've always been that kind of child," Pikra said. "The kind who
wants to know. Sometimes I like that about you, and sometimes it
worries me."

I smiled a little at that.

"…And today?"

Pikra looked at me a long while before answering.

"…Today it's the good kind," Pikra said.

*I lay awake late into that night. Xene. Stranger and guest. Lytron. The
price paid to get someone back. I laid the two names side by side and
thought. One was the name of a person being received. The other was the
name of a person paying a price. I couldn't find an answer, that night,
to whether the two names had met by chance or not. I still can't find
it, now. I know only this. The two names really did meet. That isn't a
meaning on a page. It's something I watched happen with my own eyes.*
