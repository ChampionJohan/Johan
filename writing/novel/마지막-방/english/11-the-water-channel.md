# Chapter 11. The Water Channel

> _Previously_ — Mone gathered the five cities' records on one table and read them for the first time. Each city was heading, in its own way, toward something — but Loop's record had no such direction. It was only asking and answering, on and on.

I need to write properly about the water channel.

The channel I saw that first day, the color of soil, shallow, always making its sound. I'd walked past it every day since then. Usually, growing used to a thing means it stops being seen — but I was now looking at that water with different eyes than before.

*In this chapter I set down what I came to learn about the water channel. I'll say ahead of time — there are more questions than answers. This water channel is one of the last things in this whole record to remain fully unexplained.*

---

The channel runs behind the repository and out the far side of the city. I didn't know exactly where it began, or where it went. So one day I decided to walk along it.

I asked the Keeper for permission, and he nodded without much comment.

"…Going alone?"

"…Yes."

"…Don't go far. Be back before dark."

I kept to that, and walked exactly half a day.

---

Walking along the channel, I saw it passing all through the city. Behind the market, under the school's wall, through back alleys people rarely walked. Wherever it went, the water flowed at the same pace, making the same sound.

*I felt, for the first time that day, how this channel cut across the whole city. That beneath, or beside, the roads I walked every day, this water had always been flowing. I hadn't truly looked at it, in sixteen years.*

---

Reaching the edge of the city, the channel passed through a small sluice gate under the wall and went outside. I stood before that gate a long while and looked. It was the spot where the water left the city.

"…Arche, where does this water go?" I asked.

*The waterway beyond the sluice gate connects to a neighboring district; the route past that point is outside Loop's jurisdiction.*

"…Then how far does it go?"

*What can be confirmed extends only to Loop's boundary. Beyond that, it is not recorded.*

That answer left me disappointed, for the first time, that day. It wasn't that Arche didn't know — it was that this lay outside Arche's jurisdiction. It was the first time I'd heard that phrase, outside its jurisdiction. It felt unfamiliar, and I turned it over a long while.

---

I wanted to check the other direction too — where the channel entered the city. I went out again, another day, and walked that way.

The water flowed in from a hill on the far side of the city. I climbed that hill. From the top, I could see the channel winding in from far away, bending and turning. Even from there, I couldn't see all the way to where it began.

"…Arche, where does this water start?" I asked again.

*The source is presumed to lie outside Loop's boundary, but the exact point cannot be confirmed.*

---

I passed this along to the Keeper that day.

"…Arche doesn't know where the channel comes from, or where it goes, either."

The Keeper paused his hands and looked at me.

"…I know that too. I've asked before, myself."

"…Weren't you curious?"

"…I was. But I checked as far as I could get on foot. I couldn't go further than that."

"…Why not?"

"…There wasn't time. And—" The Keeper paused a moment. "…It wasn't something I had to know."

---

That sounded strange to me. "…Why aren't you curious? It's the water channel — something this important to our city."

"…It's important, true. But not everything important is something you have to know."

I turned that over. I think I understand it a little, now. I didn't understand it well then.

*This water channel comes back again, much further into this book. Someone, then, comes to tell me a little more about what this channel really is. For now I'll only set down that no one knew, exactly.*

---

I also learned that this channel had never once come up as a matter of city business. Somewhere like the Equity writes down every single thing in its minutes — but no one had ever once held a meeting about the water channel.

"…Arche, is there someone specifically in charge of this channel?" I asked.

*No designated managing party can be confirmed.*

"…Then who manages it?"

*This cannot be confirmed. However, the channel has continued flowing throughout the observable period, and has been maintained with no significant change.*

I found that remarkable. No one managed it, and the water kept flowing anyway.

---

I came to see the water channel differently, from that day. No mark on it anywhere, no owner anywhere, never once brought up as an item of business. And yet flowing every day, without a single day's rest.

*I felt, that day, that this channel resembled the way records arrived in this record. What came by hand, you could know who'd sent it. What came by the channel, you couldn't. And the channel itself — who had made it — no one knew that either.*

---

I asked Arche again.

"…Arche, why do all the records that come by this channel flow here?"

*By route, Loop appears to lie downstream. The waterways of the other cities merge into this one.*

"…Other cities' waterways? So all five cities' water comes here?"

*As far as can be confirmed, yes.*

I was a little startled by that answer. It meant that, by the flow of water, this city lay lowest of all. The water of five cities was all gathering into Loop. I thought that couldn't be a coincidence.

---

That evening I passed this on to the Keeper.

"…All five cities' water gathers here, apparently. Because we're the lowest point."

The Keeper nodded. "…I'd guess so."

"…So that's why the records come by the channel, too?"

"…Could be that too."

I knew that wasn't a full confirmation. But it felt like something fitting together, all the same. Just as water flows downward, records seemed to flow, gathering, toward somewhere too. I wrote that feeling briefly in my notebook.

---

Closing this chapter, I think back again on that moment standing before the sluice gate, looking down at the water. I never did find out exactly where that water came from, or where it went. But this much was clear — that water flowed cutting across the whole city, and that water carried something to this repository, every single day.

*I want to call this water channel the quietest character in this whole book. No one knew it exactly, and yet it was always there.*

---

Walking along the channel that day, I met an old man along the way. He was sitting by the water with no fishing rod, just watching it.

"…What are you doing here?" I asked.

"…Just watching the water. I come here when I'm bored."

"…Is it fun?"

The old man laughed. "…Not fun, exactly. But watching it puts my mind at ease."

I sat beside him a while. The water kept flowing, making the same sound. The old man said nothing, just watched the water. I found the silence strangely comfortable.

---

"…Grandfather, do you know where this water comes from?" I asked.

"…No. Wouldn't be any use even if I did."

"…Why no use?"

"…Knowing doesn't make the water flow any differently, does it."

That line stayed with me strangely. That knowing doesn't change anything. I wanted to argue against it, right then, but I didn't know exactly what to say back.

*Thinking about it now, the old man's words weren't entirely right. Knowing doesn't change the water — but it changes the one who knows. My eyes for looking at the water channel changed, from that day on. But I couldn't say that, in front of the old man.*

---

A little later I asked the old man a bit more. "…Have you lived here a long time, Grandfather?"

"…A very long time."

"…Has this channel ever changed, then?"

The old man thought a moment, then shook his head. "…Not that I know of. It's always flowed about this much. Not more, not less."

"…That's remarkable."

"…Nothing remarkable about it. That's just how it is."

I thought that answer sounded a lot like the Keeper's way of talking. Everyone in this city seemed to say much the same thing, faced with something strange. *That's just how it is.*

---

Walking home, I kept thinking about what the old man had said. That knowing was no use, and that it was just how things were. Both sounded like resignation, somehow, and yet not quite resignation. I couldn't put my finger on that difference precisely, at the time.

*I think I understand it a little now. Resignation is not trying to know any further. That old man, and the Keeper, weren't refusing to know any further. They simply knew how to get along fine, without knowing.*

---

A few days later, I went back to the channel. This time I didn't just walk — I studied the water closely. Small stones lined the bottom, and now and then a small fish passed by. I watched that little fish swim against the current for a long while, and then asked again.

"…Arche, does this water have fish living in it?"

*Confirmed. Several species of small fish are observed to inhabit it.*

"…Do they have names?"

*General names exist, but there is no record distinguishing individual fish.*

I found that answer, somehow, a little funny. It knew the kind of fish, but not how many lived there, or where they'd come from. Everything about this channel was like that. Known, to a point — but never known all the way.

---

That evening I told this to the Keeper, and asked,

"…Keeper, is this channel the strangest thing in this city?"

The Keeper thought a moment and answered,

"…It isn't the only strange thing here."

"…What else is there?"

The Keeper looked at me steadily and said,

"…You'll find out soon enough, yourself."

I had some vague sense of what he might mean, at that, but I didn't know exactly what he was pointing to. I didn't ask further, and just nodded. There was always this corner to the Keeper's way of speaking, foretelling something further ahead.

---

I should set down one last thing about this channel. From then on, every time I went to and from the repository, I heard that sound of water. At first it had just been background noise. Now it carried a different meaning to me.

*This water had come all the way here, past six cities. No one knew where it began, or who had made it. And yet it flowed without a single day's rest. I thought this water shared the same fate as the records that came to this city. Not knowing why it came — but coming, all the same, continually.*

---

After that, I came to love the small bridge crossing the channel. Walking home from the repository, I crossed that bridge. Stopping at its middle a moment to look down at the water became a habit.

One day, I happened to run into the Keeper on that bridge. He seemed to be on his way home too.

"…What are you doing here?" the Keeper asked.

"…Just watching the water."

The Keeper came and looked down beside me. "…I stop here sometimes too."

"…Why?"

"…I don't know either. I just end up stopping."

---

We stood on that bridge a long while that day, saying nothing. Only the sound of the water. I didn't find that silence awkward. If anything, it was comfortable.

"…Keeper." I broke the silence first. "…Aren't you curious where this water comes from? Really."

The Keeper looked at the water a moment, then answered.

"…I am curious. But being curious and having to know are different things too."

"…What's different about them?"

"…It means it's all right to leave it, curious. Turns out you can get by fine even without finding the answer."

I thought about that a long while. Getting by fine even without finding the answer. It wasn't giving up — it felt like a different kind of peace.

---

From that day, I called that bridge, to myself, "the curious bridge." Like the six-record table, this too was a name I gave and told no one.

*Writing this record, I realize afresh how many names I gave to things. To a table, to a bridge. I think I was trying, by naming things, not to lose them. A habit learned moving nine times, showing up here too, this way. Once I gave something a name, it stopped being something that just passed by, and became mine.*

---

At the end of this chapter, I want to make one thing clear. I never did find out, in the end, what this channel truly was. Even now, writing this book, I still don't fully know. But I do know what this channel was, to this city, and to me.

Something always flowing. Something that never stopped, even with no one managing it. And above all, something that carried things here, past five cities. That alone was enough to make me love this channel.

---

Writing this chapter, I picture myself again, standing before that sluice gate. The sixteen-year-old me turned back that day without finding an answer. The me now thinks it was a good thing, actually, that I didn't find the answer that day. If I'd known the answer beforehand, I don't think I'd have gone back to look at that channel so many times, after.

*Curiosity sometimes outlasts an answer. I learned that, thanks to this water channel.*

The water channel comes back, again and again, in many places in this record. Closing this chapter, I want to promise one thing ahead of time. I won't put off this channel's answer to the very end — I'll write it, in order, into this record, as I come to know it. For now, in this chapter, I think it's right to set down, simply, that I still knew nothing at all.

---

After that, I visited that bridge once more, each time the season changed. It wasn't exactly a habit I'd set for myself. My feet just went that way.

On rainy days the water swelled and its sound grew louder. On dry days it thinned, and more of the bottom stones showed. Even so, it never once ran dry. I found that remarkable, at first, and later just took it for granted. It didn't take long for the wonder to turn into the ordinary.

*I don't quite know whether that change was good or bad. Losing the eyes that find something wonderful is, in some sense, a loss. But I gained something else, in its place. An ear that could tell the water's sound was a little different, each time. I wanted to set that down in this record.*

Once, there was a day the water ran unusually clear. That day, for the first time, I saw the color of the bottom stones distinctly. Not the color of soil, but gray. I wrote that down in my notebook too. Knowing it was a nearly useless thing to record, I wrote it down anyway. If I didn't write it down, I felt I'd soon forget the color of the water, that day.
