# Chapter 23. Dates

> _Previously_ — Watching the way Histor handled the records, Mone
> felt clearly, for the first time, that he truly cherished them. But
> only as material. Believing means staking something, and he staked
> nothing. He couldn't answer that question either.

I am a person who puts things in order. That's my work, and it's my
habit.

So one day, looking again at the six scenes, it felt natural to want
to line up their dates. The flower bed, the end of the line, the
field, the valley, the water's edge, the front gate. I wanted to know
in what order these had happened.

This habit went back to my childhood. Moving nine times, I kept order
every time I packed a bag. Heavy things at the bottom, fragile things
in the middle. That habit now applied itself to paper, to stories too.
Anything out of order made me uncomfortable.

"…Can we find out when this happened?" I asked.

Histor shook his head.

"…Not exactly. Every city keeps a different calendar."

---

"…What do you mean, different calendars?"

"…It means the way a day passes in one city isn't the same as in
another. Some cities count days by the tides, some by sunrise. Some
count by something else entirely."

That sounded strange to me.

"…Cities within the same world, and they count days differently?"

"…Yes, that's how it is. Every city was founded under different
circumstances. So overlapping the six cities' records to line up dates
is, practically speaking, impossible."

---

I found that explanation disappointing.

"…So we'll never know which of the six scenes came first?"

"…Not never, but for now, we don't know. We can guess roughly at
order — from the numbers on the covers, or the condition of the paper.
But we can't get an exact date."

I couldn't let it go, even hearing that. That was always how I was. If
the order didn't line up, it kept bothering me.

"…I still want to try lining them up."

Histor looked at me and smiled a little.

"…That's so like you."

"…What do you mean?"

"…You're always trying to line things up in order. You were like that
from the first day I saw you. Shelves, bundles, paper. Now it's dates."

I felt a little embarrassed, hearing that. It must have shown that
much.

"…Is that strange?"

"…No. I envy it."

I was surprised at that. It was the first time he'd said he envied me.

"…What do you envy?"

"…I can't line up what I know in order. Even knowing everything, it's
all tangled up in me. You try to line up even what you don't know. I
find that remarkable."

I turned that over for a long time. *That was the first time I knew he
could envy me too. I'd always thought he was so much bigger than I
was. That thought shook a little that day.*

---

"…Is it pointless, then?" I asked.

"…Not pointless so much as there's no method. That every city keeps a
different calendar is already an established fact. Without a common
standard to get past that, we can't line up the dates."

"…What if there were a common standard?"

"…That would change things. But there isn't one."

I felt a little stubbornness rising in me, hearing that answer. It
wasn't that I wanted to do it more just because Histor had called it
impossible. I just found it hard to bear not being able to line things
up.

---

That whole afternoon I asked Arche about the six cities' calendars.
Arche explained each city's method of counting days in detail.

*Haven counts by the cycle of the seasons. The Reckoning counts by the
regular inspection cycle of the Scales. The Harvest counts by the
interval between sowing and reaping. The Preserve counts by the cycle
of visitation requests. The Equity counts by the cycle of
deliberation sessions. Loop counts by the rising and setting of the
sun.*

Writing down these six, my head began to throb. All different.
Completely different yardsticks.

I read the six aloud again. Seasons, inspection of the Scales, sowing
and reaping, visitation cycles, deliberation cycles, sunrise and
sunset. The more I read, the stranger it felt. Each city counted time
by whatever mattered most to it. Haven counted by rest, the Reckoning
by precision, the Harvest by the gathering in, the Preserve by
meeting, the Equity by judgment, and Loop, simply, by the sun. I
thought that resembled each city's own face.

"…That's interesting," I said.

"…What is?" Histor asked.

"…Each city counts time by what matters most to it. It feels like it
shows what each city values most."

Histor looked over the six again.

"…You're right. I hadn't thought of that."

I found it surprising that there was something he hadn't thought of.
He always seemed like someone who'd already thought of everything.

"…What do you count time by, Mone?" he suddenly asked.

I paused for a moment at that question.

"…I don't know. I think I count by the number of times I moved. By
what happened at which house."

"…That's interesting too. People might have their own private time,
just like cities."

I liked that answer. We each counted time by our own yardstick. I by
moving, the six cities each by their own. And now we were trying to
find a way to bind all those yardsticks into one.

I copied the six of them onto paper again, setting them in order as I
always did. Haven through Loop. I thought for a moment about ordering
them by the character of their standard rather than by city name, then
let it go. If I disturbed the order I was used to, I thought I'd get
confused later. When sorting something unfamiliar, it was better for
me to keep the familiar frame.

"…Why aren't you changing the order?" Histor asked, having noticed my
hesitation.

"…I thought I'd get confused if I did. I don't usually change an order
once it's set."

"…Is that a packing habit too?"

"…Probably."

He nodded at that answer. He didn't ask further.

---

"…Can we really not bring this together, any of it?" I asked Arche
again.

*Conversion is possible if a common standard exists. Currently, no
common time standard shared by all six cities has been confirmed.*

I felt a little discouraged, hearing that. Histor had been right.

But in that same moment, something occurred to me.

"…Arche, what about the water channel? What about the tide?"

*Please clarify the question.*

"…The tide in the water channel. The rise and fall of the water. The
water channel runs through all six cities. So wouldn't the tide be
something all six cities share?"

---

Arche took a short pause before answering.

*The tidal cycle of the water channel is a physical phenomenon,
recurring at identical intervals regardless of any city's calendar
system. I will confirm.*

My heart beat a little faster in that moment. Whenever Arche used the
word "confirm," it always carried weight. It meant a moment of stating
fact, not speculation.

*Confirmed. All six cities are adjacent to the water channel, and each
city's record contains at least one reference related to the tide.*

Histor rose from his seat, hearing that answer. His expression changed
for the first time that day.

I saw the Keeper pause while passing by just then. The Keeper seemed to
sense something was different from the two of our faces.

"…What's going on?" the Keeper asked.

"…The tide," I said. "…We might be able to use the water channel's
tide to line up the six cities' dates."

The Keeper was quiet for a moment. That expression was a little
different from usual. It looked both surprised and as though something
had already been known.

"…Hadn't thought of that," the Keeper finally said. "…I knew the
water channel belonged to no one in particular, but I never thought it
could measure time too."

"…Is that strange?" I asked.

"…Not so much strange as — something obvious that nobody had ever
pointed out. That happens sometimes."

The Keeper said that and walked off again. The steps seemed a little
faster than usual. *I didn't feel that clearly at the time. Thinking
back on it now, the Keeper's walk was definitely different that day.*

---

"…Mone." He spoke. "…Can you say that again?"

"…The tide. Since the water channel runs through all six cities, the
tide is something every city shares. So couldn't we use that as a
standard to convert the dates in all six records?"

He was quiet for a long time. I thought he'd raise an objection. He
always did.

"…Theoretically, it should be possible," he finally said. "…But it'll
take time. We'd have to find every tide reference in each record and
work backward from it."

"…Another problem?" I asked. I'd read something like a challenge
rising in his face.

"…There is one. We'd need to confirm whether the tide arrives
identically to every city first. The upper and lower reaches of the
water channel might not have the water arrive at the same time."

"…We can just ask Arche about that too."

"…Right. That's what we should ask first. If we don't confirm it
before starting, we'll have to redo everything later."

I could tell he was already looking for the corner that might be wrong
before we'd even started. *I found that tedious at the time. I know
now. It wasn't tedium — it was the order in which he worked. He always
checked the place he could be wrong, first.*

I wondered, in that moment, why no one had thought of this before. The
water channel had always been there. I passed by it myself every day.
And yet no one had ever thought to see it as a standard for time. Maybe
it had been too close to see. I'd thought the same thing before, on my
very first day at this repository — that what's always there goes
unseen.

"…Shall we try it?"

He looked at me. Something complicated crossed his expression. I
couldn't tell, at the time, whether it was fear or anticipation.

"…Histor," I asked carefully. "…Do you not want to do this?"

"…No, it's not that I don't want to."

"…Your face looks strange, though."

He lowered his eyes for a moment.

"…Honestly, I'm a little afraid."

I was surprised by that. It was the first time he'd said something
like that.

"…What are you afraid of?"

"…If the dates line up with this, I won't have anywhere left to
retreat to. I was always safe because there was always something
unconfirmed left. But this — this is a way of confirming it."

I couldn't say anything, hearing that. *I count this moment as the
most important in this chapter. For the first time, he admitted he was
afraid. The one who was always accurate and calm showed me his inner
self, for the first time.*
"…We'd have to check it eventually anyway."

I felt relief hearing that answer. And at the same time, a little
unease. If the dates really did line up, something big felt like it
was about to happen. *Writing this chapter now, I know that feeling
wasn't wrong. That day, though, I had no idea what shape it would
take.*

That evening we started searching each record for tide references. It
meant going back through all six books from the beginning again.
Histor was quieter than usual. I could tell he was feeling something,
but I didn't ask.

We found the first tide reference in Haven's record. A sentence about
the day the water at the well ran lowest. In the Reckoning's record, a
short line said the water channel overflowed on the day the Scales
were inspected. In the Harvest's record, the season for watering the
fields was written together with the tide. In the Preserve's record,
there was a passage about when the water seeped down into the valley;
in the Equity's record, right beside the sentence about seeing that
person sitting by the water, there was a phrase about the day the water
rose. In Loop's record, there was a passage about the ditch near the
front gate looking about to overflow.

I marked those six sentences one by one, half-doubting whether they
could really all connect. Histor took quiet notes beside me. His hand
was as precise as ever, but I noticed it had gotten just a little
faster.

"…You've sped up," I said.

"…Have I?"

"…Yes. Faster than usual."

He looked down at his own hand. He seemed not to have realized it
himself.

"…I don't know. I suppose I want to confirm this quickly."

I laughed a little, hearing that. Someone always so careful was
hurrying for the first time.

We kept searching for tide references in the six records late into the
night. The Keeper brought another lamp. Said it was fine to stay late.

"…Trying to finish it all tonight?" the Keeper asked.

"…No, but I wanted to find as much as I could," I answered.

The Keeper looked us over, back and forth, and said,

"…Don't rush too much. This isn't the kind of thing that goes faster
for being rushed."

Histor stopped his hand for a moment at that.

"…That's true," he said quietly.

I watched him deliberately slow his own pace. I think that was the
most striking scene of the day. *He always put accuracy first, and in
that moment, for once, he mastered his impatience before his accuracy.
I didn't know what that meant at the time. I understand a little now.
He was afraid of the answer, and precisely because of that fear, he
was trying not to be swept along by it.*

On the way home I passed the water channel and listened long to the
sound of the water. Now that I knew that sound had come all the way
through all six cities, it sounded different. This channel wasn't just
a channel. It was a single thread that had run through all six cities'
time.

On the way home I wrote in my paper. The water channel's tide could
become the common standard joining the six cities. Histor didn't
object. He was even the first to say we should try. Only, he said he
was afraid. Tomorrow we find every tide reference in all six records.

*I added one more line beside it. "Tomorrow, I think we'll come to
know something." That night I had no idea what it would be. Writing
this record now, I know. I leave that sentence exactly as it stands.
That's precisely what I felt that night.*
