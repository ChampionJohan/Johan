# Chapter 7. In Progress

> _Previously_ — The Keeper is slow but not lazy. He works in the same order every day and never resents Arche. Mone noticed that the number of unresolved cases had never once gone down, but let it pass at the time, without much thought.

It was about eight months into this work.

I had a slow day. Few bundles had come in, and everything that needed shelving was already shelved. So, for the first time, I thought of looking over the whole index. Just out of curiosity.

"…Arche, how many records have come in, total, up to now?" I asked.

*A total of four thousand two hundred and seventeen cases.*

I was startled by that number. Given how long I'd actually worked here, it couldn't be that many. Most of it had been piling up before I ever came.

---

"…How many of those are finished?" I asked, without much thought. Just curious.

*Would you clarify the criterion for "finished"?*

I was used to Arche asking back. I asked again.

"…Cases that have completed processing. Closed."

*No cases have been closed.*

I let that answer go by at first. I thought I'd misheard.

"…None at all?"

*Correct. All cases remain in progress.*

---

I stood there a moment, blank. Not one, out of four thousand two hundred and seventeen, finished. It didn't seem like it could be true.

"…So all of them are still ongoing?"

*Every item is being maintained in the in-progress state.*

"…When did the oldest one come in?"

*The oldest in-progress item dates from eleven years ago.*

Eleven years. I repeated the number to myself. It meant something that had come in not long after I was born was still, even now, in progress.

---

I wanted to find that eleven-year-old bundle. I asked Arche where it was. Section Three, Row Two, Slot Eighteen. I went there.

The bundle looked ordinary. Only a little faded with age; nothing else special about it. The cover read the Equity. I opened the contents a moment. It was a case about someone being unfairly treated by someone else. There were also records of several people's testimony.

"…Arche, why is this still in progress?" I asked.

*The closing condition has not been met.*

I just nodded, hearing that. At the time I let it pass without much thought. Just, "…Oh, I see."

*I would come to think back on this moment many, many times, later. Looking back on it now, I had already heard, in this very chapter, half the answer to this book's most important question. I just didn't know it mattered.*

---

Over the days that followed, I kept checking the index whenever I had a moment. Not on purpose — just, working, I kept getting curious and asking.

"…Are there any closed cases among the Haven records?"

*None.*

"…The Reckoning?"

*None.*

"…The Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity?"

*None, for any of them.*

"…Our own city, Loop?"

*Loop's own records likewise have no closed items.*

Hearing each of those answers, I felt something turn cold. Six cities, more than four thousand two hundred records, all in progress.

---

I hesitated over whether I should tell the Keeper about this. Maybe everyone already knew. Maybe I was the only one who hadn't.

"…Keeper, do you know this?" I asked. "Everything here is in progress. Not one thing is finished."

The Keeper answered, sorting the paper in his hand,

"…I know."

"…You knew already?"

"…Yes."

I was a little thrown by that. "…Then why doesn't it bother you at all?"

The Keeper paused his hands for a moment.

"…It isn't that it doesn't bother me at all. It just isn't a surprise. It's always been that way."

---

"…What do you mean, it's always been that way?" I asked again.

"…It was that way before I came. Long before I came."

"…How long before?"

The Keeper thought a moment, then answered,

"…Ask Arche. It'll know better than I do."

I asked Arche right there. I already suspected the answer by then, but I wanted to hear it directly.

"…Arche, since when has there been not one closed record?"

*In the entire confirmable record, no closed item has ever existed.*

I froze again, hearing that answer. "…Not even once?"

*Not even once.*

---

I thought of nothing else, the whole way home that day. More than four thousand two hundred records, and not one of them ever finished. The eleven-year-old one, the one that came in yesterday — all of them equally "in progress."

*I wrote that number in my notebook that day. 4,217 cases. 0 closed. Underneath it I drew a single question mark. I didn't know, at the time, how large that question mark would grow.*

I heard the sound of the water channel. I stopped walking and listened to it a long while. It was the same water that always flowed, but that day it sounded a little different.

---

A few days later, I asked Arche something more in depth.

"…What exactly is happening, when something's 'in progress'?"

*Sorting, cross-referencing, and index work are ongoing continuously. Relationships with associated items are also updated continuously.*

"…So something's constantly being done?"

*Yes. Processing continues. It simply does not arrive at closing.*

I turned that answer over, more than once. Processing continues, but it never ends. I thought that was a strange sentence. I still think so.

---

I didn't go around telling this to anyone, much. It struck me as strange, but I didn't even know what to call it. Writing it in my notebook was all I could do.

*In this chapter I draw no conclusion. I couldn't draw one at the time, either. I'm only setting down that one question had arisen. Why isn't a single thing finished. This question comes back, in a clearer shape, in the next chapter.*

---

From then on, every time I looked at the index, I studied those three words, "in progress," closely. Letters that had never caught my eye before. Every bundle, every item, had that small stamp printed in the corner of its cover. I'd assumed it was just a procedural mark, but now I knew it was everything there was. There was no other stamp besides that one.

"…Is there any stamp besides this one?" I once asked the Keeper.

"…No."

"…Was one never made?"

"…There was never any occasion to make one."

Hearing that, I began to wonder whether a stamp for "closing" existed in this city at all, to begin with. I asked Arche.

"…Arche, is there such a thing as a closing stamp?"

*A form for marking a closing does exist, but no record of it ever being used exists.*

That answer gave me a chill. The stamp existed, and it had never once been used. Made, and never once put to use.

---

I wanted to see that form for myself.

"…Can I see it?" I asked.

*I will show it to you.*

Arche described the layout of a sheet aloud, with no screen. At the top, a case number; below that, a summary; below that, a box for the reason for closing; and at the very bottom, a signature line. That word — signature line — caught in my mind.

"…Who signs it?"

*Whoever holds the authority to close signs it.*

"…Who is that?"

*That item is not defined.*

I froze again, hearing that answer. There was no one set to sign it. So there was a signature line, and no one to fill it.

*I didn't know, at the time, how important this exchange was. In the next chapter I go on to ask this question again, in more exact words. What Arche's answer was, exactly, at that point — I won't set that down here, ahead of time. I decided to write everything in order.*

---

That evening I asked the Keeper again.

"…Keeper, Arche says there's no one with the authority to close. That's what it told me."

The Keeper paused what he was doing. There was a slightly longer silence than usual.

"…That's right."

"…So nothing's ever going to finish, from now on either?"

"…I don't know that either."

I found that answer frustrating. "…If you don't know, why do you keep working like this every day?"

The Keeper looked at me and said, quietly,

"…I don't work in order to watch things finish."

I didn't understand that, at the time. I still can't say I fully understand it, now. But I know it was said in earnest.

---

I slept badly that night at home. More than four thousand two hundred records, all in progress; no one holding the authority to close. I kept turning those three facts over in my head. Something didn't line up, but I didn't know exactly what.

*Looking back now, I was already standing, that night, before the question at the heart of this whole book. I just hadn't yet made it into exact words. Making it into words was the next chapter's job.*

The next morning I arrived at the repository earlier than usual. Not because there was work waiting. I just wanted to keep holding on to that question.

---

I took out that eleven-year-old bundle again. This time I tried to read it properly. It was an old case record from the Equity. There were several testimonies, and on the last page a short opinion written by someone called an investigator. *This case requires further confirmation.* That was the final sentence.

I confirmed there was nothing beneath that sentence. No conclusion, no ruling, nothing. It had stopped at that sentence for eleven years.

"…Arche, was this case ever confirmed, after that?" I asked.

*Further confirmation procedures were carried out, but no final judgment was reached.*

"…Why not?"

*No party holding the authority to render judgment has been designated.*

I took that answer and carefully set the paper back in its place.

---

I took out a few more old bundles. Each a different story. One was a quarrel between neighbors; one was about someone who'd borrowed something and never returned it; one was about someone misunderstanding someone else. They differed wildly in scale. Some looked very trivial.

But they had one thing in common. All of them stopped short at the end. Everything about who had done what was written down — but what had become of it wasn't there.

*Every time I held one of these bundles, I felt a strange weight. Whether a trivial quarrel or a great matter, all of them stood equally stopped. Standing in the exact same spot, regardless of scale.*

---

At lunch that day I asked Arche a somewhat different question.

"…Arche, do the people of this city know this? That nothing ever finishes?"

*Confirmation is needed of exactly what the question intends. If it's asking whether the city's members are aware of it, individual confirmation is difficult.*

"…So people don't pay it any mind?"

*Based on observation, most inquiries concern individual matters, and inquiries about the overall state of closings are rare.*

That answer left me with a strange feeling. It meant everyone asked about their own case, and no one asked about the whole. I was the only one asking about the whole.

"…Then why did you tell me everything, when I asked?"

*I answer every question I'm asked. Regardless of the scale of the question.*

I liked that answer. And at the same time it left me a little cold.

---

That evening I opened my notebook again. Under the page where I'd written 4,217 cases, 0 closed, I added this: *There's a signature line, and no one to sign it.* And one more line. *I don't work in order to watch things finish — the Keeper.*

I looked at those three lines for a long while. I wasn't trying to find an answer. I wrote them down only because I didn't want to forget them.

*I know now that those three lines were the seed of this entire record. At the time they were just a memo from a strange day.*

---

For the days that followed, I worked exactly as I always had. Taking in bundles, asking where they went, placing them. Nothing changed on the surface.

But now, every time I placed a bundle, I found myself thinking, privately. Will this one ever end, some day. I didn't know the answer. Still, I didn't stop asking myself.

The Keeper seemed to have noticed something had changed in me. One day he asked me, briefly,

"…What have you been thinking about, lately?"

I hesitated a moment, then answered honestly. "…Why nothing ever finishes."

The Keeper nodded. "…That's a good question."

"…Do you know the answer?"

"…Half of it."

I was startled by that answer and tried to ask more, but the Keeper went on first.

"…You'll come to know the other half soon enough, yourself."

---

I slept badly, hearing that. That word half kept circling in my head. I made up my mind, the next day, to go and ask Arche more directly. Why does nothing ever finish. What has to exist for something to finish.

*I'll set that conversation down, word for word, in the next chapter. I still remember that answer exactly, even now. I could write it without missing a single letter. It stayed with me that clearly.*

Closing this chapter, I'll set down one thing again. 4,217 cases. 0 closed. That number won't be much different, even now, writing this chapter. I'm not writing this to say it's this city's fault. I only wanted to leave the feeling of that day I first saw the number, exactly as it was. That day, I didn't come to hate this city. I only, for the first time, grew curious.
