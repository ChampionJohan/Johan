# Chapter 27. Pheugon and Trope

> _Previously_ — Zeteo and Elpis came and went. Zeteo still said he
> hadn't received an answer, but left with the words "I think someone's
> listening." Elpis said she was still waiting. Both left unchanged.

The two who came from the Reckoning arrived separately.

The man came first. He looked a little older than Zeteo. He held a single sheet of paper in his hand and kept looking down at it as he walked. He wasn't reading aloud, but his lips moved a little.

"…What are you reading?" I asked.

He looked up.

"…A sentence. One I used to read, a long time ago."

"…You still have it?"

"…Yes. I can't throw it away. It was my work."

The Keeper recognized him from the side.

"…Pheugon."

"…Yes." He answered shortly.

---

Pheugon said he still worked reading sentences somewhere, even now. Not at the Reckoning. He went around the smaller cities, reading sentences aloud for people who needed it, in place of the people the sentences belonged to.

"…Someone assigned you this?" I asked.

"…No. I offered. I read the ones nobody else wants to read."

"…Isn't that hard?"

"…It is. But if I don't read them, nobody will."

That struck me as odd. In the Reckoning's record, he'd been someone who fled from sentences. And now he went looking for them himself. I asked again, carefully, trying not to let this sound like a conversion.

"…You used to run from it," I said, carefully.

Pheugon looked at me. He didn't look angry.

"…That's right. I ran. Afraid the city would be forgiven. It's different now. Now… I know there's such a thing as mercy, so I decided to become someone who reads it aloud for others."

"…Is all the anger gone?"

Pheugon was quiet for a moment before answering.

"…No. It still comes, sometimes. But I read anyway, even when I'm angry. That's what's changed."

Histor heard that and asked,

"…Why does a sentence need to be read aloud? Can't the Scales just announce the result?"

"…No. That's how our city's provision works. A sentence only takes effect when a person reads it aloud. Arche can't do it alone, and neither can the Scales."

"…Why is that?"

Pheugon shrugged.

"…I don't know. Only whoever wrote the provision knows that. But lately I think I understand a little why."

"…Why?"

"…Because if there's someone reading, you know there's someone listening. If the Scales just spits out a result on its own, nobody's listening."

I wrote that down. Histor seemed to be writing it too.

Pheugon looked down again at the sentence in his hand and added,

"…Honestly, at first it was just habit. I'd trained a long time at reading aloud. But lately it's different. I feel like what I'm reading actually reaches someone. I never used to feel that."

"…What changed?" Histor asked.

"…I don't know. Maybe I changed. Or maybe I'm only just now listening."

He didn't explain further than that. I didn't press him either.

---

Trope arrived alone, later that day. Her steps were quiet. She stood there without knocking, so I went out first to see who it was.

"…Who is it?"

"…Trope. From the Reckoning."

The moment Histor heard the name, he came over. He remembered Trope's record precisely.

"…You said, 'Mercy might exist. But it won't come for me.'"

Trope looked a little startled.

"…Even that's written down?"

"…Yes. Word for word."

Trope said nothing for a while, hearing that. I couldn't tell whether she was uncomfortable or not.

"…Do you still think that?" Histor asked.

Trope answered slowly.

"…No. Now I'm… not sure. I'm less certain than I used to be. Whether it could come for me too or not."

"…So which side are you on now?"

"…Neither. I just don't know. But it used to be 'it won't,' and now it's 'I don't know.' That's a fairly big difference for me."

---

Hearing that, I felt how careful a sentence it was. She wasn't certain. She wasn't declaring anything either. She'd only said that one old certainty had shaken.

*I read this conversation over many times. Trope hadn't converted. She
didn't say "I believe now." She said only that "it won't" had become
"I don't know" — that one step, and nothing more. I liked that
honesty.*

"…What changed it?" I asked.

Trope thought for a moment.

"…It's not one thing. It's several. That the Scales weighed all of me and still didn't push me away. That no one beside me ever once got angry. And… I'm not sure myself. Just — time passed."

"…Time passing did it?"

"…Yes. I thought it would take some huge event to break a certainty like that. But I just dulled, a little at a time, every day. My certainty did. I don't mean that badly."

I liked that word. Not breaking, but dulling. I copied that word straight into my notebook.

Pheugon listened beside her. He wasn't surprised.

"…That's how it was back then too. Trope doesn't change her certainties easily. When she does change, it's in very small steps."

"…Do you two know each other?" I asked.

"…Same city, so. We run into each other now, at the Reckoning."

Trope nodded at that.

"…Whenever he comes to read sentences aloud, we cross paths. I don't run from it anymore."

"…You used to run?" I asked.

"…Yes. The first time Pheugon came back to the Reckoning and read a sentence out loud, I couldn't stand to be in the room. Not out of fear — I was afraid my name would be called."

"…Was it?"

"…No. It wasn't called. But for a while after that I still kept avoiding those days. It's only lately that I can even stay."

Hearing that, I felt I vaguely understood that a certainty changing isn't one moment, but many repeated moments of just barely managing to stay.

---

Trope looked slowly around the repository. The shelves, the index, even the corner where Arche's voice came from.

"…Is this place something like the Scales too?" she asked.

"…No. We don't judge here. We just lay things down."

"…No judging?"

"…No. What Arche can do is classify and compare. It doesn't rate right and wrong."

Trope turned that over.

"…The Scales weighed. Precisely. And still I don't think that explained all of me."

"…How is the Adjudicator?" I asked. It was a name I'd seen in the record.

"…He's well. Still at the Reckoning. Lately he talks about the Forty-Day Provision more often than before. He used to say it was shameful that he never once invoked it, his whole life."

"…And now?"

"…Now he doesn't call it shameful, just says it as fact. 'I didn't see it then.' That's all. He blames himself a little less."

Pheugon added, from the side,

"…The Adjudicator's actually been re-learning how to read sentences aloud from me, lately. Says his voice doesn't carry the way it used to, now that he's older."

"…The Adjudicator reads them himself?" Histor asked.

"…Yes. In our city that's not just assumed anymore. Used to be, they'd have someone like me do it. Now the adults read them themselves too."

"…What does that mean?"

"…What the Scales weighed was something I did — it's not me. For a long time I thought those two things were the same. That's how I got so certain 'mercy won't come for me.' Because what I'd done was who I was."

"…And now?"

"…Now I think those two are a little different. I don't know yet how different."

---

It was after that I took Trope toward the water channel. Same as with Mena, I wanted to show her for no particular reason.

Trope stopped walking when she heard the water.

"…This is similar to the well in our city."

"…They might be connected. I don't know for sure."

"…The Reckoning's well didn't exist originally either. Not until Speon came."

"…You know Speon?"

"…Only by rumor. Pheugon mentions the name a lot, talking about the water channel."

Trope sat down by the water. After a long silence, she asked,

"…What the Scales can't do — can that water do it?"

"…What do you mean?"

"…The Scales only weighs. It can't measure direction. But that water… it just flows. It's not decided where it goes, and still it keeps going."

I had no answer for that. I just sat beside Trope and watched the water.

After a while Trope asked,

"…Does he come here too?"

I knew right away who she meant. Histor was the one who asked.

"…Which person?"

"…The one who used to be in the Reckoning. The one who knew before the Scales did, and never once got angry. Ask Pheugon — he'd know."

Pheugon heard that and came closer to the water.

"…I haven't seen him since I came here. But on the way here I heard something odd. That people in other cities have seen someone like that too."

"…The same person?" I asked.

"…Can't say. Everyone describes him differently. Some say young, some say old. But strangely everyone says the same thing — that he never once got angry."

Trope nodded.

"…I'm not curious who he is. I just think… that he existed matters."

I just listened to that conversation quietly. Histor said nothing either. Neither of us asked further.

*Writing this part, I made a deliberate choice not to press further.
Neither Trope nor Pheugon knew more than that. Not because they didn't
know that they weren't pressing — I think it was simply a place that
didn't need pressing.*

---

Pheugon asked Arche something.

"…Is there any misquotation among the Scales's case law?"

*A misquoted instance of Mena's record has been confirmed within the
Reckoning's case-law database.*

"…Has it been corrected?"

*That item is in progress.*

Pheugon nodded. No surprise showed on his face.

"…I expected that, coming here. That nothing just finishes."

"…Aren't you angry? The evidence you carried in is still in progress."

"…I would have been, once. Now… I know that in progress is different from not existing at all. Mena herself came through here too, I heard."

"…How do you know?"

"…Elpis sent me a letter. News travels fast these days."

---

Before evening, Trope asked one last thing.

"…If I come here, can I check on mine too? Anything under my name?"

I asked Arche. A few records came up classified under Trope's name. Most were the Reckoning's deliberation records. One of them had "in progress" attached.

Trope looked at that item for a long time.

"…This is me? A decision about me?"

"…Something like that. I'm not sure exactly. You'd have to ask Arche to know precisely."

*That item is in progress.*

"…How long?" Trope asked.

*In progress for seven years.*

Trope laughed hearing that number. A sad laugh. I guessed it was probably close to the laugh she used to wear at the Reckoning, back then. Of course, I never saw that time myself. I only know it from the record.

"…Seven years I've waited, and it's still in progress."

Pheugon said, low, from the side,

"…That's not bad news. It means it hasn't ended yet."

Trope didn't answer right away. After a while, she nodded.

"…Maybe."

The two left together that night. I watched their backs, walking side by side, and found myself wondering what that "in progress" would someday become.

Before leaving, Trope turned back to me once more.

"…Ms. Mone."

"…Yes."

"…Do you know what my name means?"

"…No. Can I ask?"

Trope smiled.

"…I don't know either. Nobody ever told me. But somehow I think it's a name that suits me. I'd like it to mean turning back."

Hearing that, my heart beat a little faster. I already knew. Trope's meaning is τροπή — turning, a turning back. Histor had told me, before. But I didn't say anything, right there, right then. It didn't feel like the time yet.

"…I think it suits you," was all I said.

Pheugon laughed beside her.

"…Let's go, Trope. There's another sentence to read."

Trope nodded. The two walked out. Pheugon's hand still held that old worn sentence.

The Keeper spoke as he closed the door.

"…Those two were the strangest pair in the Reckoning. Seeing them now, they fit."

"…They were strange?" I asked.

"…The one who ran and the one who couldn't. Strange, right? But maybe the two of them met and each got a little less strange."

I wrote that phrase at the back of my notebook. "A little less strange." It wasn't a precise expression, but it was the closest I could come to what I'd felt that day.

I read that sentence again that night and realized, freshly, that no one who'd come through this repository had ever become entirely un-strange. Only a little less strange. That didn't sound like a bad thing.

*Writing this part, I picture Pheugon's back as he walked away again.
He's still reading sentences aloud. He didn't stop. I thought that was
strange, at first, and then revised the thought — it isn't strange at
all.*
