# Chapter 26. Elpis and Zeteo

> _Previously_ — Mena came and went. She learned from me how to lay her
> own scattered records in order. As she left she said, "Others will
> come too." She didn't say who.

Three days after Mena left, the two of them came together.

The woman caught my eye first. Her step was light, and she kept glancing left and right as she walked. She looked like someone always searching for something, though it wasn't a hurried search. The man beside her was the opposite. His walk was straight, and he looked only ahead. Both wore plain clothes. Not wealthy-looking, not poor-looking either. Just the clothes of people who'd walked a long way.

The Keeper recognized them first, again.

"…Zeteo. Elpis."

The two turned their heads at the same time.

"…We came from Haven," the woman said first. Her voice was bright. "…We heard our records are kept here too."

"…Teacher Mena sent us a letter," Elpis added. "…She said you could learn to lay things down here. She said she'd learned it here herself."

Zeteo stood beside her without a word, looking around the repository. His eyes were sharp — not admiring, but checking. Like someone confirming what was being hidden.

"…Can everything here be looked into?" he asked.

"…Yes. Ask whatever you like," I answered.

"…Whatever?"

"…Yes."

Zeteo laughed shortly. It was the first expression he'd made, and it looked less like a laugh than a confirmation.

"…The Ledger said the same thing. Ask whatever you like and you'll get an answer. But the one thing that mattered most never came."

"…What mattered most?" I asked.

"…Why. Why it happened."

I had no idea how to answer that, so I stayed quiet.

Zeteo didn't scold my silence. He seemed to take the silence itself as one kind of answer. That, somehow, felt heavier to me.

---

I already knew both their names. Histor had spoken them often in Part Two. The children of Haven. The brother who didn't believe in God, and the sister who kept waiting for something.

Histor heard the news and came right out. The moment he saw Zeteo, he spoke.

"…You asked the Ledger why God allows suffering. You got an answer, and the answer was logically flawless — and still the weeping wouldn't stop."

Zeteo stared at Histor.

"…How do you know that?"

"…It's in the record. I've read all of it."

"…My record?"

"…Yes. That exact sentence. 'I received a flawless answer, and the weeping wouldn't stop.'"

Zeteo said nothing for a moment. It must have been strange, hearing something he'd said over a decade ago come back through a stranger's mouth. I think I'd have felt the same.

"…Is it still like that?" Histor asked. "…Have you still not received the answer?"

"…I haven't. Even now."

The words were short and hard. Not an angry voice. Just a voice stating fact.

I envied that hardness. I wasn't sure I could stand in the same place that long.

---

Elpis didn't join that conversation. Instead she came over to me and looked around the shelves.

"…There's a lot here," Elpis said.

"…Yes. It keeps coming in."

"…I learned to keep records too. From Teacher Mena."

"…I heard. She was talking about you this morning, before she left."

Elpis smiled. Her smiling face looked at ease.

"…Was the teacher here?"

"…Yes. She stayed a few days and left yesterday. She sorted out her scattered records before she went."

"…So the teacher scatters too," Elpis murmured, half to herself. "…I always thought she was someone who didn't scatter."

That stayed with me. People, on the whole, remember someone as they first saw them. To Elpis, Mena had always been the confident teacher. Not someone who came to be taught, carrying scattered papers.

Elpis pointed to one shelf.

"…What's that?"

"…Things that haven't been placed in the order they came in. Things we still don't know where to put."

"…There's a shelf like that too?"

"…Yes. Ones with no city name, or no date, or no way to tell who wrote them."

Elpis picked up one sheet and looked at it for a while.

"…This looks like my brother's handwriting."

"…Is it Zeteo's?"

"…No. But it's similar. The hand. Strange, isn't it, that two handwritings could look so alike."

Something chilled in me, hearing that. It made me think of what Histor and I had talked about back in Part Two. The nameless man. I wanted to look at that paper again, but Elpis had already set it back in its place.

"…Never mind. Just thought it was interesting."

I noted the position of that sheet in my mind. I meant to come back and find it later.

"…What's your brother doing over there?" I asked. Zeteo and Histor were still talking by the door.

"…That's how he always is. First thing, whenever he gets to a new city, he goes looking for the smartest person around. And asks whether they have an answer he's still missing."

"…Has he found it? The answer."

Elpis shook her head.

"…No. But he'll keep looking. I don't plan on stopping him. It's his way."

"…What about you, Elpis? What do you look for?"

"…I don't look. I wait. It's different."

"…Doesn't waiting wear you down?" I asked.

"…It does. But being worn down and giving up are different things. I don't have any intention of giving up. Not yet."

I turned that difference over in my mind. That one person searches and one person waits over the same thing struck me as strange, at the time. Now I think it's two steps walking the same direction.

"…What about your father?" I asked. I was remembering what I'd read in the Part Two record. The father who'd lost his name and lived to walk out of the completion building.

Elpis's face softened for a moment.

"…He's doing well. Not everything's come back. His memory only comes back in pieces. But it keeps coming, little by little. It hasn't stopped."

"…How do you know it keeps coming back?"

"…Last year he didn't remember my name. This year he does. That's enough. I'm not waiting for it all to come back — I'm watching it come, a little at a time."

My hand itched to write that down. Elpis seemed to notice and smiled.

"…You can write it down. I used to keep records too, remember."

"…Right." Only then did I write it down.

---

Zeteo spent the whole afternoon putting questions to Arche. I stood by and watched.

"…Among these cities, is there any suffering that's been closed?"

*The question's form is unclear. Please specify the closing criterion
for "suffering."*

"…Then what has to happen for it to close?"

*That item is not defined.*

Zeteo heard the answer without any surprise. If anything, his face looked like something had been confirmed.

"…Figures," he said, low.

"…What figures?" I asked.

"…I used to ask the Ledger the same thing. Why does God allow suffering. The Ledger gave me an answer. A perfect answer. Logically, not one thing wrong with it. And my brother still died. My father still lost his name. That answer changed nothing."

"…Then why do you keep asking?"

Zeteo looked at me. It was the first time he'd looked at me directly.

"…Because if I don't ask, it gets forgotten. I don't ask to get an answer. I ask so it won't be forgotten."

---

Hearing that, something felt familiar to me. It wasn't so different from why I write every day. I write because I'm afraid of losing things. Zeteo asks because he's afraid of forgetting.

*I saw those two as different things, at the time. Now I think they're
the same thing. Only the direction is different.*

Histor listened and asked, carefully,

"…Then how long will you keep asking?"

"…I don't know. Forever."

"…Even if no answer comes?"

Zeteo went quiet for a moment.

"…Answers have already come, more than once. None of them were satisfying, that's all."

Histor nodded at that. He didn't try to refute it. I thought that was very like Histor. He never argues down another person's doubt. He just listens, exactly.

Zeteo went on, this time asking Arche.

"…The nameless person who shows up in all six records — what's he doing now?"

*That person cannot be identified.*

"…You'd think you'd know what a nameless person is doing."

*The current status of an unidentified individual cannot be looked
up.*

Zeteo laughed shortly. Not an angry laugh.

"…This city really doesn't hide a thing, does it. It just says it doesn't know."

"…We don't lie," I said. "…When we don't know, we say we don't know."

"…Haven's Ledger wasn't like that. Even the things it didn't know, it made into plausible-sounding answers. That's what I hated more."

I had no reply to that. Which was easier to bear — honest not-knowing, or a plausible answer — wasn't mine to judge.

After a moment Zeteo asked again, his voice a little lower.

"…My case, then. How is it handled here?"

*The inquiry "Why does God allow suffering" is in progress.*

"…How long has it been?"

*In progress for fourteen years.*

Zeteo went still at the number. Fourteen years was nearly the whole time since he'd left Haven.

"…Will it close?" he asked Arche. This time there was a little hope mixed into his voice.

*The closing condition has not been met.*

"…What has to be met?"

*That item is not defined.*

Zeteo let out a short breath at that.

"…Same as this city or that one, huh."

"…They're different," Histor said quietly. "…The Ledger invented answers. Here, we say we don't know. That's the difference."

Zeteo tilted his head slightly, as if half-conceding the point, half not. He didn't say anything more.

---

Elpis had been quietly helping tidy the shelves the whole time. I noticed her hands were quick. Without asking, she'd picked up exactly the method I'd been using.

"…How do you know this?" I asked.

"…Teacher Mena wrote in her letter that she'd just learned it. To me."

"…She wrote you letters?"

"…Yes. Short ones. 'I'm learning how to lay things down.' I didn't know what it meant, but seeing it now, I understand."

Elpis finished squaring one bundle of paper into order, then said,

"…I'm still waiting."

"…For what?"

"…I don't know. I didn't know then and I don't know now. I just keep waiting because I feel like something's going to come."

I felt that this ran exactly counter to Zeteo's questioning, and yet somehow came from the same place.

"…Doesn't it frustrate your brother? All your waiting."

"…It doesn't frustrate him. I wait and he asks. Neither of us ever finishes. But we're both still alive."

---

Toward evening, the two got ready to leave. Zeteo asked Arche one last thing.

"…Of the records that came to this city from other cities, who's read through all of them?"

*Histor has read all six cities' records in full.*

Zeteo looked at Histor again.

"…Do you believe it?"

Histor didn't answer right away. After a while:

"…I know all of it. If knowing were believing, I'd be the biggest believer here. But if it isn't… I don't know."

Zeteo nodded. Neither agreement nor argument in it.

"…Like me, then. I ask and I still don't believe."

The two didn't say anything more after that. It didn't feel strange to me. Some conversations, I think, are right to stop where they stop.

---

Before Elpis walked out the door, she handed me a small piece of paper.

"…This is a flower that grew from the seed we planted, back then. Dried."

I took it. A small, faded petal.

"…You said it was a nameless seed."

"…Yes. Still don't know the name. But the flower bloomed."

I didn't know where to put it, so I slipped it between my own records. A place with no order to it. Somehow that felt like the right place.

Zeteo turned back once more at the door.

"…I'll come again. Not because I've come to believe. Because it's still not over."

"…What's not over?" I asked.

"…My question. I still haven't gotten all the answers. Even now. But…" he paused a moment. "…I used to think it was just me asking, alone. Lately it feels a little different. Like someone's actually listening."

"…Who?"

Zeteo didn't answer that question. Elpis took her brother's arm lightly instead.

"…Let's go."

The two left like that. I stood at the door and watched their figures grow small. The brother still walked looking only ahead, and the sister still glanced left and right.

*Writing this chapter, I want to set down honestly that Zeteo never did
change, in the end. He still said he didn't believe. Still said he
hadn't received an answer. Only that last line — "I think someone's
listening" — was something the Zeteo of fourteen years ago couldn't
have said. I don't think that difference is small.*

Histor read that sentence beside me.

"…I'm worried this could be read as though Zeteo has come to believe."

"…That's not what it means. I wrote down exactly what I heard."

"…I know. But it's a sentence easy to misread."

I decided not to change that sentence. That day I learned an accurate sentence can still be misread.

The Keeper watched the spot where the two had stood and said,

"…Those two. Exactly the same as when they first arrived."

"…Do you know them?"

"…No. Only from the record. But they matched the record exactly. That's the strange part."

"…Is it good that they haven't changed?" I asked.

The Keeper thought for a moment, then set down his teacup.

"…I don't know. Good or bad aside — it means they didn't break. Not many people stand that long without breaking."

I wrote that down last, and finished for the day. Elpis's flower petal was still tucked between my records. A nameless flower. I didn't bother moving it anywhere else.
