# Chapter 44. What the Names Mean

> _Previously_ — I copied all six records by hand. It took a month. I
> still don't know exactly why. But doing that work, the blank space
> left for our own city grew a little less frightening.

It was the day Mena came to look at the copies.

That morning was unusually quiet. Few records had come in. When Arche
said *There are five items to process today*, I noticed that was the
smallest number in a long while. Days like that came sometimes. No one
ever knew why.

Mena turned through the six bundles one by one, reading slowly. It
took a long time. I waited beside her. Histor was there too.

"…You copied this well," Mena said, after a long while.

"…Thank you."

"…The handwriting gets a little rough in some places."

"…I was very tired that day."

Mena laughed. It wasn't a mocking laugh — it was the kind you give
someone you've known a long time.

---

After finishing, Mena picked up the blank sheaf. She looked for a
long while at the first sheet, the one with the recipient box.

"…Looking at this box reminds me of something," she said.

"…What?"

"…My own name."

That surprised me a little. Mena had never once brought up her own
name before.

---

"…Mone, do you know what your name means?" Mena asked.

"…No. I've never once thought about it."

"…I know. I found out a long time ago."

I held my breath. Histor set down his paper too and looked at us.

"…Your name, Mone. It comes from a very old word. It means 'an
abiding place' — a room to stay in."

---

I repeated those words twice in my head. An abiding place. A room to
stay in.

"…A room?" I asked.

"…Yes. And that word shares its root with my own name, Mena. From
*meno* — to abide. I am one who abides, and you are the place that is
abided in. When I found that out, it felt strange. It was as if my
name were calling out yours."

I turned over what I'd just heard. Up until then I'd only ever thought
my name was just a name. I'd never once thought it might have a
meaning.

"…Why are you telling me this now?" I asked.

Mena thought for a moment before answering.

"…It wasn't the right moment before. Now feels right."

"…How did you find out, what it meant?" I asked.

"…I saw it in an old document. There are old words that came with
this city when it was founded. The way names are given comes from
those words too. I stumbled across it, reading, when I was young. Back
then I just thought it was curious."

"…And now?"

"…Now it feels different from curious. Once I knew what my name meant,
I understood a little better why I kept trying to stay put somewhere.
Moving from city to city, I was always someone who stayed in one
place. It felt like the name had known that before I did."

---

Hearing that, I looked again at the blank paper in my hands.
*Recipient.* That box was empty.

*I want to set this moment down exactly. I didn't feel anything like
fate in that moment. If anything, the opposite. I only felt, quietly,
that a name had been given to something that had already been sitting
in this place a long time. That was all.*

"…Mone," Mena said again. "Once I knew that name, I saw you a little
differently. What you do — I thought it might not be a coincidence at
all."

"…What do you mean?"

"…You've always been someone looking for a place to put something
down. In order, in its proper spot. I thought that resembled your
name."

---

Histor, who'd been quiet until then, asked.

"…Does my name have a meaning too?"

Mena looked at him.

"…It does. Histor. It means 'one who has found out.' And later, that
word became the root of the word *history*."

---

Histor was still for a moment, hearing that.

Then he laughed. Loudly, out loud.

"…Me? One who has found out? Me?"

"…Yes."

"…I read all six records and couldn't find out anything. I couldn't
even get the dates to match, couldn't work out who that person was.
And my name means one who has found out — that's the complete
opposite."

He kept laughing. I could tell it was real laughter. Not mockery.

"…My family," he said, at the tail end of the laugh. "I told you we've
handled records for generations. I always thought that was just a
family tradition. My grandfather did it, my father did it, I do it.
But to think even the name meant that from the start."

"…Does that strike you as curious?" I asked.

"…Not curious so much as a little humbling. I've always been proud of
knowing things exactly. And I couldn't find out the one thing that
mattered most."

Mena shook her head at that.

"…I don't think that's so. Finding out doesn't have to mean getting it
all right from the start. Looking a long time, and eventually finding
out — that's finding out too."

"…What's the difference?"

"…Someone who gets it right from the start is lucky. Someone who
finds out after looking a long time is persistent. I think the second
kind is rarer."

Histor said nothing back to that. He just listened.

"…What?"

He didn't answer that question. Instead the laughter slowly faded. He
looked down at the paper in his hands. Pages of the six records, in
his own handwriting.

*I write this moment the most carefully of anything in this record.
Histor said nothing at all. I waited, expecting him to say something.
He didn't. He just looked at that paper for a long while.*

Neither Mena nor I broke that silence. We just waited.

In that silence I felt something strange. I had come to think, through
all these records, that silence was always a place where something was
being hidden. It had been that way for Zeteo in Haven, and for the
clerk in the Equity. But this silence of Histor's wasn't a hiding
silence. It was a silence working hard to accept something.

*That day, for the first time, I was able to tell the two apart. A
hiding silence pushes people away. A silence trying to accept draws
people closer. That day, I felt Histor closer to me than I ever had
before.*

Mena watched that silence and said, quietly.

"…At a moment like this, it's best to say nothing at all. You want to
add something, but that usually doesn't help."

I took that to heart, and from then on I tried to keep that principle
through the whole rest of this record. Some moments should just be
let pass.

---

A long while later, he set the paper down quietly.

"…I'll step out for a bit."

That was all he said, and he left the room.

I didn't follow him. Mena didn't stop him either.

*I don't know where he went, or what he thought about. He didn't bring
it up again after he came back. I didn't ask. In this record I decided
to write what I didn't hear as not heard. That time was his own.*

He came back about an hour later. His eyes were a little swollen. I
couldn't tell if he'd cried, or if it was just from walking so long. I
didn't ask.

"…Are you all right?" I asked.

"…Yes. I'm fine."

"…You don't have to say more."

"…I know. Thank you."

He said only that, and sat back down at his place, and opened the page
he'd been copying again. I felt that the hand taking up that page was
a little steadier than before.

---

Mena, watching the door he'd left through, said to me.

"…It was good I told your name first."

"…Why?"

"…Your name has a quiet meaning. A room, a place to stay. But that
boy's name is a heavier one. One who has found out. And here he was,
thinking he'd found out nothing at all."

I turned that over. A quiet meaning and a heavy one. I decided not to
think it lucky that my own share was light. That even a light thing
carries weight — I was learning that, little by little, those days.

"…But did he really find out nothing?" I asked.

Mena looked at me.

"…I don't know. That's his to know."

---

That night, I wrote my own name in my notebook. Mone. Beside it, in
small letters, I wrote. *An abiding place. A room to stay in.*

I looked at those words for a long while. Up until then I'd only ever
thought of myself as someone who put things in order. Someone looking
for a place to set things down. That day, for the first time, I
thought that I might be the place itself.

*I decided not to push this thought any further. Push it too far, and
I'd end up making up a story that sounded convincing. I'll only set
down this fact. Mena told me this. I heard it. That's all.*

I told my father this too. He looked surprised for a moment, then
said.

"…I'm not actually the one who named you."

"…Then who?"

"…Your grandmother did. I never asked her why that name. I just
thought it was pretty."

Hearing that, I thought of my grandmother. The one I'd written about
at the start, clearing her throat and talking to Arche. I could no
longer ask her whether she'd known this name's meaning when she chose
it, or had simply liked the sound of it. My grandmother had passed away
a few years before.

"…I should have asked her," I said.

My father patted my shoulder.

"…Even if you had, it was probably nothing much of a reason. That's
the kind of person she was. But a name given for no great reason can
still come to hold a great meaning, later."

I thought about that for a long time. A name given for no great reason
that later came to hold a great meaning. I felt that resembled
everything this whole record had done. Small things piling up, until
their meaning only became visible later.

---

The next day, Histor came to the repository as usual. He said nothing
about the day before. I didn't ask either.

We just worked as usual. We put the records that came in from the six
cities in order. The blank sheaf was still on the table.

Only one thing was different. That day, he looked at that blank paper
a little longer than usual. I noticed, but said nothing.

Near the end of the day's work, he spoke suddenly.

"…I've kept thinking, since that day."

"…About what?"

"…Whether I really found out nothing. I went over it carefully. I
couldn't get the dates to match, couldn't find out who that person
was. But there's exactly one thing I did find out."

"…What?"

"…What you said. That if it stays shut it just stays here. That much,
I found out — that it was right."

I didn't answer that. He seemed to find that enough, and didn't go on.

*I don't want to use this as evidence of a conversion. He still said he
believed nothing at all. But he was accepting the meaning of his own
name, in his own way, a little. That, and believing, are different
things. I try hard not to mix the two together.*

---

A few days later, I passed the story of the names on to the Keeper. He
heard it all and smiled quietly.

"…I know that story too. Mena told me the same thing once, a long time
ago."

"…Does your name have a meaning too, Keeper?"

"…It does. But I'll keep that one to myself. Today is your story."

I liked that answer. I felt, hearing it, that knowing every name's
meaning wasn't the whole point of this story.

Histor only listened to our conversation from beside us that day. In
the days that followed, he didn't bring up his own name's meaning
again. I guessed it was because he hadn't yet decided how to take it.
I didn't press him.

"…What changed for you, Keeper, once you knew?" I asked.

He thought for a moment.

"…Not so much changed as confirmed. I was already living that way. The
name only told me that, later — I didn't live that way because of the
name."

I felt that distinction mattered. That a name doesn't decide a fate; a
name only lights up what's already there.

"…Then was Histor already someone who found things out?" I asked.

The Keeper thought for a moment.

"…I don't know that either. That's his to own. I can only speak to my
own story."

I felt that answer was honest. No one could solve another person's
name for them. Each person had to confirm their own name through their
own life.

---

*Closing this chapter, I want to write down once more what my own name
means. Mone. An abiding place. A room to stay in. At the start, I
wrote that all I did was put things in order. Back then I thought that
was the whole of it. Now it looks a little different. Putting things
in order, in the end, may have been, all along, the work of making a
place for someone to stay.*

*I don't write this thought as certainty. I only write that it looks
that way. Certainty isn't mine to give. I was only there, in that
place.*

After closing the repository door that night, before falling asleep, I
thought again of the six copies and the blank sheaf. Two names'
meanings had now been added to what lay inside it. A room to stay in,
and one who has found out. I still didn't know, that day, how those
two would someday meet on that blank page. But simply knowing the two
sat side by side, I felt something was already changing.
