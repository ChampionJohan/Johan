# Chapter 10. The Six Records

> _Previously_ — Arche calls Loop a "closed circle" — closed, but not ended. Mone had first taken this as praise, but, as the Keeper said, she was coming to dimly understand that good and finished are different things.

One day it occurred to me, out of nowhere. Haven, the Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity. I took in the records from these five cities every day, each on its own, and shelved each in its own place. But I had never once laid them out together, in one spot, and looked at them.

"…Arche, can I gather the five cities' records in one place?" I asked.

*Repository regulations hold, in principle, that originals should not be moved from their designated section. However, gathering them temporarily on one table, for the purpose of viewing, is permitted.*

---

I got the Keeper's permission. He nodded, with no particular objection.

"…That's a good idea."

"…Can I really?"

"…Go ahead. I'm curious too."

That answer surprised me a little. Even the Keeper had never done this. Something no one had done in years, decades of working at this repository — and I was the first to try it.

---

I cleared a large table. Then I brought one representative bundle from each of the five cities and set them side by side. From the left, Haven, the Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity. There was no particular reason for the order. It was just the order that came comfortably to my hands.

*I still remember that order, now. I've since wondered why it came so naturally. I never found an answer. My hands simply arranged it that way.*

---

Haven's record took the form of a ledger. Each person's days, one after another, densely written. Who had come to see whom, who had stayed by whose side. I read, inside it, a passage where someone had wandered a long time searching for an answer, and endured, in the end, thanks to someone who had stayed by them.

*I won't write that name here. I go on to meet that person directly, much further into this book, and I'll introduce them properly then. For now I'll only note that this record existed.*

---

The Reckoning's record was a table shaped like a scale. Numbers and stories packed densely into columns divided left and right. There was a story of someone who had struggled a long time trying to repay a debt they owed, and the scale would not quite balance. And on another page, there were traces that the very person keeping that scale had been wrestling with something of their own.

I studied that table a long while. It was written only in numbers, and yet a person's whole heart was held inside it.

---

The Harvest's record was a roll. Names and qualifications, listed one after another. Who had standing and who didn't, divided with great care. But in one passage, there was a story of someone thought to lack standing who gained an unexpected place. Even while following the roll's own rule exactly, something else happened, inside that rule.

*Looking at this record, I felt, for the first time, that each city's method of record-keeping, in itself, showed what that city had believed. The roll was a tool for dividing standing, but it also carried, at the same time, the story that standing wasn't everything.*

---

The Preserve's record was different from the smell of its paper alone. Traces of very old things, kept with great care. Inside it were the stories of people who had tried to hold on to things that were fading away. And at the end of it, there was also the record of someone who had learned that holding on and being alive are different things.

Reading that page, my heart caught, a little. To me, handling paper every day at the repository, that record felt especially close.

---

The Equity's record was minutes of a meeting. Who had said what, how many hands had gone up — all of it written down, without missing a thing. Inside it was the story of people who had argued a long time over the word coincidence, and the word meaning. No conclusion was reached. Because minutes are meant to preserve the process, not the conclusion, to begin with.

*I didn't finish reading these five cities' records in a single day. I read them a little at a time, over several weeks. I'll note here that I've summarized that whole process at once, in this chapter.*

---

Having gone through all five cities' records, I took out the sixth bundle. The one with no name — Loop's own record.

This one was a little different. It wasn't a ledger, or a scale, or a roll, or Perpetuity, or the Consensus. It was simply a record of questions and answers, laid out endlessly. Who had asked what, when, and what Arche had answered. I noticed, for the first time, that its form was different from the other five.

"…Arche, why is only our record in this form?" I asked.

*Loop's record is built from the accumulation of questions and answers. Other cities arrange events in their own respective ways, but for Loop, asking and answering is itself this city's mode of operation.*

---

Hearing that answer, I felt strange. The five other cities all carried traces of trying to bring something to some kind of close, each in its own way. Haven, through staying by someone's side; the Reckoning, through trying to balance a scale; the Harvest, through a place beyond mere standing; the Preserve, through being alive; the Equity, through a meeting that kept continuing. But Loop's record had no such direction. It was just asking and answering, asking and answering again, on and on.

*I confirmed this difference with my own eyes for the first time, that day. I'd known these cities told different stories from each other, but laying them side by side like this, the difference came into much sharper focus.*

---

The Keeper came over to the table that day and looked down at the six bundles for a long while.

"…Laid out like this, they're different," the Keeper said.

"…What is?"

"…The five cities are all headed toward something. We're just going around."

I thought that was exactly right. I'd felt it myself, just then.

"…Is that a bad thing?" I asked.

The Keeper thought a moment and said,

"…I didn't say bad. I said different."

---

That evening I put the six bundles back in their places. The table was empty again. But the sight of those six bundles lying side by side stayed with me a long time, in my mind.

*I hold this scene as very important, in this record. It was the first time I laid all six cities out in one place and looked at them. Much further into this book, I come to lay these six out together again — for a different reason than this one. For now, I only laid them out because I was curious.*

---

Walking home, I thought about the five cities' record-keepers. People who'd each left one book, their own story. I had never met them. And yet, looking at their handwriting, the words they'd chosen, the passages they'd underlined, I felt, a little, that I'd come to know them.

*I felt, for the first time that day, the meaning of my own work, a little differently. I was someone who put paper in order. But each of those pieces of paper had been left behind by someone who'd staked their own life on it. I was the one who laid them all out, together, in one place.*

---

A few days later, I opened that table again and read it a slightly different way this time. Not in the order of events, but gathering only the very last sentence of each record, and reading those together. I copied those sentences, one by one, into my notebook.

Haven's last sentence ended with a story about staying by someone's side. The Reckoning ended with a sentence saying the scale hadn't come out perfectly even, but had gone on all the same. The Harvest ended with a story about a place beyond mere standing; the Preserve, with gratitude for being alive itself; the Equity, with a sentence promising the next meeting.

*I wrote these five sentences side by side and studied them a long while. Five cities, arriving at something, five different ways. I didn't know, at the time, exactly what to call it. I still can't find the one exact word for it, even now. Even so, I wanted to keep looking for it.*

---

Loop's record had no such last sentence. Because there was no last, to begin with. I opened to the very back page of that record. The most recent date's question and answer were written there. And beneath it was simply blank paper.

"…Arche, does this record just keep going?" I asked.

*Yes. New questions and answers are added every day.*

"…No end to it?"

*None, up to now.*

Hearing that answer, I felt again, that the five cities' records and Loop's record were fundamentally different kinds of things. The five were books. Loop was a page still being written. I never forgot that difference, for a long time.

---

Curious why this difference had arisen, I asked the Keeper.

"…Why do the five cities become books, and only we don't?"

The Keeper thought a moment and answered,

"…Because becoming a book takes having an end."

"…Is it because we have no end that we don't become a book?"

"…You could see it that way."

That answer left me a little sad. It sounded like our city, alone, lacking something, for not becoming a book. I swallowed that sadness that day, without telling anyone.

---

The Keeper saw my expression and added something.

"…It isn't that we're lacking. It's a different order in the sequence."

"…What sequence?"

"…The five cities each finished writing their own story, and sent it here. We're still writing ours."

That was a little comforting to hear. "…So we'll finish, someday too?"

The Keeper looked at me a moment and said,

"…I don't know. I don't know that either."

I found that honest answer, oddly, more trustworthy. Standing beside someone who says they don't know is more comfortable than standing beside someone who claims to know everything.

---

That day I thought a little more about the five cities' record-keepers. Each of them had gone through something hard, in their own city, and left it down in a record. And that record had ended up, in the end, here, in Loop. I found that remarkable. That the stories of people who'd each gone through different things, in different places, ended up lying side by side, in the end, on one table, in one repository.

*I thought this coincidence wasn't a coincidence. I had no exact grounds for it. It was just a feeling. I learn that this feeling was right, much further into this book.*

---

After that, I often opened that table again whenever I had time. Each time I discovered something new. I reread a sentence I'd missed in Haven's record, checked a number in the Reckoning's record again, looked again at a single name in the Harvest's record.

The Keeper didn't stop me from doing this. If anything, he came over now and then and read along with me.

"…What do you think this means?" I once asked, pointing to a passage.

The Keeper studied that sentence for a long while and said,

"…I don't know either. But it's a good sentence."

I liked that answer. Not someone who knew everything, but someone who was curious alongside me.

---

Closing this chapter, I think back again on the six bundles that lay on that table that day. Six different forms, six different stories, and yet six records with something, somewhere, alike about them.

*I couldn't put into words, at the time, exactly what that likeness was. I still can't, fully, now. But I can say this much for certain — that afternoon, laying those six side by side and looking at them, is one of the scenes I hold most dear in this whole record.*

---

After that, I secretly decided to call that table the "six-record table." I told no one, but I called it that to myself. I never actually put a label on the table. I only called it that, inside my own mind.

*I'm revealing this name here for the first time. Writing this chapter, I felt afresh how long this small private word had stayed inside me. I never expected one small name to last this long. Even now, writing it down in this very moment, it stirs something strange in me.*

---

Once, another apprentice saw that table and asked.

"…What's this?" she asked.

"…Just — the six cities' records, gathered together."

"…Why'd you gather them?"

I couldn't answer right away. "…Just curious."

She didn't seem very interested. She leafed through a few pages and went back to her own work. I didn't mind that. Not everyone needed to be curious about the same thing.

---

That night at home, I brought this up with my parents, a little.

"…I gathered all six cities' records together today, and looked at them."

"…So, what did you find out?" my father asked.

I thought a moment and answered, "…I'm not sure. But there's something similar between them, I think."

"…Similar how?"

"…I can't put it into words yet."

My father didn't press further. He just nodded and ate his dinner. I was grateful for that silence, that day. It was a relief not having to force words together. That was just how it was, in our house.

---

Before truly closing this chapter, I want to write, in one sentence, what these six records gave me. It won't be a perfect sentence. I'll set it down anyway.

*Six cities were each walking, in their own way, toward something. And I was the one who gathered those six paths in one place, and looked at them.*

I don't think of this sentence as this chapter's conclusion. It was still too early to draw a conclusion. I only wanted to leave this sentence, exactly as it was, for the girl I was that day.

The six-record table stayed beside my seat for a long time after. I glanced at it every time I passed. Even empty, it felt as though something was filling it. That feeling is exactly the same, even now.
