# Chapter 15. If Knowing Were Believing

> _Previously_ — Mone tested Histor seven times. He got all seven
> right. He says there's nothing when there's nothing, and says
> exactly what's there when there is. He was also the first to tell her
> that the man with no name appears in all six records. Mone still
> hadn't asked why he does this work.

On the third morning, I asked.

The sentence I'd written on my paper the night before kept bothering
me. "Why would someone like this come to do cross-checking instead of
going to an apprenticeship of his own?" I couldn't just let it go.

"…Histor, why do you do this work?"

He stopped what he was doing and looked at me. Not a surprised
expression. If anything, it looked like a face that had been waiting
for this question.

"…I'm glad you asked," he said. "…I've been wondering since yesterday
when you'd get around to it."

*I felt a strange mood hearing that. He wasn't someone who brought up
his own story first. He was someone who answered when asked. I thought
that was modesty. I know differently now. He thought talking about
himself wasn't important. That's different from modesty.*

---

He straightened his things for a moment first. He pushed the paper
square, laid the pencil in line, and then began to speak.

"…I've read records since I was six. At first it was only because I
had time on my hands. There weren't many books in our village, but the
records that came from other cities — there were plenty of those. From
the water channel."

"…It comes by the water channel too, to other cities?"

"…Yes. Loop isn't the only one who receives it. It just accumulates
here the most. Because this is where it all originally gathers."

I let that pass lightly. *I learned much later it wasn't something to
let pass.*

"…What village was it?" I asked.

"…A small one. You wouldn't know it even if I said the name. Upriver."

"…Do records pile up there too?"

"…There, actually less. I collected up whatever there was, from as
early as I can remember. Even a stray sheet of paper drifting around a
neighbor's house. People started giving me a nickname for it — 'the
paper-picking kid.'"

He said that nickname without smiling. It wasn't a bad nickname, but he
didn't seem to like it much either.

"…Did you hate that nickname?"

"…No. Because it was accurate. I really did pick up paper."

"…So you kept reading. In the time other kids spent playing. At some
point I knew more than most people, and that was a little fun. People
started asking me things. Whether I remembered this scene, where that
passage was."

"…Did you like that?"

"…Yes. At first."

---

"…At first?" I echoed.

He paused for a moment. He fidgeted with the pencil.

"…Later it changed a little. People asking me things turned into
people asking me what to believe. I was just saying where something
was written, but people took what I answered as a kind of belief."

"…Wasn't it?"

"…It wasn't. I was just knowing."

*I should have asked something more at this point. I didn't quite grasp
then what he was trying to separate. I know precisely now. He was
talking about knowing and believing as two different things. That day
I didn't even know those two words were different words.*

"…Weren't you angry?" I asked. "…That what you told people got used
differently than you meant it."

"…At first I was angry. But being angry felt strange too. I'd only
told the truth. What people did with it wasn't up to me."

"…And now?"

"…Now I just answer what's asked, when someone's curious. Nothing
more. The moment you add more, you end up saying more than you know.
I don't want to do that."

I thought this answer was honest. It really was honest. *Only now do I
know honesty isn't everything. At the time I thought honesty was the
greatest virtue there was. Histor had that virtue completely. But that
completeness wasn't taking him anywhere.*

---

"…Then what about you, Histor," I asked carefully. "…Don't you
believe?"

He looked straight at me. It was the first time he'd held eye contact
that long.

"…I know all of it," he said. "…If knowing were believing, I'd be the
biggest believer here."

His voice was exactly as usual. It didn't rise or fall. There was no
mockery in it. It was just a reporting voice.

I didn't know what to say, so I stayed quiet for a moment.

I suddenly remembered him looking up at the doorframe that first day.
Those eyes that looked as if they were confirming something. I thought
I understood a little now what that had been. He was always checking.
Whether what he knew was right, whether what he saw was right. But that
checking always ended inside himself. It never once went outward.

"…But it isn't," he went on. "…Knowing and believing are different.
I know all six records. Where everything is, who said what, what it
means as it's written — I know all of it. But it hasn't done anything
for me."

*I turned this line over many times that day. However I tried to hear
it as mockery, I couldn't. He really was reporting. A report on his own
condition. Not anger, not sadness. Just a fact.*

---

"…Done what?" I asked.

"…I don't know. Not knowing what that is — that's the accurate answer.
People say something changes when they go through it. I haven't
changed. So I don't know what it is. Saying I don't know — that's the
honest thing."

I thought this answer sounded like Arche. Arche always answered that
way too. Saying it doesn't know when it doesn't know. Saying there's
nothing when there's nothing. I almost said so and stopped.

"…That sounds like something Arche would say," I said in the end.

He looked a little surprised.

"…Does it."

"…Yes. Arche says it doesn't know when it doesn't know too. Says
there's nothing when there's nothing."

"…So I suppose that makes me something like Arche."

He said this with a small smile, but the smile was strange. Not an
amused smile — more like a smile acknowledging something.

"…Does that offend you?" I asked.

"…No. Actually it sounds about right. I can give an answer. I just
can't finish anything."

I didn't understand that at the time. *I know now. He knew himself that
he resembled Arche. And he knew that wasn't especially welcome. But he
didn't deny it either.*

"…Then why do you keep reading? If nothing changes."

He thought about this question a little longer.

"…I like reading. Still. Cherishing a record and believing it are
different things. I can do the first one."

*This is the most important sentence in the chapter. I didn't fully
understand it until two years later. That day I just thought it was a
careful answer.*

---

"…Cherishing and believing." I repeated the phrase back. "…Are they so
different?"

"…They're different. I get upset seeing someone treat these records
carelessly. If the paper gets wet, or the order gets mixed up, I can
hardly stand it. That's cherishing."

"…Then what's believing?"

"…Believing, I think, is living the way it's written. I don't do that.
I only know what's written here. I don't live it."

"…For example?"

He looked at me for a moment. His expression said giving an example
wasn't easy.

"…In Haven's record, there's a scene where Elpis just stays beside her
brother. No argument. I know exactly what that scene means. But I
can't do that. I argue first. I check first. Just being beside someone
doesn't come easily to me."

I heard something click into place, but it was too soon to put it into
words.

---

"…Histor," I said. "…Can I be honest?"

"…Yes."

"…I envy you. But hearing you talk now, I feel a little sad too."

He looked at me. His expression wavered slightly. He wasn't angry. It
was something else, and I didn't know what it was then.

"…There's nothing to be sad about," he said. "…I'm all right as I am,
right now."

"…Really?"

"…Yes. I don't lie. You know that, Mone."

I couldn't say anything more to that. He didn't lie. That much was
true. If he said he was fine, then he was fine. At least, that's how I
chose to take it that day.

I hesitated for a moment over whether to tell my own story. About
moving nine times, about the eleven things I'd lost. In the end I told
only a little.

"…I'm someone who holds on to things too," I said. "…But I can't hold
them in my head the way you do, Histor. So I write them down."

"…What are you trying to hold on to?"

"…We moved a lot when I was little. Every time, I lost something. So
if I put things in order and write them down, it feels like I won't
lose anything."

He looked at me for a while after hearing that.

"…Then what do you believe, Mone? That writing things down means you
won't lose them?"

I couldn't answer that question right away. I'd never thought about
it.

"…I don't know. It just puts my mind at ease, doing that."

"…Isn't that believing something?"

I couldn't answer that. *I think, that day, I should have turned the
question back on him. He was asking me what belief was, while saying
he had none of his own. I couldn't see that imbalance then.*

---

He opened the paper again. A signal that the conversation was over.

"…Shall we go on cross-checking today?" he asked.

"…Yes."

That afternoon we went back and forth across the six rows again. On the
surface, it was a day no different from the one before. I found the
bundles, he cross-checked and marked them.

*But from that day on, I began to see him differently. He was still
more precise than anyone, still more courteous than anyone. But now I
knew where that precision and courtesy were aimed. Not at people —
at records. Between cherishing a record and believing a person, there
was a line he never crossed.*

Late in the afternoon the Keeper came by again. After looking at the
two of us for a moment, the Keeper asked me quietly, alone.

"…Are you all right?"

"…Sorry? About what?"

"…No, just — I wondered if Histor said something difficult."

I was startled. There was no way the Keeper had heard our conversation
earlier, and yet the question came as if the Keeper already knew.

"…How did you know?"

"…I saw someone like that once, back when I was your age. Someone who
knew everything and didn't believe. It's not uncommon."

"…Not uncommon?"

"…No, actually more common than you'd think. People who know more
don't believe more often than people who know less."

That sounded strange to me. I'd assumed it was the opposite. I'd
thought knowing more meant believing more.

"…Why is that?"

The Keeper shrugged.

"…I don't know either. That's not something Arche can answer for you."

"…Do you believe?" I asked his back.

The Keeper stopped walking. Answered without turning around.

"…I do. But I don't believe every single day either. It changes from
day to day."

"…Is that all right?"

"…If it weren't all right, nobody could believe anything. People
aren't made that way."

With that the Keeper went back to work. *I set down that exchange at
the end of this chapter. That day I just let this short conversation
pass by. I would take it out again several chapters later.*

---

Walking home that evening, I wrote in my paper.

That Histor knows everything is true. And that he doesn't believe is
also true. I'd thought it was strange for the two to sit together, but
seeing it today, it wasn't strange at all. That was simply who he was.

*I added one more line beside it. "…I know all of it. If knowing were
believing, I'd be the biggest believer here." I copied this line down
exactly as he'd said it that day. I didn't change a single letter. I'll
come back to this sentence often in this record.*

*I read this line over many times that night. At first I tried hard to
make it sound like mockery. If it was mockery, it would be easier to
argue with. But however many times I reread it, it wasn't mockery. He
really had said he knew the most, and it was true, and he had still
said he didn't believe. All three of those sentences were true. That
was what struck me most. Nothing false in it at all, and yet something
was still missing.*

*I don't get to name what was missing until much further along in this
book. I can't name it now either. I only remember his face that day.
Not an angry face, not a sad face. An accurate face. That was the first
time I found accuracy frightening.*

---

Before falling asleep I thought over the Keeper's words once more too.
"I do. But I don't believe every single day either. It changes from
day to day." I set that beside Histor's words.

One person said he knew everything and still couldn't believe. Another
said he wasn't believing every single day. I couldn't say which of the
two was better off. Both were honest answers.

*That night I asked myself the same question. Am I a believer? I
couldn't answer. I want to tell the sixteen-year-old I was, from where
I am now, that it's all right not to be able to answer a question like
that.*
