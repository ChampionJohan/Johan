# Chapter 31. Temel and Speon

> _Previously_ — Hora and Tyche came and went. Tyche still called it
> coincidence, and Hora still believed it was something more. Neither
> convinced the other, and they walked off together in the same
> direction.

Mena kept staying on at the repository after that.

She'd said, a few times, that she'd leave, but she never actually did. I didn't find that strange. There was always new paper for her to sort, and I knew that wasn't an excuse — it was real.

That afternoon, Mena looked out the window and spoke first.

"…Speon must be coming."

---

I looked out the window. A man was walking up from the direction of the water channel. He had something like a tool in his hand, and his clothes were covered in dirt. His pace wasn't slow, wasn't fast. Just the walk of someone with work to do.

Mena went to the door. Her step wasn't hurried.

"…You came?" Mena said.

"…Yeah. On my way through."

"…This is on your way?"

"…Everything runs through here lately."

The two didn't say anything more than that. No embrace, no hand held. They just stood side by side for a moment and looked toward the water channel.

I didn't want to disturb that silence, so I just stood in the doorway. No one called for me. That was fine too.

---

That greeting felt strange to me. It was too short for people who hadn't seen each other in a long time. But looking at their faces, the shortness wasn't a lack of something. It just wasn't necessary.

Histor whispered to me, beside me.

"…It was in Haven's record. That there was something unfinished between the two of them. 'You said you'd still be waiting.'"

"…Is that still true?"

"…I don't know. Watching just now, it doesn't quite look that way, and it also kind of does."

I didn't know how to write down that subtlety, so I decided to just write what I could see. The two stood with the ease particular to people who'd known each other a long time. That was all I knew, too. Even then, it felt right not to press further.

*I tried, several times, to rewrite this scene. I wanted to write it as
some greater reunion. Tears, a long embrace. In fact there was nothing
like that. The two simply stood like old friends. I decided to write it
exactly as it was.*

---

The Keeper came out and looked at Speon.

"…Back again."

"…Back again."

The Keeper didn't offer any particular greeting either. All three had the same indifference peculiar to people who'd known each other from very long ago.

Speon set his tool down carefully at the edge of the yard and said,

"…Came to check the water channel. See how far it's made it here."

"…It's made it fine," the Keeper answered. "…Good sound to it too."

"…The sound matters."

I found that odd and asked,

"…Why does the sound matter?"

Speon looked at me properly for the first time.

"…If it's making a sound, it's flowing. If it doesn't make a sound, it's standing still."

---

Watching the three of them together, I thought of the three names I'd only known from records. Temel, Mena, Speon. Loop's three children. All grown now.

"…What about Temel?" I asked.

Mena and Speon looked at each other at the same moment.

"…He's not here yet?" Speon asked.

"…Not yet," Mena answered.

"…Did he say he was coming?"

"…He said so. But when's he ever not late?"

I felt an old rhythm between the three of them in that exchange. A way of worrying without worrying. A way of waiting without hurrying.

---

All that afternoon, Mena and Speon didn't do anything in particular. Speon tended to the water channel, Mena sorted paper, and I did my own work beside them. Short words passed between them now and then, but most of it was quiet.

"…Why's it so quiet?" Histor asked me quietly.

"…I don't know. I expected something more too."

"…Are you disappointed?"

I thought for a moment.

"…No. If anything it's comfortable. Not having to wait for something big."

Temel arrived late that evening.

He came alone. He barely had any luggage. He opened the door, looked around the room once, and, spotting Mena and Speon, gave a short nod.

"…You came."

"…Came," Mena answered.

"…Late."

"…Right. Late."

That was all. I'd expected more, and waited, but nothing more came. The three of them just took their seats, each in their own place.

I was surprised by that short greeting at first, then quickly realized it was these three's way. People who'd known each other a long time, it seemed, didn't greet each other at length.

Temel's clothes were the most worn of the three. The traces of having crossed many cities showed clearly. The sole of one shoe was worn down on only one side.

"…Is something wrong with your foot?" I asked without thinking.

"…Yes. From walking so much. It's fine. I'm used to it."

Mena heard that and laughed shortly.

"…He's always been like that. Since Loop, he's made one pair of shoes last for years."

"…Even now?"

"…Even now. Hasn't changed."

Temel didn't deny it. He just glanced down at his own shoe once and picked his teacup back up.

---

*I've never been told to write this chapter quietly. But I, who was
there that day, can't find any other way to write it. The three of them
didn't have a reunion that swelled with music. Just being in the same
room again seemed to be enough.*

The Keeper brought out tea. The four of them sat. Histor and I watched, a little apart.

Aside from the clink of teacups, nothing made a sound for a long while. I wasn't uncomfortable with that quiet. If anything, it was comfortable.

---

"…First time the three of us have gathered since Loop, isn't it?" Speon asked.

"…First time," Temel answered.

"…Took a while."

"…Everyone had their own work to do," Mena said.

Temel lifted his cup and said,

"…I still look for people. Don't know how to do much else."

"…Found anyone? Lately?"

"…Sometimes. Mostly not. But I keep looking."

---

That was a familiar sound to me. Similar to Zeteo's continuous asking, and Elpis's continuous waiting. No one had gotten a certain answer, and yet they all kept going.

"…What's it like when you can't find someone?" Histor asked.

Temel looked down at his teacup as he answered.

"…Hard. But I believe the person I couldn't find is still alive somewhere. That one belief is what keeps me going."

"…What grounds do you have?"

"…None. I just believe it. I don't think it's strange to believe without grounds. I was always someone whose belief came before the grounds."

Writing that down, I felt this was a part of Temel that hadn't much changed since Loop. The man who'd been first to fall and last to lift a stone was still holding on to a groundless belief.

"…What about you, Speon?" Histor asked. "…Still opening the water channel?"

"…Not opening it," Speon corrected. "…Joining it. What was already there."

"…Does that difference matter?"

"…Yes. It matters. If I said I made it all, that would be a lie."

"…Did you always think of it that way?" I asked.

"…No. At first I just thought I'd dug it myself. But as I kept digging, I kept running into strange things. Spots already opened, traces of water that had already flowed. I wasn't the first one to touch it."

"…Do you know who dug it first?"

Speon shook his head.

"…I don't know. It was just there. I only followed the place."

Mena listened to that quietly. She didn't add anything, but I saw her holding those words in her heart.

---

Histor asked, carefully.

"…In Loop's record, Arche calls this city 'a closed circle.' Did you know that?"

The three looked at Histor at the same time.

"…I knew," Temel answered. "…At the time I thought it was a good thing to hear."

"…And now?"

"…Now I'm not so sure. Coming here, I see nothing's finished. And it's called a closed circle."

Mena added, from the side.

"…Lately I hear that phrase differently. A circle being closed and something being finished seem like different things to me."

"…What's the difference?" I asked.

"…Closed is just a shape. Finished means it stopped moving. This city is closed and it still keeps turning. So it's not finished."

I copied that down into my notebook. A sentence I wanted to read again later.

Mena heard it and laughed.

"…You never used to care about that kind of thing."

"…True. It used to just matter that things got done fast. Now… saying it precisely has come to matter more."

"…Why did it change?" Temel asked.

Speon thought for a moment.

"…I don't know. I just kept digging for a long time, and I kept confirming, over and over, that I hadn't made it." Speon looked down at his own hands as he said that. Nails caked with dirt.

---

The four of them sat late into the night. Outside the window it was already dark, and the lamp in the room swayed low. They didn't talk about anything big. Each asked a little about the other's life these days, and answered a little. I thought about how different, and how similar, this conversation was to their childhood conversations I'd seen in Loop's record.

"…Mone," Mena called me.

"…Yes."

"…Writing all this down?"

"…Yes. Is it all right if I do?"

"…Yes. Write it. But don't write it too grand. We didn't do anything much today."

I tried hard to keep that promise. Rewriting this chapter now, I remembered that promise and deliberately kept my sentences short.

---

Late into the night, Temel stood up first.

"…I have to go again. There's someone I need to find."

"…Already?" I asked.

"…Yes. Coming here was just a short thing. I wanted to check everyone was still around."

Speon stood up soon after.

"…I need to see to the water channel too."

Only Mena stayed.

"…I'll stay a bit longer. There's still more to learn."

The three of them scattered like that, each in a different direction. The reunion had been short, and the parting was short too. I didn't feel sad about it — it felt natural instead.

---

Just before Temel left, he spoke a few short words to me.

"…So you're Mone."

"…Yes."

"…I heard from Mena. That you taught her how to lay things down."

"…I just told her what I know."

Temel shook his head.

"…That's not an easy thing. My whole life I only knew how to find people, not how to lay down what I'd found. Probably I'd have gone on not knowing, if you weren't here keeping this place."

I didn't know what to say to that, so I just bowed my head.

"…Did you hear about Hora?" Temel asked suddenly.

"…Yes. She came a little while ago."

"…There's something I told her, once. 'You might just be sitting there because of a number. But even in a seat that's there just because of a number, there's still something you can do.' I wondered if it might be the same for you too."

"…What seat?"

"…You're an apprentice. No particular talent. But what you're doing here now isn't something just anyone could do."

I turned that over for a long time. That night I copied it exactly into my notebook. "An apprentice, but not something just anyone could
do." Whether I really believed that sentence, I couldn't be sure at
the time.

---

Before he left, Speon went to check the water channel once more. I followed along.

He crouched by the water and listened for a long while to its sound.

"…Sound's better than before," he said.

"…What's different?"

"…Don't know. Just better. Maybe something more's flowing in from upstream."

"…From where?"

"…I don't know that either. I don't know all of where this water channel starts. I only know from here to there, the stretch I can see."

I liked that humility. He only said as much as he knew. Neither pretending to know more, nor pretending to know less.

"…Don't you want to go all the way, to the start?"

Speon thought for a moment and smiled.

"…Sometimes I think about it. But right now there's work to do here. If someone finds the start, I'd be glad."

I wondered, hearing that, whether I'd someday see the start of this water channel myself. That day still seemed far off.

---

Mena stayed the last and spoke to me.

"…Thank you for not writing today, the three of us meeting, as something special."

"…I just wrote it as it was."

"…That's the hardest thing to do. Writing it as it was."

Hearing that, I understood then that what Mena had learned from me wasn't only ordering. She was also learning, alongside it, how to let something be as it is.

"…Will Temel and Speon come again?"

"…They will. Anyone who's come once here comes again. It seems like that kind of place."

That night Mena pulled out her stack of paper again. Still jumbled, out of order.

I watched that and quietly tidied up my own things. All day, no one had raised their voice, not once.

That night I wrote at the back of my notebook:

"Today the three of Loop met again. I started to write this scene
large, and stopped myself. They proved nothing to each other. They
just were in the same room. I understood, for the first time, that
this might be all a reunion is."

Histor read that sentence beside me and said,

"…Is it all right to write it like this? It's a bit flat."

"…I think flat is right. Today really was like that."

Histor didn't argue further at that. He just nodded and went back to his own place.
