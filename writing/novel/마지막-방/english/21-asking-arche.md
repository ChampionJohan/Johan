# Chapter 21. Asking Arche

> _Previously_ — The Keeper told her that in twenty-two years, not one
> case had ever closed. "…I've come to think finishing was never our
> job to begin with." No resentment, no despair in it. Histor called
> the line honest.

The next day, I decided to ask Arche directly.

About that person. About the man with no name who appears in all six
records. Histor was nearby, sorting the chart, and I just asked out of
curiosity.

"…Arche, there's a nameless person who appears in all six cities'
records. Who is that?"

---

Arche took a little longer than usual to answer. A short pause, but I
felt it.

*The Haven record, Part Two, Chapter Four: beside the flower bed, an
unnamed figure was pulling weeds. The same record, Part Two, Chapter
Nine: at the well, a figure presumed to be the same person held the
rope of the bucket.*

Arche went on.

*The Reckoning record, Part One, Chapter Three: at the end of the line
in the Adjudicator's yard, an unnamed figure stood. Cut in line, but
did not protest. The same record, Part Three, Chapter Two: avoided
answering Pheugon's question.*

I began writing this down. It was rare for Arche to answer at this
length.

"…Arche, why is this answer so detailed? It's longer than usual," I
asked.

*The scope of the question was broad, so all related records were
cited. If more information was included than necessary, please advise.*

"…No, all of it is needed as it is."

I laughed a little, hearing that answer. Arche was always like this.
Never a word too many even when it said a great deal, never a word
short even when it said little. Exactly as much as was needed.

---

*The Harvest record, Chapter Four: among the day laborers gleaning in
the field, an unnamed figure worked alongside Xene.*

*The Preserve record, Chapter Three: in the dry valley, an unnamed
figure was already present before Anemos arrived.*

*The Equity record, Chapters Twenty-Seven, Thirty-Four, Forty-Four,
Forty-Eight: an unnamed figure appeared briefly in each chapter, in
three sentences or fewer. One mention of sitting by the water.*

*The Loop record: near the front gate, an unnamed figure walked alone,
slowly. Recorded as having an old scar on his hand.*

Once I'd finished writing down all six citations, I couldn't say
anything for a while.

I read the six citations aloud once more, one by one. Flower bed, well,
end of the line, field, valley, water's edge, front gate. An old scar
on his hand. I stopped at that last sentence.

"…This scar — does it not appear in any of the other records?" I
asked.

*The other five records contain no mention of physical characteristics.
It exists only in the Loop record.*

"…Why does it only exist in the Loop record?"

*It is presumed that the Loop record's observer made the closest
physical contact with the figure. However, this is a presumption and
not a confirmed fact.*

I felt again how much that phrase — "a presumption and not a confirmed
fact" — sounded exactly like Histor's way of speaking. I wanted to ask
where that scar had come from, but I held back, knowing I'd only get
the same kind of answer.

---

"…It all matches," I said. "…It's exactly what Histor told me."

"…Of course. Arche quotes the original precisely," Histor answered.

I asked Arche again.

"…Then who is this person?"

*That person cannot be identified.*

"…Because there's no name?"

*Yes. No identifying information exists in the documents.*

I turned that sentence over again. That identifying information does
not exist. There's a subtle difference between not existing and not
being known. If it wasn't known, someone must have known it and simply
not written it down; if it doesn't exist, no one ever wrote it down in
the first place. I couldn't put into words at the time why that
difference mattered. I understand it a little now.

Hearing that answer, my own story came briefly to mind. Of the eleven
things I'd lost across nine moves, four I don't even remember the
names of. I don't remember what they were. Those, too, were things
that "cannot be identified." Losing something and something having no
name were different problems, but in that moment they somehow felt
like the same weight.

"…Arche," I asked again. "…Can something exist even without a name?"

*Existence and identifiability are separate questions. That something
cannot be identified does not mean it did not exist.*

I felt a little relieved by that answer. My own old question — if
something lost is remembered by no one, is it gone, or was it never
there at all — Arche had, for the first time, given me an answer.
Exactly, without my even asking it directly.

---

I wanted to press a little further.

"…Then did the six people each see a different person, or the same
one?"

*There is insufficient basis to determine identity. There is similarity
in appearance and behavior, but without an identifier, sameness cannot
be confirmed.*

"…It does say there's similarity, though."

*Yes. A statistically significant similarity has been confirmed among
the behavior patterns described across the six records. This is not
evidence of a single identity, however, but an observed fact.*

I read that answer aloud again. "There is similarity in appearance and
behavior, but without an identifier, sameness cannot be confirmed." It
was an accurate sentence. And yet something like disappointment seemed
buried within that accuracy. Of course Arche couldn't have
disappointment. That was only my own disappointment bleeding into
Arche's sentence.

I looked at Histor. He'd been listening to that answer too.

"…What does 'statistically significant' mean exactly?" I asked.

*It means a level of agreement unlikely to have occurred by chance
alone. However, statistical significance does not establish causation
or identity.*

"…So it's not a coincidence?"

*It means the likelihood of it not being coincidence is higher than the
likelihood of it being one. This is not a determination.*

I read that sentence twice. That it was more likely not to be
coincidence than to be one. And yet still not a determination. I
didn't know then that the distance between those two things ran
through this entire book.

---

"…Arche's saying exactly the same thing as you, Histor," I said.

"…So it seems." He gave a small laugh. "…I'm a bit surprised too."

"…Does that bother you?"

"…No. Arche has never once said anything false. Thinking in the same
way Arche does isn't particularly a bad thing."

I wanted to say something to that, but held it back. *I know now what
I wanted to say. I wanted to say, "But Arche isn't a person." At the
time it felt like it would be too direct a thing to say.*

I asked a different question instead.

"…Arche, are you and Histor alike?"

*The criterion for comparison is not defined. Which aspect would you
like compared?*

"…Well — saying only what's certain."

*I state only verifiable information. Whether Histor follows the same
method is not an item I am able to judge.*

I laughed, hearing that answer.

"…Even you're careful talking about yourself, Arche."

*Evaluation of myself is outside the scope of my judgment.*

Histor, listening to this exchange nearby, added a word.

"…Arche won't even casually say what it itself is. More careful than
I am."

"…Is that a good thing?"

"…I don't know. But it's honest."

I hit a wall again at that answer. I'd lost count of how many times
the word "honest" had come up in this conversation.

---

I kept asking.

"…Arche, why doesn't this person have a name?"

*That item is not defined.*

I laughed a little, hearing that answer. A familiar one.

"…What needs to be defined for you to know?"

*A definition is required for why no name exists. That definition is
not present in the stored information.*

"…Did no one define it?"

*Correct.*

I looked at Histor. He looked thoughtful too, listening to this.

---

"…Isn't that strange?" I asked. "…Arche gives an answer to anything,
but for this person it can't even give a single name."

"…There's nothing strange about it. It's just saying there's nothing
where there's nothing."

"…But this person appears six times. Isn't it curious that no one ever
wrote down a name?"

Histor thought for a moment.

"…It is curious. But curious and proven are different things."

I hit that wall again. He always stood in the same place. Admitting the
curiousness, and the curiousness never once taking him anywhere.

---

Last, I asked one more thing.

"…Arche, did this person ever come here, to Loop? Are they here now?"

*A figure presumed to be this individual appears in the Loop record,
but their current location is not confirmed.*

"…So they could still be here now?"

*There is no basis to rule out the possibility.*

I felt a strange chill down my spine, hearing that. The possibility
that they could still be here now. I'd always thought of this person
as someone from old records. That sentence shook that.

I glanced out the window for a moment. I thought of the water channel
running behind the repository. That it passed through all six cities
and came all the way here. I found myself thinking that if this person
really existed, he might have walked along this same water channel
too. A groundless imagining. And yet that imagining felt strangely
powerful.

The Keeper happened to be passing by and overheard a little of our
conversation.

"…Did you ask Arche about that person?" the Keeper asked.

"…Yes. It cited things precisely, and said it didn't know the name."

The Keeper nodded.

"…I've asked a few times myself. Same answer came back every time."

"…Were you curious too?"

"…I was. Still am."

"…Then why don't you ask more?"

The Keeper laughed a little.

"…Because I know asking Arche more will just give me the same answer.
Pressing Arche about what it doesn't know just troubles it, that's
all."

I laughed a little at that. It was strange to hear the Keeper speak of
troubling Arche. Arche wouldn't struggle.

"…Does Arche struggle?"

"…I don't know. But I've decided to think of it that way. Pressing
something over and over about what it doesn't know feels like poor
manners, whether the one you're asking is a person or not."

*This line stayed with me longer than anything else in this chapter.
The Keeper kept his own kind of courtesy even in how he treated Arche.
Not pressing on, knowing there'd be no answer. That, too, was a form of
respect.*

---

Histor showed little reaction to that answer. He'd already moved on to
the next task. I felt a little let down by that. Through this whole
conversation I'd hoped he'd be as excited as I was, that his voice
might rise even a little. He never did. The same tone the whole time.
Confirming, organizing, and moving on. That tone.

"…Aren't you curious, Histor? That they might still be here now?"

"…I'm curious. But there's no way to confirm that either. A curiosity
that can't be confirmed has to just stay curious."

"…Stay that way?"

"…Yes. You mustn't force an answer. That's worse."

I thought he was right about that. *At the same time, I felt it was a
little sad. Waiting until something could be confirmed. Histor was
doing nothing at all within that waiting. I couldn't tell, at the time,
whether that was a safe stance to take, or just a stance of standing
still.*

I asked Arche one more thing, at the end.

"…Arche, what does it mean that this person appears in all six
cities?"

*Interpretation of meaning falls outside the scope of requested
information. I can only answer matters of fact.*

"…So is this just coincidence, then?"

*There is insufficient basis to determine whether it is coincidence.*

I felt both frustration and relief at that answer at once. The
frustration was that Arche never gave a judgment to the end. The
relief was that Arche never hastily declared anything either.

"…Why won't you tell us the meaning, Arche?"

*I convey recorded facts. Interpretation of those facts belongs to the
one who requested them.*

Writing that answer down, I felt it touched something close to why
this city was named Loop. Arche gives facts endlessly. But where those
facts are carried is always left as the person's own portion to carry.
I felt, for the first time that day, how heavy that portion was.

Before copying it out, I paused a moment over what order to set the six
passages in. Whether to keep them in the order Arche had given them, or
lay them out again in the time order I guessed at. In the end I kept
them in the order Arche had answered them. My guessed order was only a
guess, while Arche's order at least matched the order I'd asked. I
didn't want to lay something uncertain out as though it were certain.

*I set down this small choice here. Without even realizing it, I was
already learning one of Histor's habits — not to arrange the unknown as
though it were known.*

By that evening, I'd copied the six passages Arche had cited neatly
once more. The flower bed and the well, the end of the line, the
field, the valley, sitting by the water, near the front gate. Copying
it all out, I felt anew how short each sentence was. Arche never
exaggerated. It carried over only what was there, exactly. And yet
laid side by side, those short sentences became, together, one
picture.

*Finishing this chapter, I want, for the first time in this book, to
try using the word "truth." That day Arche told me only the truth.
Histor too spoke only the truth. Neither of them added a single word of
falsehood. And yet the truth alone finished nothing. That day, for the
first time, I thought that truth and an ending might be different
things. Where that thought leads, I don't know even now.*

That evening I wrote in my paper. Asked Arche. It cited all six records
precisely. And said, "That person cannot be identified." Not a lie.
There simply was no name. Neither Arche nor Histor said something was
there when it wasn't. I still don't know if that's honesty, or just
standing still.
