# Chapter 16. Side by Side

> _Previously_ — Histor said his own position for the first time.
> "…If knowing were believing, I'd be the biggest believer here." Not
> mockery — a report. The Keeper said someone like that wasn't
> uncommon. Mone began to see him differently.

From the next day on, we began in earnest.

I only learned that day what cross-checking actually meant. Histor
explained it.

"…We put all six records in one place and look for passages that
overlap. Overlap can mean the same person appears, or the same event
is written from a different angle, or one book mentions another."

"…Is there a lot of that?"

"…Quite a bit. Like the story of Speon digging wells. A story that
starts in Haven continues all the way to the Preserve."

I nodded. I'd seen that before.

---

The problem was physical. To put six records in one place, we had to
pull the necessary bundles from all six rows. And it wasn't one or two
bundles. Each city's record was split across several volumes, and some
were loose leaves that had come apart.

"…There's nowhere to lay all this out," I said.

"…Isn't there somewhere with a big table?"

"…There's an empty room in the back."

We went to that room. It was the room the Keeper had given us to use
for cross-checking. One table. Roomy, but not enough to spread out six
records.

"…Can we use the floor too?" Histor asked.

"…I think we'd have to ask the Keeper about that."

We asked. The Keeper answered without much thought.

"…Go ahead. Just keep good order. Otherwise nobody will know later who
stepped on what."

*That sounded funny enough that I laughed, but Histor took it
seriously. From that day on, he even set an order for stepping across
the floor.*

I asked Arche whether it could help. I wanted to know if moving all six
cities' records at once could go faster.

"…Can you help move this?"

*Relocation is a physical task and requires human hands.*

"…Then can you tell us where to put things?"

*Yes. Which item would you like moved?*

I heard that answer and looked at Histor.

"…Arche tells us where things are, but doesn't move them itself."

"…That's how it is. Arche has no hands."

"…Not a figure of speech?"

"…Not a figure of speech. It really has no body. That's why it needs
us."

*I really heard that for the first time then. That Arche answers
anything, but can move nothing. I'd always known this as a matter of
course, but hearing it again from Histor's mouth made it feel new.*

---

We laid a cloth on the floor and began setting bundles out in six rows.
Haven, the Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity, and Loop's
row with no name.

This was my job. Putting things in order. I liked doing this work.
Within each city too, I set things in time order. Which came first
could roughly be guessed from the numbers written on the covers.

Histor watched me lay things out and corrected the order a few times.

"…This one shouldn't be here, it should be there. This was written
later. There's a sentence inside it referring to an earlier volume."

"…How do you know that, just from the cover?"

"…The color at the corner is different. Later ones haven't faded as
much."

I'd never even thought of that. *I'd thought putting things in order
was my specialty, but he read order more finely than I did. I felt a
little hurt, but I didn't show it.*

The work took a long time. It was past noon before we were even
halfway done. Zeteo and Elpis's brother-and-sister record sat thick on
Haven's row. The record of Pheugon's three days running away sat on the
Reckoning's row as a thin bundle by itself. The Harvest's row was the
longest. That was because Xene's record of going to the field every
single day was piled up by date.

"…This row is the longest," I said.

"…Yes. The Harvest is the one who wrote something down without
skipping a single day. The overseer filled the roll every day."

"…Was the overseer a kind person?"

"…No. Diligent and kind are different things. The roll was very
accurate. Being accurate isn't always the same as being kind."

Hearing that gave me a small jolt. I was someone who kept order
accurately too. It felt like the line was aimed at me as well —
accurate and kind not being the same thing.

---

Once all six rows were laid out, the floor was completely full. I
stood in the doorway and looked at that sight for a moment.

It felt unfamiliar. Up to that point I'd seen these six cities' records
each on its own — each row on its own shelf, its own place. Laid out
together in one spot, they looked different.

"…It looks strange, seeing it like this," I said.

"…What does?"

"…When I saw them separately, they just seemed like different stories.
But laid side by side, it feels like they're all connected somehow."

Histor heard that and looked at me for a moment.

"…It's natural that it looks that way. They all came by the same water
channel. They can't help but be tangled together to some degree."

"…Isn't that strange, though?"

"…No. It would be stranger if they weren't tangled."

*I found this answer a little unsatisfying at the time. It sounded like
saying strangeness wasn't strange. Looking back, this was the seed of
an argument he'd make later. He'd already taken his own position by
this moment.*

---

That afternoon, we found and marked overlapping passages one by one.
Histor would point them out, and I'd stick a slip of paper.

"…Here — Mena's name comes up in the Reckoning's record."

"…Mena? Our sixth recorder?"

"…Yes. But the quotation is wrong. It's written differently from what
Mena actually said."

I read the passage. It really was strange. It seemed to record, as
though Mena had said it herself, words she'd only had secondhand.

"…How did that happen?"

"…Probably whoever was in the Reckoning never saw Mena's record
directly, and only heard it as rumor. That happens sometimes. Records
shift a little as they pass through people."

At the time I heard this only as an interesting fact. *I didn't know
this explanation would become the core of the rebuttal he'd raise
several chapters later.*

Sticking another slip, Histor pointed between the Preserve's row and
Haven's row.

"…Look at this too. There's a well story near the end of Haven's
record, and there's the same well story near the beginning of the
Preserve's record. Speon digs the well in Haven first, then moves to
the Preserve and digs another well later."

"…Does the timing line up that way?"

"…Yes, roughly, going by the cover numbers. Each city keeps its own
calendar, so it's not exact, but the order should be right."

I didn't pay much attention to "not exact" at the time. *I'd come back
to that phrase too. That every city keeps a different calendar remains
both an obstacle and a key, all the way to the last chapter of this
part.*

A slip went up between the Reckoning's row and the Equity's row too. It
was a passage where, before Hora took up the deliberation, some
document from another city had Pheugon's name written wrong.

"…Is this a mistake too?" I asked.

"…Probably. He was likely mixed up with someone else with a similar
name. The Equity's record doesn't say where Pheugon went after leaving
the Reckoning. His name just drifts through, that's all."

I read that passage again. It really was short. One line. And that one
line connected two cities.

"…How many more like this do you think there are?"

"…I don't know. That's why we're looking."

---

By evening, more than twenty slips had gone up among the six rows.
Overlapping people, overlapping events, passages referring to one
another.

I felt strangely proud watching it. It wasn't as though we'd made some
great discovery, but it felt like a big accomplishment somehow.

"…Is this a lot?" I asked.

"…Isn't this enough? Twenty is quite a lot."

"…This much comes up between any two of the cities. They're all
connected by the water channel, and people pass between them too.
Overlap is natural."

That answer sounded a little deflating. I'd thought it was a big
discovery.

"…This is just the start. We haven't found the real thing yet."

"…What is it?"

He looked at me for a moment. His face said he was deciding whether to
say something.

"…Let's talk tomorrow. What we've done today is enough."

I didn't ask further. *That night I couldn't sleep for curiosity. I had
no idea what he'd say the next day. If I'd guessed, I might have been
less surprised. I didn't.*

---

Tidying up, I sat on the floor and swept my eyes once more over the
six rows. Laid out this way, I noticed each city's color was
different.

Haven's record was thin paper, well-worn from handling. Marks of much
hurried reading. The Reckoning's record was folded neatly, the
handwriting stiff. The Harvest's record was the thickest, and the
paper quality was the best. The Preserve's record still held a faint
damp smell. The Equity's record had the cleanest cover, and opening it,
there was almost no scribbling in the margins. Loop's row, last, was
the thinnest, with nothing written on the cover at all.

"…They all look different," I said.

"…Yes. Every city records things differently. But what's interesting
is, the methods are all different and yet some passages resemble each
other strangely."

"…What do you mean by resemble?"

"…I'll show you tomorrow."

He only said that and started tidying up. I felt a little frustrated
but didn't press.

---

Finishing up and heading out, the Keeper was standing at the door.
Glancing at what we'd laid out, the Keeper said,

"…It's been a while since I've seen everything spread out like this."

"…Has it happened before?" I asked.

"…A long time ago. When I first came here."

"…Was there someone like Histor back then too?"

The Keeper looked thoughtful for a moment.

"…There was someone similar. But he didn't come already prepared like
this."

"…What was different?"

"…That person came and learned here. Histor already knew when he
came."

I didn't quite understand what that difference meant at the time. *I
know now. Someone who comes to learn and someone who comes to confirm
walk differently. The one who comes to learn changes as he walks. The
one who comes to confirm only checks, as he walks, whether he's right.*

"…Where's that person now?" I asked.

"…Left. A long time ago. I heard he went to another city."

"…What did he become?"

"…I don't know. I never asked."

The Keeper said that and didn't say more. I was curious why the Keeper
hadn't asked, but I didn't ask that either. There was always something
in the Keeper's slow way of speaking that kept you from asking further.

---

On the way home I passed the water channel. The water flowed the same
as always. Listening to it, I thought again of what Histor had said in
the afternoon. "They all came by the same water channel." I'd let that
go lightly at the time, but I know now that was the most important
sentence in the whole chapter. The water channel hadn't only carried
records.

*That night I wrote briefly on paper. "Laid six records out on the
floor. Found more than twenty overlaps. Histor said he'd show me the
real thing tomorrow." That was all I wrote that day. I already felt
that something was coming, though I didn't know what.*

*Writing this chapter again now, I try to picture exactly how those six
rows looked laid out on the floor that day. The narrow room, the
cloth-covered floor, six records with six different colors and smells.
And Histor's footsteps moving quietly among them. He was never once in
a hurry that day. And yet he moved much faster than me, much more
precisely. I thought at the time that was talent. I know now. It was
talent, but before that, it was habit. He was a person who'd spent a
lifetime building the habit of checking.*

*That night I also had this thought. What if, instead of losing things
across nine moves, I'd grown up the way Histor had, never forgetting a
single thing? Looking back it's a strange thing to wonder. I grew up
into the person I am, and he grew up into the person he is. Only that
night, the thought kept circling in my head.*

---

I went to the repository early the next morning. I've already
mentioned that I couldn't sleep, curious what Histor was going to show
me. But that morning, on the way there, I deliberately slowed my steps.

*There was a reason. I've never had much practice holding my curiosity
in check. But that day I wanted to try. What came back to me was how
Histor never grew impatient, every time he put me off with "let's talk
tomorrow." I wanted to be like that too, just for one day.*

I stopped for a moment on the water channel bridge. I stood there
longer than usual. Listening to the water, I told myself that whatever
I saw today, I'd take it in in order. Calling it a resolution was too
grand a word for it, but at the time it was what I needed.

When I reached the repository door, Histor was already waiting inside.

*I should be honest about how well I kept that mindset that felt too
grand to call a resolution, from the day before. The moment I opened
the door, I forgot it entirely and all but ran inside. My resolve to
take things in in order didn't survive a single step. I was still,
that day too, sixteen years old.*
