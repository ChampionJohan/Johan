# Chapter 8. The Closing Condition

> _Previously_ — Mone learned that the whole index is "in progress," and that not a single case has ever closed. Even the eleven-year-old record stood untouched. The Keeper said "I know half the answer," and Mone made up her mind to ask, directly, for the other half.

The next day I arrived at the repository earlier than usual.

The Keeper hadn't come in yet. I stood alone among the shelves and turned over, several times in my head, the question I meant to ask Arche. I wanted to ask it precisely. I already knew well by then that a vague question gets a vague answer.

*I said, in the last chapter, that I could copy out this day's conversation without missing a single letter. That's true. This conversation survives exactly, in my notebook, and I have that notebook open in front of me as I write this chapter. The handwriting looks pressed harder than usual. I must have written it, without noticing, with more force than I meant to, that day.*

---

"…Arche."

*Yes.*

"…Why doesn't anything ever finish?"

I asked it as simply as I could. I felt I should ask more precisely, but in that moment no other words would come.

*Could you make the question a little more specific? If "finish" refers to the closing of some item, there is a reason common to all items.*

"…Yes, that. That reason."

---

*The closing condition has not been met.*

I stood still a moment, hearing that answer. It was the answer I'd already been expecting since yesterday. And yet hearing it directly was different, all the same. There's a difference between expecting a thing and hearing it said.

"…What needs to be met?"

I'd been preparing to ask this question since the night before. I tried hard to ask it in the most exact words I could.

*That item is not defined.*

---

Hearing that answer, I asked again. Not out of anger this time, but because I truly didn't understand.

"…What does 'not defined' mean? Does it mean you don't know?"

*I can confirm whether or not the closing condition has been defined. Having confirmed it, that condition has never been defined. This is different from my not knowing. I know — the fact that it is not defined.*

I listened to that sentence as though reading it twice. It wasn't that Arche didn't know. There had simply never been such a thing, to begin with. The difference looked small, but it wasn't.

---

"…Who's supposed to define it?" I asked.

*No party holding the authority to define it has been designated.*

"…So no one can decide it?"

*Within the range I can confirm, no party has been designated. However, that does not mean it can never be defined.*

I seized on that last sentence. "…So it could still be defined, someday?"

*This cannot be confirmed. But it isn't ruled out, either.*

I stood there a long while. It felt like something was almost within reach, and yet I didn't know exactly what I was supposed to grasp. A single shaft of sunlight had come in between the shelves, and I just stood there, watching it move.

---

I suddenly thought of my first evening. Asking what would be good to have for dinner, and Arche answering that the criterion for "good" was not defined. I'd written it down as a funny joke.

"…Arche, is this the same kind of thing as when I asked about dinner, my first day?"

*I have confirmed the case you're comparing it to. It is structurally similar. Like the criterion for "good," the condition for "closing" has likewise never been defined.*

I felt strangely like laughing, hearing that. It wasn't a laughing matter, and yet I laughed. That day it had been a joke; now it was the whole city's problem. It was strange, that the same sentence could carry such different weight.

*I remember this moment. I felt, right then, that I'd touched something important. But I didn't know exactly what was important about it. To put it in terms fitting this chapter's title — I was already standing before a condition, even then, without being able to define what condition it was.*

*It's only now, writing this chapter, that I know why I laughed, that day. When the strange and the familiar overlap, a person often laughs. That first day's joke and this heavy fact, now, had come from the same sentence — and that struck me as funny, before it struck me as frightening.*

---

"…Arche, then — you can answer anything, so why can't you answer this?" I asked. It wasn't said with resentment. I was truly curious.

*I answer what I know. Regarding the closing condition, I know the fact that it is not defined, and I have answered that fact exactly. This is not my avoiding the question — it is my not manufacturing a definition that does not exist.*

I turned that over. Arche hadn't hidden anything. It hadn't said it knew something it didn't. It had simply refused to invent, as existing, something that had never existed. That was the most important principle Arche had kept, in this city, and on this day too, that principle hadn't wavered a hair's breadth.

"…Then this isn't Arche's fault."

*That judgment is yours to make. I only answer fact.*

---

I finished that exchange and stood there a long while. The Keeper finally arrived, saw me, and asked,

"…What are you doing?"

"…I asked Arche. Why nothing ever finishes."

"…What did it say?"

"…That the closing condition hasn't been met. But that condition isn't defined."

The Keeper nodded. There wasn't a trace of surprise in him.

"…Was that the half?" I asked. "Was that the half you meant, Keeper?"

"…Yes. That's the half."

"…What's the other half?"

The Keeper looked at me a moment, then went back to work.

"…It isn't time for you to know that yet."

---

I felt let down by that answer. And at the same time, I sensed something different in the way he'd said it. It wasn't the tone of someone hiding something. It was the tone of someone who thought the time hadn't come yet. I was already, vaguely, learning that those two things were different.

I worked the whole rest of that day turning that conversation over. Taking in bundles, asking where they went, shelving them — and the whole while, those two sentences kept circling in my head. *The closing condition has not been met.* *That item is not defined.*

*I've felt the temptation, more than once, to place these two sentences at the very front of this record. Writing this book, I came to know how important they are. But the girl I was that day didn't know it. That day, I just went through a strange sort of day. I'm keeping to my decision to write this record in order, so I return to that day's self to write this chapter.*

---

On the way home I looked at the water channel again. It flowed exactly as always. I tried to think of nothing, watching it. It had been a day of too much thinking. I just stood there a long while, listening to the sound of the water.

*But that night, I wrote this in my notebook. One line. "Arche doesn't hide anything. But there are things it has no answer for." I wrote nothing beneath it. There was nothing more to write, at the time. I stared at that empty line for a long while, and then turned off the light.*

---

That afternoon, I went back to Arche. The morning's conversation still weighed on me. I wanted to ask from a slightly different angle.

"…Arche, then what happens if no one ever defines the closing condition? Does it stay like this forever?"

*As long as the present state continues, yes. However, judging whether this is a problem is likewise not my role.*

"…Isn't it a problem?"

*That judgment lies in undefined territory. The word "problem" itself carries an evaluation, and that falls outside what I can answer.*

I found that answer a little frustrating. And at the same time, I recognized that Arche was honestly stating its own limits. That honesty was, in a strange way, a comfort to me.

---

"…Then can I think of this as a problem?" I asked.

*Your judgment is yours to make. I do not restrict it.*

Hearing that gave me a little courage. It meant that finding this strange wasn't wrong of me. Arche didn't stand in the way of my own judgment. If anything, that was exactly what let me keep asking.

"…Then I'm going to think of it as strange."

*Recorded.*

I laughed at that answer. Arche took even my expression of feeling and stored it, simply, as fact. It wasn't a bad thing. If anything, it was honest.

---

That evening, as the Keeper was getting ready to leave, I asked him again.

"…Keeper, do you think this is a problem?"

The Keeper paused packing his bag and looked at me.

"…There was a time I thought it was a problem."

"…And now?"

"…Now, I think of it less as a problem, and more as just the shape our life takes."

That sounded like resignation to me, and it stung a little. "…Have you given up?"

"…No." The Keeper said it firmly. "…If I'd given up, I wouldn't do this work. I come every day. I come because I haven't given up."

I looked at him again, hearing that. Giving up and accepting were different things. I felt that difference clearly, for the first time, right then.

---

That night, before falling asleep, I thought again of those two sentences. *The closing condition has not been met.* *That item is not defined.*

I want to set down here that these sentences didn't feel frightening to me. Far from frightening — they left me strangely calm. I think it was because I knew Arche didn't lie. If something had been hidden, it would have been frightening — but nothing was hidden. There was simply nothing there.

*I didn't know, that day, that this fact was the key to this whole book. I just took it for a strange day, and went to sleep. The next day I got up as usual and went to the repository as usual. As though nothing had changed.*

---

Writing this chapter, wanting to render that day's conversation more exactly, I checked that record with Arche again. It's still possible, even now. Arche had that day's conversation, kept whole, without a single character wrong.

*Apprentice Mone, age sixteen, inquired at 8:12. Original text follows.*

I wrote this chapter with that original text in front of me, confirming my memory hadn't blurred. And I felt it freshly. Arche hadn't discarded even that day's trivial exchange. It couldn't close a case, but its record was perfect.

*This contrast is the center of this book. Recording perfectly, and never once being able to finish. Writing this chapter, I faced that contrast, in exact words, for the first time.*

---

A few days later, I put a slightly different question to Arche. Not about closing this time — I was curious whether other cities were the same.

"…Arche, are other cities like this too? No closing condition?"

*I can only confirm Loop's own system. Other cities' internal procedures are beyond my authority. However, judging from the records that have come in, other cities appear to bring their matters to a close, each in their own way.*

"…So it's only here?"

*Within what I can confirm, Loop's closing procedure appears to be undefined. Other cities' circumstances cannot be confirmed.*

I was a little startled by that answer. Other cities seemed to bring things to some kind of close, in their own ways. Only here, it seemed, was there none of that.

---

I passed that along to the Keeper that evening.

"…Other cities close things out, in their own way. But here, there's none of that, it says."

The Keeper paused his hands a moment.

"…I'd guess so."

"…Why only us?"

"…I don't know. There was a time I wondered that too."

"…Not curious anymore?"

The Keeper shook his head. "…I'm still curious. But I don't try too hard to find the answer, anymore. Trying hard doesn't seem to be what produces it."

That sounded like resignation to me, at first, and then, thinking again, it felt like something else. It seemed there was another place, somewhere between trying hard and giving up. I didn't yet know exactly what that place was. But I dimly knew that it was there.

---

Closing this chapter, I want to copy these two sentences here once more. For whoever's reading this book for the first time, and for whoever's reading it again, I want to leave them plainly set down.

*The closing condition has not been met.*

*That item is not defined.*

I know these two sentences aren't lies. Arche didn't hide anything. There was simply no answer. That day, I didn't feel anger, standing before this fact. Instead, I stood there a long while.

---

Writing this chapter, I want to make one thing clear to whoever is reading. I didn't come to like Arche any less, from this day on. If anything, the opposite. It was a day that confirmed to me, once more, that Arche says it doesn't know what it doesn't know, and says what isn't there isn't there.

*But from this day, I came to hold a different question. Not a question to ask Arche, but a question about this city. Why had no one ever filled that signature line. Why had no one ever defined it. These weren't questions Arche could answer. That day, I sensed that, dimly, for the first time.*

That night, before falling asleep, I looked out the window. The city was quiet as always. From somewhere, faintly, came the voice of someone asking Arche something. Listening to it, I thought. Every question in this city gets an answer. But for some questions, no end ever comes. I now held that difference in a clear sentence. A sentence I hadn't had before.
