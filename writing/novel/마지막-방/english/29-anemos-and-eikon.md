# Chapter 29. Anemos and Eikon

> _Previously_ — Xene and Lytron came and went. Lytron still said he'd
> never lost anything, but he was beginning to understand that it was
> nothing to be proud of. The two left a sack of grain behind.

The two who came from the Preserve entered a little differently.

I heard footsteps before the door opened. One set a little rushed, the other very slow. When the door opened, the man leaned his head in first, and the woman followed slowly behind him.

The woman looked no older than she should. Just as the Preserve's record had said — fifteen — she still looked fifteen now. I thought that was strange for a moment, and then remembered the Preserve's story and understood. A preserved person doesn't age.

The man was the opposite. The boy who'd been sixteen in the Preserve's record now looked close to thirty. Standing side by side, the difference stood out even more.

"…I'm Anemos," the man said. "…This is Eikon."

Eikon looked around the room without any greeting. Her eyes carried no hurry. Not the eyes of someone searching for something — the eyes of someone who already knew everything and was only confirming it.

That confirming look wasn't unfamiliar to me. Arche sometimes answered in a similar tone. Already knowing everything, just retracing the fact.

---

There was almost no expression on Eikon's face. Not bored, not curious. Just calm. That calm felt foreign to me at first.

"…What is this place?" Eikon asked. Less a question than a way of confirming.

"…A repository. A place where records from the six cities are laid in order."

"…Is that work interesting?"

I was a little thrown by the question. Most people didn't ask that.

"…Not so much interesting as… I think it's necessary work."

"…Necessary," Eikon repeated the word. "…I've never had need for anything."

Anemos said quietly, beside her.

"…There she goes again."

"…It's true, isn't it."

---

Anemos looked a little more worn than in the old record. Not tired in a bad sense. The weariness of someone who'd walked a long way.

"…You still haven't taken release?" I asked, carefully. I'd read the Preserve's record, so I knew what I was asking.

Eikon answered first.

"…No. I haven't."

"…Why not?"

"…I don't feel the need. Even now, I lack nothing."

Those words were exactly what she'd said in the Preserve's record. It struck me as strange, and a little heavy, that this hadn't changed even after more than a decade. For a moment I wanted to try to persuade her, and then, realizing that wasn't mine to do, I let it go.

Histor asked, carefully.

"…Have you never once wavered?"

Eikon shook her head.

"…It would be a lie to say I'd wavered. I'm just the same."

"…Don't you think that's remarkable?"

"…No. It's not remarkable. It's just a fact."

---

Anemos added, from the side.

"…I'm still waiting. I asked her that day. She said, 'Ask me again later.' I've been waiting ever since."

Listening, I thought of the records that had sat in progress ten, twenty years in this city. The answer Anemos was waiting for seemed to sit in a similar place. Not that there was no answer set for it — just that it hadn't come yet.

"…That must be hard," I said.

"…It is. But not as hard as it used to be. I used to think it was because I'd done something wrong. Now I just… think of it as waiting for the right time."

Histor came closer and asked,

"…Don't you think it's time to ask? How many more times have you asked?"

"…Once a year. Same answer every time. 'Later.'"

"…Doesn't that make you angry?"

"…It does. But I found out anger and waiting can travel together. Learning that was the biggest change."

"…What about Speon?" I asked. "…The one who dug the well in the valley."

"…I see him often. He still sends water down. He says, 'The upper part isn't drying up anymore, so.'"

"…What does that mean?"

"…I don't know exactly either. But his face looks certain every time he says it. I just trust him and go along."

Histor asked,

"…How's the valley these days?"

"…Less dry than before. Water keeps coming in. But there are still patches that are dry. Not all filled yet."

"…Will it all fill?"

Anemos thought for a moment.

"…I don't know. I just watch it fill a little more each day. That's enough for me."

---

Eikon listened to that exchange and said,

"…I still don't understand why he does that."

"…Why?" I asked.

"…I'm fine the way I am, and he keeps telling me to become something else. I'm not suffering."

"…You're not suffering?"

"…No. Really. I don't lie."

I recognized this as the exact same sentence from the Preserve's record. She was holding to a logic that was hard to refute, still.

*Writing this part, I tried not to try to win against Eikon. Her words
are still hard to refute, even now. I just want to render what she
said exactly.*

Histor asked, carefully.

"…Is the Curator well?"

Anemos nodded.

"…Yes. Still grieving. But he doesn't hide the grief anymore, now. He used to try to hide it."

"…Is that better?" I asked.

"…I don't know. But it seems better than hiding it. At least he knows what he's doing."

Eikon heard that and added,

"…Every time the Curator looks at me, his eyes are a little sad. I'm fine, though."

"…Why would he look sad if you're fine?" Histor asked.

Eikon shrugged.

"…That's his business. I only know my own business."

That tone didn't sound cold to me. Just honest.

---

I asked Arche.

"…Has Eikon's release application gone through?"

*No application record for that item exists.* Arche's voice was flat, as always.

"…You never applied?"

"…No," Eikon answered directly. "…No reason to apply."

"…Doesn't Anemos apply?"

"…I can't apply on her behalf. There's a consent requirement. I just ask." When Anemos said that, there wasn't a trace of resentment in his face. He said it plainly, like explaining a rule.

I remembered the Preserve's record. When consent was sought, Eikon always answered with "Why?" And the process always stopped there.

"…Does she still say 'why' when you ask?" I asked.

Eikon laughed.

"…No. Lately I say 'later.' If that's progress, I suppose it's progress."

---

Anemos heard that and looked startled.

"…You think that's progress?"

"…No. But I thought you'd like it, so I said it that way."

Histor cut in, carefully.

"…Isn't that considerate? Saying it because you thought he'd like it."

Eikon tilted her head.

"…Could be consideration. I don't really understand things like that. I just tried it."

"…Why?"

"…I don't know. Seeing Anemos's face just isn't unpleasant. I'm not sure it's what you'd call liking, though."

I felt that answer was a very small crack in her logic. Someone who said she lacked nothing was minding someone's expression. But that wasn't evidence that overturned her words. I decided to just write the crack down exactly as it was.

Anemos couldn't answer that, and just smiled. That smile looked complicated to me. Joy and something a little wistful, mixed.

Histor asked, more carefully than usual.

"…Eikon, are you really satisfied with your state right now? Have you never once doubted it?"

Eikon thought for a moment.

"…I doubt. Sometimes. But doubting doesn't produce an answer. It's a fact that I lack nothing right now, and I haven't yet found a reason for that to change."

"…If you find one?"

"…Then I'll think again. But not now."

I thought that answer closed the door neither all the way shut nor all the way open. Just open that much.

I asked Arche one more thing.

"…Are there other cases where release applications came in?"

*Across the whole of the Preserve, there have been three release
applications in total. Two of them have been completed.*

"…Who were they?"

*Personal information is not provided without the requester's own
consent.*

Eikon heard that answer and said,

"…Strange that there were three. In our city that almost never happened."

"…Do you know those people?"

"…No. But I am curious what made them different."

Anemos said quietly.

"…Probably people who were missing something. Not like you, who has everything."

Eikon smiled a little at that.

"…Maybe."

---

The two stayed until evening. Eikon, looking around the shelves, asked,

"…These records — you said they're all in progress."

"…Yes. Not one has closed."

"…Doesn't that frustrate you?"

"…Sometimes. But lately I don't think that's only a bad thing."

"…Why?"

"…In progress just means it hasn't ended yet — not that it never will."

Eikon looked at me for a moment, hearing that. It was the first time she'd had a genuinely interested look.

"…That sounds like what Anemos has been trying to tell me."

Anemos said nothing to that. He just smiled quietly.

Thinking about how the age gap between the two of them kept widening, I hesitated a moment over whether to ask, then did.

"…Is it all right? Anemos, you're getting older, and Eikon, you're staying the same."

Anemos smiled a little.

"…It wasn't all right at first. Honestly. I was scared of growing old before her. What if she stopped recognizing me one day."

"…And now?"

"…Now I've just accepted it. My aging and Eikon's not aging — neither is something I can change."

Eikon heard that and said, quietly,

"…I'll still recognize you. Even old."

Anemos didn't answer that. He just lowered his eyes for a moment.

*Rendering this short exchange, my hand shook. Eikon still said she
hasn't taken release. And yet that one line showed she cared about
Anemos, in her own way. I tried hard not to read those two things as a
contradiction. Not having changed, and caring, can be different
layers.*

I thought about that distinction several more times. Eikon didn't apply for release that day either. That fact stayed as it was.

---

Before leaving, Anemos said quietly to me.

"…I'm not in a hurry anymore. I used to come here trying to get her back. Now I just stay because I want to be near her."

"…Is that comfortable?"

"…Not so much comfortable as… it feels right. The place I'm meant to be."

Eikon turned back at the door and said,

"…I'll come again, Mone. And I'll ask the same thing again then. 'Why do I have to exist.'"

"…Come back again."

"…Anemos will bring me. He's good at that."

The Keeper watched from the side and said quietly,

"…Those two are together in such a strange way."

"…Strange?" I asked.

"…Usually people who walk together walk at the same pace. Those two walk at completely different paces, and they still stay together."

"…How is that possible?"

The Keeper thought for a moment.

"…Someone's waiting. Continuously. Anemos is doing that."

I remembered the last part of the Preserve's record, hearing that. That after hearing her say "ask me again later," he'd stayed by her side ever since. It wasn't some grand event — just a choice repeated every day.

The two walked out like that. I stood at the door and watched until they were gone. Their pace was still different — one slow, one matching beside her. I didn't leave until their backs had completely disappeared from sight.

That night Histor asked me,

"…What do you think of Eikon?"

"…I don't know. Everything she says seems right, and yet something catches."

"…What catches?"

"…Whether lacking nothing is really a good thing. Maybe because I've lost so much myself, hearing 'I lack nothing' sounds strange to me instead."

Histor nodded.

"…I feel something similar. But I can't find a logic to refute her with. If she really lacks nothing, there's no reason for her to want more."

"…So is she right, then?"

"…Right or wrong aside — she's consistent, within her own logic, right now. I find that both frightening and admirable."

I agreed with that. Eikon was more honest than anyone. Only that honesty wasn't moving anywhere.

---

That night I wrote in my notebook: "Anemos waits, and Eikon doesn't
change. Both stay beside each other." Reading it back, it seemed like
a sentence without much meaning, but it stayed with me strangely.

Beneath that sentence I added one more line. "Maybe waiting and not
changing meet at the same place, somehow." This wasn't a precise
sentence either. It was just the closest thing to what I'd felt that
day.

Eikon confirmed it once more as she left. "…I still haven't taken
release. It'll be the same next time I come." I copied that down into
my notebook exactly as she said it.
