# Chapter 19. They Never Read Each Other

> _Previously_ — Histor laid out three counterarguments. That
> traveling stories naturally resemble each other, that the recorders
> might have read each other's records, that it could have spread by
> word of mouth. Mone couldn't refute him. The two agreed to check
> whether the recorders had actually read one another's records.

The next day, we went through all fifteen combinations one by one.

The method was simple. Check within each record for any trace of
another city's record being mentioned, or any record of the two
recorders having actually met, asking Arche and checking again against
the physical copies.

"…This is going to take a while," I said.

"…Yes. But we have to, to be sure."

I redrew the chart from morning, bigger than the day before. I laid
the five city names across and down, and left a box at each
intersection to mark whether it had been confirmed. Adding Loop made
six, so there were fifteen boxes.

"…I don't think one day is enough to fill all this in," I said.

"…That's fine. It can take a few days. No rush."

That answer surprised me. He always liked being accurate, but he
wasn't someone in a hurry. *At the time I took that for ease. Looking
back now, it wasn't ease so much as an attitude that it wouldn't
matter much to him which way the answer came out.*

---

The first was Haven and the Reckoning. Arche had already given us the
answer the day before. They'd never read each other. The quote on the
Reckoning's side was presumed to have come by word of mouth.

"…This one's certain, at least," I said.

"…Yes. First confirmation done."

Partway through, I asked Arche a few more things. Arche answered fast
each time, but the answers weren't always tidy. Words like "confirmed,"
"unconfirmed," "partially confirmed" kept mixing in. The vagueness
frustrated me a little.

"…Arche, are 'unconfirmed' and 'confirmed not to be' different
things?" I asked.

*Unconfirmed means there is insufficient material to determine the
matter. Confirmed not to be means the matter has been checked and
found not to be true.*

I was a little surprised, hearing that distinction. Arche split
knowing from not-knowing as strictly as Histor did. *I felt this
resemblance between the two again in this chapter. But this time I
felt it a little differently. Arche had no choice but to divide things
that way — that was its nature. Histor was a person, and he'd chosen
to divide things that way.*

The second was Haven and the Harvest. Histor laid the two records side
by side and cross-checked. It took a while.

"…This one's never been read either," he said. "…The timing doesn't
work. The Harvest's record was already finished before Haven's record
was even written."

The third, Haven and the Preserve. This one was different. Arche
answered that the two had actually met. Because Speon had passed
through both cities.

"…So the well story comes from here," I said.

"…Yes. This isn't so much reading each other's records as it is the
same person carrying it along. Still, a connection is a connection."

I imagined Speon as I wrote that in the chart. A man who'd gone from
city to city along the water channel, digging wells. I'd never met
him. But it struck me as remarkable that a story had stayed behind in
the places he'd passed through. It wasn't only water that stayed
behind when a person passed by — the story flowed along with it too.

"…Where is Speon now?" I asked.

"…I don't know. It's not in the records. Maybe he's still off digging
a well somewhere."

I liked hearing him say that. It was rare for Histor to speak so
easily about something he wasn't certain of.

---

Fourth, Haven and the Equity. This one took a long time to check.
There was a character in the margin of the Equity's record that looked
like it might be another city's name. Histor stared at that character
for a long while and finally asked Arche.

"…Arche, is this character definitely referring to Haven?"

*The character in question has been damaged in transcription and only
part of it survives. It could plausibly be Haven, or it could be an
entirely different place name. It cannot be determined.*

"…If it's Haven, doesn't that count as a connection?" I asked.

"…It does. But since it can't be confirmed, I'm not counting it as a
connection."

I found it strange that he wouldn't use this in his own favor. With
one damaged character, he could have marked the box the way that
suited his own conclusion, and he didn't.

"…You could count this as Haven, Histor, it'd be in your favor."

He looked at me for a moment.

"…What's in my favor isn't necessarily what's right."

We went on. Haven and the Equity had never been read. Haven and Loop
had. Loop was the oldest city, and it kept coming up, over and over
across the combinations, that the other cities had drawn on Loop's
records to some degree.

"…Loop's connected to everything," I said.

"…Of course. It's where it all began. The other five learned about
the water channel through Loop."

The Reckoning and the Harvest had never read each other. The Reckoning
and the Preserve had never read each other. The Reckoning and the
Equity had one short mention, but it was no more than a misspelled
name — not evidence they'd actually read each other.

"…Put this one in unconfirmed too," Histor said.

I caught something in his voice. Not disappointment, not
satisfaction. Just the voice of someone still checking.

At lunchtime the Keeper stopped by and looked at the chart we were
drawing. This time, a little longer than usual.

"…Confirming all of this?" the Keeper asked.

"…Yes," Histor answered. "…Whether they read each other or not."

"…Sounds interesting. Let me know how it turns out."

"…Are you curious?" I asked.

"…I am. But whatever the result is, I don't think I'll be surprised."

"…Why not?"

The Keeper shrugged.

"…Either way makes sense. It makes sense if they all read each other,
and it makes sense if none of them did. So I'm less curious about the
result than about what the two of you do once you have it."

I let that pass at the time. *Reading it again now, the Keeper already
knew. That the result wouldn't change Histor. The Keeper wasn't
watching the result — the Keeper was watching us.*

---

By evening we'd finished checking all fifteen.

The results were these. Loop was connected to all five other cities.
That made sense — Loop was the oldest city, the start of the water
channel. Among the other five, it was different.

Haven and the Preserve were connected through Speon. That much was
certain. But Haven and the Reckoning, Haven and the Harvest, Haven and
the Equity, the Reckoning and the Harvest, the Reckoning and the
Preserve, the Reckoning and the Equity, the Harvest and the Preserve,
the Harvest and the Equity, the Preserve and the Equity — all nine of
those combinations had never read one another.

"…Let me sum this up," Histor said. He began drawing a new chart on
paper.

"…Leaving Loop out, looking only at the other five — Haven, the
Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity. Of these five, only
one pair is connected — Haven and the Preserve. The other four, the
Reckoning, the Harvest, the Equity, and Haven, never read the other
three."

---

I studied that summary again.

"…So you mean four of the five cities were completely ignorant of one
another?"

"…Precisely — the Reckoning, the Harvest, and the Equity never read
each other, and never read Haven either. Only the Preserve is
connected to Haven."

"…Then what about the second possibility you raised, Histor? The one
about reading each other and being influenced?"

He was quiet for a moment. He looked at the paper again.

"…It's weaker now. Nine combinations came back never-read. That means
the possibility doesn't explain the whole thing."

For a moment I expected something. I thought he'd finally admit it. I'd
actually been waiting, in my heart, for this very moment. Once it was
shown that four of them had never read each other, I thought he'd say
at least, "…Then this really does seem real." A sixteen-year-old's
expectation.

---

"…So? Do you admit it now?"

He looked at me. His expression was complicated.

"…Admit what?"

"…That this person is real."

"…No."

I was startled by how short that answer was.

"…But you just said the second possibility got weaker."

"…It's weaker, that's true. But it hasn't disappeared. The first
possibility still stands — that traveling stories naturally resemble
each other. And so does the third, that it spread by word of mouth.
That's harder to confirm than reading a record directly. What travels
by word leaves no trace."

I hit a wall again at that. It was true. What's passed by word of
mouth leaves no evidence.

"…Then, Histor," I asked, "what would you need to check to rule out
even the third possibility?"

"…I don't think it can be ruled out. Probably. What travels by word
leaves no trace. It can't be proven or disproven."

"…So we'll never know?"

"…That part, yes."

I heard that answer and felt something strange. It looked as though he
had built himself a place he couldn't get out of. If he left one
possibility standing that could never be proven, he could stay safe
there forever.

*I didn't see this clearly that day. I do now. Histor wasn't beaten by
logic. He'd left, inside his own logic, one last door that he himself
had built. A door no one could open. Because it couldn't be proven.*

---

"…Histor," I said. "…I don't understand right now. It's confirmed
that four of them never read each other. Why hasn't your position
changed?"

He looked at me for a long while. Not an angry face. A serious one.

"…Mone, I don't lie. I've told you exactly what I found. That the
four never read each other — that's true. I haven't hidden that from
you."

"…But."

"…But it doesn't carry me to belief. I still think there are other
ways to explain it. And even if every one of those ways were also
ruled out, I don't think that would automatically become belief."

Something felt like it was collapsing in me, hearing that. *Looking
back now, it wasn't him collapsing anything. I'd expected he'd change
his mind if beaten by logic. It was that expectation that collapsed.*

---

"…Then what would it take for you to believe?" I asked.

He took a long time to answer that question.

"…I don't know. I really don't know. If I knew what that was, I'd be
more at ease too."

That answer sounded strangely sad to me. He didn't even know what he
was waiting for. The fact that four had never read each other, the
fact that a man appeared in all six records — none of it had yet
reached him as evidence.

"…Then what am I supposed to do?" I asked. The question came out
before I'd thought about it.

"…Me?"

"…Yes. What do I have to show you, Histor? If six records aren't
enough, if four never reading each other isn't enough."

He looked at me steadily for a moment.

"…You don't need to show me anything, Mone. I never asked you to prove
anything to me. I'm just not believing right now, that's all."

"…Then why do you look so sad?"

He looked a little surprised at that question. He hadn't seemed to
know he looked sad.

"…I'm not sad. I'm just trying to be accurate."

I believed him. I knew he didn't lie. But it occurred to me for the
first time that day that being accurate and being all right don't
always go together.

*Writing this chapter, I look again at myself that day. I wanted to
refute him. I thought if I pushed hard enough with facts, he'd change.
He did admit the fact — that the four had never read each other. And
having admitted it, he stayed exactly where he was.*

*I know now. "Refuted" is a strange word to begin with. If a person can
be moved somewhere by logic, that means they were standing on that
logic to begin with. Histor wasn't standing on logic. He was standing
on fact. Fact hadn't changed. So neither did he. I learned that
difference in my body that day.*

We showed the chart to the Keeper again, after redrawing it neatly.
The Keeper studied it for a long while and said,

"…What's changed because of this?"

"…Histor's position hasn't changed," I answered.

"…Then nothing's changed, has it."

"…No." This time Histor answered. "…I know now that the four never
read each other. I didn't know that yesterday. That's changed."

The Keeper heard that and gave a small laugh. A low laugh.

"…Yes, that's changed too."

I didn't know what that laugh meant at the time. *I know now. The
Keeper was watching knowledge pile up a little at a time. Even if it
never led to belief, knowledge piled up as knowledge. The Keeper never
looked lightly on that accumulation itself.*

On the way home, passing the water channel, I ran back over the boxes
we'd filled in that day. Six of the fifteen boxes were connected, and
most of those were through Loop. Four of the five other cities had
truly known nothing of one another. And yet the same person appeared
in all six records. I still thought that was strange. However many
possibilities Histor left standing, my heart kept leaning toward the
strangeness.

On the way home I wrote in my paper. Four of the five cities never
read each other. Histor admitted it. But his position hasn't changed. I
think I understand now what that means. Facts don't move a person. Not
by themselves, at least.

*I added one more line beneath that. "Then what does move a person?"
That day I didn't know the answer. Even now, writing this book, I
can't write the answer in a single sentence. But I believe there's a
trace of that answer somewhere in this record. Whether "believe" is
even the right word to use, I'm still not sure myself.*

That night I opened the chart again. Six connected out of fifteen
boxes, and of those, only one — the one through Speon — was certain.
The rest were mostly unconfirmed or presumed word of mouth. I ran my
finger over those boxes and counted them. I counted three times. Each
time the same number came out. I didn't know then that this was
similar to the checking Histor did. I know now. The two of us were
doing the same thing with different hands.
