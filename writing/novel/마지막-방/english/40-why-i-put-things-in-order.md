# Chapter 40. Why I Put Things in Order

> _Previously_ — Histor asked, "…What changes if you open it?" I
> answered, "…I don't know what changes. But if it stays shut it just
> stays here." He didn't argue with that.

Around that time, one small trouble came up in the repository.

A record that had come in couldn't find its place. The city name on
the cover sheet didn't match the contents inside. It said it had come
from Haven, but the inside was about the Reckoning.

I stood there holding it for a long while. I asked Arche.

"…Where does this go?"

*The original source of this record is unclear. Classification is
pending.*

Hearing that, I set the record on the temporary shelf. But walking
home that night, it kept bothering me.

---

The next day I took the record out and read it again.

After reading it a few times, I thought I understood. The cover sheet
was the mistake. The contents inside were correct. It was a Reckoning
record — someone had simply attached the wrong cover.

I fixed it and put it in its proper place. It wasn't a big thing. But
doing that work, I felt something strange. I felt at ease. I could
really feel the tension leave my body.

The Keeper passed by and saw that, and asked.

"…Something good happen?"

"…No. I just found this its place."

"…Why so happy about that?"

I only laughed at that. I didn't know the exact reason myself. I just
liked it. A cover sheet finding its right place and sitting on its
shelf. I liked that.

The Keeper walked on without saying more. But that evening he came
back and left one more thing.

"…Mone, I know that thing you love. I was the same way."

"…What thing?"

"…Finding things their place. I've stayed here this long because I
love that too."

I let that go at the time. Later, I'd come back and ask about it
again.

---

That afternoon I asked the Keeper again.

"…That thing you said earlier. Did you stay here this long because you
lost something too?"

The Keeper's hand stopped, and he looked at me.

"…Something like that. I won't go into the details. It's an old story,
and it involves other people too."

"…Even just a little."

He hesitated a moment, then spoke.

"…I failed to hold onto something too. A long time ago. After that, I
came to love holding onto things. Coming here, at least the things
here — those, I can hold onto."

I didn't press him further. I understood that what he'd said was all
he could give.

"…But," I said. "Lately I think maybe that's not all of it. Just
holding on."

"…What do you mean?"

"…I don't know. If you only hold on, in the end no one gets to use it,
right? That's started to scare me too. As much as losing does."

The Keeper looked at me for a long while. And then he made an
expression I'd never seen from him before. I couldn't tell if it was
surprise or gladness.

"…Mone, when did you grow up like this."

I laughed at that.

---

*I held onto that moment for a long time. Was fixing one cover sheet
really something to feel so at ease about? That day, for the first
time, I properly put that question to myself.*

---

That afternoon, Histor heard the story and asked.

"…Is it really that important? Arche would have found it again on its
own anyway."

"…That's true."

"…So why did it put you so at ease?"

I couldn't answer that right away. Histor was always like this. He'd
ask something lightly, and it would become a heavy question for me.

---

That evening, after finishing work, I didn't go straight home. I sat
in the spot behind the repository where you could see the water
channel.

I'd written, early on, that I'd moved nine times. And that each time,
I'd lost something. Eleven things, I'd written, and four of them I
didn't even know what they'd been.

*When I wrote that sentence, I thought it was just background — a way
of explaining how I'd happened to get good at putting things in order.
That evening, for the first time, I saw clearly that it hadn't been
background. It had been the reason.*

I hadn't taken this work because I was good at putting things in
order. I'd chosen this work because I was afraid of losing things.

---

I counted those eleven things again.

A spoon, from the second move. A blanket, from the fourth. A wooden
doll, from the sixth. After that it blurred. The seventh, eighth,
ninth. Only the feeling that something had gone missing remained; I
couldn't remember what.

I'd been calling those, for a long time, simply "the forgotten
things." That day, for the first time, I tried to count them properly.
I took out my notebook, wrote down each of the nine moves one by one,
and beside each, what I'd lost.

Two. Three. Five. Six. Eight. Nine. At the ninth, my hand stopped.

At the ninth move, I truly didn't know what I'd lost. I still don't.

---

That night I called my mother.

"…Mom. Did we lose something at our ninth move?"

My mother paused a moment.

"…Why are you asking that all of a sudden?"

"…Just curious."

"…Well. I don't remember clearly. We were in such a rush that time.
Your father's work had been decided all of a sudden too."

"…I don't remember either. But I have this feeling something went
missing."

My mother was quiet for a long while again.

"…Mone, you don't have to remember that. It's fine to let some things
just flow on."

I didn't know how to answer that, so I just said I understood. After
hanging up, I thought about that for a long time. *It's fine to let
things flow on.* I had never once said that to myself, this whole
time.

---

*Writing this part, I felt the temptation to invent something. To make
up something plausible for what I'd lost at the ninth move. I decided
not to. I truly don't know. That's the most honest thing I can write in
this record. What I don't know, I write that I don't know.*

I looked at that blank for a long while. Four blanks. Lost things
without even names.

That day, for the first time, I had this thought. I hadn't taken this
work because I was good at putting things in order. I'd taken this
work because I never wanted to make a blank like that again.

---

I told Histor that thought. The next day, when the two of us were
alone in the repository.

"…I realized something yesterday," I said.

"…What?"

"…This thing I do, putting things in order. It's not because I'm
diligent. When I was young, I moved nine times, and lost something
each time. Eleven things. Four of them, I don't even know what they
were. I started putting things in order because I was afraid of that."

Histor listened quietly.

"…Why did you realize that only now?" he asked.

"…I don't know. It's just that yesterday, fixing one cover sheet, I
felt so at ease. That felt strange, so I kept thinking about it."

---

"…Is that a bad reason?" Histor asked.

I paused at that question.

"…A bad reason?"

"…You've looked, this whole time, like you did this work out of some
sense of mission. But hearing this now, you're just doing it because
you're afraid of losing things. Isn't that a little… a selfish reason?"

That didn't hurt me. If anything, it felt exact.

"…That's right," I said. "It's a selfish reason. I took this work
because I was afraid of losing things. Not for other people. For
myself."

---

*I want to write this part the most honestly of anything in this
record. I didn't begin this work out of goodness. I began it out of
fear. Moving from place to place, losing something each time — that
frightened me. I soothed that fear by putting things in order. That
was all of it.*

*Anyone who's read this record this far will know that I wasn't the
only one like this. Xene was afraid of recording and stopped, then
started again. Anemos was afraid of leaving someone's side, and so
stayed. I was afraid of order coming apart, and so I put things in
order. None of us started with some grand reason from the beginning.*

---

Histor said nothing for a moment, hearing that.

"…But," he said after a while, "a thing that started for a selfish
reason doesn't have to stay only a selfish reason."

"…What do you mean?"

"…What you put in order, this whole city uses now. All six cities'
records are sitting here in order because of you. You started it out
of your own fear, but the result isn't yours alone anymore."

I turned that over for a long time. It was true. I'd begun for myself.
And somehow it had become work for other people too.

"…What about you?" I asked. "Reading all six records — did that have
some reason at first too?"

He thought a moment before answering.

"…My family has always handled records. Since my grandfather's time.
I grew up thinking that was just natural. I was taught you had to know
things properly."

"…Is that all?"

"…At first. But reading and reading, without noticing, another reason
grew in me."

"…What was it?"

Histor chose his words for a moment.

"…I hated the thought of anyone being remembered wrong. When what a
person actually said was different from what people remembered them
saying. I felt like that was doing that person wrong."

Hearing that, I understood a little better why he was someone who
quoted things so exactly.

"…Is that a selfish reason too?" I asked.

He laughed.

"…Probably. Maybe I just hated being wrong."

We laughed together at that. I felt, that day, that we both stood in a
similar place. We'd begun for different reasons, but now we'd each
arrived somewhere neither reason alone could explain.

---

That night, I wrote this in my notebook.

*I began in order to hold onto things. So as not to lose them. But now
I know that isn't only the work of holding on. What I've put in order
has, in the end, been for the moment someone else comes looking for
it. I was someone who held on, and at the same time, someone preparing
to hand it over.*

I read that sentence twice. *Someone who holds on* and *someone
preparing to hand it over.* This whole time, I'd only thought I was
the first.

---

That evening I stood in front of the seal again.

Standing there, I thought again about why I kept coming to this room.
In the last chapter, I said I couldn't open it because I didn't know
what would change. Now it felt a little different.

It wasn't that I hadn't opened it because I was afraid to. I'd been
hesitating because I didn't know what I'd have to do after opening it.
That day, for the first time, I recognized that difference.

*Through nine moves, I lost eleven things. I became someone who put
things in order to stop that. But in front of this room, I was
learning something else. That nothing finishes if you only hold on.
That someday, what you've held onto has to be given to someone.*

*I didn't push that thought all the way through that day. I felt I
wasn't ready yet. But that night, for the first time, I thought of this
room not as "the room I hold onto" but as "the room I will someday
hand over." That thought stayed with me a long time.*

I went home and opened my drawer. In it were the few things I'd kept
through all those moves. An old key. A few old photographs. A
handkerchief I'd used as a child.

I took them out one by one. All of them were things I'd held onto.
Things I'd never once thought of giving to anyone.

*That night, looking at those things, I asked myself again what I was
really afraid of. Was I afraid of losing things, or was I afraid of
being left with nothing useful to anyone. I didn't find that answer
that day.*

*But putting those things back in the drawer, I had this thought for
the first time. Would a day come when I'd give even these to someone.
That thought was unfamiliar to me. At the same time, not entirely
unfamiliar.*

---

The next morning, I arrived at the repository a little earlier than
usual. No one else was there yet. I stood in front of the last room's
door and thought back over the night before.

*From someone who holds on, to someone who hands over.* I said that
sentence out loud. No one was there to hear it. But saying it aloud, I
felt like I'd taken a step forward.

I still hadn't opened the seal. That would be the next chapter's work.
But that morning, walking into this room for the first time, I didn't
feel afraid. Instead I felt like I was getting ready. That difference
was a large one, to me.

I sat in front of the seal for a while, then asked Arche.

"…Arche, am I allowed to open this?"

*Permission is not required. There is no function preventing access
to this item.*

"…Then why has no one opened it?"

*My records hold no answer to that question.*

Hearing that answer, I laughed quietly. That morning, for the first
time, I understood clearly that I had to find that answer not from
Arche, but from myself.
