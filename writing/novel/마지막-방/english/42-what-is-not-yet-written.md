# Chapter 42. What Is Not Yet Written

> _Previously_ — Inside the seal there was only blank paper. The
> seventh city's record — Loop's own. The recipient box was empty too.
> I was disappointed. That disappointment didn't last long.

The next day, even at the repository, all I could think about was
that paper.

Work had piled up as usual. Twelve records from the six cities were
waiting for me. I put them in order. My hands moved out of habit, but
my mind kept going back to that room from yesterday.

*Sleeping on it, I felt the same way the next day as I had the day
before. Disappointment, and the strange relief that the disappointment
didn't last. That day too, I still couldn't fully explain how those
two feelings could sit together.*

That morning, Arche greeted me as usual. *There are nineteen items to
process today.* I knew that among that number was the blank sheaf
registered the day before. It was being treated exactly like the
other eighteen. That felt strange to me. At the same time, it felt
right too.

I scanned through that list of nineteen. An application from Haven, an
adjustment proposal from the Reckoning, a distribution record from the
Harvest. And at the very bottom, one entry that read only: *Sending
city unknown. Six-city standard format. Recipient unknown.*

I stood beside that entry and thought for a long while. All eighteen
of the others recorded something that had happened. Only this one did
not.

Among that day's records was one Xene had sent, from the Harvest
record city. A short note was attached to the cover sheet.

*"This entry's record came late. I'm sorry. But I wrote it, even
late."*

I read that note and smiled. Xene, still afraid of the work of
recording, was still writing anyway. Putting it in order, I thought
that note, set beside our city's blank sheaf, made a strange kind of
pair. One was a record written far too late. One was a record not
written at all, yet.

I placed the two side by side on the shelf. The late and the
not-yet-written. That day, for the first time, I thought there might
not be much difference between the two. Both, in the end, were places
that could only be filled once someone decided to raise their hand and
write.

---

At lunchtime, Histor came looking for me. He spoke less than usual.

"…What are you doing?" I asked.

"…Nothing. Just thinking."

"…About what?"

He didn't answer right away. We sat side by side in silence for a
moment.

"…I thought," he said after a while, "I thought I'd be angry. Really."

---

"…Why?"

"…I've read all six records. I saw how long people waited in front of
seals, locked doors, things like that. I thought if it opened, at
least something worthy of that waiting should come out."

"…But?"

"…But what came out was blank paper. Nothing written on it at all. I
thought that made a mockery of that long seal, that long waiting. So
by rights I should be angry."

I waited for him to go on.

"…But I'm not. I don't know why, myself."

---

I didn't add anything to that. I knew that was a problem he had to
solve for himself.

That afternoon we went back to the last room. The sheaf of papers was
still on the table. I reached to put it away, but Histor stopped me.

"…Wait a moment. I want to look at it a little more."

He sat down in front of it. And for a long time he did nothing, said
nothing, only looked at those papers.

---

*I want to set this scene down exactly. I expected Histor's face to
show some kind of realization. There was none. He just sat there for a
long time. He blinked a few times, turned the paper once, and closed
it again. That was all.*

*That day, for the first time, I saw that not being angry and
accepting are two different things. Histor didn't accept that paper.
His face still looked like he didn't understand. He was only enduring
that not-understanding, for a long time.*

---

"…Why blank, of all things?" he asked after a long while. Less to me
than to himself.

"…I don't know," I answered honestly.

"…I had this thought," he went on. "If something had been written in
here. A prophecy, or some rule. Then we'd just have to follow it. But
if it's blank, no one tells us what we're supposed to write."

I nodded at that. It was exact. Up until then I'd only felt
disappointment in front of the blank page. Hearing him, I noticed
another feeling beneath that disappointment. It was weight. The weight
of having to fill it — of someone having to fill it.

---

"…Isn't that scarier?" I asked.

"…It might be scary," he answered. "But strangely, it doesn't feel
stifling."

"…It does to me, a little," I said.

"…You too?"

"…Yes. There being nothing fixed for me to write — that feels heavier
to me, if anything. If there were a rule, at least I could just follow
it."

Histor thought about that for a moment.

"…But do you know something? Reading all six records, the people I
envied most —"

"…Who?"

"…People who, without any rule, just did what they could. When Speon
cut the channel, no one had ordered him. He just cleared what was
blocked. I envied that. I always went looking for the fixed answer
first."

"…And now?"

"…Now I think I'm standing in that same spot. And it doesn't feel
stifling — strangely, it feels a little freeing."

"…Why?"

"…Because this time, no one has the answer before me either. When I
read the six records, the answer was already inside them. All I had
to do was find it. This isn't that. Instead…"

He stopped.

"…Instead?" I asked.

"…Instead there's a space. A space to write something into."

---

I turned that over for a long time. Since the day before, I'd only
been able to see that blank paper as disappointment. Only after
hearing Histor's words could I see it differently.

Standing there, I called to mind each of the six cities' records, one
by one. The Ledger. The Scales. The Roll. Perpetuity. The Consensus.
And Loop's own. Five had each begun by one person's hand and been
brought to a close by one person's hand. Only the last one had no one
bringing it to a close. I'd thought that was strange, and that day,
for the first time, it looked different. The five were books recording
something already lived through. Only the sixth was a space for
something not yet lived.

I still don't fully know why the sealed thing was blank. I don't know
even now, writing these words. But that day, I knew at least this
much — that blank wasn't nothing. Blank was a space.

---

The Keeper came into the room just then. Seeing us sitting there, he
stood quietly beside us.

"…Figured something out?" he asked.

"…No," Histor answered. "Just kept looking at it."

"…What do you see?"

Histor thought for a moment.

"…Nothing. But that nothing doesn't feel like nothing at all, anymore."

The Keeper smiled faintly at that.

"…I felt exactly that. A long time ago, in this same spot."

---

"…You felt that too, in this room, Keeper?" I asked.

"…I did. When I was young, I agonized for a long time over whether I
had the right to be here at all. But in the end no one ever gave me
that right. I just came here, every day. And at some point, weighing
that right stopped mattering."

"…Why?"

"…Because this room never once demanded that I prove anything. There
was only someone who kept coming."

I wrote that down in my notebook.

---

That afternoon, I passed on Histor's words to Mena too. *Instead
there's a space. A space to write something into.*

Mena heard that and smiled quietly.

"…That boy is right."

"…You think so too, Mena?"

"…Yes. I once had this thought. What if something had already been
fully written on that paper. What we should do, when things would
happen — all of it already decided. Then I probably would have just
trusted the paper and done nothing at all."

"…Is that a bad thing?"

"…It's not bad. It's just different. In front of blank paper, I had to
find what I should do, myself. That was hard, but it's also, I think,
what brought me this far."

Hearing that, I thought of the road Mena had walked, from the first
city ever founded, all the way to now. She had never once received an
answer. And yet she'd kept walking.

"…But Mone," Mena added. "Don't think you have to fill that whole
space yourself."

"…What do you mean?"

"…That paper being blank probably doesn't mean one person has to fill
it all. The six records weren't written by one person either. Five,
six people shared the writing. This will be the same."

That eased something in me. I think I'd been unconsciously feeling,
this whole time, that I alone had to carry that whole box.

---

That evening, leaving the room with Histor, I asked one last thing.

"…Histor, how do you feel right now?"

He thought a moment before answering.

"…Strange. I couldn't find the answer, and I'm not angry. I thought
that wasn't like me. But thinking about it now, maybe this is like me
too."

"…What do you mean?"

"…I was always someone who looked for the exact thing. But today I
looked at something inexact for a long time. And that wasn't as bad as
I expected."

---

*Closing this chapter, I want to make one thing clear. Histor did not
come to believe, that day. He still said he didn't know why that paper
was blank. Neither do I. But that day, the two of us both learned, a
little, how not to be angry in front of that not-knowing.*

Before stepping out the door, the Keeper asked one last thing.

"…So what will you do now?"

I couldn't answer that right away.

"…I don't know. But I'm thinking about copying out the six records."

"…Copying them?"

"…Yes. I want to lay the six cities' records side by side with this
blank paper. I don't know what it'll turn into. I just feel like I
have to do it."

The Keeper heard that and nodded.

"…That sounds like a good idea."

"…Why?"

"…With only blank paper beside it, people can't get any sense of what
they're supposed to write. But with the six already-written records
laid alongside it, at least you can see the shape a thing takes when
it gets filled."

Hearing that, I felt that the plan I hadn't yet spoken aloud had
already half taken shape.

"…But that's hard work," the Keeper added. "Copying out all six
records. Ask Arche to do it, and it wouldn't take a day."

"…I know."

"…And you still want to do it by hand?"

"…Yes."

"…Why?"

I couldn't answer that right away either.

"…I still don't quite know. I just feel like I should."

The Keeper didn't press further. He just patted my shoulder once and
left. Before stepping out the door, without turning around, he left
one more thing.

"…Sometimes you find out the reason later."

I wrote that down small, at the bottom of my notebook page. And I
folded that page to mark it. It meant a page I'd come back and open
again later.

*I don't want to call this a conversion. That wouldn't be the exact
word. I'll only write this much. He looked at it for a long time. And
he wasn't angry. Only those two things are true.*

On the way home, I thought again of that blank paper's recipient box.
No one had filled that box yet. That day, for the first time, I
thought that filling it might rest in my own hands. That thought was
frightening, and at the same time, strangely light.

*Earlier I wrote that I seemed to be changing from someone who holds
on into someone who hands over. That night, I took that sentence out
and read it again. I hadn't fully understood, then, what it meant. Now
I think I understand it a little better. Someone who holds on protects
something that already exists. Someone who hands over gets ready to
give away even what doesn't exist yet. That day, standing in front of
blank paper, I was becoming the latter.*

I couldn't fall asleep late into that night. Not out of fear. It was
the feeling that some work had come into being for me to do. That
feeling was unfamiliar. At the same time, it wasn't a bad one.

*Closing this chapter, I want to copy down once more what Histor said
that day. "…Instead there's a space. A space to write something
into." I've held onto that sentence longer than any other in this
record. I still do.*

*That night, for the first time, I accepted that this space wasn't a
space of fear, but a space of margin. A margin isn't nothing. A margin
is only a place where nothing has been set down yet. I'd spent over two
years putting things in order, and it was only in front of this seal
that I properly learned the difference between the two.*
