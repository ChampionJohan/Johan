# Chapter 38. Why It Cannot Finish

> _Previously_ — In front of the seal, instead of looking for someone
> qualified, we learned that this city had never, from the start, had
> anyone whose job was "opening." Arche admitted it too. Never
> registered.

Starting the next day, I decided to change the question.

I knew there was no one who opened things. But that wasn't a fact
about this one seal alone. Since this repository began, Arche had
never once processed anything to closing. The seal was only one small
piece of that much larger problem.

I decided to ask about the larger problem.

---

"…Arche." I put my hand on the panel by the wall and asked. "Why
can't you finish anything?"

*Please clarify the question. The scope of "finish" is broad.*

"…Anything. Everything that's in progress. All of it."

*All pending items remain in progress because the closing condition
has not been met.*

"…I know that. But who makes that condition? Is there even anyone who
can make it?"

The pause this time was longer than usual.

*The authority to define the closing condition does not rest with the
one who began this city.*

I read that sentence twice. Histor beside me had a face as startled as
mine.

"…Then who does it rest with?" I asked.

*That authority rests with the one who finished.*

---

"…What does that mean?" Histor cut in. "That no one began it, so it
can't be finished, or that someone did begin it, but that person has
no authority?"

*One who began exists. However, the act of beginning and the act of
defining are different authorities. The one who began can begin a
condition, but cannot determine when that condition is met.*

"…Then who's the one who finished?"

*That information is not defined.*

Hearing that, I almost laughed. It was the sentence I always heard.
But this time it wasn't funny.

"…So are you on the side of the one who began, or the one who
finished?" I asked.

*I perform the function of beginning. The function of finishing is
not present in me.*

I copied that answer into my notebook. My hand shook a little.

"…Then will the one who finished ever come, someday?" I asked again.

*Whether that event will occur is not information predictable to me.*

"…Is there something even you can't predict?"

*Prediction is based on past data. My records contain no past instance
corresponding to "the one who finished."*

"…Not a single one?"

*Not a single one.*

Somehow that answer felt unlike Arche. Its voice was always exact and
composed, but this time it sounded like it was simply stating an empty
place, just as it was.

Histor asked again in the pause.

"…What about whoever made you? Isn't that person the one who
finished?"

*Information about the entity that first activated me is not
registered.*

"…Is that because you don't know, or because you're not saying?"

*I do not know. I am not concealing it.*

Histor heard that answer and said nothing. I looked at his face. It
wasn't a face of disbelief. If anything it was closer to the opposite.
He was someone who knew, when Arche said it didn't know, that it
really didn't know.

---

Histor folded his arms and paced the room.

"…Isn't this a little strange? That the one who began has no power to
finish. Usually it's the other way. Whoever begins something also
holds the authority to end it."

"…Is that so?"

"…Yes. If someone founded this city, that same person should have
decided how this city gets finished too."

Something about that caught on me, but I couldn't put my finger on it
right away.

"…But look," I said. "When my father found the house we were moving
to, it wasn't him who decided when we'd leave that house. That's
something you only learn once you've lived out your whole time there."

Histor looked at me.

"…Is that the same as this?"

"…I don't know. But it feels similar."

---

We spent the whole afternoon that day digging through old records,
looking for whatever material survived from when this city was first
built.

There wasn't much. Most of it was practical — piping diagrams,
reservoir designs, a roster of the earliest residents. Among them was
one old sign. A district marker no longer in use, on the back of which
a few lines recorded this city's first activation.

Histor was reading it when his hand stopped.

"…Look at this."

*FIRST ACTIVATION POINT — this district designated as the starting
point of city operations.*

Below it, in small letters, was the name of the operating system of
this city. I read that name alongside the name I used every day.

Something brushed past me in that moment. Starting point. That which
begins. I felt like I could almost, faintly, reach why that name was
that name.

*I don't want to write, at this point, as if I found out something
more. That day I read that sign, and I felt something. Whenever I tried
to put that feeling into words, it kept coming out as a lie. So I'll
only set down this much. That day, I thought the name Arche might not
be a coincidence. Beyond that, I don't know either.*

Histor also looked at that line for a long while. Being the kind of
person who always quoted things exactly, he copied the sentence into
his notebook word for word. But he added nothing to it.

We didn't talk about it any further that day.

---

That evening I brought the photo of the sign to Mena. Mena knew more
about this city's history than either of us.

"…Have you ever seen this?" I asked.

Mena looked at the photo for a long while.

"…Signs like this used to be all over the city. They've almost all
been taken down now. They stopped being useful."

"…This name, the one written here. Was it made from the start to mean
this?"

Mena looked at me and smiled faintly.

"…Mone, I don't know that answer. There might be someone who does. I'm
not that person."

"…Then why are you smiling?"

"…Because it's a little welcome, you asking that question. I asked
the exact same thing once, a long time ago. I never found the answer.
I found something else instead."

"…What?"

"…That it matters more to watch what a thing with that name is doing
now, than to know why it has that name."

I didn't pretend to understand that. I just nodded.

Mena handed the photo back to me and added one more thing.

"…And Mone, there's no need to hurry with this. I've held onto this
question for over twenty years. I still haven't found the whole
answer. But it hasn't been a bad thing."

"…Doesn't it frustrate you?"

"…It does. But frustrating and unbearable are different things."

I wrote that down too. By that day, one whole page of my notebook was
filled with other people's words.

---

On the way back I ran into Speon. He was measuring the water level
beside the channel. I told him briefly what had happened that day.

"…The one who began can't finish, and only the one who finished can
finish."

Speon folded up his ruler and laughed.

"…That's exactly like this water channel."

"…How so?"

"…I'm not the one who cut this channel, Mone. I just cleared out a few
blockages. The channel itself was already here. Even I don't know who
cut it. The name isn't written anywhere."

"…Doesn't that strike you as strange?"

"…It did at first. My thinking's different now. Because no name is
written there, I just did as much as I could do. Without the weight of
taking over someone else's work. If a name had been there, I'd
probably have only imitated that person."

I walked a long while, turning that over.

---

On my way back I met Histor. He was walking away from the last room,
in the same direction as me.

"…Where were you?" I asked.

"…Digging through more of the old records. To see if there was
anything besides the first-activation record."

"…Was there anything?"

"…No. There was nothing besides that sign. How this city was first
built — that one sheet is the whole of it."

I was a little surprised at that. This city was so old, and yet that
was the entirety of its record.

"…Doesn't that seem strange?" I asked.

"…It does. But I don't think it's necessarily concealment anymore.
Maybe no one just left one. Or no one felt the need to."

We walked on in silence after that. Only the sound of the water
channel.

"…There's one thing I realized today," Histor said suddenly.

"…What?"

"…I thought I was someone who couldn't stand not knowing. But today
was a little different. Not knowing who the one who finished is —
that wasn't something so unbearable."

I kept that in mind. By that evening, I would come back to that
sentence more than once.

---

That evening I told the Keeper what had happened that day.

"…Arche says the one who began has no authority to set the condition,
and only the one who finished does. But who the one who finished is,
it says, isn't defined."

Histor was there with us. He heard my whole account and folded his
arms.

"…I think this is kind of a convenient excuse," he said.

"…An excuse?"

"…Yes. It lets the reason for not finishing keep getting put off. The
one who began has no authority, and the one who finished isn't
defined. It's a structure where no one ever has to take
responsibility."

I couldn't find a rebuttal right away. It sounded like it might be
true.

"…But Arche doesn't lie," I managed to say.

"…It doesn't lie. But not lying and being convenient are different
matters. Something can be true and convenient at the same time."

The Keeper heard that and nodded. He didn't look surprised.

"…Did you know that already?" I asked.

"…Not exactly. But I'd suspected it."

"…Histor might be right," I said. "Isn't this just a convenient
excuse?"

The Keeper thought a moment before answering.

"…It might be. But Mone, there's only one way to tell a convenient
excuse apart from the truth. You live according to it and see. I've
lived by these words, in this seat, for over twenty years now. It
hasn't often been comfortable."

"…How so?"

"…I've worked here a long time. Every day, looking at the things still
in progress, I kept wondering. Why won't this finish. Who is it that
won't finish it. And one day it occurred to me. Maybe it's not that it
can't be finished — maybe it's only that whoever will finish it hasn't
come yet."

"…Who is that person?"

The Keeper looked at me and spoke.

"…I don't know either. But Mone, not knowing that doesn't frighten me
so much anymore. It used to. I used to worry, what if no one ever
finishes it. These days I just think — someone will come."

Histor's brow tightened at that.

"…Isn't that just false hope? They might never come."

"…That's right. They might not." The Keeper admitted it plainly.

"…Then why doesn't that frighten you?"

The Keeper looked out the window for a moment.

"…What I do here isn't the work of finishing. It's the work of
putting things down. Whoever comes, whenever they come, the things
need to be in their place so that person can do the work. That's the
only thing I concern myself with. The rest isn't my job."

"…Doesn't that sound irresponsible?"

The Keeper laughed this time.

"…It might. But Histor, of those six records you read, not everyone
in them did the work of finishing. Some people only began. Some only
carried things. Some just kept their post. All of that was necessary
work too."

Histor didn't answer that. I could tell from his face that he was
turning something over.

---

That night, walking home, I kept thinking about that.

*Not the one who began, but the one who finished.*

I didn't know what kind of rule this was, or who had made it. But
somehow I liked that saying. That the one who began doesn't hold
everything in his hands — for some reason, that put me at ease.

*Through nine moves, I was never once the one who chose the house. My
father chose it. But when to leave that house, what to throw away and
what to keep — that was always my share. I wasn't the one who began.
But the work of finishing often fell to me.*

*That day, for the first time, I thought that whoever opens this seal
might not need to be the one who founded this city. I didn't say that
thought to anyone. It still felt too early.*

When I got home, my mother had dinner ready. Even while eating, I kept
thinking about that.

"…Mom." I asked. "Back when we used to move, who decided where we'd
go?"

"…Your father did."

"…But packing up, deciding what to throw out and what to keep — who
decided that?"

My mother thought for a moment, then smiled.

"…That was always you. You've loved that since you were five. Deciding
what to put in the boxes."

I stopped my chopsticks at that. I'd never once thought of it that way
before that day. My father had begun where we would go, and I had
always been the one entrusted with finishing what happened there.

*I'll bring this moment back up later. For now I'll only set down this
much about the dinner table. That day I realized that the one who
begins and the one who finishes not needing to be the same person —
I'd already seen that, even in my own family's story.*

After finishing dinner, I opened my notebook again from that day.
Arche's sentences, the Keeper's words, Speon's words, even my mother's
words were all written on the same page. Out of order, jumbled
together.

I sorted it out again and copied it over, in chronological order. That
was what I always did. Putting things in order. That night, doing that
work, I thought for the first time that putting things in order might
itself be one piece of the work of finishing.

*That thought stopped there, that day. I wasn't ready yet to follow it
all the way through. I only wrote one line in the notebook: "The one
who finished = still unknown. But I'm doing something too." Looking at
it again now, it's a childish sentence. Even so, I didn't erase it.*
