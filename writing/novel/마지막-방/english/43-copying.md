# Chapter 43. Copying

> _Previously_ — Histor wasn't angry in front of the blank paper.
> "…Instead there's a space. A space to write something into." I held
> onto that for a long time. That night, I thought about copying out
> the six records.

The next morning, I brought out all six records.

The Ledger, the Scales, the Roll, Perpetuity, the Consensus, and
Loop's own record. I laid them side by side on the table in the last
room. I set the blank sheaf beside them too. I readied a fresh sheaf
of paper.

"…What are you going to do?" Histor asked.

"…Copy them. All six."

His eyes went wide.

"…All of them? That's hundreds of pages."

"…I know."

---

I opened the first record and began to copy.

By hand. Letter by letter. Even copying the first sentence took time.
I wasn't someone with beautiful handwriting. I crossed things out and
rewrote them several times.

Arche, watching, spoke up.

*Mone, I can perform this task in your place. Copying the whole set
would take under three minutes.*

"…I know. But I'll do it."

*May I ask why?*

I stopped my hand at that question. Why. I had no exact answer to it.

"…No reason. I just want to."

*Understood.*

Arche didn't ask further.

A few days later, Arche asked again.

*Mone, I have checked your progress rate. At the current pace,
completion will take roughly one month. If I perform it instead, it
will be completed instantly, with no error. Would you like to
reconsider?*

I laughed a little, hearing that.

"…Are you worried about me?"

*I do not perform the function of worry. However, presenting an
alternative upon identifying a low-efficiency method is my default
behavior.*

"…I know. But this time I'll go with the low-efficiency method."

*Understood. I will continue.*

I liked that answer. Arche didn't try to stop me. It just accepted it,
exactly, as it was.

---

Histor watched all this from beside me.

"…Can I ask why too?" he said.

"…You can ask, but I don't really know the answer."

"…Arche is much faster."

"…I know."

"…Then why?"

I sat holding the pen, thinking for a moment. A few reasons came to
mind. That copying by hand would stay in memory longer. That if Arche
did it, it would be so easy it might end up feeling weightless. But
none of those reasons felt completely right.

"…I don't know," I finally said. "I just feel like it has to be by
hand."

Histor didn't press further. He just nodded and sat down beside me,
and began watching me copy.

---

The first day I managed only a few pages.

My wrist ached. My finger joints were stiff. That night I wrapped a
cloth around my wrist and slept.

My mother saw that and asked.

"…What did you do to make your hand like that?"

"…I'm copying something."

"…Can't Arche do that?"

"…It could."

My mother didn't press further. She just brought me something to warm
my wrist with. That night I wondered why she hadn't pressed me, but I
didn't ask. Maybe she too, like me, had once done something she
couldn't explain.

*I want to write these days as the most tedious part of this record.
Because they were tedious. Every morning I went to the repository and
wrote until evening. Nothing dramatic happened at all. My hand just
hurt, my shoulders knotted, my eyes grew blurry.*

The second and third days were similar. I copied out the first part of
the Ledger. Elpis and Zeteo's story. Copying those sentences by hand,
I felt something strange. Different from how it had felt reading it
the first time.

---

"…What's different?" Histor asked. He seemed to have noticed me
stopping my pen and staring off for a moment.

"…Reading and writing are different."

"…How?"

"…Sentences that pass by quickly when you read don't, when you write.
You have to make every letter with your hand. Elpis waiting by the
well — when I read that, it was just one paragraph. Copying it took a
long while. The whole time, I felt like I was there at that well."

Histor heard that and brought over a sheet of paper himself, and began
copying alongside me. I looked at him, surprised.

"…Can I do it too?" he asked.

"…Sure. But why?"

"…I want to see for myself whether what you said is true."

---

From that day we copied together.

We didn't talk much. We each just moved the page in front of us. Now
and then one of us would stop and look out the window, or shake out a
wrist.

*I remember those days clearly, even now. There was no great
conversation. We just sat side by side and wrote. That felt strangely
comfortable to me. Some silences are awkward, and some aren't. Those
days' silence was the latter kind.*

The Keeper came sometimes too and helped with a few pages. Mena came
one day as well. People took turns sharing that work a little at a
time. Even so, copying all six records took many days.

Zeteo and Elpis stopped by around that time too. Zeteo looked for a
long while at the part where his own name appeared, then silently took
up a pen. Watching him copy his own story, I felt he was still
searching for something. His face was still the face of someone who
didn't believe. But his hand pressed down hard as he wrote.

Elpis, beside him, simply put the papers in order. As always, he
stayed in the place of waiting.

---

Pheugon came for a day too and helped with a few pages. Copying the
verdict portion of the Scales record, he said one thing.

"…This is the sentence I wrote back then, but writing it again now,
with my own hand, it feels different somehow."

"…How so?" I asked.

"…Back then I wrote it with certainty. Now I'm just copying, but that
certainty feels a little heavy."

Trope didn't come. Instead he sent a short letter. *"Thank you for
copying my part too. I'm still not confident enough to sit in that
seat."* I set that letter side by side with the six records.

---

Xene and Lytron came for a day too. Xene's hand shook as she copied
the part where she'd stopped recording. Lytron, beside her, quietly
took on other pages. He no longer said, as a kind of boast, that he'd
never lost anything. He just wrote quietly.

Anemos and Eikon stopped by briefly too. Eikon still said he lacked
nothing. And yet he copied out to the end the few pages placed in
front of him before he left. Anemos, beside him, said with a smile,
"…Will you ask me that again, later too?" I didn't know exactly what
that meant, but somehow I felt I understood.

Hora and Tyche came too. Tyche still called it coincidence. And yet she
said she would copy out the last part of the Consensus with her own
hand. That contradiction didn't strike me as strange. It had always
been that way, in these records.

---

In the second week we copied the Roll and Perpetuity. The Roll's
sentences were stiff and dry. Registration numbers, review results,
grounds for ruling. Copying it, I retraced what Lytron had gone
through — the moment he was turned away at the door for lacking
qualification. Copying that sentence, I felt a chill at my fingertips.

Perpetuity's texture was different. The part Anemos had written moved
slowly, circling back to the same spot again and again. It felt to me
like it was showing how long he'd waited in that same spot. Eikon's
part was short and composed. The words "I lack nothing" repeated
several times. Copying that repetition exactly, I felt like the
repetition itself was saying something.

Around the third week, copying the Scales record, I stopped my hand.

It was the part Trope had written. The story of the time he couldn't
believe in mercy. Copying that sentence by hand, I thought of the note
Trope had sent just now. *"Still not balanced. But closer than
before."*

I set those two sentences side by side and looked at them. Trope of
before, and Trope of now. Neither had certainty. But something had
changed.

---

That evening, Histor sat looking for a long while at the page he'd
copied.

"…What are you doing?" I asked.

"…It's strange. I've already read this part more than ten times. But
copying it, it reads differently again."

"…How?"

"…When I read, I judged it. Whether it was right or wrong, whether it
proved something. But when I copy, all I have to do is move it over,
so I stop judging. I'm just making that person's words again with my
own hand."

I turned that over for a long time. It was an exact observation.

"…But do you know something?" he went on. "At first I read these six
records to know them exactly. Where everything was, who had proven
what. But copying them now, I keep rethinking what knowing exactly
even means."

"…How is it different?"

"…I thought knowing meant putting something into your head. But
copying is something you do with your hands. That there's work the
head and the hands do together — I only learned that this time."

I started to write that down in my notebook and stopped. Some things
are better just remembered than written down.

---

*I don't want to write this chapter as though I gained some grand
realization. I still don't fully know the reason. When Arche asked me
why, I had no answer. When Histor asked, I had no answer. Even writing
this now, I can't give the exact reason.*

*But I can say this much. Copying these six records out by hand, I
was with the people inside them differently than before. Not just
knowing them, but watching their words be born again through my own
hand. I still don't know what that means. But for those few weeks, I
didn't want to stop doing it.*

---

Copying all six records took nearly a month.

On the day I copied the last page, my hand was trembling so badly I
dropped the pen twice. Histor laughed beside me.

"…Not many pages left now."

"…I know."

I copied the final sentence. The last part of the Consensus, the
sentence Hora had written. *I never once met him. And I cannot bring
myself to believe all of this was chance.*

Copying that sentence, my grip on the pen tightened.

---

Once everything was copied, I laid the six new sheaves side by side
with the originals. The paper colors were different, and the
handwriting varied a little too. On days I was tired, the letters
sprawled; on days I felt well, they were clear. It wasn't a perfect
copy.

*Looking at those copies, I thought it was a good thing they weren't
perfect. If they'd been perfect, they would have only been a
reproduction. This wasn't a reproduction. This had passed through my
own hand.*

That night, walking home, I noticed for the first time that my wrist
no longer hurt. The pain hadn't gone away — I'd simply grown used to
it. I thought that was the most certain thing I'd gained in that whole
month.

---

The next morning, the Keeper turned through the copies one by one and
asked.

"…Now that these are done, what will you do?"

"…I still don't know. But I feel like I have to do something with
them."

"…Will you put them beside the blank paper?"

"…Yes. For now."

I set the six bundles of copies side by side with the blank sheaf.
Five were stories already written; one still held nothing at all. It
looked to me as though the six copies were standing in a ring around
the blank one.

The Keeper stood beside them and looked at them for a long while.

"…This is the first time in twenty-some years I've seen this," he
said.

"…Seen what?"

"…All six cities' things, in one place, this neatly arranged. Before,
they were always scattered. Some here, some there."

"…Does it look different?"

"…Yes. Strangely steady."

Hearing that, I looked again at the six bundles and the blank paper.
It really was so. Scattered, each had been its own story. Laid side by
side, they felt like they were joining into one.

"…That's strange," Histor said, looking at it.

"…What is?"

"…Five records are all finished stories, and yet that makes the blank
one look even more empty. But at the same time, it gives it the
feeling that it's about to be filled."

I agreed with that.

---

*Looking back on that month, I still don't have a complete answer for
why I did it by hand. I don't think that's strange. Some things you
don't begin only once you know the whole reason — some things stay
that way even after you've done them, the reason still not fully
known.*

*But I do know this. Copying these six records by hand, I felt like I
was meeting each person inside them again, one by one. Zeteo's doubt,
Elpis's waiting, Pheugon's verdicts, Trope's hesitation, Xene's
silence, Lytron's boast that wasn't quite a boast, Anemos staying by
someone's side, Eikon lacking nothing, Hora's belief, Tyche's
probability. I remade all of it, once, with my own fingertips.*

*And beside all of it was our city's own space, still holding nothing
at all. That night, I was able to look at that space with less fear
than before. I still didn't know the reason. But I wasn't afraid. I
decided that was enough.*
