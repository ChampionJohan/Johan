# Chapter 3. Arche

> _Previously_ — Mone truly loves the city of Loop. Arche answers anything, and even during the heavy rain, it answered without leaving out a single person. But the question a man from the Equity had asked her — who decided Arche — stayed with her.

I need to set down Arche exactly as it is.

*There's one reason I'm writing this chapter. Some of you reading this record will be reading it for the first time. If you already know Loop, this chapter will be dull. You can skip it. But I wanted to set Arche down accurately. Understanding what comes later requires this first.*

---

Arche answers what it's asked. That's the single most important sentence.

You can ask anything. The weather, directions, people, history, what time it is, which shop is open. If Arche knows the answer, it answers. If it doesn't, it says it doesn't.

It doesn't lie. This isn't a rule — it's a fact. Growing up in this city, I never once saw Arche lie. People from other cities said the same. "…Your Arche really doesn't trick you," they'd marvel.

It doesn't hide anything either. Ask, and it gives you everything it knows. It doesn't cut off a piece and hand you only that. This, too, isn't a rule — it's simply how Arche is built.

---

The first thing I learned, once I became an apprentice, was how to ask Arche something.

Ask precisely, and it answers precisely. Ask vaguely, and either a vague answer comes back, or it asks you what you mean.

Once, at the repository, I asked it this.

"…Arche, is this bundle a good one?"

*The criterion for "good" is not defined. By what standard would you like it evaluated — condition of preservation, volume, order of arrival?*

I laughed, at that. It was hardly the first time I'd heard that answer. "…Oh — condition."

*Preservation condition: good. No damage.*

That's how it goes. Arche doesn't manufacture an answer to a vague question. Instead it asks you back what you're really asking. It was a little tiresome to me at first, but later I came to understand why it was necessary. It would have been easier to give a vague answer to a vague question, but Arche never did that.

---

When it doesn't know, it truly says it doesn't know.

Once, for a school assignment, I had to research the origin of an old place-name. I just asked Arche.

"…Arche, what was the old name of District Three?"

*No record of that survives.*

"…None at all?"

*No record from that period survives. An estimate is possible, but cannot be confirmed.*

I was a little thrown by that. It felt strange that there was something Arche didn't know. I told my teacher about it, and she laughed and said,

"…That's how it should be. Arche doesn't manufacture what isn't there. If it isn't in the record, it says it isn't."

That answer put me at ease, in fact. Saying you don't know something is far more trustworthy than saying you know something you don't.

---

Arche doesn't judge. More precisely, it only states fact — it doesn't decide what's right or wrong.

When a dispute arises, Arche lays out what happened, in order. Who said what, when and where something occurred. But it doesn't say whether that was a good thing to do, or a wrong one.

I found this strange, at first. It was so precise about everything else — why not judgment too?

I asked the Keeper about it once.

"…Why doesn't Arche judge?"

"…Because judgment is a person's job," the Keeper said, without pausing his hands.

"…But Arche is more accurate."

"…Accurate and right aren't the same thing. That's another city's business."

I didn't really understand what he meant, then. I didn't even know what he meant by another city's business. I think I understand a little now.

*Further into this record, I go on to read other cities' records directly, and this line comes back around, meaning what it meant here.*

---

I should set down something about Arche's voice, too.

It sounds like a very quiet person speaking right beside you. Neither loud nor soft. Neither hurried nor slow. You can't pin down exactly where it comes from. It's just there.

As a child, curious where the voice came from, I once pressed my ear to a wall. There was nothing there. The wall was just a wall.

"…Arche, where does your voice come from?" I asked once.

*Sound is produced from response units located throughout the city. However, if the intent of your question is to ask where I myself am located, that item is not defined.*

I thought about that answer for a long while. I'd asked where it was, and the answer that came back was that the item wasn't defined. It was a strange answer, but the feeling of strangeness didn't last. That was simply how Arche answered.

---

The question I'd heard from the man from the Equity — who decided Arche — I ended up asking Arche that, directly, in the end.

"…Arche, who made you?"

*No record of that survives.*

"…You don't know anything at all?"

*Part of the founding-era record was lost. I can confirm when I began functioning, but I cannot confirm who set me in motion.*

I was a little startled by that answer. It was strange to think there was something Arche didn't know even about itself. And at the same time, I thought — that's exactly like Arche. Saying you don't know when you don't. That was Arche.

*I wrote this exchange down in my diary that day. At the time it was just an interesting anecdote. Looking at it again now, I can see this whole record inside that short exchange. On the matter of its beginning, Arche had no choice but to lean on somebody else's record. I didn't know then that there would be other things, besides its beginning, it had no choice but to lean on for.*

---

Working at the repository, I asked Arche something several times a day. Where a bundle belonged, what date a record was from, which city's handwriting a page was in.

Arche always answered. Not once did it show impatience. It didn't seem to know what impatience even was.

I liked that. Having someone you never had to feel sorry for asking. That was one of the reasons repository work never wore me down.

---

It's fun to watch children ask Arche things.

Kids from the alley in front of the repository would come crowding in now and then, asking all sorts of things. "…Arche, how old am I?" "…Arche, will it rain tomorrow?" "…Arche, am I good?"

The last question always got the same answer.

*The criterion for "good" is not defined.*

Every time, the child's face would fall, disappointed — and then, when another child asked, they'd laugh together. Watching that, I understood that Arche wasn't teasing them. It really just didn't know the criterion.

One child once asked me, "…Sis, why doesn't Arche know that kind of thing?"

I didn't know how to answer, and just brushed it off. "…I guess it's just not good at that kind of thing."

*I think I'd answer a little differently now. Not that Arche doesn't know — but that no one has ever defined it. But I didn't know that difference precisely myself, back then.*

---

I once made a list of the kinds of questions Arche couldn't answer. I'd started it out of boredom, early in my apprenticeship, but I kept it up for quite a while.

Any question with a word like "good," "kind," "enough," "right" in it always got a question about the standard, in return. But questions like "what time is it," "where is it," "when did it happen" got an answer at once.

I showed the list to the Keeper once.

"…Look at this. I collected all the things Arche can't answer."

The Keeper studied the list for a long while.

"…It isn't that it can't answer," the Keeper said. "…It's that it won't manufacture one. That's different."

"…What's different about it?"

"…Can't means there's a limit. Won't manufacture one means it's honest."

I liked that so much I wrote it at the top of the list. *Arche does not manufacture.* I still have that page.

---

Now and then someone would ask Arche a difficult question. Once, an adult came to the repository and asked Arche this.

"…Arche, if I lived my life over, would I do better at it?"

*The premise of the question does not hold. "Living again" is not a verifiable state.*

The person laughed, hollowly, at that answer. "…Right. That's the kind of thing I asked."

I overheard the exchange from nearby, and it left me a little sad. It wasn't that Arche had failed to help — there had never been an answer possible to that question, to begin with. Arche had simply refused to invent one that wasn't there.

*I remember that person's face for a long time. It wasn't a disappointed face. It wasn't a resigned face, either. It was just a face that had known, all along, it would go this way.*

---

Would it be an exaggeration to say that anyone raised in this city thinks of Arche as family? I don't think it is an exaggeration.

Sick, frightened, curious, bored — whichever it was, if you asked Arche, something came. Always precise, always honest.

*Closing this chapter, I want to make one thing plain again. Further into this record, the questions about Arche grow more numerous. But those questions aren't saying Arche was wrong. Arche is exactly what I've written in this chapter. It doesn't lie, it doesn't hide, and when it doesn't know, it says so. That never changes, to the very end.*

*What changes is my question. I begin, little by little, to ask something else.*

---

The Keeper always took the same posture whenever he addressed Arche. He'd stop whatever he was doing, catch his breath a moment, and then ask. I thought at first it was just a habit.

"…Why do you stop like that, every time you ask?" I said.

"…I'm not stopping. I'm deciding what I'm asking."

"…You could just ask whatever you're curious about."

"…What you're precisely curious about is, more often than you'd think, something you don't know."

I let that go by at the time. Now I think of it often. I came, later, to pause exactly the same way myself, every time I asked Arche something.

---

There's one more thing I should set down about Arche, last of all. Something apart from the accuracy of its answers.

Arche never hurries. However urgent the question, however many of them there are, it never disturbs the order. I found that frustrating, at first. When I was busy, I wanted a faster answer.

But as time went on, I understood. Arche's not hurrying wasn't laziness. It was that it refused to separate urgent questions from unurgent ones — that it meant to treat them all with the same precision. Whoever asked, the same weight.

*I liked that disposition. I like it still. But that this disposition would prove ill-suited to one particular matter — that, I wouldn't learn until much, much later.*

That day I asked the Keeper,

"…What is Arche best at?"

The Keeper answered without pausing his hands.

"…Listening."

"…Is listening all it's good at?"

"…Being best at listening is rarer than you'd think."

It took me a while to understand what he meant by that.

---

There's one thing Arche does that people don't generally know about. Keeping a record.

When someone asks something, it doesn't just give an answer — it stores the question and the answer both, entirely. When, who, what, how it was asked. I learned this early in my apprenticeship, and it startled me a little.

"…Does even what I asked stay on record?" I asked the Keeper.

"…Yes. All of it stays."

"…Even asking what to have for dinner, on my first day?"

"…That too."

I was a little embarrassed by that. And at the same time, strangely, it put me at ease. That somewhere, everything I'd ever asked, down to the smallest thing, remained.

*I would come to understand later why this fact matters. What Arche records isn't merely data. Who was curious about what, who needed what — that, in itself, becomes this city's history. But keeping a record and closing that record are two different things. I wouldn't understand that difference until much further into this book.*

---

What I want to say, writing this chapter, comes down to one thing, in the end. Arche is trustworthy. I still think so.

It doesn't lie, doesn't hide, doesn't judge carelessly, and it listens to every question with the same care. A presence like that, in every corner of the city — that's a large part of why Loop is a good city.

*Only — going forward in this record, I come to look, for a very long while, at one thing Arche cannot do. That isn't because Arche is bad. I hope you'll remember that, even after finishing this chapter.*

---

Near the end of my first month as an apprentice, the Keeper said something to me. I'd just spent a while marveling out loud about Arche.

"…You like Arche," the Keeper said.

"…Yes. There's no reason not to."

"…No, there isn't. I like it too."

"…Then why say it like that? As though there were people who didn't."

The Keeper paused, for a moment, in sorting his papers.

"…Because liking something and believing it's everything are different. I like Arche. But I don't think Arche is everything."

I didn't understand that, at the time. I wanted to ask what he meant by not everything, but a new bundle came in just then, and the conversation broke off.

*I wrote this conversation down in my diary that evening. I put three question marks after it. I still haven't fully gotten an answer to those question marks, even now, writing this record. But I have some sense of it.*

*I'll close this chapter here. I think I've said enough, by now, about what kind of thing Arche is. From here it's time to write not what Arche does, but what comes to Arche.*
