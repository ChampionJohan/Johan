# Chapter 36. The Seal

> _Previously_ — In the margin of the plan, we found one line of
> handwriting. "Leave the rooms generous — you never know how many
> more will come." A sentence like a carpenter's note. Histor stared
> at that writing for a long time, but in the end never said what he
> was thinking.

Beneath the plan, there were still more sheets we hadn't looked at.

We'd only pulled the plan out first; the rest of the bundle we'd left untouched. Now we decided to go through the rest, one sheet at a time.

I said I'd take that work on. Laying things in order was my job.

---

The first few days there wasn't much to it.

Old scraps of ledger. A list of something counted, a paper with only a date on it, a calculation half-erased. I unfolded them one by one, put them in order, and stacked them beside me.

Histor read alongside me.

"…What's this a count of?"

"…I don't know. Just numbers, no note of what was being counted."

"…Can something like this even be called a record?"

"…Yes. That's how I was taught. Even if we don't know what was counted, the fact that someone counted it stays behind."

A few more days passed. Among the ledger scraps there was an old list too. A paper that looked like it noted who received what from where, a paper listing only dates, a paper with a few names written and then crossed out.

"…This is a little odd," Histor said one day. He pointed to a sheet with names written on it, more than half struck through with a line.

"…Crossed out?"

"…Yes. But it doesn't say why they were crossed out. Just crossed out."

I laid that sheet in its place in order too. By then I'd grown used to laying things down without knowing the reason.

Mena came by from time to time to look too. She knew ways of handling old documents, and showed me how to carefully peel apart paper that had stuck together.

"…Pull it apart too fast and it tears. Wet it a little, gently."

I did as she said. It took a long time, but not one sheet tore.

Histor, meanwhile, made a list, writing a short summary of each sheet's contents. I noticed, freshly, just how meticulous a person he was.

"…Why are you organizing all this?" I asked.

"…So I can answer, if someone asks later. What was there."

"…Who would ask?"

"…No telling. But it's good to be ready. I like that — being able to answer right away, when asked."

The Keeper looked in now and then too. He never looked long. He'd glance over it once and just say, "…Keep going," and nothing more. I knew that wasn't a way of hurrying me. It just meant he was watching what I was doing.

"…Isn't this tedious?" the Keeper asked one day.

"…Sometimes. But seeing it fall into order makes it bearable."

"…Same for me. That's the pleasure of this work."

---

On the fifth day, I reached the bottom of the bundle.

What was left was a single sheet. Thicker than the rest. At first I thought it was just a thick sheet of paper, but looking closer, several sheets of paper were layered together, held by something.

I lifted it. The weight was different. Heavier than the other single sheets.

Several sheets were bound together, not simply stacked but fixed together by something. Feeling along the edge, something rough caught against my fingers.

My hand stopped. For the first time, I had the feeling that this was something I shouldn't touch. I hadn't felt that, anywhere else in this room, until now.

The room was quiet. Only the low sound of water came from outside the window. I stayed still for a moment, holding that paper. My hands weren't shaking. Only, it seemed I had to move more slowly than usual.

"…Come look at this," I called for Histor.

---

He came over and looked. His face changed a little.

"…This…"

"…What is it?"

Instead of answering, he pointed at the edge of the paper with his finger. Something was attached there. Dark in color, smooth on the surface. At first I thought it was a stain.

Looking closer, it wasn't a stain.

---

Wax.

A small lump of it. The color had faded so much there was no telling what it had originally been. Something was pressed into it. Letters, it looked like.

I brought the candle close. Histor leaned in too.

We read it at the same moment.

I thought, in that moment, of the day we'd opened the door. That day too, I'd expected something great. The door had just been a door. This time was similar. The wax was just wax. Only the four letters pressed into it were far heavier than the door itself had been.

---

*UNTIL THE TIME OF THE END.*

I read those words several times over. Wondering if they might read some other way. They didn't. That was exactly what was written there.

"…This is a seal, isn't it?" I asked. It wasn't a question I asked because I didn't know the answer.

Histor nodded.

"…Yes. It's a seal."

He said nothing more after that. He didn't touch it either. He just stepped back a pace and looked at it. I thought that distance was his answer.

---

I called Mena and the Keeper in. The two of them came and looked at the paper. Neither reached out a hand first.

Mena stopped still, looking at it. Her expression changed.

"…This…" she said, low.

"…Do you know it?" I asked.

Mena nodded slowly.

"…I've seen it before. A long time ago."

"…When?"

"…A while back. I don't remember exactly, myself."

Mena said nothing more than that. I didn't ask further. Her face was telling me this wasn't the time to talk about it.

"…Was it unopened, even then?" I asked, carefully.

Mena nodded.

"…Yes. The same."

"…Still strange, that there's no mark at all," I said. "You'd expect a seal to show whose stamp it is."

Mena nodded, as if in agreement.

"…True. That was strange even then."

Histor added, from the side.

"…I've seen seals like this a few times in the six records too. In the Roll, in the Consensus's documents. But they all had a stamp. So you could tell whose it was. This one really has none of that."

"…What could that mean?" I asked.

"…I don't know. It might mean nothing at all."

Mena shook her head at that.

"…I don't think it means nothing. Not stamping it — that's a choice too."

---

The Keeper reached a hand toward the paper, then drew it back.

"…Can we touch it."

"…I don't know," I answered.

"…And I don't know whether it means open it, or don't."

I agreed with that. The words "until the time of the end" could be read two ways. It might mean keep it sealed until the end comes. Or it might mean, once the end comes, open it.

*I set both readings down here, side by side. That day, all four of us
held on to this sentence and each read it a little differently. No one
insisted their own reading was the right one.*

Looking at that sentence, I thought again of my nine moves. Every time I lost something, I used to think it was gone forever. This seal was the opposite. Nothing had been lost — it had simply stayed there, unopened.

*I know this contrast is too neat. Even so, what I felt that day was
exactly this. What's lost and what's not-yet-opened can look, on the
surface, the same — both absent — but they're different.*

---

"…Should we open it?" I asked. Having said it, I didn't know myself why I'd asked.

No one answered right away.

Histor spoke first.

"…I… don't know. I want to open it, and I don't want to, both."

"…Why don't you want to?"

"…If we open this, it feels like something's going to end. But I don't even know what it would be ending."

---

Mena said quietly.

"…My hand won't move either."

"…You too, Mena?" I asked.

"…Yes. Strange, isn't it. I've spent my whole life opening things, checking things. And in front of this, my hand won't move."

The Keeper said nothing. He just looked at that paper for a long while.

I didn't find that silence heavy. If anything, it felt natural. All four of us, for our own separate reasons, had stopped in the same place.

"…What about you, Keeper?" I asked. "…Why won't your hand move?"

The Keeper answered slowly.

"…I'm not someone who opens things, to begin with. I'm someone who lays things down. This is opening work."

"…Then who is the one who opens things?"

The Keeper shook his head.

"…I don't know. That, even I don't know."

I felt, hearing that answer, that I'd heard something important, though at the time I didn't know exactly what.

"…Do you know when 'the time of the end' is?" Histor asked the Keeper.

The Keeper shook his head.

"…I don't. I've never even heard of anyone who knows that."

"…Then doesn't that mean it can never be opened?"

"…Could be. But I don't think that's a bad thing either. Most everything here is like that. Left in progress."

Listening, I thought this seal might share the same fate as everything else in this city. Something that never opens, and yet never disappears either.

---

"…Should we call the others?" I asked. "…Zeteo, or Lytron. Everyone's come through, one time or another."

Histor shook his head.

"…I don't think it's that yet. This… feels like something we should see among ourselves, first."

"…Why?"

"…I don't know. Just a feeling."

I agreed with that feeling. It felt like this seal wasn't yet something to bring out in front of many people. I couldn't explain why.

---

I asked Arche.

"…Can we open this?"

*Checking.*

There was a pause.

*Access to this item is available. No restrictions on breaking the seal
have been confirmed.*

"…So we can open it?"

*No restriction existing and it being something that should be opened
are different matters.*

I read that answer over again. Arche didn't block us. It didn't encourage us either. It just handed the judgment right back to us.

I asked one more thing.

"…The phrase 'until the time of the end' — has it appeared in the six cities' records before?"

*Confirmed. Similar expressions are found scattered across each city's
records. However, no case uses the exact same wording as this seal.*

"…Which cities?"

*Partially confirmed across all six cities. The form of the expression
differs in each.*

I lost words at that answer. It meant something similar had been scattered across all six records. I didn't press further than that. It didn't seem like pressing would bring anything more to light.

---

"…What do you think this means?" I asked. Not really asking anyone in particular.

No one answered.

I reached out and lightly touched the edge of the seal. The wax was hard. It looked like it would crack if I pressed. I didn't press.

*I still remember the feel that stayed on my fingertips, in that
moment. Cold and hard. I took my hand away instead of breaking it.*

Histor watched that and said quietly.

"…You could have broken it."

"…Yes. But I didn't."

"…Why?"

I couldn't answer that right away. After a long moment, I said,

"…I don't know. I just felt that now wasn't the time."

Histor nodded at that answer. He didn't press further.

---

"…Let's leave it for today," Mena said.

No one objected.

The Keeper carefully set the paper back down on top of the bundle. We decided to leave that paper together with the others, in that small room, on the low table beneath the wall where the plan hung.

The next day, and the day after that, we still went to that room. We kept looking at the plan. We read the margin sentence again too. But no one ever reached a hand toward the seal. We only confirmed it was still there, and turned away.

"…Are we going to keep doing this?" Histor asked, on the third day.

"…I don't know. Someday we'll open it, I suppose."

"…When?"

I couldn't answer.

That day too, we ended up leaving the room without doing anything. I didn't think of it as putting it off. It just felt like all four of us, not just me, were feeling equally that it wasn't the time yet.

---

Closing the door and walking out, I turned back once.

The small room, the plan on the wall, the bundle of paper on the table beneath it. Once the candle went out, it all sank into darkness. That darkness didn't frighten me. It was just quiet. I stood a moment longer in front of that lockless door, then turned away.

*Writing this chapter, I want to set down, exactly as it was, that we
did nothing that day. We didn't open it. No one forced it. All four of
us just stood in front of it. I don't think that was running away. It
just felt, I think, like all four of us sensed, in equal measure, that
it wasn't the time yet.*

---

That night I wrote briefly in my notebook.

"The seal reads 'until the time of the end.' We didn't open it."

I didn't write anything more beneath it. I didn't want to attach my own thoughts after this sentence. I just wanted to leave what had happened as it was. Before closing my notebook, I read that sentence once more.

Histor read it beside me and said,

"…Is that it, for today's record?"

"…Yes. That's all there is, today."

He nodded. He didn't ask any more.

*I cut off this stretch of the record here. We met the ones who arrived.
We learned the water channel joins the cities. We opened the door not
in the index. We read the plan, and the writing in its margin. And we
stood in front of this seal. Nothing opened. I don't think that's
strange. Some doors, I think, need time spent standing in front of them
before they can be opened.*
