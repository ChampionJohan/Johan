# Chapter 28. Xene and Lytron

> _Previously_ — Pheugon and Trope came and went. Trope had shifted
> only a little, from "it won't" to "I don't know," without settling
> into certainty. Pheugon still carried his sentence with him, still
> did the work of reading it aloud.

The two who came from the Harvest arrived together, but at different paces.

The woman arrived first, the man trailing a bit behind. At first I didn't even realize they were together. The woman carried nothing in her hands; the man had a large sack over his shoulder.

"…I'm Xene," the woman said first. "…This is Lytron."

Lytron was broad-shouldered and tall. He didn't look very different from what I'd imagined from the Harvest's record. Only his expression looked far more careful than the person in that old record had been.

Lytron set the sack down and bowed his head.

"…This is from the Harvest. Grain. There was nowhere to give it, so I brought it here."

I was a little flustered taking the sack. This was the first time anyone had ever brought grain to the repository. People had come from all six cities, but they'd all come empty-handed, or with bundles of paper. Grain was new.

The Keeper came out, saw the sack, and looked pleased.

"…That's a first."

"…Sorry. Is it strange?" Lytron asked.

"…No. It's good. It's always just paper piling up here."

Xene heard that and laughed.

"…He's just clumsy at giving things, so he probably gave it strangely. Be generous with him."

Lytron looked embarrassed. I found it hard to overlay that expression onto the confident boy I'd pictured from the Harvest's record. Time seemed to have made him a little different.

I looked at the sack again, remembering the passage from the old record about how strict he'd been with his calculations. Now here he was, having brought something without calculating at all. I couldn't tell whether that meant he'd changed, or whether his hands had simply grown restless.

---

"…Why did you bring it?" I asked.

Lytron hesitated a little before answering.

"…I don't know. It just felt wrong to come empty-handed."

Xene laughed quietly beside him.

"…He's always like this. Used to be, he went empty-handed so he'd never lose anything. Now he keeps carrying things around."

"…What do you mean?"

"…He'll explain it himself."

Lytron picked up on that.

"…I've never lost anything in my life. Truly. My qualification score was high, my family was good, I had everything I needed. So I never understood why anyone would give something to someone else."

"…And now?"

"…Even now I've never lost anything. That's a fact. But lately I know it's nothing to be proud of."

---

I knew this was exactly the opposite of what he'd said in the Harvest's record. The man who'd once said "I haven't done anything wrong" was now saying "that's nothing to be proud of." But Histor put his finger precisely on it.

"…Do you still think you've done nothing wrong?"

Lytron thought a moment before answering.

"…Yes. That part hasn't changed. I haven't discovered some new sin of mine. It's just… I've come to know that never once losing anything isn't something remarkable."

"…If it's not remarkable, what is it?"

"…It's just how I was born. Xene was born with a qualification score of zero and I was born with a perfect one. I didn't do that."

*I want to render this answer carefully, so it doesn't sound like a
conversion. Lytron didn't confess his sin. He only conceded that what
he'd received wasn't his own doing. I want to write that difference
exactly.*

Histor asked once more, as if confirming.

"…So in the end, it's still true you never needed mercy?"

"…Yes. That doesn't change. I don't lie."

"…Then why isn't that something to be proud of?"

Lytron thought for a moment and answered.

"…For it to be something to be proud of, I'd have to have done something. But I just stood in the place I was given."

Histor asked again.

"…So what do you believe now?"

Lytron couldn't answer that right away. After a while:

"…I don't know. I still think I never needed mercy. That's real. But… whether not needing it was actually a good thing — I'm not so sure anymore."

"…What's the difference?"

"…If I'd needed it, I'd have received it. Since I didn't need it, I never got to receive it. That's not a fortunate thing — it's just an experience I never had. I only realized that difference recently. I used to mistake never having received it for not needing to."

I read that answer several times over. Lytron still said he'd never needed mercy. He'd only newly admitted that this wasn't something to boast of — just an experience he'd never had.

Xene added quietly, from the side.

"…It took him years to say that. At first, no matter what I said, he wouldn't listen."

"…What did you say to him?" I asked.

"…Nothing much. I just went out to the field every day. Watching that, he felt something on his own. I didn't persuade him."

Lytron nodded.

"…That's right. Xene never persuaded me of anything. She just kept being there. That's what actually made me think about things more."

---

Xene had been quietly examining the shelves the whole while. Her eyes weren't searching for anything in particular — just looking around.

"…This place is like gleaning too," she said.

"…What do you mean?"

"…The dropped things. Every city leaves things behind, uncared for, and they come piling up here. Gathering those up used to be my job."

"…Do you still do it?"

"…Something like it. Lately I go around teaching other cities the Harvest's corner provision. The rule about leaving a corner of the field."

"…Does it take?"

Xene tilted her head slightly.

"…Some cities listen, some don't. But I just keep going and saying it."

I recognized that manner of speaking. The attitude of continuing without expecting a great result. I felt a similar thing, laying paper in order every day.

"…How's Mother Glyke?" I asked. It was a name I'd seen in the Harvest's record.

Xene's face softened.

"…She's well. Still asks to be called Pikra. Nobody tries to force her back to it."

"…Doesn't she go back to her original name?"

"…No. Mother says — the name I wrote isn't gone, I've just learned to live even while carrying it written. That's exactly how she put it. She keeps the name and lives with it."

"…Did you meet Mena?" Histor asked.

"…Yes. She's the one who taught me how to keep records. Years ago, at the Harvest. I heard she came through here recently."

"…That's right. A few days ago."

Xene smiled.

"…Even the teacher goes around learning things. She only ever taught us."

---

Lytron pointed at his sack again.

"…This — it was originally my share. What was left after transferring the qualification."

"…Are you still handing out qualification?" Histor asked.

"…No, that was only the once. It's recorded in the Roll, that case. But… since then, I keep wanting to give things away. It's strange."

"…Is it strange?"

"…Yes. I never used to have the concept of giving at all. Giving anything felt like a loss to me. Even now that's not entirely gone. It's still a little painful every time I give something."

Xene laughed at that.

"…Honest of you."

"…Better honest than pretending it doesn't hurt."

---

I asked Arche. This was the first time I'd asked directly about a person's qualification. I was a little cautious about it.

"…In the Harvest's Roll, what's Xene's qualification standing now?"

*Xene's qualification score meets the registration threshold through
Lytron's transfer.*

"…Has the process finished?"

*The registration is valid. Review of the associated case law is in
progress.*

Xene heard that answer and nodded.

"…So the review still isn't done."

"…Does it bother you?" I asked.

"…No. I just need to be registered. The review is the Roll's business to see to."

Lytron said, from the side,

"…It bothers me. Since I lowered my own share, I worry it might get reversed."

"…Do you think it will?"

"…I don't know. But even if it reverses, I'll give it again. I've decided that."

Histor asked, at the end of that exchange,

"…Is the administrator still unashamed?"

Lytron thought a moment.

"…Yes. Still is. Proud that his system never broke a law. I used to think that was just natural. Lately it looks strange to me."

"…What's strange about it?"

"…Not breaking the law and doing right seem like different things to me. But when I said that to him, he only said, 'I did my job.'"

"…Weren't you able to convince him?"

"…No. He won't change. I just do what I can differently, in my own share."

Listening, I thought that no one changing anyone else seemed to be a common thread running through all these cities. Everyone just changed their own small share, a little at a time. Xene, Lytron, and I were all the same in that.

---

Xene said she wanted to see the water channel, so I took her there too. Watching the water flow, she said,

"…This is like our field. Wherever the water passes, it leaves something behind. Small, but something."

"…What does it leave?"

"…Soil. Good soil. The water sets it down as it passes."

I liked that. I'd thought water only carried things away. But it set things down too.

Xene crouched by the water and touched the soil.

"…I wonder where this water starts."

"…I don't know. I'm curious too."

"…In our city, people think the water wasn't always there. That Speon opened it. But sometimes I think that might not be right."

"…What do you mean?"

"…When Speon first dug, I don't think water appeared — I think something already there just came to light. No real grounds for it. It's just a feeling."

I listened without answering. That feeling wasn't unfamiliar to me either.

Lytron set the sack down beside them.

"…I'll leave this here. It's for the Keeper."

"…I can eat this?" the Keeper asked.

"…Yes. No qualification asked. Just leaving it."

The Keeper heard that and laughed out loud for the first time.

"…It's been a while since I heard words like that in this city."

Lytron looked a little surprised.

"…People don't say that often?"

"…People in this city have everything they need. So not many people leave things behind. When there's no need, people don't tend to think about sharing either."

Xene heard that and looked thoughtful.

"…A city where everyone has everything they need — that's strange, hearing it. In our city there's always someone who's short of something, so sharing came naturally."

"…Which is better?" I asked.

Xene shook her head.

"…I don't know. I only know the way I grew up."

Lytron added quietly from the side.

"…Lately I'm learning that the way I grew up wasn't necessarily right either. I was just lucky."

---

The two left as evening fell. Xene said to me last,

"…You're like a gleaner too, Mone. All this paper here — it's things dropped somewhere, isn't it."

Hearing that, I thought about my own work in a new way for the first time. Picking up dropped things. It felt right. Until then I'd only thought of what I did as "laying down." Hearing Xene's words, I realized "picking up" had come first, before that. You had to pick up what had dropped somewhere, before you could lay it down.

Lytron walked out, looking at his hand, lighter now without the sack.

"…Odd — it's light, but it doesn't feel empty."

Xene laughed.

"…Sounds like you're starting to understand a little."

The two walked off like that. I split the grain sack they'd left behind with the Keeper and cooked it for dinner. Eating, I turned over each thing I'd heard that day. The rice tasted a little better than usual. Maybe because the face of the person who'd given it kept coming back to me.

That night Histor asked me,

"…Do you think Lytron believes?"

I couldn't answer that right away.

"…I don't know. He didn't say he doesn't believe, and he didn't say he believes."

"…Right. I can't decide either. In the Harvest's record he's a person certain he never needed mercy. The person we saw today had that certainty a little shaken. But shaken isn't overturned."

"…Which side do you think is right, Histor?"

Histor thought for a moment and said,

"…I don't judge. I just record. What he said today, exactly."

I thought that answer was very like him. He never refutes anyone, never takes anyone's side. He just listens exactly and writes it down. I was coming to understand that better and better as his way.

---

After we'd finished eating, I looked again at the sack Lytron had left behind. There was small writing on the inside. Xene's hand, it looked like.

"…Corner's share."

I looked at those two words for a long time. The handwriting was small and careful. I didn't know exactly what it meant, but it seemed to relate somehow to the field corner from the Harvest's story.

I tucked it between my records. Beside the petal Elpis had given me.

*I felt something strange, laying these two things side by side. A
nameless flower, and a scrap of grain sack marked only "corner's
share." Neither came with an explanation. I was, around this time,
slowly learning how to let things sit without explanation.*

That night I wrote briefly at the end of my notebook. "Lytron still
says he's never lost anything. But now he knows that not losing
anything wasn't his own doing either." Reading it back, this sentence
didn't conclude anything. It was just what I'd seen that day, carried
over as it was.
