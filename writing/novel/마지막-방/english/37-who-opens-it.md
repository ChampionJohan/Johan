# Chapter 37. Who Opens It

> _Previously_ — At the very bottom of the bundle of documents, only one
> sheet carried a seal. The seal read "UNTIL THE TIME OF THE END." No
> one had touched it. We only stood in front of it.

The seal was smaller than I'd expected.

I'd thought it would be bigger, harder. In fact it was a piece of wax
about the size of half a palm. The color had faded so much I couldn't
even guess what it had originally been. Only the letters pressed into
it were still clear.

*Until the time of the end.*

"…Is it all right to open this?" I asked.

No one answered right away.

We'd already moved the bundle of documents to the low table in the
middle of this room. The plan was already unfolded and hung on the
wall. We'd already read all the writing in the margin. All that was
left was that one sheet at the very bottom. Everything else we could
read. Only that, we couldn't.

*I've come into this room quite a few times by now. The first time I
opened the door, I was afraid. I'm not anymore. The room is narrow, has
no window, and holds nothing but one table and one shelf. And yet
coming into this room, my voice drops on its own. I know now that isn't
because the room is frightening.*

---

Histor spoke first.

"…Things like this usually require some kind of qualification. Someone
specific meant to open it."

"…Qualification?"

"…Yes. There was a lot of that in the six records I read. Doors no one
could open, things no one could touch. Usually there was a fixed
person for it."

Hearing that, I looked around the room. There was no one but us. Mone,
Histor, and the Keeper, who'd come in a little later.

"…So is there someone among us who has that qualification?"

Histor looked at me.

"…I don't know. That's the problem."

"…Which books were like that?" I asked.

Histor thought for a moment.

"…The Harvest record, the Roll. There the inner columns of the
register could only be touched by the Registrar. Lytron, too, was
turned away at first for lacking qualification. It was similar in the
Preserve record, Perpetuity. The deep compartments of the vault only
opened with the Custodian's permission."

"…So in those books, who ended up opening them?"

"…Someone qualified set the qualification down. Or someone
unqualified was given it. Either way, the key passed from person to
person."

Hearing that, I looked at the seal again. There was no key here. No
one holding a key, either.

"…But here there's no such person at all." Histor went on. "That's
what's strange. All six of the other records had one."

---

The Keeper came closer to the seal. His steps were as slow as ever.

"…I don't think it's a matter of qualification," he said.

"…Why not?"

"…If qualification were the issue, someone would have gone and found
a qualified person. That's the kind of thing people are good at. But
no one went looking. There's been no talk of it at all, not since we
first heard about this room."

I turned that over. It was true. When we'd looked at the plan earlier,
when we'd read the writing in the margin before, we had never once
worried about who was qualified to open this seal. We simply hadn't
been able to.

"…Then what's the problem?" I asked.

The Keeper looked at the seal for a long while before answering.

"…That there's no one to open it."

---

At first I heard that as the same thing Histor had just said. Hearing
it again, it was different.

Histor had said there was no *qualified person.* The Keeper said there
was no *one who opens.*

"…Is that different?" I asked.

"…It's different," the Keeper answered. "Qualification is something
you can give to someone. If no one has it today, you can decide on
someone tomorrow. But there being no one who opens at all means this
city has no one who does that kind of work. From the very start."

"…Can't it just be anyone in this city?"

The Keeper looked at me and smiled faintly.

"…Mone, how many years have you worked in this repository?"

"…This is my second year."

"…In that time, how many cases have we closed?"

I couldn't answer. None. Not a single one.

---

Just then Mena came into the room.

Mena was someone who had known this room before I did. She said she'd
passed through here once, when she was young. She saw the seal and
stopped walking.

"…Still hasn't opened, has it."

"…You know about this too, Mena?" I asked.

"…I do. I saw it once, a long time ago. It was exactly the same as it
is now. No one opened it."

"…Why didn't they?"

Mena crouched down in front of the seal.

"…We asked the same thing back then. Who opens it. We never found an
answer. So we just left it. The way you'd hand it on to whoever came
next."

"…How many years ago was that?"

"…Twenty-some years."

I lost words at that number. It meant that for twenty-some years,
people had come and stood in front of the same question, and simply
gone away again.

"…Then how are we supposed to decide?" Histor asked Mena. "Draw lots?
Or pick one person from each of the six cities and make some kind of
committee?"

Mena laughed at that. It wasn't a mocking laugh.

"…That's exactly what Lytron proposed in the Harvest record. A
registration committee for the Roll. Pick five people, one vote each.
Do you know what happened with that?"

"…What happened?"

"…Nothing happened. The votes split. The very method of weighing
qualification was a method that divided people. Whoever got picked,
the other four would have asked why that person."

Hearing that, I studied the seal again. I could see a thread running
under the wax. That thread wound once around the whole bundle and was
knotted. There was no lock, no keyhole. Just thread and wax. It looked
like anyone could tear it off with enough force.

"…Doesn't this just tear if you pull it?" I asked.

Mena nodded.

"…It tears. No one could stop that by force. But that's never been the
problem. None of us wanted to tear it by force."

"…Why not?"

Mena didn't answer that question. She got up from where she was
sitting.

"…I'll head off now. See you at dinner."

---

I decided to ask Arche.

I put my hand on the panel by the wall and asked.

"…Arche, can you open this seal?"

*Checking.*

There was a pause. It's rare for Arche to pause.

*Access to the item is possible. However, unsealing cannot be
performed.*

"…Why not?"

*The act of unsealing constitutes a closing action. I do not perform
closing actions.*

I knew that answer. I'd heard it before, always. *The closing
condition has not been met.* This time it wasn't about a condition. It
had simply, flatly, said it couldn't.

"…Not because there's no condition?"

*This act itself constitutes closing. It is not a matter of
condition.*

I read that sentence again. Arche doesn't lie. It doesn't hide things
either. It wasn't hiding anything now. It was simply stating, exactly,
what it couldn't do.

"…Then if someone else opens it for you, can you authorize that?" I
asked again.

*Authorization is possible. However, no authorization has been
requested.*

"…What if I request it now?"

*I will register the request. However, I have no criterion by which
to evaluate the request. A request with no criterion remains pending.*

I almost laughed. In progress again. Just like every case that had
stayed in progress for ten years across six cities, this too was about
to remain in progress.

"…Arche." I asked. "Do you want this opened?"

The pause was longer this time.

*"To want" is not a function defined for me.*

"…Then it doesn't matter to you either way?"

*Not mattering and being unable to want are different things.*

I turned that answer over for a long time. That was something I'd
never heard it say before.

---

Histor folded his arms and looked at the seal.

"…So Arche can't open it, and there's no qualified person either. Does
that mean this can never be opened?"

"…I don't know that," the Keeper answered. "But I don't think finding
who's qualified to open it is the answer. That's the method every one
of the six cities already tried. Finding the qualified person. Nothing
was ever solved that way."

Hearing that, I thought of Haven. What Elpis and Zeteo had gone
through. People asking whether they had the qualification to put their
own name on the Ledger. Then the Reckoning, the Harvest. All much the
same. People argued over qualification, and no one ever opened
anything.

"…Then what has to be different?" I asked.

The Keeper didn't answer. He just kept looking at the seal.

Histor pressed his hand against the table, looking frustrated.

"…So we're just going to leave it like this? I read all six of those
records, and not one of them ended with nothing opened. Something
always opened. The Ledger opened. The Scales, in the end, balanced.
Names went onto the Roll."

"…They did open," the Keeper said. "But were the people who opened
them qualified from the start?"

Histor stopped.

"…No. Elpis wasn't qualified at first either. Neither was Trope."

"…Then what made those people open them?"

Histor had no answer. Neither did I. The three of us stood there a
long while, saying nothing.

---

We stayed there until the sun went down.

Histor crouched in front of the seal, studying the wax closely. He
kept changing the angle, like someone searching for something.

"…What are you looking for?" I asked.

"…A name. Whoever seals something like this usually leaves their own
name or a mark. It was like that in all six records. On the back of an
envelope, on a signet, at least one line of signature."

"…Is there one?"

Histor shook his head.

"…No. There's nothing at all. Just 'until the time of the end.'"

"…Does that strike you as strange?"

"…It does. Making something be kept this long, and not saying who
ordered it. I don't know if that's irresponsible or what."

I didn't know how to answer that. The plan had no designer's name
either. This seal had no name of whoever had sealed it. I had a
feeling that wasn't irresponsibility, but I couldn't explain why I felt
that.

---

I stayed in that room late into that night.

Histor left first. As he went, he said this.

"…I still think, honestly, this is a matter of qualification. The
qualified person just hasn't shown up yet."

I didn't answer that. I didn't think he was wrong. I only felt it
couldn't be the whole of it.

*That night I stayed alone in this room and looked at the seal for a
long time. The wax was small, and the letters on it were faint. I
wasn't afraid of it. Strangely, I was sad. As if someone had put
something into this room, and waited for someone to open it, and
waited, and waited, until the waiting itself had simply become this
room's job.*

I decided nothing that night. But one thing had become clear. The
problem wasn't who was qualified to open it.

This city — no, perhaps all six cities — **had no one at all whose job
it was to open things.**

On my way out I met the Keeper. He was still by the door.

"…Why are you still here, Keeper?" I asked.

"…I don't know. There isn't really anything left for me to do."

"…Then why don't you go?"

The Keeper looked toward the door.

"…I was never an opener, Mone. I'm someone who puts things down. Like
you. But I have this feeling that if the one who puts things down
keeps standing in front of it, something might change. A strange
feeling."

I thought about that the whole way home. The feeling that if the one
who puts things down keeps standing there, something changes. I didn't
know yet what that meant. I only knew that I, too, hadn't wanted to
leave that room.

*That night, before falling asleep, I laid this room's whole story out
again from the beginning. The door not in the index. The old plan. The
nameless designer. And this seal. Up until then I'd been reading these
records — no, this record — as simply a curious story. That night, for
the first time, it occurred to me that this might be work someone had
handed on to me.*

*I didn't know who had handed it on. But the feeling of having
received it was unmistakable.*

---

The next morning I went to the repository earlier than usual.

Before opening the door, I wanted to ask Arche one more thing.

"…Arche, has this city ever, until now, had someone whose job was
'opening'?"

*Checking records.*

The pause this time was longer. I waited. The sound of the water
channel carried in from far off.

*That role has never, at any period, been registered in this city.*

I'd expected that answer. Even so, hearing it for real chilled
something in me.

"…Not once?"

*Not once.*

I stood there a moment. There being no one to open it, and there
having never once been such a person to begin with, felt like two
different problems. From the very start, we had never assigned the
work of opening to anyone at all.

That day I copied that sentence into my notebook exactly as it was. I
added no other explanation. I just wrote down that one sentence.

The Keeper came in just then. I showed him the notebook.

"…Look at this. It says the work of opening has never once been
registered."

The Keeper read the sentence and was quiet for a moment.

"…Does knowing that make things any easier?" he asked.

"…No. It got more complicated."

"…Same for me," he said. "But strangely, today I feel a little less
stuck than I did yesterday."

I didn't understand what he meant that day. Now I think I understand
it a little. Knowing exactly what the problem was, and still not being
able to solve it, were different things. That day, we had only come to
know one more problem exactly. That was all. And yet that all had felt
fairly large, that day.
