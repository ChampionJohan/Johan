Records keep arriving in Loop. Nobody asked for them. They come by hand and they come down the water channel, from five cities that named themselves after what they promised: Haven, the Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity.

Mone is sixteen and works in the repository. She is not gifted. What she does is put things in order, because her family moved nine times and she lost eleven things along the way, four of which she can no longer name. Arche — the system that answers anything, that has never once lied — sorts and indexes every record that comes in. It has also never closed a single case. Everything in Loop is still in progress. When Mone asks what condition must be met, Arche answers truthfully: that item is not defined.

Histor is seventeen and has read all six records. He knows where every passage sits. He does not mock them and he handles them with care, and none of it has done anything to him. "I know all of it," he says. "If knowing were believing, I'd be the biggest believer here."

Read side by side, the six records have one thing in common: a man with no name, in a flowerbed, by a well, in a field, in a valley, by the water. Histor can explain that — travelling stories resemble each other — and he is largely right. Then the tide records let the six cities' calendars be lined up, and two entries place the same nameless man in two different cities on the same day.

The Last Room is the finale of the Loop Chronicles, a seven-book Christian fantasy series for readers who would rather be asked an honest question than handed an answer. Nothing here is defeated and nobody is converted. A seal is opened, a record is copied out by hand, and someone leaves the recipient's name blank on purpose.
