# Chapter 12. The Door Not in the Index

> _Previously_ — Mone walked the length of the water channel but never found its source or its end. All she learned was that the water of all five cities gathers into Loop, and that this city lies lowest of all, by the flow of water. Arche didn't know the answer. Neither did the Keeper. Neither did the old man by the water.

I should talk about the corridor.

The repository corridor runs straight. Coming in from the entrance, rooms line up on either side, and the corridor passes between them, all the way to the back. I walked this corridor several times a day. Carrying a bundle, pushing a handcart, sometimes just walking.

There's a door at the very end of the corridor.

*I've already written this once, before. On the first day I saw that door and thought nothing of it. I wrote, at the time, that I'd tell about that door at the end of Part One. This is that place, now.*

---

There's something I should say first. In this chapter, I do not open that door.

*I'm saying this ahead of time in case someone reading this chapter is waiting for something big to happen. It doesn't. I only stood before that door, that day.*

That door didn't look any different from the corridor's other doors. A wooden door, with a handle, and no marking above the frame. Every other door had a section number on a small wooden plate. Section Fourteen, Section Fifteen, and so on. This door had no plate.

The first day I saw it, I just walked past. A newly arrived apprentice had no time to spare for one door at the end of a corridor. Just placing bundles where Arche told me to was more than enough to keep me busy.

*Many months had passed since then. By now I had nearly memorized twenty-two rows, eight slots, forty bundles. And yet, passing that door, I never once registered that it had no number plate. What had gone unseen only began to be seen recently.*

---

The trigger was nothing much.

I was on my way back that day, having shelved a bundle in Section Fourteen. I'd walked all the way to the end of the corridor and was turning back when, out of nowhere, that door caught my eye. I don't know why it suddenly came into view, that day.

*I won't try to explain this coincidence. Some days, something that's always been there suddenly comes into view. Some days there's no reason you can point to.*

I stopped walking and looked at the door. No marking at all. The frame was the same wood as every other door. Nothing special about it.

And yet the very fact of nothing was strange. Because there was nothing in this repository without a marking. Every shelf had a number, every bundle had a name, even the fish had their kind. Arche answered whatever you asked it. The only nameless thing in this city was Loop's own record — and even that had a reason: "no name, because it's from here."

This door had no name, and no reason either.

---

I decided to ask Arche. It wasn't some great resolve. I just asked, the way I always did.

"…Arche, this door at the end of the corridor — what section is it?"

The answer came a little late. Only slightly. Half a beat slower than usual, maybe. I'd like to say I noticed that faint delay right then, but the truth is I only realized it afterward.

*That area is not registered.*

I didn't understand the words at first.

"…What does 'not registered' mean?"

*No registration record exists for that location.*

"…Then you don't even know what's inside this door?"

*I do not.*

I was a little startled. It wasn't as though I'd never heard Arche say it didn't know something. When I'd asked about the closing condition, Arche had said it didn't know, too. But that had been the not-knowing of "not defined." This was different. This was the plain not-knowing of never having seen something that was there.

---

I asked a little more.

"…Is it hidden?"

*No. Hidden and unregistered are different.*

"…What's different about them?"

*Hidden means restricting access to a registered item. Unregistered means it never went through the registration process to begin with.*

That distinction felt unfamiliar to me. Up to then, I'd always assumed that if something went unseen, someone had concealed it. Even the things I'd lost, moving nine times, I'd always thought of that way. That they were somewhere, and I just couldn't find them. But Arche was saying something different. That some things had simply never been given a place, from the start.

*I thought about this distinction a long while, that evening. What's hidden, and what's never been registered. I learned, that day, for the first time, that the two might look the same in the end, but they start from different places.*

"…Then who made this door?"

*Cannot be confirmed.*

"…When was it made?"

*Cannot be confirmed.*

I asked one more thing, even so.

"…Then who does the registering? Who registered the other sections?"

*Registration is carried out by the administrator at the time of first intake.*

"…So no one ever brought anything into this one?"

*Cannot be confirmed. Only the fact that no registration process was carried out can be confirmed. The reason is unknown.*

I turned that answer over. Every other room had had someone bring something in for the first time, and give it a name, at some point. But this door had never once gone through that process. It wasn't that there'd been no one to make it — it meant that even if someone had made it, no one had given it a name. That difference kept catching on something in my mind.

"…Doesn't Arche think that's strange?"

*Strangeness is not something I judge. I report fact.*

I thought that answer was exactly like Arche. Even faced with something strange, it never said strange. It only said what was there.

There was nothing left for me to ask. Arche hadn't hidden anything. It simply didn't know. And Arche did nothing at all, beyond saying that it didn't know what it didn't know. No lie, no evasion. That, if anything, held me there longer.

---

I reached out and took hold of the handle.

It wasn't locked. It turned, slightly. I still remember that feeling, the handle turning. It wasn't stiff. It was smooth, for a door that hadn't been used in a long time.

*I want to set down this feeling exactly. If it had been locked, I think I would have written this chapter differently. One simple fact — that the door wouldn't open — would have made this story much simpler. That it wasn't locked made me draw back, instead, all the more.*

I stood there a moment, hand on the handle. Push, and it would open. All I had to do was push.

I didn't push.

---

Asked for a reason, I can't give a good one. I couldn't, that day, and I still can't, fully, now.

*Throughout this record I've tried hard to attach a reason to everything. Why I got good at packing, why I came to put things in order, why I came to write things down. Finding a reason set my mind at ease. This time I found none. I'm writing this chapter without having found one.*

I took my hand off the handle. The door stayed closed, exactly as it was. No sound came from inside. None came from outside, either. The corridor was quiet as always.

I stepped back and looked at the door again. A wooden door, a handle, an unmarked frame. It still looked like a door that was nothing at all.

*But I already knew it wasn't a door that was nothing at all. I just didn't know what it was.*

---

The next day I went outside and walked a circuit around the repository building.

The reason was simple. I wondered whether, seen from outside, I could tell where the inside of that door lay. Maybe there'd be a window, and something to see through it.

*I wrote, once at the start, that this building looks low and small, seen from outside. I also wrote that, once you're in, it runs back a long way. That day, I went, in effect, to check that again.*

I walked along the wall, counting windows. Five in front, six along the side, three in back. I tried to match that number against the number of rooms in the corridor, inside. It didn't match. There were more rooms than there were windows.

In particular, on the outer wall around where that door should have been, at the very end of the corridor, there wasn't a single window. Only brick. I stood before that wall a long while. It was the same brick as everywhere else. No trace of having been newly built, no trace of having been sealed shut.

"…Arche, is the far side of this wall the inside of that door?" I asked.

*The positional correspondence cannot be confirmed.*

I laughed at that answer. Asking from inside, it didn't know; asking from outside, it didn't know either. Arche was equally honest, whichever side you asked from.

---

That evening I told the Keeper about this.

"…That door at the end of the corridor — Arche says it isn't in the index."

The Keeper paused what he was doing. His face wasn't surprised.

"…Yes, it isn't."

"…You knew?"

"…I knew. You come to know, staying here long enough."

"…Then why didn't anyone tell me?"

The Keeper shrugged.

"…Because no one had asked."

That answer sounded, strangely, exactly right to me. For months I'd walked past that door and never once asked. I hadn't even thought to ask. What had gone unnoticed had only just now, suddenly, become something I noticed — what hadn't been curious had only just now, suddenly, become curious.

"…Have you opened it?" I asked.

"…No."

"…Why not?"

The Keeper's face turned thoughtful for a moment.

"…If you're asking me for a reason I never opened a door that isn't even locked, I don't have a good one myself. I just never opened it."

Hearing that answer put me a little at ease. It wasn't only me.

---

"…Aren't you curious?" I asked again.

"…I am."

"…But?"

"…Being curious and opening it are two different things, too."

I felt I'd heard that line somewhere before. The Keeper had said something similar, talking about the water channel. That it was fine to leave something, curious. That you could get by fine, even without finding the answer.

*I tried, right then, to tie those words together into one thing. The water channel, this door, and all those cases Arche could never close. All of them were things left standing, curious, unresolved. I thought that seemed like some common thread running through this city. Whether that thought was right, I wouldn't learn until much further into this record.*

"…What do you think is inside that door, Keeper?" I asked.

The Keeper paused his hands and thought a moment.

"…I don't know. Could be something grand in there. Could be completely empty."

"…What if it's empty?"

"…Then that'd be strange too, in its own way. Nothing there, and not even registered."

I liked that answer. Either way, it was strange. And this city ran along just fine, not knowing which. I found that remarkable.

"…Don't you want to open it? Really." I asked once more.

The Keeper looked at me steadily.

"…Mone. I've worked here more than twenty years. If that were the only door I ever wanted to open, I'd have opened it long ago."

I didn't fully understand what that meant, at the time. *I understand it a little now. That door wasn't the only door, in this city, someone might want to open. All those cases that never closed were, in a sense, closed doors too. The Keeper was someone who had lived twenty years among those doors.*

---

That night, at home, I wrote this on paper.

There's a door at the end of the corridor. It isn't in Arche's index. It says it isn't hidden — it just was never registered. It isn't locked. I took hold of the handle, and let go. I don't know why I didn't open it.

*I wrote one more line underneath, and left it, without crossing it out. "I don't know why this weighs on me so much." Copying this record out now, I'm leaving that line exactly as it was. I didn't know the answer then, and I still don't know it exactly, writing this chapter.*

*But I can set down this much, for certain. It wasn't the first time I'd seen that door. I'd already seen it once, at the start. That day I thought it was nothing at all. This time it wasn't. The door was the same. What had changed was me.*

---

After that, every few days, passing that door, I found my steps slowing a little.

Once, I deliberately went back late in the evening, when no one else was around. Some of the corridor lights were off, and it was dim. The door stood the same, even in the dark. I stood before it, saying nothing, and looked a long while.

"…Arche, do you know I'm standing here right now?" I asked.

*Your position is confirmed.*

"…But you don't know inside the door?"

*I do not.*

That was, strangely, a comfort. Arche knew me exactly, and yet knew nothing of this one door. Even in a city that seemed to know everything, there was one corner it didn't know. That didn't feel like a bad thing.

Another time, in daylight, I took hold of the handle again. Still not locked. I didn't push that day, either. Instead I turned the handle slightly and counted. Counted to ten. Then let go.

*I can't explain this action well, even to myself. Counting to ten didn't change anything. I think I just wanted to stay before that door, that way, a little longer.*

---

Closing this chapter, I picture that door once more.

The unmarked frame, the handle that turned smoothly, the corridor with no sound from inside or out. I end Part One without having opened that door.

*This door comes back again, in this record. When, and how, I won't say now. All I can set down now is this. I found out nothing at all, that day, standing before that door. I turned back, having found out nothing. That's all there is to this chapter.*

*I close Part One of this record here. Across twelve chapters I put things in order, I asked Arche, I walked the water channel, and now I stand before this door. Not one thing has finished. Not the channel's source, not Arche's closing condition, not what's inside this door. I thought, at the time, that this was strange. I know now. In this city, this is simply how Part One ends.*
