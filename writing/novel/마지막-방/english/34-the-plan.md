# Chapter 34. The Plan

> _Previously_ — I opened the door not in the index. It wasn't locked.
> Inside was a small room, and on a low table sat an old bundle of
> paper. When we untied the string, something like a plan came out.
> Familiar city names appeared too. We decided to take it somewhere
> brighter.

The next morning, we spread that bundle of paper across the big table in the reading room.

The reading room has three windows. Morning light comes straight in through the east wall. I looked at the papers again, under light entirely different from what they'd had in that small room the night before.

The first sheet was just old paper. It had some stains, and the edges were a little crumbled. From the second sheet on, lines began to appear.

---

"…Turn them carefully," the Keeper said. "…Paper like this tears from the folds first."

Instead of turning them one by one, I decided to spread all the sheets out and lay them side by side. The table wasn't big enough, so we set a few sheets on the floor too. Histor put something heavy on each corner. We had no paperweights, so we used old scale weights that Arche used to use instead.

Once everything was spread out, one picture came into view all at once.

Seven circles.

---

They were all different sizes. Some large, some small. The lines were fine and precise. At first I thought it was a map.

"…Is this a map?" I asked.

Mena looked it over for a long while and shook her head.

"…Strange for a map. The directions don't line up. This… looks more like a building plan."

"…A building?"

"…Yes. These circles look like rooms, seen from above. They're joined by corridors."

I looked again. It was true. Thin lines ran between the seven circles. It resembled what I'd seen in Speon's water-channel map, but this looked far older.

The paper was thin, and the ink had faded to a shade near brown. Whether it had originally been black or originally brown, there was no telling. Though it was a hand-drawn line, there was almost no tremor in it. I found that fascinating, and started to trace a line with my fingertip, but Histor was watching, so I stopped.

"…Don't touch it," he said. "…The oil from your hand isn't good for it."

"…I know."

---

"…Is one circle one city?" Histor asked.

Mena pointed at one circle.

"…This is Haven. Look at the writing here."

Small letters spelled out the city's name. The Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity. And in the middle, the large circle read Loop.

A seventh circle stood apart. Smaller than the other six, and off to one side. It had no name.

"…What's this?" I asked.

No one could answer.

"…Judging by the size, it looks like a single room," Histor said carefully. "…Much smaller than the other six."

"…What room could it be?"

"…No telling. There's no name."

I looked at that circle for a long time. Small, in a corner, nameless. I thought for a moment that it might be this room — and set the thought aside at once. There was no grounds for it.

---

I remember that moment precisely. The room went quiet.

*Writing this scene, I felt the temptation to guess that the nameless
seventh circle was this city. I still don't know, even now, whether
that's so or not. None of us said it out loud, that day. I try hard
not to write what wasn't said as though it had been.*

Histor found something else first.

"…Look at this. The lines are all connected. No breaks anywhere."

---

What he meant was the corridor lines between the circles. At first I didn't understand what that meant.

"…Why does it matter that there are no breaks?" I asked.

"…Think about it. If these seven cities were each built separately, and someone later joined them with a water channel or a road, there should be a seam. A change in ink color, a shift in line thickness. But this is the same from start to end."

Mena leaned in closer to the paper.

"…That's right. I was looking at that too. It looks like it was drawn by one hand, all at once."

---

Speon's map came to mind, suddenly.

"…What if we laid this over Speon's map?" I asked.

Mena's eyes went wide.

"…Where is that map?"

I brought the map tucked into my record shelf. Laying the two side by side, they weren't identical, but the places where the lines ran largely overlapped. The water channel Speon had walked and confirmed, and the corridors in this old plan, were pointing to the same spots.

"…These corridors — might they have been the water channel?" Histor said.

"…Speon said it himself, didn't he. That he didn't open it, that he just set water flowing through a place that was already there," I said.

Mena looked back and forth between the two maps and said quietly,

"…This plan came first, then. The water followed this plan, not the other way around."

No one added to that. We just sat looking at those two sheets, side by side, for a long while.

---

The Keeper leaned over the paper. He looked at it longer than usual.

"…Is this certain?" he asked.

Histor put his finger to the edge of the paper.

"…At least this sheet, yes. Same paper stock, same ink bleed. I've seen a lot of this — in the six records, whatever was added on later always shows. The handwriting shifts slightly, or the paper color changes subtly."

"…So this isn't something added on later?"

"…No. This was drawn as one thing, all at once, from the start."

Hearing that gave me a strange feeling. It meant the seven cities hadn't been built separately and later bound into one — they'd been inside a single picture from the very beginning.

Hearing that made me think again of my own work. I always laid things in order. Placing records that came in separately into their spots, one by one, I'd always thought of it as gathering things that had scattered. But seeing this plan, maybe they'd never scattered at all, to begin with. Maybe I was only confirming, again, something that had always been one.

*I hesitated a little, carrying this thought into my notebook. It
sounded too large a conclusion. So I added one more line after it.
"Not certain."*

---

"…So these cities weren't originally built separately?" I asked.

Mena didn't answer right away. She looked over the paper once more and said,

"…At least within this plan, that's true. Seven were one design."

"…Doesn't that surprise you?"

"…It surprises me. But it isn't frightening. If anything… it feels like something's settled a little."

"…Why?"

Mena thought for a moment before answering.

"…I've wondered for a long time why the six cities resemble each other so much. Every one of them thinks their own city is the most special. But the problems were all similar. Seeing this, I think I understand a little. They resemble each other because it was one design to begin with."

Histor said, carefully,

"…This never came up, not once, in all six records. I read every one of them, and nowhere does it say the cities were originally one."

"…Not anywhere?" I asked.

"…There was a similar feeling. Everyone had some vague sense that their own city was connected to something. But no one ever confirmed it in a picture, like this."

Mena nodded at that.

"…I had that feeling too. Not certainty, but."

"…So the feeling turned out to be right," I said.

Mena shook her head.

"…Not that the feeling was right — it's that there's material now. That's different."

I recognized that distinction. Speon had said the same thing.

---

Histor had been examining every corner of the paper this whole time. The motions of a hand searching for something.

"…What are you looking for?" I asked.

"…A name."

"…Whose name?"

"…Whoever drew a plan like this usually leaves their name somewhere. Small, tucked in a corner. Every record I've ever read did that. Designer, builder, supervisor. I've never seen a thing like this made without a name."

Hearing that, I looked over the paper again myself. Every corner, every margin, inside the circles, even where the lines overlapped.

---

Nothing.

The four of us searched several sheets of paper for over an hour, and nothing that could be called a name turned up anywhere. No mark, no seal, nothing resembling a signature.

"…Strange," Histor said. There was more plain bafflement than irritation in his voice.

"…What is?" I asked.

"…I've never seen anyone do a piece of work this size and not leave a name. Even if not to boast. It's just… that's what records do. They set down who did what, and when. And this has none of that."

I thought I understood why he found that frustrating. He was someone who respected records. Someone who believed a record should always have the person who left it. This plan was quietly slipping past that belief of his.

"…So is this not a record, then?" I asked.

Histor shook his head.

"…No. It's a record too. It's just… not a record left by someone. It's a record that simply is. I've just never seen one like that, so it feels foreign."

---

Listening, I felt an urge rising in me to say something. To at least venture a guess as to who it might have been. I didn't.

*I think it was the right thing, holding that urge back. A guess would
have come easily. Through all six records, there'd been a story of
someone who passed through, nameless, throughout. But I want to set
down, as it was, the fact that no one, standing in front of this plan,
named that name. Not Histor, not Mena, not the Keeper. No one said it.*

Mena broke the silence.

"…Let's leave it unknown."

"…Are you all right with that?" I asked.

"…I'm all right. I've come to learn, a little, how to leave things unknown too. Here."

---

I asked Arche, my hand against the wall panel.

"…This plan — can we confirm who drew it?"

*Not confirmed. This document carries no author information.*

"…What era? Do we know when it was drawn?"

*An estimate is possible based on the material and handwriting
characteristics, but the exact date cannot be confirmed. However, it is
estimated to predate this repository's earliest record.*

I read that answer over again. It meant this drawing existed before this repository ever came to be.

"…So it can only be estimated, not confirmed?"

*That is correct. There is not enough basis for confirmation.*

The Keeper murmured that phrase back to himself, as if turning it over.

"…Estimated but not confirmed, huh…"

"…What are you thinking?" I asked.

"…Just that it's exactly like the work of this repository. It's always like this here. You can guess, but you can't close it out."

---

The Keeper listened to that conversation and said quietly.

"…Arche doesn't know either."

"…It's not hiding it, is it?" Histor asked. He seemed to already half-know the answer, even asking the question.

"…Arche has never hidden anything," I answered in his place. "…It says it doesn't know because it truly doesn't. I can tell the difference now."

Histor nodded. He didn't argue.

---

We kept looking over the plan all through that afternoon. We found nothing new. Seven circles, an unbroken line, a nameless blank space. That was all.

*Writing this chapter, I felt the temptation, several times, to write in
some more dramatic discovery. A hidden mark, erased writing. There was
none of that. What we saw was just an old plan. But one fact the plan
stated was clear. The seven cities hadn't been built separately and
later joined — they had been one from the very start.*

As the sun leaned toward evening, the Keeper rolled the plan back up and said,

"…Shall we hang this back in that room?"

"…Hang it?" I asked.

"…Yes. We can't leave it spread out on this table forever. Seems right to send it back where it belongs."

---

That evening we carried the plan back to that small room. There were already a few nails driven into the wall. No one knew who had driven them. They looked like nails that had been there for a long time.

Histor hung the plan on those nails and said,

"…These nails — looks like they were put there just for hanging this."

"…Looks that way," I answered.

The four of us stood looking a long while at the plan hanging on the wall. The small room looked different, with that one plan on it. Not an empty room — it looked like a room made for a purpose.

Mena said quietly.

"…I think we'll need to keep coming to this room."

"…Why?" I asked.

"…I don't know. Just a feeling."

I nodded at that. I felt something similar too. I couldn't explain it, but I had a sense this room would become somewhere we came in and out of often, going forward.

*That night I wrote briefly in my notebook. "The seven cities were one
design. There is no designer." Reading it back, the two sentences
seemed not to fit together. But within this record, it seemed right
that the two should stand side by side.*

We closed the door again as we left the room. This time no one hesitated. That surprised even me. Just a few days before, I'd lingered in front of this door for days. Now it had simply become a door we came and went through.

Walking out down the corridor, Histor said,

"…That there's no designer's name — it keeps bothering me."

"…Why?"

"…Not leaving a name and there being no name to begin with are different things. I can't tell the two apart."

I had no reply to that. I didn't know either.
