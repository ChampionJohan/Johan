# Chapter 46. The Seal Turns Around

> _Previously_ — We decided to make seven sets. Everyone raised a hand
> for a different reason. Histor raised his too. "…Not because I
> believe it. Because this must not be lost." Before that winter
> ended, three of the six sets were finished.

The remaining three finished over the following two months.

We hadn't set any particular order. Whichever hands finished first,
finished first. I liked that. No one weighed who was more or less
urgent — it simply happened as it happened.

*I won't write anything grand in this chapter. I only watched people
carry a set away, one after another. That's all of it. I set it down
exactly as it was.*

---

Through those two months, the repository was always full of the smell
of paper and the sound of voices. Every morning when I opened the
door, I never knew whose hand would stop that day, who would turn the
last page. I just went every day, and kept my post every day.

Arche went on receiving new records from the six cities all the
while. I still put those records in order. New records came in on one
side, and old ones went out on the other. I did both kinds of work
with the same hands.

"…Aren't you busy?" my mother asked once.

"…I am. But the good kind of busy."

My mother didn't press further, and only smiled.

---

The Ledger finished first.

Zeteo turned the last page, and Elpis took it from him and closed the
cover. The two of them set the bundle on the table and looked at it
for a long while.

"…Can we take this now?" Zeteo asked.

"…Yes," I said.

Zeteo picked up the bundle in his hands. It wasn't heavy, but he
carried it with both hands.

"…What should we wrap it in, for the road?" Elpis said.

I brought a cloth from the back of the repository. I had a way of
wrapping plates from all those nine moves — set it in the center, fold
the four corners in, tie it once more with string. I wrapped the
bundle that way.

"…Where did you learn that?" Elpis asked.

"…A long time ago," I answered.

I didn't explain further. Elpis didn't ask further either.

---

The two of them left early the next morning.

I saw them off to the door. Zeteo tucked the bundle under his arm.
The old Zeteo would have checked it over once more, worn a
suspicious face, whenever he carried anything. This time he just
carried it carefully.

"…How long is it to Haven?" I asked.

"…Four days," Elpis answered.

"…Where will you put it, once you're there?"

Zeteo thought for a moment.

"…We haven't decided yet. But we won't just set it anywhere."

"…Have you still not found the answer?" I asked, carefully.

Zeteo looked at me and shook his head.

"…No. Still not. But I wanted to carry this with me. Whether those two
are the same thing, I don't know either."

Elpis smiled quietly beside him.

"…I'm just good at one thing — waiting. This time I'll put it in the
place I wait."

The two of them walked off along the road that follows the water
channel. I stood there until they turned the corner.

*Standing there, I thought back briefly to my moving days. Back then I
was always the one packing boxes. Deciding what to keep and what to
throw out was always my share. What I wrapped this time wasn't my own
family's things. It was baggage headed for someone else's city. And
yet my hands remembered the same method. Set in the center, fold the
four corners, tie once more with string. I found that strange. I won't
write more than that.*

---

The Scales finished second.

Pheugon sat for a long while in silence in front of the finished
bundle. Trope waited beside him.

"…I'm still a little afraid to carry this," Pheugon said.

"…Of what?" I asked.

"…I used those sentences in here to judge people, once. Now I'm going
to carry it back and stand in that same seat again."

Trope heard that and spoke slowly.

"…Then stand there differently, this time."

Pheugon looked at him. Trope said nothing further. He just took up
the bundle together with him.

Watching the two of them carry it together, I thought of how, in the
Reckoning record, they'd stood in such different places. Even now
they weren't standing in quite the same place. But they were carrying
the same load.

"…Have you found some certainty, Trope?" I asked.

Trope readjusted his hold on the bundle and answered.

"…No. Still not balanced. But it feels closer than before. That's
enough."

I started to write that in my notebook, then stopped. Some things were
better just heard, and left.

---

The Roll came third.

Xene and Lytron's turn came. Lytron took the finished bundle and
weighed it lightly in his hands.

"…Lighter than I expected," he said.

"…The contents aren't light," I answered.

"…I know. I just mean the weight of the paper."

Xene laughed beside him.

"…Lytron always talks that way. Says it's light, says it's nothing.
But he seems to be carrying this one a little differently."

"…What's different?" I asked.

"…Before, whenever he received something, he'd turn it right into a
boast. This time he's looking at it a little longer."

Lytron said nothing much to that. He just readjusted the bundle
against his chest once.

"…I still think I've never lost anything," he said at the door. "But
these days that doesn't sound like a boast anymore. I don't know why."

Xene heard that and took his arm once. The two of them left that way.

---

Perpetuity came fourth.

Inside was the part Anemos had copied with his own hand. Taking the
bundle, he ran his fingertip once over the inner cover.

"…Will you ask me that again, later," he said quietly.

I didn't know who he was speaking to. Eikon, or just himself. I
didn't ask.

Eikon, beside him, packing up, said.

"…I still lack nothing. I don't know what will change by carrying
this."

"…But you're carrying it," I said.

"…Yes. That's one thing, and this is another."

That answer felt like him. I didn't press further.

The two of them left together the next day. They didn't say long
goodbyes. Eikon raised a hand once, and Anemos bowed his head beside
him. That was all.

Watching the place where they'd left, I noticed for the first time
that one shelf compartment in the repository was empty. There had
always been something set there before. Not anymore.

---

The Consensus came fifth.

Hora and Tyche were the last to remain. Even with the finished bundle
in front of her, Tyche still said the same thing.

"…This is all just coincidence lining up."

"…But you're taking it, aren't you?" I asked.

"…Of course I'm taking it. Coincidence or not, this is our city's
story."

Hora smiled quietly beside her. He added nothing. He just let Tyche
be as she was.

As the two of them gathered up the bundle to leave, I handed them a
map of the water channel. Speon had drawn it.

"…What's this for?" Tyche asked.

"…So you don't lose your way."

Tyche took the map, smiling.

"…Turns out this became necessary too, by coincidence."

I said nothing to that. I only smiled.

"…What will you do, Hora, once you're back?" I asked.

Hora thought for a moment.

"…Just what I always do. Go out to the field, tend the water. Set this
somewhere I can see it, and open it now and then — there's nothing
special beyond that for me to do."

"…Is that enough?" I asked.

"…Yes. I never expected anything special, to begin with."

I liked that answer. I wrote it down.

---

Each time a set left, I told Arche.

"…Arche, one copy of the Ledger just went out. Zeteo and Elpis took
it."

*Confirmed. I will record it.*

"…Is this processed as closed?"

*No. It remains in progress. The item is updated to: dispatch
complete.*

I let that answer pass, the first time. The second, the third time a
set left, the same answer came back. Dispatch complete. Not closed.

"…Isn't this one finished either?" Histor asked one day, hearing that
answer.

*Dispatch complete is a status distinct from closing. It reflects only
the fact that the item has moved.*

"…Then what would make it close?"

*That item is not defined.*

Hearing that, I laughed. It was the sentence I always heard. This time
it didn't frustrate me the way it used to. I just copied it into my
notebook, and wrote a small line beside it. *Dispatch complete. That's
enough.*

Histor saw that sentence beside me and said nothing. He just copied
the same thing into his own notebook. Later I snuck a look at what
he'd written. It was the same as mine.

---

The six sets left, one by one, that way.

The repository slowly emptied out. Even the last room, always
crowded with people, grew quiet now. That quiet felt unfamiliar. For
months this room had been full of people's voices.

I walked the shelves and counted the empty spots. One, two, three,
four, five. Five compartments stood empty. I wrote that number in my
notebook. It was the kind of number that once would have made me
anxious, as though something were lost. This time it wasn't.

Mena hadn't left yet. Neither had Speon. Both of them belonged to
Loop, this city itself. There was nowhere for them to go.

"…It's strange," I said to Mena. "Everyone's leaving, and only we're
staying."

Mena thought about that for a moment.

"…We're already in the place we belong. That feels all right, in its
own way."

Hearing that, I thought of the work still left. The seventh set.
Loop's own. That one still wasn't finished.

---

One day Speon spread out the water channel map again and called me
over.

"…Mone, look at this."

He marked six small dots on the map. The six cities. And he drew lines
between those dots — the paths the water channel followed.

"…This line, it used to flow only one direction. This way." He
pointed to Loop. "But lately it goes the other way too. Because
people walk this road back and forth now."

"…That doesn't mean the water flows backward, does it?" I asked.

"…No. The water flows the same as always. But the road is used both
ways now. It used to be a road that only led in."

Mena, looking at the map beside us, said.

"…I think that's a good thing. Water that only flows one way ends up
pooling somewhere."

I wrote that down in my notebook. Speon, folding up the map, added one
more thing.

"…I'm not the one who cut this road. I said that before. But these
days I think it's a good thing I wasn't. If I'd cut it myself, I never
would have imagined it being used both ways like this."

---

Histor stayed on through those months.

He watched the others leave, one by one, and every time, walked them
out to the door. I didn't ask why he went to such lengths. I just
watched.

"…Everyone's carrying it back to their own city," he said one day.

"…Yes."

"…Before, it had to stay here. Sealed, even."

"…It did."

"…But now everyone just takes it. No one stops them, no one asks if
they're qualified."

I only nodded at that. I added nothing more.

He said only that and didn't go on. I didn't press to know what that
silence meant. I only watched him standing at the door, each time.

---

The seventh set — our own city's share — still wasn't finished by the
end of that year.

There was a reason for that. The other six sets only had to copy
something already written. Ours was blank. Copying a blank page blank
didn't move quickly by hand. Histor and I were carrying that work
together, but even between the two of us it went slowly.

The Keeper never rushed the delay. He simply came every day and wiped
down the now-empty shelves once, before leaving.

"…You clean the empty spots too?" I asked.

"…I do. If you don't clean them just because they're empty, dust just
piles up there."

That answer felt like him. From the start he'd been someone who put
things down, someone who kept watch over things.

One evening, sitting beside Histor in the last room, copying blank
pages, I asked suddenly.

"…What happens once this is finished?"

"…I don't know," he answered. "This is my first time doing this too."

I only nodded at that. He turned his eyes back to the blank page in
front of him. The only sound in the room was the turning of paper. I
listened to that sound for a long while, then picked up my pen again.

*Closing this chapter, I want to set down one thing. All that autumn
and winter, I watched people carry their share away. Every time
someone left, I wanted to add something. I didn't. I just packed the
bundle with them, walked them to the door, and turned back.*

*That winter, for the first time, the repository was full not of
people, but of empty spaces. I didn't think that was lonely. I'll only
set down this much — that these empty spaces were a kind that hadn't
existed here before.*

The seventh set wasn't finished until the following spring.
