# Chapter 9. A Closed Circle

> _Previously_ — Mone asked Arche directly why closing never happens. *The closing condition has not been met.* *That item is not defined.* Arche hid nothing. There was simply no answer. The Keeper said it wasn't time for Mone to know the other half yet.

A few days later, the Keeper brought up the past himself. That wasn't a common thing.

We were staying late that evening, sorting old bundles. Our hands were slow, so it took time. Turning a page, the Keeper said, out of nowhere,

"…When I first came here, this city was a little different from now."

I stopped what I was doing, startled. "…How was it different?"

---

"…Arche wasn't as accurate as it is now. It sometimes gave a vague answer too," the Keeper went on, sorting paper. "…People trusted Arche a little less, back then."

"…And now?"

"…Now nobody doubts it. Not once wrong, for that long."

That sounded like something to be proud of, to me. "…Isn't that a good thing?"

"…It is," the Keeper said, nodding. "…Arche is a good system. I've never denied that."

---

I felt something in that tone. It seemed like there was something more, tucked inside "never denied."

"…But?" I asked.

The Keeper looked at me a moment, then went back to moving his hands.

"…There's no but. It's just a good system."

I took that answer at face value, at the time. Looking back now, I think he was holding something back. Or he thought it wasn't yet time to say it. Probably both.

---

"…Do you know what Arche calls this city?" the Keeper asked, out of nowhere.

"…What do you mean?"

"…Ask it, sometime."

I asked, right there. "…Arche, what do you call this city?"

*Loop is classified as a closed circle.*

That sounded good to me. "…A closed circle? What a striking phrase, really."

The Keeper said nothing.

---

Curious about the expression, I asked further.

"…Arche, what does a closed circle mean?"

*It means a cyclical structure is being stably maintained, and that a state is self-sustaining without need of outside intervention.*

Hearing that, I liked it even more. "…So it means our city runs really well."

*That is an interpretation you may draw.*

I wrote that expression in my notebook that day. *A closed circle — praise.* I even put an exclamation point after it.

---

*Writing this chapter now, I want to set down precisely how naive I was that day. I took the phrase as boasting. I thought "closed" only meant something good. That a circle being closed and that circle being finished were different things — I had no idea of that at all, at the time.*

*A closed circle. A circle shut, and yet not ended. What that phrase truly meant comes back again, much further into this book. For now I'll only note that the sixteen-year-old I was took it as praise.*

---

The Keeper glanced at my notebook and said nothing. I didn't think that silence strange. He'd never been much of a talker.

"…What do you think of this phrase, Keeper?" I asked.

The Keeper thought for a moment and answered.

"…It's an accurate phrase."

"…Is that praise?"

"…Accurate and praise are different things."

I didn't understand that, then. I understand it a little now.

---

That night I asked Arche a bit more. The phrase "cyclical structure" kept weighing on my mind.

"…Arche, what is it that's cycling, in a cyclical structure?"

*The process of question, response, record, and indexing repeats continuously. Every case is processed, cross-referenced, and updated, but the cycle continues with no point of closing.*

Hearing that answer, I felt strange for the first time. "…So it just keeps going around, not finishing."

*Correct. "Closed" refers to the circle's shape being shut. It does not mean the circle has stopped.*

I listened to that sentence as though reading it over, again and again.

---

The next day, I brought this back up with the Keeper.

"…Keeper, a closed circle meant it just keeps going around. Not that it's finished."

The Keeper nodded. "…That's right."

"…You knew that from the start?"

"…Yes."

"…Then why didn't you tell me?"

The Keeper paused his hands a moment.

"…I was waiting for you to ask."

---

That answer stung a little. "…Why wait? You could've just told me."

"…What I tell you and what you find out for yourself are different. What you find out for yourself, you don't forget."

I had nothing to say to that. It was true. I still remember, exactly, to this day, the answer I got that day by asking Arche myself. If someone had simply told me, it wouldn't have stayed with me so clearly.

*I came to realize, belatedly, that this had been the Keeper's way of teaching. He wasn't withholding answers — he was letting me get them by asking myself. A way of his own, different from the six officials before him.*

---

From that day on I came to see the phrase "closed circle" differently. Not a boast, not a flaw. Just an accurate description. A circle that's closed, a circle that keeps turning, and yet a circle that doesn't finish.

*I won't reveal yet, in this chapter, why this expression is exactly the same as this city's name. I don't come to know that precisely myself until much later. For now I'll only note that I went back and looked at the sentence I'd once written as praise. I returned to that page in my notebook, and didn't erase the exclamation point. Instead I drew a small question mark, beside it.*

---

The Keeper told a bit more of the past that night. He talked more than usual.

"…When I first came here, someone else was in this seat," the Keeper said.

"…Before you, Keeper?"

"…Yes. I learned from them. The way I'm teaching you now."

I was curious what kind of person that had been. "…What were they like?"

"…Slower than me." The Keeper gave a small smile. "…It was frustrating, back then. I understand it now, though. Why they were that slow."

---

"…Why were they slow?" I asked.

The Keeper paused a moment, then went on.

"…Because they knew this work never ends. There was no reason to hurry."

I fell quiet, hearing that. It sounded heavy. I couldn't tell whether no reason to hurry meant no hope, or something else.

"…Where is that person now?"

"…Retired. Living on the far side of the city. A letter comes now and then."

"…What does it say?"

The Keeper gave a faint smile. "…Nothing much. Whether I'm doing all right. Whether the water channel's still the same."

---

Something about that letter kept lingering in my mind, somehow. "…Do they know the phrase closed circle too?"

"…Of course they do. They're the one who told me that phrase, in the first place."

"…How did they tell you?"

The Keeper thought a moment and said,

"…They had me ask Arche directly too, just like you. They did the same thing. Not giving the answer, and waiting."

Only then did I sense something like a current running down through this city. Not giving the answer at once, having you ask for yourself, having you find it out yourself. That was the way of everyone who'd passed through this seat.

---

"…But that way, you find out late," I said. "…You'd know faster if someone just told you."

"…Knowing fast and knowing properly are different things," the Keeper said. "…What you know fast, you forget fast too."

I couldn't argue with that. I really hadn't forgotten a single letter of the answer I'd heard, asking Arche myself that day, even now.

*Through this conversation I felt, for the first time, that this repository's way wasn't mere chance. There was a way handed down across generations. Slowly, letting you find things out yourself. I stood right in the middle of that way. And it crossed my mind, briefly, that someday I too would hand that way on to someone else.*

---

Walking home that day, I thought again about the phrase closed circle. When I first heard it, it had been praise; now it was a sentence with a question mark attached. I decided it was fine to hold both feelings at once. This city being a good city, and this city having a question with no answer, didn't erase each other.

*I tried hard, throughout this whole book, to hold on to this frame of mind. Liking something and harboring a question about it can exist together. At sixteen I didn't know that in exact words, but I was already living it, in my body.*

---

A few days later, curious to know more about that retired predecessor, I asked Arche.

"…Arche, is there a record of the previous Keeper?"

*There is. Records of their tenure and duties survive. However, personal reflections are not recorded.*

That answer disappointed me a little. I wanted to know why they'd been so slow, what exactly it was they'd known. What Arche held was accurate, but it held no heart inside it.

*I felt, for the first time, right then, that there was a place even Arche's precision couldn't reach. Something a person hands to another person, directly. That wasn't something a record could fully carry over.*

---

I grew curious again about why this city was called Loop. What I'd learned at school was only that it had been called that since long ago.

"…Arche, who named this city Loop?" I asked.

*No record of who named it survives. However, the name "Loop" is presumed to be an expression describing the city's cyclical mode of operation.*

"…Is that the same meaning as closed circle?"

*They appear to refer to similar concepts. However, the origin of the name itself cannot be confirmed.*

I came to see that the name and the nickname pointed to the same meaning. I thought that couldn't be a coincidence.

---

That evening I asked the Keeper again.

"…Is the name Loop connected to closed circle too?"

The Keeper smiled briefly. "…Sharp."

"…Am I right?"

"…Probably. I can't be sure myself. But it's natural to think so."

I was satisfied with that answer. It wasn't a complete answer, but it was a connection I'd found myself. I wrote one more thing in my notebook that day. *Loop = closed circle = something that turns and never ends.* I drew two lines under it.

---

From about this point, I began to see this city through slightly different eyes. That doesn't mean I came to see it badly. I still loved this city. But now I looked at it knowing it ran perfectly closed, and yet, unfinished.

Every time I saw someone asking Arche something as I walked, I thought about how those questions ended up piling somewhere, all "in progress." Most of those people probably didn't know. I hadn't known either, until half a year before.

*I didn't think I needed to tell those people this. But walking along, being the only one who knew that fact, felt like a slightly different kind of weight.*

---

Before closing this chapter, I want to set down the last thing the Keeper said that day.

"…Mone." The Keeper called my name. Calling me by name wasn't common, either.

"…Yes."

"…Don't take the phrase closed circle the wrong way. This is truly a good city. I've never once doubted that."

"…Yes, I know."

"…But good and finished are different problems. Just remember that."

I copied that line to the very bottom of my notebook that day. Even now, opening that page, the sentence is still there, exactly as it was. The handwriting shakes a little — my hand must have been trembling a little, that day.

---

After that, I tried mentioning this to other people too, lightly. I asked a neighbor woman I met at the market.

"…Ma'am, have you heard the phrase closed circle?"

"…No, first I'm hearing it. What's that?"

"…Arche calls our city that."

The woman laughed. "…Really? Must be a good meaning, then. Would Arche ever say something bad about us?"

I had nothing to say to that. Most people were like this. Because they trusted Arche, they assumed whatever it said must be good. I'd been exactly the same, not long before.

*I don't think this reaction was wrong. Arche truly hadn't spoken with a bad meaning. But I now knew that a good meaning and a simple meaning are different things. The woman still heard the expression only as praise, and I didn't bother correcting her. I didn't know enough myself to have the right to correct her.*

---

Closing out this chapter, I want to set down how the way I felt, the first time I heard this phrase, differs from how I feel using it now, writing it again. At first, I was glad. Now, it's a little complicated. But that complication didn't make me love this city any less. If anything, it made me look at it more closely.

I walked home that night, too, listening to the sound of the water channel. I turned the phrase closed circle over in my mouth a few times. Strangely, I didn't dislike it. If anything, it felt a little closer to me now.
