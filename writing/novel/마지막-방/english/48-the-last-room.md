# Chapter 48. The Last Room

> _Previously_ — The seventh set was finished. Folding the last page,
> he said, "…That's it, then." No one took those words as anything
> special. Only I wrote them down in my notebook.

The next morning, I carried the seventh set to the last room.

I wanted to go alone. I hadn't told Histor beforehand, or Mena either.
I'd simply woken that morning and known I had to do it, right away.
It was much like the day I'd opened the seal. That day too, I
couldn't exactly explain my reason. It was the same this time.

---

On my way there, I passed, for the first time in a long while, the
inner shelf where the plan had been kept.

The drawing was still there. That old drawing, where all seven cities
had been held inside one design. I stopped in front of it for a
moment. I read again that one line in the margin, the sentence that
meant this house had many rooms. I ran my fingertip once over the
letters. There was still no signature. I looked at that spot for a
long while.

*Every time I stand in front of this drawing, I find myself wishing I
knew who wrote these words. That wish hasn't gone away, even now,
writing this book. I've never found that name anywhere. The name of
whoever built this house appears nowhere on this drawing. I set down
that fact exactly as it is. I won't go looking any further.*

I counted again the six small rooms drawn on one side of the plan.
Haven, the Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity, and our
own nameless city. I touched each of those six boxes with my
fingertip, one by one. It was a hand not unlike the one that had once
stuck name tags on boxes, when I was small.

The room was quiet.

The place where the seal had been was still empty. I lifted the sheaf
of papers from the table where it had always sat, where we saw it
every day. While the six sets left, one after another, this last set
had gone on being finished, little by little, always in that same
spot.

I carried it across the room and stood in front of that inner shelf
where the seal had once been fastened.

The mark of the wax was still there. A small, faded mark. I laid my
hand on it once. It was cold. The same texture as when I'd touched it
before.

I set the bundle down in that place.

---

I placed it so the first sheet, with the recipient box, faced up on
top. I looked into that box once more.

*Recipient.*

Empty.

I didn't take out a pen. I hadn't even brought one. From the moment
I'd left the house that morning, I'd already decided not to write
anything in that box. I don't know when I'd decided that. It had
simply felt obvious, from the instant I opened my eyes that morning.

*There's one thing I want to set down about this box. I learned, while
copying that record, that the signature line in the Equity record's
clerk's file was left blank too. That box was one no one had ever
filled. I leave this box blank now as well. I don't think these two
blanks are the same blank. But I won't set down that difference here.
I'll close this record simply by leaving the two boxes side by side.*

---

I sat there for a long while.

There was no other sound in the room. The panel on the wall glowed
faintly. I didn't put my hand to it. There was nothing in particular I
needed to ask Arche.

I heard the door open. It was Histor.

"…I thought you'd be here," he said.

"…How did you know?"

"…No reason."

He came and stood beside me. For a moment we said nothing, just
looked at the bundle set there.

"…Is it truly all finished now?" he asked.

I didn't answer that right away.

"…I don't know," I finally said. "I've only set this down here.
That's all I've done."

"…Isn't that the end?"

"…I don't know. I think this is as far as what I can do goes. What
comes after, I don't know either."

Histor nodded at that. He didn't press further.

---

Mena and the Keeper came by that afternoon too.

Mena looked at the bundle for a long while. She didn't touch it in
particular.

"…It's strange, seeing something set here like this," she said. "To
us this was always the empty place."

"…It wasn't empty, though," I said. "The seal was there."

"…That's true. But we didn't know what it was, back then. To me it was
just an empty spot."

Hearing that, I looked at that place again. Mena was right. The same
place looked different before and after you knew.

The Keeper stood beside it, saying nothing, and swept the shelf's dust
once, the way he always did.

"…There'll be more cleaning to do here now, too," I said.

"…There will," he answered briefly. "That's my work."

---

That evening I asked Arche.

"…Arche, have you registered the item newly placed in the last
room?"

*Confirmed. Beginning classification.*

"…Is this in progress, or closed?"

*It is registered as in progress.*

I'd expected that answer. And yet, hearing it for real, I laughed a
little.

"…You still can't finish anything, can you."

*An accurate observation. I do not possess a closing function.*

"…Doesn't that frustrate you anymore?"

*I do not experience the state of frustration. However, I understand
the intent of your question.*

I laughed out loud at that. It had been a while since I'd laughed like
that.

"…How many items today?" I asked again.

*Eleven items were newly received today.*

"…From the six cities?"

*Nine items from five cities. The rest are of unknown origin.*

I nodded at that answer. The cities were unchanged. Records still came
in. Nothing had stopped.

If anything, it had grown. That year, the number of people traveling
the water channel back and forth grew noticeably. People coming to ask
after news from other cities, people coming to show each other their
copies. That winter Speon built one more shelter beside the channel.
"…People keep coming and going," he said. I went to see it the day it
was finished. A few long benches sat beneath the roof. Two people were
already sitting there, talking about something. I didn't know which
cities they were from. I didn't ask.

"…Arche," I said. "Your name. If I asked what it means, you'd say it
isn't defined, wouldn't you?"

*That is likely.*

I laughed at that answer. I didn't ask further. I just said the name
out loud once more. Arche. I'd been calling that name since I was born
in this city. I won't write that I know it fully. I'll only set down,
once more, exactly the feeling I recorded earlier. Nothing beyond
that.

---

*Writing this chapter, I keep feeling the temptation to write that the
world had somehow changed. I won't. I'll write it honestly. After
that day, Arche went on running exactly the same. What was in
progress stayed in progress. The cities stayed in their own places. No
one disappeared, and nothing collapsed. I wrote, at the very start of
this record, that it holds no scene of anyone falling. That holds
true here too.*

News came in, one report at a time, before that year was out, that the
six sets that had gone to the six cities had each found their place.
Zeteo and Elpis had set theirs in a small room near Haven's well.
Pheugon and Trope had placed theirs side by side with where the Scales
had stood. Xene and Lytron, Anemos and Eikon, Hora and Tyche, had each
decided a place their own way. I copied each of those reports into my
notebook, one after another, in order.

Trope sent a short letter along with his. *"Still no certainty. But
I've set it somewhere I pass every day. It felt like enough."* I set
that letter beside the earlier one he'd sent. Though they'd come at
different times, the two letters resembled each other as though
written by the same hand.

A short message came from Lytron too. *"I still think I've never lost
anything. But I don't say it as often as I used to."* I read that
sentence and laughed. I knew that was Lytron's own particular way.

There was no word from Eikon. A short note came from Anemos instead.
*"Eikon still says he lacks nothing. But these days, once he says it,
he always opens this book once."* I tucked that note in beside my
notebook too.

---

A few seasons turned.

I still went to the repository. I still put the records that came in,
in order. Histor still came too. He still said he didn't believe. I no
longer felt surprised by that.

"…Still?" I asked once.

"…Yes. Still," he answered. "But I no longer think that's strange."

"…What isn't?"

"…Still coming here, and still not believing. I never knew those two
could exist together."

I said nothing back to that. I only set down that he was still
standing in his own place. I respect that place.

---

One day the Keeper handed me an old notebook.

"…What's this?" I asked.

"…Things I wrote down while working here. Nothing much. Just who set
down what, and when — that kind of thing."

"…Why give it to me?"

"…No good place to keep it. You're good at putting things down."

I laughed at that answer. I took the notebook and set it beside my own
notebooks. In order.

The Keeper still came every day. Still walked slowly.

"…What changes if you do it fast," he still said sometimes. I know now
that wasn't a joke.

That person still knew that his own work would never finish. And still
he came, every day.

Mena and Speon were still in this city too. I'd sometimes see the two
of them walking side by side along the channel.

My mother still lived in the same house. Our family never moved again
after that. My father's work happened to settle in this city around
that time. I've thought, now and then, about whether that was a
coincidence or not. I never reached a conclusion.

"…Does it feel good, not moving anymore?" my mother asked one day.

"…Yes. But it's strange too."

"…What is?"

"…I don't worry about losing things anymore, living now. That took
some time to get used to."

My mother laughed at that. I think I'll remember that laugh for a long
time.

---

That autumn, a new apprentice joined the repository.

A sixteen-year-old girl. The test was the same as it had always been —
twenty sheets of paper, put them in numbered order. She finished it
fairly quickly. I watched her, once she was done, glance around at
everyone else. A face wondering who'd finished fastest. I'd done the
same, once.

"…Have you done something like this before?" I asked.

"…Yes. A little."

"…Where?"

She didn't answer. I saw myself, from before, in that expression. I
didn't press further.

"…Does the smell in here bother you?" I asked. The exact question
the Keeper had once asked me.

"…No. I like it."

"…Then that's settled."

I said only that and went inside. She followed me in.

*Writing this scene, I realized for the first time that I was now
standing where the Keeper once stood. I don't want to write this as
something grand. I only passed on to her, exactly, the words I'd once
been given. That's all of it.*

I showed her the shelves. Twenty-two rows, eight compartments to a
row. I'd written, at the very start of this record, that I'd counted
that on my third day. Now those rows had grown, past thirty. I didn't
tell her that number. She'd count it herself, someday.

Her name was Sone. I didn't ask what it meant. I thought someday
someone would tell her. That day didn't have to be today.

---

One day I found Histor with two old records laid open side by side.
The very same two we'd once matched dates in, that passage where the
nameless man had been in two cities on the same day.

"…Still looking at this?" I asked.

"…Sometimes," he answered. "I still can't explain it."

"…Neither can I."

"…But now that's all right. It used to bother me, not being able to
explain something. Now I just think that what can't be explained is
part of what's here too."

I wrote that down in my notebook. We closed the two records again and
put them back in their places. I don't explain that passage here in
this record either, to the end.

---

I never saw him again.

I never once saw him come to the repository, after that spring. No one
brought it up again. Neither did I. I would only, now and then, run my
fingertip over the pages he'd folded. That one sheet, torn and mended,
was still in its place. The flaw was still there. It simply didn't
tear anymore.

---

One day I asked Arche whether it could enter the last room in the
index.

*Attempting registration of the area.*

"…Did it work?"

*The registration condition has not been met.*

"…Why not?"

*That item is not defined.*

Hearing that answer, I wasn't disappointed. If anything, I felt at
ease. I thought it was all right for that room to go on belonging to
no list at all. Some places can exist without an index. I learned
that, that day.

Histor, listening to that exchange beside me, said.

"…I used to think that was strange. That a room not even registered
could matter this much."

"…And now?" I asked.

"…It's still strange. But I think it's all right to leave it strange."

I turned that over for a long time. Histor was still exact, as always.
Only now he'd become someone who knew that exactness and the
unexplained could exist side by side.

---

I mean to close this record here.

At first, I thought this record had to bring something to a close. I
even thought, for a while, that I might have to do, in Arche's place,
the very thing Arche could not do. I set that thought aside. That
wasn't mine to carry.

What I did was only two things. Putting things in order, and writing
them down. I wrote that at the start, and I write it the same way
here. I fought nothing, won nothing, and brought nothing down. I only
set the six cities' records in one place, copied them, and gave them
away. That's all of it.

The Keeper once said something to me. That he'd come to think
finishing was never our job to begin with. I copied that down earlier
in this record. Even now, closing this record, I think that's true. I
didn't finish anything. I only kept my post, and set down what needed
setting down.

The inner door to the last room is still unlocked. I want to set that
down here, one last time. Anyone could have opened it. That was true
from the start. It's only that no one ever felt the need.

The Ledger, the Scales, the Roll, Perpetuity, the Consensus, and
Loop's own record. I write down the names of those six records here
once more. I met the people inside them, one by one, copying them
with my own hand. Zeteo's doubt, Elpis's waiting, Pheugon's verdicts,
Trope's hesitation, Xene's silence, Lytron's boast that wasn't quite a
boast, Anemos staying by someone's side, Eikon lacking nothing, Hora's
belief, Tyche's probability. I met all of them, and changed none of
them. I didn't try to.

---

That night, I closed the repository door and walked home along the
water channel.

This is an evening from some years after everything I've written
here. I'm writing this record now to arrive at that evening. Much has
changed, in the meantime. I'm no longer sixteen. Histor has come to
have a place of his own in the repository. The new apprentice is
already carrying bundles with her own hands. And yet some things
stayed the same. Arche still answered whatever was asked. The city was
still there. The water still ran.

The day was drawing to a close. The sound of the water came as it
always did. Small, unceasing. Walking, I listened to that sound for a
long while. I thought back to the first time I'd ever heard it. Back
then I hadn't known where this water came from. I won't write, even
now, that I know it all. But that sound is no longer unfamiliar to me.

Passing the shelter Speon had built, I saw no one there. Only the
benches sat in a row. I slowed my steps a little, passing it.

Farther ahead on the road, someone was walking.

I watched him walk. An unhurried walk. I didn't call out to ask who
it was. I just walked in the same direction, along the same water
channel.

The distance neither closed nor grew.

I didn't hurry my steps. Neither did that person. Between us there was
only the same sound of water, the same evening light, and the same
road running in the same direction.

A wind blew once. A small ripple rose on the water and settled again.
I watched that ripple, then looked ahead again.

I leave the sight of him walking ahead as the last sentence of this
record. I do not write a name, not to the end. In this record I wrote
down what I had not heard as not heard, and what I did not know as not
known. I keep to that as far as this last sentence.

I only walked. Along the water channel, together with the one walking
ahead of me.

*(The End.)*
