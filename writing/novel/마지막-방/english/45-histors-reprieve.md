# Chapter 45. Histor's Reprieve

> _Previously_ — Mena told us what our names meant. Mone: an abiding
> place, a room to stay in. Histor: one who has found out, the root of
> *history*. Histor laughed, and in the end had nothing to say.

A few days later, the Keeper gathered people in the last room.

Mena, Histor, me, and a few others still staying in the city around
that time — Zeteo, Elpis, Trope, Xene. They'd all come to see the six
copies, and simply stayed on in that room.

The Keeper held up the blank sheaf and spoke.

"…I think we need to decide what to do with this now."

---

"…Decide how?" Zeteo asked.

"…There's one set of the six copies now. But one set isn't enough.
Everyone's going back to their own city, aren't they. Shouldn't each
person take a set with them?"

I was surprised to hear that. I'd thought all along that one set would
be enough. I hadn't considered that we'd need several.

"…How many sets do we need?" I asked.

"…At least six. One for each city."

"…Isn't it seven?" Histor asked. "There's ours too."

"…Right. Seven sets."

Hearing that, I felt the size of this plan for the first time. One for
each of the six cities, plus the original that would stay in this
city. I thought this might have been, all along, what this old seal
had meant to do.

---

Hearing that number, I felt my wrist begin to ache again already. One
set alone had taken a month to copy. Six sets would be half a year.

"…Are you going to do all of that by hand?" Histor asked.

"…I don't know," I answered. "I hadn't thought that far yet."

I put my hand on the panel by the wall and asked Arche.

"…Arche, can you help make six copies?"

*Possible. Using the copy function, it can be completed instantly.*

I hesitated at that for a moment. This time, I might really need the
help. One set, I could manage by hand. Six was different.

"…But if we do that, this just becomes a reproduction," Histor said
beside me.

"…Yes. That's what stops me."

The room went quiet for a moment. Everyone looked at each other.

---

Xene spoke first.

"…I can help. I'm a record-keeper by trade, after all."

"…Me too," Elpis said.

Trope quietly raised a hand. He said nothing, but the gesture was his
answer.

Watching that, something in me moved. I'd thought I'd have to do all
of this alone. But here, in this room, people were raising their
hands, with no one telling them to.

---

"…I'll help too," Histor said.

I looked at him, a little surprised at that.

"…Really?"

"…Yes."

Mena raised her hand quietly too. The Keeper didn't raise his. Instead
he said this.

"…I'll keep the place, instead of the hands. So nothing gets lost —
who puts what where."

That felt like him. From the start he'd been someone who put things
down, someone who held onto things.

"…But Histor," I said, choosing my words, "why do you want to help
with this? Last time I asked, you said it wasn't out of certainty."

---

He thought for a moment.

"…That's right. I still have no certainty."

"…Then why?"

"…Not because I believe it. Because this must not be lost."

I heard that sentence twice. The first time I nearly let it pass. The
second time it settled into me.

Mena heard that and asked quietly.

"…Histor, does that have to do with what happened a few days ago?"

Mena meant the day she'd told us what our names meant. Histor
hesitated a moment.

"…Maybe. But I can't say it's exactly because of that. I just keep
having this feeling, lately, that it mustn't be lost. I can't explain
the reason either."

"…You don't have to explain it," Mena answered. "Not being able to
explain something can be a good enough reason too, sometimes."

I thought that sounded like something close to an answer I'd once
given myself. *I don't know what changes. But if it stays shut it just
stays here.* Histor was now saying something similar, in his own way.

---

*I copy this sentence down with the most care of anything in this
record. There's a temptation to write that he wore some dramatic
expression as he said it. He didn't. He spoke as he always did.
Exactly, and evenly.*

"…What do you mean by that?" I asked.

"…I still don't know what any of this means. I don't know why the
blank paper was blank, I don't know who that person is. But I know one
thing. If this is lost, no one will ever be able to stand in this
place again. I don't want that."

---

Zeteo heard that and nodded.

"…Same for me. I still haven't found an answer. But I don't want to
lose this."

Elpis, beside him, smiled quietly.

"…The two of us are still standing in different places."

"…True enough."

Watching those two siblings, I confirmed again that they were nearly
unchanged since Haven. Zeteo was still searching, and Elpis was still
waiting. And yet both of them, in this room, right now, had their
hands raised the same way.

---

The Keeper, watching all this, said.

"…I think this is a good thing."

"…What is?" I asked.

"…Everyone's here for a different reason. Some because they believe,
some because they don't, some just because they hate the thought of
it disappearing. But the hands are all moving together. I think that's
the shape this work is supposed to take."

I wrote that down in my notebook.

---

We began dividing up the work that day.

Xene and Elpis took the Roll and Perpetuity. Zeteo and Trope took the
Ledger and the Scales. Histor and I decided to take the Consensus and
Loop's own blank record.

That arrangement pleased me, strangely. Rather than each person
copying their own city's record fresh, we each took a different city's
records. It wasn't copying your own story — it was carrying someone
else's story through your own hand.

The Keeper saw that arrangement and said.

"…Who decided this?"

"…No one, really. It just worked out this way."

"…Curious. I'd think copying someone else's story would sit easier
than copying your own."

I nodded at that. It was true. I'd taken on our own city's blank paper
together with Histor, and that was far less heavy than taking it on
alone.

---

The work took several weeks. During that time I watched the others
too.

Xene, copying the Roll, sometimes stopped her hand. She was still
someone afraid of recording. But this time, after stopping, she took
up the pen again. Faster than before.

"…Are you all right?" I asked once.

"…Yes. It's just that this part resembles the part where I stopped
before."

"…You don't have to do it, if it's hard."

"…No. This time I want to see it through to the end."

I heard that and added nothing to it.

---

Trope, copying the Scales, spoke almost not at all. One day, passing
by, I saw his fingertips. He was copying the verdict section. His hand
was trembling slightly.

"…A hard part?" I asked.

"…Yes. This is the sentence I feared most."

"…Then why did you take this on?"

Trope stopped his hand and looked at me.

"…Because if I only avoid what frightens me, I think I'll stay this
way my whole life."

I wrote that down in my notebook.

---

He was still exact. He never misquoted. But something a little
different had been added to that exactness. Before, that exactness had
been the attitude of someone verifying. Now it was closer to the
attitude of someone protecting.

"…You seem different, lately," I said one day.

"…How so?"

"…I don't know. Just a feeling."

He only laughed at that. He neither admitted it nor denied it.

---

Zeteo and Elpis had split the Ledger and Scales between them, and one
day traded places. Curious, I asked.

"…Why did you switch?"

Zeteo answered.

"…I kept wanting to copy only what I already knew well. But that
would just mean doing what I always do. So I took Elpis's part."

Elpis added, smiling.

"…I'm the opposite. I only chose what I didn't know. I like learning
something new."

I liked seeing the two siblings, still looking in different
directions, sitting side by side at the same table.

---

Histor would often sit unusually long in front of the blank record.
There was nothing to copy there yet. The blank had to be copied blank.
He carried each empty page over, one by one, carefully, onto fresh
paper. Copying something with no letters on it at all might sound
absurd. But he was serious about it.

"…Is that copying too?" I asked.

"…Yes. The size, the margins, even the exact position of the
recipient box — all of it, the same."

"…Why go that far?"

"…Because we're going to hand this over too. So it has to be exact."

That answer felt like him.

---

I copied a few of the blank pages together with him too. One day I
stopped my hand and asked him.

"…Isn't it hard? Coming here every day and doing work whose outcome
you don't even know."

He set down the blank page in his hand for a moment.

"…It's hard. But strangely, for something this hard, it's all
right."

"…What do you mean?"

"…Before, I could only keep doing something if it produced an answer.
If no answer came, I'd stop. But this has no answer, and I keep doing
it anyway. I don't know why myself."

"…Doesn't that bother you?"

"…It does. But that you can keep going while it bothers you — I only
learned that this time, for the first time."

I copied that into my notebook. He saw me do it and said nothing.
He just picked up a blank page again.

---

*Closing this chapter, I want to make one thing clear about Histor. He
did not believe, even by the end of this chapter. I set that down as
one of the facts I most want to leave honestly in this final record.
He raised his hand. He copied. He was exact. And he did not believe.*

*I do not refute him. This whole record has not refuted him. I only
copy down what he said, exactly as he said it. "…Not because I believe
it. Because this must not be lost." I don't think that is a weaker
sentence than belief. It's only a different sentence.*

The six sets of copies grew, little by little. We spent most of the
rest of that year on that work.

---

One evening, when we'd all been working late together, the Keeper
brewed tea and brought it in. We stopped our hands for a moment and
sat around.

"…It's been a long time since we made something together like this,"
Elpis said.

"…This is a first, for me," Xene said. "I've always recorded alone."

"…Me too," Trope added quietly.

Hearing that, I felt that what we were doing now was a kind of work
that hadn't existed in any of our own books. All six records had each
been written by one person's hand. This wasn't.

---

Histor, drinking his tea, said suddenly.

"…I feel something strange, these days."

"…What?" I asked.

"…I always liked reading alone. I never liked doing things together
with others. But lately, being here with everyone like this — it isn't
bad."

"…Because you've come to believe?" I asked, a little slyly.

He shook his head.

"…No. That's not it. It's just… it seems better than being alone.
That's all."

I felt that answer was honest. I didn't press him further.

Mena, listening beside us, smiled quietly.

"…I like that answer," she said.

"…What about it?" Histor asked.

"…Better than being alone. That's a good enough reason sometimes. At
least, I think so."

Histor said nothing back to that. He just wrapped both hands around
his teacup.

---

*Closing this chapter, I want to make one thing clear about Histor. He
did not believe, even by the end of this chapter. I set that down as
one of the facts I most want to leave honestly in this final record.
He raised his hand. He copied. He was exact. And he did not believe.*

*I do not refute him. This whole record has not refuted him. I only
copy down what he said, exactly as he said it. "…Not because I believe
it. Because this must not be lost." I don't think that is a weaker
sentence than belief. It's only a different sentence.*

*Writing this record this far, I've learned how to set the believer
and the unbeliever side by side. I've also learned, along with it, not
to make either one win. Even at the end of this chapter, Histor still
stands in his own place. I respect that place.*

At the start of that winter, we sat for a while around three finished
sets, laid side by side. No one suggested celebrating. Everyone just
stood in front of them for a moment.

"…Not even half done, and why does this feel so satisfying," Zeteo
said.

"…It does, doesn't it," Xene answered.

I laughed at that. I liked that laugh.

Three of the six sets were finished before that winter ended. The
remaining three carry into the next chapter.
