# Chapter 30. Hora and Tyche

> _Previously_ — Anemos and Eikon came and went. Eikon still hadn't
> taken release, still said she lacked nothing. Anemos was still
> waiting beside her. "Ask me again later" still held.

The two who came from the Equity arrived with a document in hand.

More precisely, one of them carried it. The woman. The other came empty-handed, walking light, with an easy expression.

The one with the document walked neatly, the walk peculiar to someone bent on checking something through to the end. Seeing that, I immediately thought of a line I'd read in the Equity's record. "She is someone who reads to the end."

"…I'm Hora," the woman with the document said. "…This is Tyche."

Tyche waved a hand.

"…Hello. I just came along."

That "I just came along" stayed with me. The woman in the Equity's record had spoken of herself in a similarly self-effacing way.

---

Once inside the repository, Hora went straight to examining the shelves closely. Then she asked Arche something.

"…Do you have a signature box here? On the documents."

"…Which document?" I asked.

"…Any of them. I'm curious whether this city's records have a signature box too."

Arche answered. *This repository's records have no separate signature
box. Only the recorder's name and the date are entered.*

Hora heard the answer and nodded. She stood there a moment, seeming to turn it over.

"…So here it's not a matter of filling it in or not."

"…What was it a matter of, originally?" I asked.

"…In our city, every document has a signature box, and it's never once been filled in. Because filling it in makes it yours."

---

Tyche's clothes and expression were both light. She looked like someone with no worries in the world, though I came to learn later that this wasn't cynicism — it was real gentleness.

Tyche laughed beside her and said,

"…She's still on about this. The signature box. I'm a little tired of it, honestly."

"…Tired of it?" I asked.

"…Not in a bad way. She's just been thinking about it so long."

Hora glanced at Tyche and turned back to me.

"…She still thinks it's all coincidence."

"…Even now?" I asked Tyche.

Tyche shrugged.

"…Yes. That hasn't changed for me. My sitting in that seat was because of the vacancy and the number, and the same for the deliberation."

Histor asked,

"…So coming here now — that's coincidence too?"

Tyche laughed.

"…Of course. Hora suggested we come, so I came. No other reason."

"…That's all?"

"…Yes. I don't want to make up a reason."

---

Histor came closer and asked Tyche,

"…Do you remember the line from the Equity's record? 'I know you want it to mean something. I do too. But wanting it to mean something isn't evidence that it does.'"

Tyche looked surprised for a moment, then laughed.

"…Wow, those are exactly my words."

"…Do you still think the same?"

"…Yes. Exactly the same. I think an explanation is usually right. Coincidence really is coincidence."

"…And someone else keeps writing that name next to Hora's? How do you explain that?"

Tyche thought for a moment.

"…That's coincidence too. Or someone copied it. There are a lot of people out there who think alike."

---

Hora heard that and said quietly.

"…I don't think it's coincidence. But I don't have any evidence to convince Tyche either."

"…Doesn't that frustrate you?" I asked.

"…It used to. Now… I think we each just see it our own way. Looking at the same thing and standing in different places."

I dimly understood this was the core of the thing between the two of them. Hora and Tyche still drew different conclusions from the same facts. That hadn't changed with time. I didn't try to persuade either of them. I knew that wasn't my place.

Histor asked, carefully.

"…What about Temel? Do you still see him?"

Hora nodded.

"…Yes. Not often. Lately he goes from city to city, still looking for people. He comes to our city sometimes too."

"…What does he say, these days?"

"…Same as always. 'You might just be sitting there because of a number. But even in a seat that's there just because of a number, there's still something you can do.' He still says that."

Tyche heard that and cut in.

"…I've heard that many times too. But I still don't agree. It doesn't look like evidence that there's meaning to it."

"…Then why does it keep sticking with you?" Histor asked.

Tyche thought a moment.

"…I don't know. It just keeps coming to mind. Even though I don't agree."

---

I asked Arche.

"…The name Hora wrote — I heard it turns up in other cities' documents too. Is that still true?"

*The name Jeongsu-a is confirmed in documents across several cities.
The writers differ in each case.*

"…Do you know who wrote it?" Hora asked.

*Author-identifying information remains in each document, but the
circumstances of the writing are not confirmed.*

Hora heard that answer and nodded.

"…Same here. It doesn't come out clear here either."

"…Disappointed?" Histor asked.

"…No. If anything, it's a relief. Because here too, there's no lying."

"…Do you worry a lot about lies?" I asked.

"…Yes. Our city hid nothing. But hiding wasn't the only problem. No one taking responsibility for anything was a problem too. Here, at least, 'I don't know' sounds like a responsible thing to say."

"…What's the difference?"

"…Our secretary says 'it's been arranged,' 'it's been settled.' No subject in the sentence. But here, Arche says 'not defined.' No subject there either, but at least it says exactly what's missing."

I felt that distinction was subtle but important. That how you say the same not-knowing matters. Histor seemed to be writing something down in his notebook at that too.

---

Tyche had been looking out the window toward the water channel this whole time.

"…Is that the water that reached our city?" she asked.

"…Yes, I think so."

"…That's strange. It never even came up as an agenda item in our city. Seeing it here, it really exists."

"…Do you believe it?" I asked.

"…Believe what?"

"…That the water channel connects all seven cities."

Tyche thought for a moment and answered.

"…I believe that part. Water really does flow. But whether someone 'intended' it — I still don't know that."

"…I heard Speon dug it."

"…Yes. But I also heard Speon didn't dig all of it either. That he just joined an already-existing path. So who made that path in the first place?"

I couldn't answer that question. I didn't know either.

Histor answered in my place.

"…Nobody knows. Speon doesn't know either. From what we've found out so far, it seems like it was already built that way, from the start."

"…Who built it?" Tyche asked.

"…No one knows. That doesn't come out here either."

Tyche laughed hearing that answer. Not a mocking laugh — a genuinely amused one.

"…Just as I thought. Everyone saying 'I don't know' is the most honest answer of all. I like that much, at least."

"…That's not proof it isn't coincidence," Histor said, carefully.

"…True. But it's not proof it is coincidence, either. I'm just admitting my own side has no evidence. That doesn't mean I've crossed to the other side."

---

Hora joined that conversation.

"…I've decided not to go looking for the answer to that question. I used to keep searching. Now I've learned that you can still do something without knowing the answer."

"…What do you do?" I asked.

"…I keep writing names down. Every time a new deliberation opens. Other names have started to be added behind mine, one by one. At first it was just me. Now there are a few more."

"…What about the secretary?"

"…He still says he's nothing. But lately, when he says that, he smiles a little. He never used to smile."

Tyche cut into that.

"…That's right. I noticed that too. But I don't know what it means. He might have just smiled, that's all."

"…Is that how you want to read it?" Histor asked.

"…It's not a reading, it's just fact. Beyond that he smiled, I don't know anything more. I don't want to say I know."

Hora said quietly.

"…I think it's more than that. But I can't prove it. So I don't push Tyche to agree. That's not something I can do."

I found it curious that the two of them saw the same smile and took it in such completely different ways. It was one fact, but it had landed in two entirely different places inside them. I pushed down, several times, the urge to decide that place for them.

I was glad for that small change. It wasn't a great conversion, but there was, at least, one new smile.

Histor asked,

"…What about the nine hundred twenty-seven? The people who never got reclassified."

Hora's expression dimmed a little.

"…Still the same. I don't erase that number. If I erase it, I'll forget."

"…Does it torment you?"

"…Yes. I still get tormented every time I see that number. But not erasing it while it torments me — that was the best I could do."

Hearing that, I thought Hora already knew how to go on living without a complete resolution. I was learning something similar myself, in this repository.

---

Before leaving, Tyche said to me,

"…Do you believe all of this, Mone? That the cities were one, that the water channel joins them all, all that?"

I couldn't answer right away.

"…I don't really know. I just write down what I see."

"…That sounds like a good method," Tyche said. "…I do that too. I only believe what I can see."

Hora said, from the side.

"…I believe a little more than what's visible."

"…I know that too," Tyche said, smiling. "…But I'm not going to follow you there."

The two walked out together. Believing different things, and walking in the same direction.

---

That night I unfolded the document Hora had left with us again. A copy of a deliberation record. There was a signature box at the very bottom, and it was empty.

Histor looked it over with me.

"…So this really has never once been filled in."

"…Is it forbidden to fill it in?" I asked.

"…Not forbidden. But no one's filled it. Hora said — if you fill it, the decision becomes yours, so nobody does."

"…Did Hora fill it in?"

"…She said she filled in the name column. When opening a new deliberation. But not this. This is just a copy of an old document."

I looked at that empty box for a long while. I knew this box was the very core of the Equity's story, but seeing it in person, it was much more ordinary paper than I'd expected. Just a line, an empty space.

*I looked at this empty box for a long time. It was strange that an
empty box with no explanation attached could hold so much. I wanted to
remember this moment, so I wrote it down separately.*

---

The next morning, before Hora left, she asked me,

"…Are you glad there's no signature box here, Mone?"

"…I hadn't thought about it."

"…Think about it sometime. Why doesn't this repository have a signature box."

I turned the question over.

"…Maybe… because this isn't a place where someone decides things. It's just a place for laying things down."

Hora nodded.

"…That sounds right. Our city was a place that decided everything. That's why the signature box mattered so much."

"…Do you envy that, here?"

Hora thought for a moment and smiled.

"…A little. But our city's changing too, little by little. Like the names starting to be added behind mine."

---

Just before leaving, Tyche spoke to me separately.

"…Do I look strange to you, Mone? Always saying it's coincidence."

"…No. I'm just… curious. Why do you keep seeing it that way?"

"…I don't know either. It's just that everything I know explains it. I don't want to explain it with something I don't know."

"…That's not a bad reason."

"…No. I like people. I like Hora too. That's just what I think."

Hearing that, I didn't come to dislike Tyche. If anything, I found her honesty comfortable. She wasn't deceiving anyone. Not even herself.

---

After the two left, the Keeper said to me,

"…Those two were the last pair in the Equity's record who never split apart."

"…You know the Equity's story?"

"…I heard it from Histor. That the two of them argued for a long time over the signature box. And now, they're still going around together."

"…Even though their views don't match?"

"…Yes. So people can stay together even when their views don't match, it seems. I find that surprising."

I wrote that down last. "They walk together even though their views
don't match." I thought this sentence had come up more often than
anything else I'd seen recently.

Zeteo and Elpis, Pheugon and Trope, Xene and Lytron, Anemos and Eikon. And now Hora and Tyche too. None of their views had ever fully aligned. And yet they were all still walking together.

*Writing this part, I felt the temptation to draw some big conclusion.
I resisted. I decided just to write down what I saw. The conclusion
didn't feel like it was mine to draw.*

Before leaving, Tyche said one more thing. "…I really haven't changed.
If I come again, I'll say the same thing. That it's all coincidence." I
listened to that with a smile. And wrote it down exactly as she said
it.
