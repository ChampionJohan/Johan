# Chapter 32. The Water Joins Them

> _Previously_ — Loop's three, Temel, Mena, and Speon, gathered together
> for the first time. There was no great reunion. Simply sitting in the
> same room again seemed to be enough. Temel and Speon soon left for
> their own places, and only Mena stayed on.

Speon didn't leave completely.

On the excuse of tending the water channel, he stayed near the repository a few more days. I knew it wasn't only an excuse. He really did walk the channel every day, checking something.

I never knew exactly where he went each day. Sometimes he came back caked in mud, sometimes soaked. When I asked, he'd only answer shortly. "…Just something I need to check."

Then one morning he came into the repository carrying a large bundle of paper.

"…Take a look at this?"

---

He spread the paper out on the table. A map. Hand-drawn, but the lines were quite precise. There were marks of many redrawings. Some parts had heavy ink, and some parts showed traces of being erased and redrawn several times.

"…What is this?" I asked.

"…A map of the water channel. I've been drawing it a little at a time, for years."

There were seven circles drawn on the map. Haven, the Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity, and Loop. Six cities plus Loop made seven.

I traced each of the seven circles with my finger. They were all different sizes. Some large, some small. But the thickness of the lines was all the same. I didn't ask whether Speon had drawn it that way on purpose, or whether it had simply come out that way.

"…Is this all? Seven?" I asked.

"…Yes. This is everything I've confirmed. I don't know if there's more."

The circle marking Loop sat in the middle of the map. The other six were scattered around it.

---

Fine lines were drawn between the circles. All six cities were connected to each other by the water channel. Some lines were straight, some curved. I could tell at a glance that the curves followed the shape of the land.

Histor studied the map for a long while.

"…Are you sure of this? Is it really all connected?"

"…I confirmed it on foot. From Haven all the way to the Equity, I followed every one of them myself."

"…How long did that take?"

"…About six years."

I was startled by that number. It meant he'd walked every one of the seven cities along the water channel for six years.

"…That must have been hard," I said.

"…It was. But each time, I got to know people in each city a little. Met the Adjudicator at the Reckoning, met the administrator at the Harvest. None of them knew where their own city's water came from."

"…No one ever asked?"

"…They must have. But when no answer came, I guess they just figured that's how it's always been. I did the same, at first."

Mena asked, from the side.

"…Then why did you keep asking?"

Speon shrugged.

"…I don't know. I just have a nature that can't stand not knowing something it's curious about. Same as when I was little."

Mena laughed shortly at that.

"…That's right. Even as a kid, it took you twice as long as anyone else to dig one well. Always trying to dig deeper."

"…Same as back then," Histor said.

Speon laughed too.

"…Same. Never changed."

---

"…Why did you go to such lengths?" I asked.

Speon chose his words for a moment.

"…At first I was just curious. Where does the well dug at the Preserve flow to. Following that, I ended up at the Reckoning. People there asked me, 'Where did this water come from,' and since I didn't know either, I just kept following it."

"…And that's how you got all the way here?"

"…Yes. But that's not the important part."

He pointed at one of the lines on the map.

"…These stretches — I didn't dig them."

---

The room went quiet.

"…What do you mean?" Histor asked.

"…I opened water channels beneath the Preserve's valley, at the Reckoning's well, and around the Harvest's fields. That much I really did. But these big stretches that join the cities together — they were already there before I ever dug."

"…Already there?"

"…Yes. Dry. Dry ditches with no water. All I did was set the water flowing through that spot."

---

*I've read this part over many times. Speon didn't claim to have done
all of it. He drew a precise line — between what he opened and what
was already there.*

Histor asked once more, as if confirming that distinction.

"…So saying you made this whole water channel would be wrong."

"…Wrong," Speon answered immediately. "…I just joined it. What was built was already built."

Mena, looking at the map, asked quietly.

"…When you first found that out, how did it feel?"

Speon chose his words for a moment.

"…Scared. Honestly. I thought I'd done something great, and it turned out I'd just set water flowing through a place that was already there."

"…Disappointed?"

"…At first. But not now. Now, if anything, it's a relief. It means I don't have to carry it all myself."

I liked that. Speon spoke of his own contribution in a smaller way, and it didn't sound diminished for it. If anything, it sounded more solid.

"…Why was the ditch there to begin with?" I asked.

Speon shook his head.

"…I don't know. That's what I'm most curious about too. Maybe the land just happened to form that way, or maybe someone dug it long ago and it got forgotten."

"…Did you check?"

"…I did. No record anywhere. Not one record, in any of these six cities, says who dug that ditch or when."

Mena asked quietly.

"…Didn't you ever want to find out who dug it?"

Speon nodded.

"…At first, yes. But at some point I let go of that question."

"…Why?"

"…Because the water flows whether I know or not. Whether I know who dug it or not, that ditch was already there, and all I had to do was set the water flowing through it. If I hadn't let go of the question, I don't think I could have done anything."

That stayed with me for a long time. That you could carry on with the work while still holding on to the question. That you could keep going without knowing the answer.

---

I asked Arche.

"…This water channel, the stretches connecting the cities — can it be confirmed whether it was the original landform?"

*No geological survey records exist for that section. The water
channel's connecting route has been confirmed, but the time of
formation and who formed it are not identified.*

"…Can't even a guess be made?"

*Without any grounding material, no estimate can be made.*

Mena heard that answer and said quietly.

"…Even Arche doesn't know."

"…It says it doesn't. Not hiding it — really no record," I said.

---

The Keeper looked over the map and asked.

"…Could this have something to do with the sealed room?"

My heart gave a little jump at that.

"…What room?" Speon asked.

"…There's a door in the back of the repository that's not in the index. She saw it in Part One. Hasn't been opened yet."

Speon looked at the map again. He put his finger on the middle of the circle representing Loop.

"…This water channel branches off right around here. It looks like it might pass under the repository."

---

I went out back to check the sound of the water again, hearing that. The sound I always heard, but that day it sounded different. Not just water passing through — it felt like water connected to something.

*I don't want to exaggerate this moment. Speon didn't claim for certain
that it had something to do with the sealed room. He only said it
"looked like it might." I'm rendering that exactly as he said it.*

The Keeper saw me coming back from listening to the water and asked.

"…Hear anything?"

"…No. Just the same as usual."

"…Then that's fine. It's not time to open the door yet."

"…How do you know?"

The Keeper thought for a moment before answering.

"…I don't. It's just a feeling. But that feeling hasn't been wrong very often, up to now."

I didn't know whether to believe that or not, so I just nodded.

---

Histor kept looking at the map and said,

"…Do the other cities know about this? That they're connected this way?"

"…No. Besides me, nobody's seen the whole picture. Each city only knows their own stretch of the water channel."

"…Will you tell them?"

Speon thought for a moment.

"…I should. But I won't be in a hurry about it. This wasn't made for anyone to be startled by."

---

Mena looked at the map for a long while and said,

"…I think now that all these cities are one, all of them. I wrote that once, a while back. Seeing this map, I don't think it was just a feeling after all."

"…Are you certain?" Histor asked.

"…Not certain. But the feeling has become material now. That's different."

I liked that distinction. Feeling, and material. Mena didn't mix the two together.

Histor traced the map with his finger and asked,

"…These six lines all gather at Loop. Is this the center?"

Speon shook his head.

"…Hard to call it the center. It's just the oldest water channel, so the other channels flow into it. It doesn't look like anyone planned for it to be the center."

"…How do you know?"

"…You can tell from the direction of the current. Everything flows this way naturally. If a current's been forced, it shows."

I listened to that explanation and felt that Speon wasn't simply treating this map as some mysterious discovery. He'd arrived at this conclusion carefully, checking even the direction of the water.

Histor asked again.

"…So what's the conclusion? Was it made by someone, or not?"

Speon didn't answer that right away. After a while he said,

"…I don't draw conclusions. I only say what I've seen. The water channel was already there, and I set water flowing through it. That's all."

"…You don't know more than that?"

"…I don't. And if I did, I wouldn't say. Pretending to know is the thing I'm most afraid of."

I thought that answer was exactly this repository's own principle. What you don't know, you say you don't know. Arche did that, and so did Speon. I liked that about it.

---

I asked Arche one more thing.

"…Along the direction the water channel flows from — do records come with it too? Records carried down by the water?"

*Confirmed. Approximately three-tenths of the records received have
been received via the water channel.* Arche's answer was flat, as
always.

"…Has that been the case from the start?"

*The exact starting point is not recorded, but the same tendency is
confirmed as far back as the earliest reception history.*

I felt a chill hearing that answer. It meant that from the very moment this repository began, water had been carrying records here. Long before Speon ever joined the water channel.

*I felt the temptation again, at this point, to draw some conclusion.
That someone must have planned this from the very start. But I pushed
that temptation down. Neither Arche nor Speon went that far. I decided
just to write down the fact as it was — that the water channel existed,
and the records came along on top of it.*

"…So there was water even before Speon came?" I asked.

"…No," Speon shook his head. "…Before I joined it, the water was dry. Just the ditch. But you're saying records came along that dry ditch too. Walking, or maybe rolling."

Histor picked that up.

"…So if there's a path, something can pass through it even without water."

"…Seems like it," Speon answered. "…Maybe all I did was make that path a little easier to pass through."

---

Speon rolled the map back up that evening.

"…All right if I leave this here? I have a copy myself."

"…Yes. I'll keep it."

He handed the map to me and said,

"…If anyone asks, don't say I made it all. I just set the water flowing. The water channel was already there."

I made up my mind to keep that exactly as he asked.

---

That night I spread out the map and traced each of the six cities one by one. Zeteo and Elpis had come from Haven. Pheugon and Trope had come from the Reckoning. Xene and Lytron had come from the Harvest. Anemos and Eikon had come from the Preserve. Hora and Tyche had come from the Equity. And these six lines all gathered at Loop.

Histor looked at the map with me.

"…Did all these people come following this water channel?"

"…Not all of them came by water. Some walked. But maybe, since the water channel was already joined, people naturally started coming this way too."

"…What's the difference?"

I thought for a moment.

"…Whether the water channel called the people, or the people followed the water channel. I don't really know either."

Histor didn't offer an answer to that question. He just kept looking at the map.

---

That night I wrote briefly in my notebook. "The water channel is not
something Speon made. It was already there. He only opened it." Reading
it back, this sentence resembled a lot of other things in this
repository. Arche didn't make anything either. It only answers. I don't
make anything either. I only lay things down.

Mena read that sentence beside me and said,

"…Speon would like that sentence too."

"…Should I show him?"

"…No. Just keep it. That kind of sentence isn't written to be shown."

I nodded at that and closed my notebook.

Outside, the water still made its sound. I fell asleep listening to it. I didn't dream. It was just a night the water sound kept going through.

The next morning, the moment I opened my eyes, I unfolded the map I'd seen the day before again. I'd half-expected something new to show up overnight, but the map was exactly as it had been the day before. Seven circles, six lines, and one center everything flowed into.

I folded the map carefully and slipped it between my own records. Beside the flower petal Elpis had given me, beside the scrap of sack Xene had left. Things without explanation were piling up on one side of my shelf, a little at a time.

Last thing that day, I asked myself once more. Speon really hadn't done nothing. He'd opened the way. Only he hadn't been the one to first make that way. I decided not to forget that distinction.
