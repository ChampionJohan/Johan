# Chapter 33. Opening the Door

> _Previously_ — Speon showed us the water channel map. All seven
> cities were joined by water. He said he hadn't opened that channel
> himself — only set water flowing through a ditch that was already
> there. And he said the channel seemed to pass beneath the repository.

For several days I lingered in front of that door.

At the far end of the corridor, the door not in the index. A door I'd first seen back in Part One and simply passed by ever since. Now, every time I stood in front of it, my steps stopped.

There was nothing special about the door. An ordinary wooden door. An ordinary handle. Nothing set it apart from any of the other doors. Only it had no nameplate.

I passed it every day. At least once a day I was bound to — that end of the corridor was on the way to wherever I went to put things away.

The first few days I just walked past. I only confirmed the door was there, and kept going. Then, at some point, my steps started to slow. I don't know exactly when.

*I once tried to count those days. I couldn't. Even I, who's in the
habit of writing down dates, never wrote those days down. The line
between the days I passed by and the days I stopped was blurred.*

Once I even touched the handle. I didn't grip it. Just brushed it with the back of my hand and pulled away. It was cold. That was all.

I mentioned the door to Histor once. He didn't say much. "…You could just open it, couldn't you." That was true, and still I didn't.

*Looking back now, I think I was waiting for something. For someone to
give me permission, or for some sign to come. No sign came. I didn't
know, at the time, that waiting for a sign was itself an excuse.*

The Keeper seemed to have been watching those days too. He didn't say anything, but every time I stopped in front of that door, his own steps slowed with mine. As though he didn't want to rush me.

---

"…Can I open it?" I asked the Keeper one day.

The Keeper looked at me a moment.

"…Why ask now?"

"…It felt like I shouldn't open it, from the start."

"…Who said you shouldn't?"

"…No one. It just… felt that way."

The Keeper nodded.

"…That feeling could have been right, could have been wrong. But it seems like the right time to ask, now."

I thought he'd tell me more. What was inside, say, or how long the door had been there. He didn't.

"…Do you know what's in there?" I asked.

"…No."

"…You've never opened it?"

The Keeper didn't stop his hands from sorting paper as he answered.

"…It wasn't my work to do."

I started to ask more and stopped. The Keeper's expression said asking more would be no use.

*I thought that answer was strange. It's the repository he manages —
was it really all right for him not to know about one door in it? I
hear that differently now. The Keeper was someone who wasn't ashamed
of not knowing. That was simply his way.*

"…Weren't you curious?"

"…I was. But being curious and opening it are different things."

---

I asked Arche too.

"…Can I open that door?"

*That area is not registered, so whether it is access-restricted cannot
be determined.*

"…Does that mean there's no restriction, or that you can't tell?"

*An unregistered area is not subject to restriction.*

I read that answer over a few times. Arche neither blocked the door nor told me to open it. It only said the door fell outside its own jurisdiction.

"…Was this room always here? Or did it come later?" I asked again.

*That information does not exist in the index.*

"…You mean you don't know?"

*There is no basis for judgment regarding an unregistered area.*

That answer frustrated me at first. Later I understood it was an honest answer. Arche doesn't say it knows something it doesn't. It was the same about this room. Not hiding — truly not knowing.

---

Histor heard that exchange and came over.

"…Can I come too?"

"…Yes."

Mena heard the same and came out too.

"…Me too."

Both of them happened to be at the repository that day. Coincidence. I thought about that coincidence a few times later, but decided not to attach any special meaning to it. Everyone had just happened to be there that day.

The three of us walked together to the end of the corridor. The Keeper didn't come along. I looked at the handle in front of the door.

"…What if it's locked?"

Histor gave the handle a slight turn.

"…It's not locked."

He let go of the handle but kept examining the door a moment longer. He touched the hinges, peered at the gap beneath the door.

"…See anything?" I asked.

"…No. Just looking."

He seemed like someone searching for something. I learned later that this was a habit of his. Always examining something first, before drawing any conclusion.

---

Somehow, that made me hesitate even more. If it had been locked, I'd have needed to work up some resolve to force it open. But since it wasn't locked, all I had to do was push. That felt stranger, somehow.

*I go back over this moment often. Why did an unlocked door make me
hesitate more? Looking back now, I think I'd been expecting some great
obstacle. A locked door, a lock to break. There was nothing like that.
The door was just a door.*

---

I gripped the handle and turned it. The door opened without a sound. It wasn't as though the hinges had been oiled, and yet it was strangely quiet.

A little dust fell from the top of the doorframe. That was the only evidence that no one had touched this door in a long time. I wiped my eyes once with the back of my hand. It was nothing much.

There was a threshold, low. I crossed it half-expecting something to change. Nothing changed. Even the feel underfoot was the same.

Inside was a small room.

Truly small. Barely big enough for three people to fit. There was one window, small and set high. A little light came in.

The floor was wood. It creaked when stepped on. The other rooms had wood floors too, but there it made no sound. The Keeper had once said, when people walk somewhere often, the floor wears a path into itself. No one had ever walked in this room.

There was dust, but not much. Not the thick dust particular to an abandoned place — just the thin dust of a room long unused.

There was no wind. There was a window, but it didn't look like wind would come and go through it. The air sat still, but it wasn't stifling. Just air that had been resting a long time.

*I went in bracing for something. A chill, a prickling at the back of
the neck. There was neither. It was just a small room, and it had the
smell particular to a room. There was no sense of anyone being there.
Because no one was.*

---

There was a low table in the middle of the room. On it sat a single bundle of paper. Tied with string.

That was all.

The string was the color of decayed thread. What color it had once been, there was no telling. The bundle wasn't thick. Something you could hold in one hand. The cover held nothing written on it.

I'd expected more. Another door, a stairway, some hidden device. There was nothing. A small room, a low table, a single bundle of paper.

"…Is this everything?" Histor asked. His voice wasn't disappointed. Just confirming.

"…Seems so."

I looked around the room once. The walls were bare. No painting, no sign, nothing else hung there. The floor held nothing. Just the table. No chair. We had to stand.

"…No chair either," I said.

"…Maybe there was no need to sit," Histor answered.

*At this point too, I think I was hoping for more. A closet, a space
under the floor, anything. There was none. The room really was all
there was. I write that fact down here exactly as it was, without
embellishing it.*

---

Mena looked slowly around the room.

"…I can hear the water running, even here."

I listened closely. It was true. A low sound of water came from beyond the wall. It really did seem the water channel Speon had spoken of ran near here.

"…Why isn't this room registered?" I asked.

Mena shook her head.

"…I don't know. But I don't think not being registered means it was hidden. I think… no one ever thought to put it on the list."

"…Aren't you afraid?" I asked. I didn't know myself why I'd asked that.

Mena smiled. Not out loud, just the corners of her mouth lifting slightly.

"…It's not fear. Everyone's like this, in front of a door they haven't opened in a long time. I was too."

"…You too, Mena?"

"…Yes. I stood in front of a door for days once, too. A different door, but."

Mena didn't say more about what door that had been. I didn't ask either.

---

I reached toward the bundle of paper. The string was old but sturdy. The knot was simple.

"…Can I untie it?" I asked. I didn't know who I was asking.

No one answered. Histor and Mena were both just quietly watching me.

I untied the knot.

---

The paper was old but not in bad condition. The writing was by hand, and it looked like several different hands were mixed together. I carefully lifted the top sheet.

There was a date. A very old date. It might have been before this repository even existed.

The contents weren't immediately legible. It looked partly like a drawing, partly like writing, mixed together.

"…Is this a plan?" Mena said, carefully.

Several lines overlapped. Some were straight, some curved. I couldn't tell at a glance what it was drawing.

---

Histor turned the next page. I saw his hand shaking a little.

"…This… has city names," he said, low.

I looked over his shoulder. It was true. Familiar names were written there.

*I want to set down honestly that this moment didn't feel grand. I'd
expected some vast revelation, and what I actually saw was just a few
sheets of old paper. Less astonishing than quiet.*

Histor stared at that paper for a long time. He didn't trace the writing with his fingertip. Whenever he handled an original, he always did that — his fingers moving a half-hand's width above the paper. Not touching it.

"…Are you all right?" I asked.

"…Yes." He answered without taking his eyes off the paper.

He said nothing more for a while after that. I couldn't tell whether that was his silence or simply his concentration. I still don't know.

*I don't know what he was thinking. I didn't ask. I said, at the start
of this record, that what I didn't hear, I would write as not heard.
This is one of those moments.*

---

Mena carefully set the papers back down on the table.

"…We can't see this properly in here. We should take it somewhere brighter."

"…Is it all right to take it?" I asked.

"…It's an unregistered area. Ask Arche."

I asked again. *Items in an unregistered area are not subject to
removal restrictions.* That was all Arche answered.

I laughed at that answer. I laughed at myself, worrying about removal. Asking about removal restrictions, by registration standards, for something taken out of a room that wasn't even registered.

---

We carefully carried the bundle out of the room. We closed the door again. We didn't lock it. There was no way to, to begin with.

As the door closed, the same dust fell again. Watching it, I had no idea then how often this door would come to be opened, in the time ahead.

Walking back down the corridor, I felt strange. For years, passing that door, I'd thought something frightening must be inside. And inside there had just been a small room and a few old sheets of paper.

There was dust on my hands. I noticed it as I walked. Not much. I wiped my palm once against my clothes. That was all.

The weight of those days spent lingering in front of the door — was this all it had come to? I thought that thought without feeling disappointed. Less disappointment than just — so that's how it was.

The Keeper saw us and asked.

"…What was there?"

"…Paper. Looked like an old plan."

The Keeper nodded.

"…Thought so."

"…You knew?"

"…No. But I'm not surprised. If there'd been something amazing in there, someone would have registered it long ago."

"…Aren't you let down?" I asked. I didn't know myself what I'd expected from asking that.

"…What by?"

"…Nothing, just — we hesitated for days, and it turned out to be nothing much."

The Keeper glanced at the bundle of paper, then looked back at me.

"…Nothing much is different from nothing at all."

I didn't understand that right away.

*I understand it now. What was in that room wasn't a revelation. It was
just old paper. But what that paper held, none of us knew, that day.*

I thought he was right.

That night, at home, I wrote about the door on paper. I didn't write much. We opened the door, the room was small, there was paper. That was about it.

*I still have that piece of paper. The handwriting I wrote that evening
was smaller than usual. Not the writing of someone who'd been through
some great event — just the writing of someone finishing an ordinary
day.*
