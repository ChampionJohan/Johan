# Chapter 24. The Same Day

> _Previously_ — They found that the water channel's tide was the only
> standard the six cities truly shared. They began searching all six
> records for tide references. For the first time, Histor said he was
> afraid. "…This is a way of confirming it."

The next morning we sat again on that same floor.

The six rows still lay exactly as before. Haven, the Reckoning, the
Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity, Loop. The tide references we'd
found overnight were pinned as slips beside each row.

"…Shall we start," Histor said.

His voice was lower than usual.

---

I'd already sorted the six rows again the night before. I regathered
the tide slips, which had been divided by city, into an order I could
follow. Haven through Loop, exactly as I always did.

*I've already used this order many times earlier in this record. Haven,
the Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity, Loop. I never
once changed it. There was no reason to. But that day, I'd learn later,
that order wasn't merely habit — it was the condition that made this
day's work possible. Without an order, I would have lost track of which
slip was being compared to which.*

Histor saw my arrangement and said just one thing.

"…This is easy to follow."

"…I just did what I always do."

"…That always-do is exactly what's needed right now."

I was a little surprised, hearing that. He wasn't someone who praised
easily.

---

The work itself was simple. Compare each record's tide references
against the water channel's actual tidal cycle, and convert. Arche
helped with the conversion.

"…Arche, please convert this tide reference in Haven's record to
Loop's standard."

*Conversion result: the event is estimated to fall on day 217, by
Loop's reckoning. Margin of error is within one day.*

I wrote that down. Day 217. We began, one by one, converting the events
in all six rows to the same standard.

Next was the date of the Scales inspection from the Reckoning's
record.

"…Arche, please convert this Scales inspection date in the Reckoning's
record to Loop's standard."

*Conversion result: estimated at day 89, by Loop's reckoning. Margin
of error is within one day.*

I wrote that down too. Day 89. Next was the sowing date from the
Harvest's record. Day 152. Next was the visitation request date from
the Preserve's record. Day 203. As I wrote, I tried to gauge how long
this would take. Six cities, each with references ranging from a
dozen or so to nearly thirty. Every one had to be worked through.

Histor rechecked each number I wrote, one at a time. If I wrote 217, he
wrote 217 separately too. He wanted to see whether the same number came
out by a different method. The first few times, my number and his
matched exactly. Even so, he wrote it down again each time.

"…Why write it again when the number's the same?" I asked.

"…Getting the same answer twice by the same method doesn't tell me
whether a different method gives the same answer. I'm checking my own
way, separately."

I heard that and looked again at the pile of paper in front of me.
This work was possible not because we were clever. I just did what I
always did. Sort what comes in by kind, arrange the sorted by
something close to date order, and copy out what's arranged. It wasn't
different from what I'd learned across nine moves. Writing down what
was in a box, putting the same kind of thing in the same box. I was
only carrying that habit over onto paper now.

*I want to state clearly, writing this passage, that there was no
special insight in what we did that day. If there was anything, it was
only the sorting method I'd learned across nine moves, combined with
the cross-checking method Histor had practiced his whole life. Neither
was a newly invented skill. We were only doing what we already did,
more of it, that was all.*

---

All that morning we worked in silence. Histor called out numbers, I
wrote them down, and arranged them into a new chart. This time not by
city, but by date.

By noon, the chart had filled up considerably. The events of six
different cities were beginning to sit on a single line. I felt an odd
sensation, watching that chart. It was the first time I'd seen matters
from different cities align on a single line.

In the middle of the chart filling, Histor kept stopping to ask Arche
something on the side.

"…Arche, could there be a problem with this conversion method?"

*The tidal cycle of the water channel arrives at different times at
different points. Haven lies upstream on the water channel; the
Reckoning lies downstream. If the difference in arrival time between
upstream and downstream isn't accounted for, an error could occur in
the conversion.*

I looked at Histor as I heard that answer. His face brightened a
little. The face of someone who'd found something.

"…That's it," he said. "…We didn't factor in the upstream-downstream
lag. If we didn't, then all the conversion we've done so far could be
off."

I felt a little disheartened at that. I thought we might have to start
over from the beginning.

"…Arche, how large is that lag?" Histor asked.

*The time it takes for the tide to travel along the channel from the
upstream point to the downstream point is under half a day. It does
not affect a conversion at the daily unit.*

Histor was quiet for a while, hearing that answer.

"…If it's half a day," I said carefully, "…the day doesn't change,
does it?"

"…No, it doesn't change." He admitted it. "…Still, it had to be
checked."

I thought his face looked like it couldn't decide between disappointed
and relieved. *Looking back, it was probably both. He hadn't wanted the
method to be wrong. He was simply someone who couldn't move forward
without confirming whether the method was right.*

He asked Arche two more things after that. Once, whether the point at
which a day began and ended — was the same across all six cities.
Arche confirmed it was the same. Another time, whether the tidal cycle
itself shifted slightly with the seasons. Arche answered that it did
shift, but that this had already been accounted for in the conversion.
Each time, Histor paused for a moment and then returned to the chart.

Watching him, I felt I understood what he was doing now. He wasn't
trying to stop something from coming out of this method. He simply
didn't want to leave any room, later, to say the method had been
wrong. That was how he did his work. I didn't find it grating. If
anything, because he worked that way, I could believe the answer that
came out of it even more once it came.

"…Looking at it this way, everything really is connected," I said.

Histor didn't answer. He called out the next number.

Once, when Haven's day 219 and the Harvest's day 220 came up, I
stopped my hand.

"…This is only a day apart," I said.

Histor leaned in to look.

"…It's within the margin of error," he said. "…But the scenes don't
match. Haven's is the well, the Harvest's is the field. No one
overlaps either."

I looked at the chart again. He was right. A date being close and the
same person appearing were different problems. I felt a little
embarrassed that I'd nearly mixed the two up.

"…Do we have to check all of these too?" I asked.

"…Yes. We can't just go by the date. We have to check what overlaps
too."

We stopped several more times like that. Combinations with close dates
but different scenes, different people. Each time, Histor crossed out
the combination and moved on. Watching his hand crossing them out, I
thought the chart was being emptied faster than it was being filled.

---

Late in the afternoon, I asked Arche for the date of the flower bed
scene. Haven's record — the day Elpis first saw that person.

*Conversion result: day 341, by Loop's reckoning.*

I wrote it in the chart. Day 341, Haven, by the flower bed.

Next, I asked about the end-of-the-line scene from the Reckoning's
record.

*Conversion result: day 341, by Loop's reckoning.*

I looked at that number again. The same number. My hand stopped for a
moment.

---

"…Histor."

"…Yes."

"…Look at this for me."

I pushed the chart across to him. He leaned in to look. He was quiet
for a long time.

"…I'll calculate it again," he said.

His voice trembled slightly. He started from the very beginning. Found
the tide reference in the original again, checked the conversion
again, asked Arche again.

*Conversion result: day 341. Same as previous result.*

He didn't stop there.

"…Arche, is it possible the tide references in these two records point
to the same event? That is — could both have recorded the same water
channel phenomenon, on the same day, from two different places, so
that the same date came out by coincidence?"

*The tide reference in Haven's record and the tide reference in the
Reckoning's record were recorded at different segments of the channel,
by different observers. The likelihood of them referring to the same
event is low.*

"…How low is low?"

*There is insufficient basis to give a numeric probability. However,
considering the distance between the two points and the difference in
manner of observation, the possibility of it being the same event is
not negligible to rule out.*

I was momentarily confused by what that answer meant. Histor sorted it
out for me.

"…It means almost certainly not. They're different tides."

Even so, he checked once more. He laid the tide description written in
Haven's record and the one written in the Reckoning's record side by
side and compared them word by word. How much the water had risen,
which direction it had flowed. The descriptions were different. A
different day, a different place, a different water each of them had
seen.

"…They're different," he finally said. "…Different tides, in the
description. It isn't the same thing written twice."

I understood that he'd crossed out this possibility too. It seemed
like there was nothing left to explain it now. And yet he didn't set
the paper down.

---

He stared hard at the paper. I held my breath beside him.

"…Could this still be within the margin of error?" I asked carefully.
"…It could be off by a day, couldn't it?"

"…No," he answered. "…Even accounting for the margin of error, it's
the same day. The Reckoning counts by the Scales inspection cycle,
Haven by the cycle of the seasons, but converted by the tide, both come
out to day 341."

"…Are you sure?"

"…I did it three times over."

I laid the two scenes side by side again. Haven's record: a person
pulling weeds beside the flower bed. The Reckoning's record: a person
standing at the end of the line in the Adjudicator's yard, not angry at
being shoved aside. The same day. Two different cities.

---

"…These two cities — they never read each other, didn't they," I
said. "…Do you remember? It was one of the four."

"…I remember."

"…Then doesn't this go beyond what word of mouth could even explain?
The same day, in two different cities."

Histor didn't answer. He looked at the chart again, and again. His
fingertip touched the number 341 over and over.

*I remember this moment exactly. He didn't get angry, didn't cry out in
surprise. He just touched the number, and touched it again.*

---

I kept converting other combinations too, wanting to check further.
Most didn't overlap. Each fell on its own separate date. But that one,
day 341, Haven and the Reckoning, came out identical no matter how many
times we recalculated it.

I filled the results into the chart one by one. The Reckoning and the
Harvest split apart, day 89 and day 152. The Preserve and the Equity
split apart, day 203 and day 276. Haven and the Equity, likewise, each
their own. Writing down those mismatches actually put my mind more at
ease. If every combination had overlapped, I would have thought it
meant our method itself was wrong. That more of them came out
mismatched felt, paradoxically, like proof that this method was
working correctly.

Histor seemed to be thinking the same thing.

"…It's actually a relief that so much of it doesn't line up," he
said.

"…Why?"

"…If everything had overlapped, I would have doubted the method
itself. This much mismatch is normal. That's what makes the one that
doesn't mismatch mean something."

I copied the two scenes out by hand and laid them side by side.

*A person was beside the flower bed. No one asked his name.*

*A person stood at the end of the line. Shoved aside, but did not get
angry.*

The same day. Haven lay upstream on the water channel, the Reckoning
downstream. A distance that would take several days to walk. Histor
had told me that once before.

---

"…You said it was days apart," I said.

"…Yes."

"…Then the same person couldn't be in two places on the same day,
could they?"

Histor didn't answer.

I looked at him. He was still looking at the chart. His eyes were on
the paper, but I couldn't tell whether what he was truly looking at
was the paper, or something else.

"…Histor?"

He was still silent.

---

I didn't press him. I waited for the words he always said. "There are
other possibilities." "More confirmation is needed." "This isn't
proven." I expected one of them to come.

None came.

*Writing this chapter, I can't say exactly how long he stayed silent
that day. I wasn't watching a clock. But I can say with certainty that
the silence felt longer to me than any words I'd heard in this book.*

Inside the room, there was hardly the sound of a page turning, hardly
the sound of breath. Only the faint sound of the water channel flowing
came in from outside.

I didn't want to disturb him. Instead I looked back over the chart from
the beginning beside him. Day 217, day 89, day 152, day 203, and day
341 twice. The numbers lined up in a row. I ran my eyes over that row
and confirmed, three times over, that the number 341 appeared twice.
Counting was my habit. As Histor checked his calculations, I checked
with my eyes.

The Keeper opened the door around then, looked into the room once, and
closed it quietly again. I heard that sound but didn't turn around.
Histor didn't turn around either.

*I asked the Keeper later why the door had been closed without a word
that day. The Keeper answered this way. "…You don't walk into a room
with a face like that." I set that answer down here, at the end of
this chapter. Because I too had been looking at Histor's face, in that
moment.*

---

I looked down at the chart again. Day 341. Beside the flower bed. At
the end of the line. The same day, two different cities.

Histor didn't set the paper down. He held it in his hand and said
nothing at all.
