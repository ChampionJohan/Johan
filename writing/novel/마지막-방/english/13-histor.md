# Chapter 13. Histor

> _Previously_ — In the deepest part of the repository, Mone saw for
> the first time a door that was not in Arche's index. ***That area is
> not registered.*** It didn't mean hidden — it meant never registered
> at all. The door wasn't locked. Mone didn't open it.

The day that person came, I had already half forgotten about the door.

That wasn't strange. New bundles came into the repository every day. Six
bundles that needed placing today mattered more than one door I hadn't
seen yesterday. *I know now that was an excuse. I didn't know it then.*

The Keeper called me in that morning.

"…Someone's coming today."

"…Who?"

"…Someone named Histor. Coming to cross-check."

"…Cross-check?"

The Keeper handed me a sheet of paper. The handwriting was neat. Not an
official hand — a personal one, and more precise than any official
hand I'd seen.

"…Says he's read all six cities' records. He wrote asking for someone
to help him."

"…Read them? All of what's here?"

"…Not what's here. He read them elsewhere. And then he wrote saying he
wanted to cross-check them here."

I found that hard to believe. Our repository alone held twenty-two
rows, eight sections to a row, forty bundles to a section. And that was
only Loop's own — put all six cities together and it would be many
times that.

"…How does anyone read all of that?"

The Keeper shrugged once.

"…I don't know. Ask him when he gets here."

*Writing this now, I remember that shrug of the Keeper's again. That
person wasn't especially surprised by how remarkable Histor was. I'd
learn the reason for that later too. Not yet.*

I spent the rest of that morning rearranging the shelves for no reason.
Squaring corners that were already square. I always did that when
someone was coming. I'm not sure why. I think tidying the place came
before tidying myself around a person — that was my way.

*Looking back now, that's funny. Histor never cared about places at
all. What he cared about was order. That looks similar but it isn't
the same thing.*

---

Histor came around noon.

I saw him pause for a moment in front of the door. Before he came in,
he looked up once at the doorframe. Just a wooden frame, nothing
special about it. But he looked at it as if he were confirming
something.

"…This is the Loop repository," he said.

There was no question mark in his voice. He sounded like a man saying
something he already knew.

"…Yes," I answered.

"…You're Mone. I heard about you."

I was a little surprised. I hadn't often met anyone who already knew my
name in advance.

"…About me?"

"…The Keeper wrote it in the letter. Said there was an apprentice.
Said she was good at putting things in order."

*I'll admit I felt pleased to hear that. I came to be wary of that
feeling often afterward. That wariness began in this moment.*

He didn't have much luggage. One bag, but it looked like it held more
paper than clothes. His steps were careful — he walked like a man
trying not to step on something.

He was a little taller than me, and he wore glasses. His nails were
short. I thought, those are the nails of someone who handles paper —
and later I learned that was exactly right. He said long nails make it
hard to handle old paper.

"…May I come in?" he asked.

He asked before he came in. I noted that too. People coming to the
repository for the first time usually just walk in — the door is open,
after all. He asked permission even standing in front of an open door.

"…Yes, come in."

He came in. The first thing he did wasn't a greeting. He looked down
at his feet, because there was a piece of paper on the floor — one I'd
missed while tidying that morning. He bent down and picked it up.
Without even turning it over, he set it neatly on the nearest shelf.

"…You can't just leave things anywhere, in a place like this," he
said.

"…I'll show you where things go later," I said.

*I have to confess I held on to this short scene for a long time. The
fact that the first thing a person did on coming to our repository was
pick up a piece of paper off the floor. I liked that. I still like it.
That liking would confuse me later, and I'll get to that.*

---

The Keeper led him all the way inside.

Histor stopped in front of the first shelf. He didn't touch anything.
He only looked. He looked for a long time.

"…This is Section Fourteen," he said.

I was surprised. You had to ask Arche to know a section number. It
wasn't written large anywhere on the outside. People who'd memorized
the layout knew it, but it wasn't something a newcomer could know.

"…How do you know that?"

"…Ah, I got a map of the sections by letter. I memorized it in
advance."

He said it without any weight to it. Not a boast. Just stating a fact.

*I would come to sum up that tone in one sentence later. He never
boasted. I'm not saying that as though it were a flaw. Only that a
moment would come later when it felt strange.*

---

The Keeper took him to a small room at the back of the stacks. Said it
was the room where the cross-checking would happen. One desk, two
chairs, a small window.

"…You can stay here," the Keeper said. "…If you need anything, tell
Mone."

"…Thank you."

After the Keeper left, I stood there a little awkwardly, not knowing
what to help with. Histor opened his bag and took out a bundle of
paper, setting it neatly on the desk. Squared at the corners.

"…What should we look at first?" I asked.

He looked at me. For the first time, a little longer than usual.

"…Have you read all six cities' records, Mone?"

"…Not all of it. I just put things in place as they came in. I haven't
read much."

"…Then let's look together starting today. I know where everything
is, so if you can find the physical bundles, it'll go faster."

I nodded. It sounded like a natural division of labor. *At the time I
thought it was just that — a division of labor.*

---

That afternoon we pulled out the first bundle.

It was Haven's. The name was written on the cover. Elpis. I read the
name aloud.

"…Elpis."

"…Yes. A record written by a thirteen-year-old girl. Her brother's
name is Zeteo."

He said that without even opening the cover.

"…You know without looking?"

"…Roughly, from the color and weight of the cover. But I still have to
check."

He opened the cover. He read the first page. He read it slowly. Histor
read slowly. That surprised me a little. I'd expected someone who knew
everything to flip through fast.

"…If you already know it, why read it again?"

He answered without lifting his head.

"…You have to read it again to be accurate. Memory is wrong
sometimes."

*At the time I just heard that as diligence. I read it differently
now. He didn't distrust his own memory. He wanted to keep his memory
accurate. Those two things are different.*

When he finished the first page, he made a small mark in the margin.
In pencil, very faint. I asked what it was.

"…It's marking where I read this before. I read this passage
somewhere else once. I'm checking whether the original here matches
what I read then."

"…Has it ever been different?"

"…Sometimes. A copyist drops a line here and there, or a word gets
changed. Usually a small difference."

"…This time?"

He looked again at the line he'd marked.

"…This time it's the same."

His expression barely changed as he said it. He wasn't pleased that
he'd been right, and he didn't look like he would have been
disappointed if he'd been wrong. It was just the face of a person
checking something.

Beside him, I asked Arche a few things — what came next in this
section, whether any more bundles would come in today. Histor watched
closely as I asked Arche.

"…Do you ask Arche often, Mone?"

"…Yes. Doesn't everyone?"

"…That's true, mostly." He paused for a moment, then went on. "…I
don't ask much. I don't need to ask again about things I already
know."

I let that pass at the time. *I learned later it wasn't something to
let pass.*

---

Toward evening, he closed the book for the first time and looked at
me.

"…Mone, why do you work here?"

It was a sudden question. I thought for a moment.

"…Because I'm good at putting things in order."

"…That's all?"

"…Yes."

He looked as though he might ask something more after that answer, but
he didn't. He just nodded once.

"…I came because I wanted to know this," he said, lightly tapping the
paper resting on his hand. "…Where things are, what's written on them.
That."

"…The reason?"

"…Let's talk about that later."

I didn't ask further. It didn't seem polite to press on the first day.
*Looking back, even if I'd heard the answer that day, I wouldn't have
understood it. It took me eleven chapters to understand that answer.*

---

Before dinner I showed him around the repository once. Not a formal
tour — just telling him roughly what was where.

"…This row is Haven's. That row is the Reckoning's, and next to it the
Harvest's. The Preserve is further in, and the Equity is at the very
end."

"…And Loop's?"

"…This middle row. It has no name on it."

"…Right. You don't write your own name on yourself."

I felt like I'd heard that somewhere before. Then I remembered. It was
what the Keeper had said to me on my first day. Like not writing your
own address on your own letter's envelope.

"…That's something the Keeper said."

"…Ah, it was in the letter. That he'd explained it to you that way."

I found that a little strange — that the Keeper had written even a
small thing he'd said to me into a letter, in that much detail. But at
the time I just thought the Keeper was a thoughtful person. *I'd learn
the reason he wrote that letter in such detail much later. I'll get to
that too.*

Standing in front of the middle row, he was quiet for a moment. He
didn't even pull out a bundle. He just looked. I didn't want to
interrupt, so I waited beside him.

"…This is the oldest one," he said.

"…Yes. That's what they say."

"…How old?"

"…I don't know exactly either. Should I ask?"

"…No. Never mind."

I didn't get to ask why "never mind" that time. I should have.

---

By evening the Keeper came again. Without even asking how Histor had
been getting on, he just said,

"…Have you eaten?"

"…Not yet," Histor answered.

"…Mone will take you. There's a place to eat nearby."

I was a little taken aback at that. I'd shown him around, but I hadn't
expected to have dinner with him too. Histor packed his bag without
much comment.

On the way out, he stopped once more in front of the door. He looked
up at the doorframe, just as he had coming in that morning.

"…What are you looking at?" I asked.

"…Nothing. Just confirming the day is over."

"…You check things like that too?"

"…I'm the kind of person who checks things like that."

He said it as if it were a joke, but it didn't sound like a joke to me.
*Reading it again now, it still isn't a joke. He really was that kind
of person. Whether a day had started or ended, whether a record was
right or wrong, whether where he'd read something matched what was
here. He was a person who checked. Not a person who believed.*

The place to eat wasn't far from the repository. He didn't talk much
on the way. I didn't try to fill the silence either. Walking with a
stranger in silence should have felt uncomfortable, but strangely it
didn't. He seemed like someone who kept even silence in order.

Once we sat down at the table, he spoke again.

"…Mone, tomorrow we really start."

"…Start what?"

"…Pulling out all six records and laying them side by side. I can't do
it alone. You're faster at finding the physical bundles."

"…I can't cross-check. I don't even know what overlaps."

"…That's fine. I'll do that. All you need to do is find things."

I nodded. I thought at the time it was simple collaboration. *It was
collaboration. Only it took longer than I'd imagined.*

That night, walking home, I wrote in my paper.

Histor came. Seventeen. Says he's read all six cities' records. Knows
things just from the cover. But he reads again. Reads slowly. Seems
like a careful person.

*I added one more line beside it. I almost erased it later but didn't.
"Envious." The first feeling I had, seeing a sixteen-year-old
confronted with someone who knew everything, wasn't awe but envy. I
decided not to be ashamed of that. It was true.*

On the way home I passed the water channel. The water sounded the same
as always. That day, for some reason, I listened to it a little
longer. Something Histor had said in the afternoon, standing in front
of the middle row — "This is the oldest one" — kept bothering me. That
I hadn't asked, when I should have, bothered me.

*Going back over that day now, I know it wasn't just one or two things
I missed. He showed me a great deal on the very first day. Looking up
at the doorframe. Picking up the paper off the floor. Checking again
what he already knew. Not asking Arche. I didn't properly read any of
it that day. A sixteen-year-old can't read a person all at once. I
learned that for the first time then.*
