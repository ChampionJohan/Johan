# Chapter 41. Opening It

> _Previously_ — I understood why I put things in order. I was afraid
> of losing things. But that day, for the first time, I thought that
> holding on might not be all there was.

That day wasn't a special day.

I want to set that down first. I would come back to this scene many
times later, and each time I felt the temptation to dress it up as
some special day. In fact it was just an ordinary Tuesday. The sky was
overcast, and nine records had come in that day.

I put those nine in order as usual. Then I walked to the last room.

On the way I asked Arche nothing. There was nothing to ask. Everything
I needed, I'd already asked days before, and had the answers. That
morning I needed no new information.

*Up until then, whenever I made a decision, I always tried to find the
reason first. This time I didn't. I just walked. My feet moved first,
and the reason followed after. Even writing this now, I still can't
fully explain why it had to be that particular morning. Only this much
is certain: that day, I felt I could no longer put it off.*

Walking down the corridor, I thought back over the past few days.
Histor's question. The answer I'd given. The record whose cover I'd
fixed. The phone call with my mother. The Keeper's words. Walking, I
felt all of it had pushed me, little by little, to this door.

---

Histor happened to be in that room. He was looking again at the
writing in the plan's margin. Seeing me, he made a face that asked
what was going on.

"…What are you doing today?" he asked.

"…Opening it."

He didn't react right away. Only after a few seconds did he set down
the paper in his hand.

"…Now?"

"…Yes. Now."

"…Why today, of all days?"

I had no exact answer to that question.

"…I don't know. It just felt like today."

---

The Keeper happened to be passing the door just then and saw us. He
saw what was happening and came in.

"…Going to open it?" he asked.

"…Yes."

"…Want help?"

"…No. I'll do it."

He nodded and stepped back. He didn't stop me. He didn't ask about
qualification either. I was grateful for that.

Mena came in soon after too. No one had called for her, but that day
people gathered, one by one, into that room. I couldn't tell if that
was coincidence or not. I didn't press to find out.

---

I sat down in front of the seal.

The wax was still small. About half a palm's width. The letters on it
were still faded. *Until the time of the end.*

I ran my fingertip over the letters once. It was cold. That particular
texture of old wax. I closed my eyes for a moment and opened them
again. I wasn't trying to work up some great resolve. I just wanted to
steady my breath once.

*If anyone is expecting something dramatic in this moment, I want to
say beforehand: I didn't pray, and I didn't take a vow. I just put
strength into my hand.*

---

The wax came off more easily than I'd expected.

There was a small sound of thread breaking. That sound startled me a
little. That was all. There was no other sound in the room. No one
held their breath, and no light leaked out.

I unfolded the bundle carefully.

Histor came up beside me. He was holding his breath. I thought that
wasn't like him. He was always composed. But not in that moment.

The Keeper also stepped closer. Mena watched from a little farther
off. I asked her once, later, why she hadn't come closer. She answered
this way: "…Because that was your hands' work to do."

I remember, even now, that my hands didn't shake in that moment. That
was strange. I was ordinarily someone whose hands shook easily in
situations like that. But not that day.

---

Inside was paper.

Not one sheet. Several. I turned them one by one.

The first few sheets were blank. I thought maybe a preface or cover
sheet was missing, and kept turning. The next sheet was blank too.
And the next. I turned all the way to the end of the bundle.

All of it was blank.

---

I sat there for a long while.

"…Is that all?" Histor asked.

"…Yes. That's all."

"…There's nothing at all?"

"…Nothing."

He took the sheaf of papers from my hands and turned through them
himself, one by one. He held them up to the light too. Checked the
back of each. There was nothing. Truly blank paper.

"…No hidden writing or anything?" he asked. "The kind that shows up
under heat or light?"

"…Have you tried?"

"…Not yet. Can I try?"

I nodded. He brought a lamp and held each sheet up to it. Nothing
appeared. Only after checking once more did he set the papers down.

"…Nothing," he said quietly.

"…Disappointed?"

"…Yes. A little."

That honest answer put me more at ease, oddly. I told him I was
disappointed too. We didn't hide that disappointment from each other.
That was the one certain thing we did that day.

---

On the very first sheet, there was exactly one box.

I only noticed it then. A box drawn in a thin line near the top of the
sheet. Beside it, small letters read something.

*Recipient.*

That box was empty.

---

I looked at that box for a long while. It was a space meant for a
name. The way you'd write a recipient's name on the front of an
envelope. But no one had written any name there.

I put my hand on the panel by the wall and asked Arche.

"…Arche, what is this? I just opened it."

*Checking.*

There was a pause.

*A new item has been identified. Classification is beginning.*

"…Do you know what's in it?"

*This item is blank. There is no recorded content.*

"…Do you know what it is?"

*In format, it is identical to the six cities' record documents.
However, author information, sending city, and recipient information
are all blank.*

"…Is this classified as in progress?"

*This item is registered as in progress. The closing condition is not
defined.*

That answer made me laugh. It was the sentence I always heard. This
one time, it didn't sound hateful to me.

*I want to write this moment as quietly as I can, in this record.
There's a temptation to write that I wasn't disappointed then.
Honestly, I was a little disappointed. I'd thought something grand
would come out. It was a thing that had been sealed for so long. I
think I'd expected something to match that long waiting.*

*But what came out was blank paper. The recipient box was empty too.*

---

The Keeper took the papers and turned through them slowly.

"…This is the seventh city's record format," he said.

"…The seventh?" I asked.

"…Yes. We have all six cities' records. This format is the same as
theirs. The cover, the inner pages, even the recipient box. But this
is the seventh."

"…Where's the seventh city?"

The Keeper looked at me.

"…Here, Mone. This is the record with no city name. You saw it
yourself, at the start. That our city, too, should have a record —
but it had no name."

Hearing that, my breath caught. This was our city's own record. And
nothing had been written in it yet.

I thought back to the beginning. That first day, when I'd received the
nameless bundle, what the Keeper had said. *You don't write your own
name yourself. The way you don't write your own address on your own
envelope.* At the time I'd let that pass. Only now did I realize that
saying had led all the way to this moment.

"…Then how long has this been here?" Histor asked.

"…I don't know either," the Keeper answered. "I don't know exactly
how long this room itself has existed. I've only ever kept watch over
what's here."

"…Then why did it open now, of all times?"

No one could answer that question. I didn't know either. I'd only
felt, that morning, that I could no longer put it off.

The Keeper broke that silence.

"…Maybe that's the answer. No one can explain why it had to be today.
Mone just did it, today. Maybe that's enough."

Histor neither agreed nor argued with that. He just looked back down
at the blank paper.

---

"…So this…" Histor said slowly. "This is our city's record, and
nothing at all has been written yet?"

"…Seems that way," I answered.

"…Then what was this seal protecting? Why seal and protect paper with
nothing on it at all?"

I didn't know the answer to that. I didn't have it yet.

Mena spoke quietly then.

"…Protecting something not yet written might still be a kind of
protecting."

None of us answered that right away.

The Keeper set the papers back down and said.

"…I don't quite know how to take this. But there's one thing I do
know. This wasn't made to give us an answer."

"…Then what is it?" Histor asked.

"…I don't know. But it's clear it isn't an answer."

Mena touched one of the sheets lightly with her fingertip.

"…I keep thinking about what it means, set beside the other six
records. The five cities' records, and Loop's own. But only Loop's is
blank. Six are written, and one isn't."

"…Why does that matter?" I asked.

"…I don't know. But strangely, it puts me at ease. If it had all
already been written, I think I'd have been frightened instead. It
would have meant our city's share was already decided."

Hearing that, I looked at the blank paper again. The emptiness I'd
first seen as disappointment looked a little different, now that I'd
heard Mena's words.

---

I carefully put the papers back in order. I set the first sheet, with
the recipient box, on top, and put the rest of the blank sheets in
order beneath it.

*In that moment, I realized I was doing what I always did. Putting
things in order. This time the object was only blank paper. Strangely,
that comforted me. Even without some grand revelation, I was still
doing the work I knew how to do.*

I set the sheaf of papers on the table, and the rest of that day
passed with everyone standing around it.

---

Histor said, at last.

"…Honestly I thought I'd be angry. After waiting this long, for there
to be nothing."

"…But?" I asked.

"…But I'm not angry. Strangely."

I would remember that for a long time. Histor had every right to be
angry. But he wasn't. He just looked at that blank paper for a long,
long while.

---

That evening, I stayed behind alone in that room and put the papers
in order again. The first sheet with the recipient box, the blank
inner pages beneath it, and the cover last. I bound them in order
again and set them on the table.

*I want to set this moment down this way. I'd thought I would discover
something grand. What I discovered was blank paper. At first that felt
empty to me. But putting those papers in order, I slowly came to know
it wasn't emptiness at all.*

*The six cities' records were already all written. What had happened
was recorded in them. Only our city's was still unwritten. I didn't
fully understand, that night, what that meant. But I decided not to
feel afraid of that blank space.*

I didn't put that sheaf of papers back where the seal had been.
Instead I moved it to the table, where we could see it every day.

*I can't exactly explain why I did that. I just no longer wanted to
keep it in a hidden place. There was a feeling that what the seal had
guarded was finished now, and that it was no longer our turn to watch
over it, but our turn to fill it.*

On the way home, passing beside the water channel, I thought back over
what had happened that day. I still hadn't fully shaken the
disappointment. At the same time, there was a feeling of having seen a
place where something new could begin. Those two feelings sat side by
side, all through that night.

When I got home, I wrote down what had happened that day in my
notebook. Briefly, as always. *Opened the seal today. Inside was blank
paper. The recipient box was empty too.*

Beneath that sentence I wrote one more line. I set down exactly the
thought that had come to me that day, for the first time. *I still
don't fully know that being blank isn't the end. But I've decided to
try believing it.*

I closed the notebook after writing that line. I still didn't know
that night what to do the next day. But for the first time, I felt
that not knowing wasn't as frightening as it used to be.

*Closing this chapter, I want to make one thing clear. That day I
proved no qualification. No one ever asked me whether I had it. I
simply walked over and put strength into my hand. That's the whole of
what happened in this chapter. Everything else was what we said to
each other afterward, standing around that blank paper.*

*I want to leave both things down honestly — that the blank paper
disappointed me, and that the disappointment didn't last long. Leaving
only one of those would be a lie. I decided, from the start, not to
leave any lie in this record.*
