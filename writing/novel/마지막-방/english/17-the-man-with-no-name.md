# Chapter 17. The Man with No Name

> _Previously_ — They laid the six cities' records side by side on the
> floor and began finding overlapping passages. They found more than
> twenty, but Histor said that wasn't the real thing. He only said
> he'd show her tomorrow.

The next morning, Histor was there earlier than usual.

When I arrived, he was already sitting on the floor, going back over
the six rows again. Something was a little different from the day
before. He'd pulled a few sheets out of each row and set them apart in
front.

"…You're early."

"…I didn't finish sorting last night. I wanted to show you this."

He pointed to the six sheets set out in front. It looked like he'd
pulled one from each city.

"…What is this?"

"…Read them. Start with Haven's."

---

The room was quiet. Morning sunlight came slanting through the window
and lay long across the six rows on the floor. Watching that light cut
across all six rows at once, I had, for the first time, the thought
that these six cities might somehow be joined in a single line
somewhere. It wasn't a grand thought. It was just a thought that came
from watching the light.

There was a reason even for the order he'd set the six sheets in. He
said he'd laid them roughly in the order the cities had received their
records — that is, roughly the order the cities had been founded, or
the events had happened. Not certain, he said, but roughly right.

I picked up the first sheet. It was Haven's record.

*There was a person beside the flower bed. While Elpis waited for her
brother, that person was pulling weeds. No one asked that person's
name. Later that person appeared again by the well, standing beside
Elpis as she drew water, holding the rope of the bucket for her.*

I picked up the next sheet. The Reckoning's.

*The line at the Adjudicator's yard was long. At the end of the line
stood a person. Not angry, even when someone shoved ahead and cut in.
Pheugon later asked that person, "Have you been in a city like this
before?" The person smiled and avoided the answer.*

Third, the Harvest's.

*Among the day laborers gleaning in the field, there was a person.
Working side by side with Xene. When Xene met someone who asked why she
came to this field every day, that person was there too, but said
nothing.*

---

Fourth was the Preserve's.

*In the dry valley, a person walked among the bones. Already there when
Anemos went down. Didn't ask what he was doing. Just walked alongside.*

Fifth, the Equity's.

*A person sat by the water. Hora passed by and saw. That person did
nothing and said nothing. Just sat. Hora recorded the scene in exactly
three sentences.*

Sixth, Loop's.

*Near the front gate, while everyone hurried, one person walked slowly.
An old scar on his hand. Mena passed him by. No one asked his name.*

---

I laid the six sheets back out on the desk. My hand trembled a little.

"…Is this—"

"…Yes."

"…Is it the same person in all six?"

Histor looked at me. His expression was the same as usual. Not
excited, not surprised.

"…It might be the same person, or it might not be. What I can say is
only this — a person with no name appears in all six records."

I followed the scenes again, one by one, in my mind. The hand pulling
weeds by the flower bed, the face that didn't get angry at the end of
the line, the back working side by side in the field, the footsteps
already there in the valley, the silence held to three sentences by the
water, the lone slow walk near the front gate. All six scenes were
short. No one lingered over that person for long. Everyone had written
it down as though the person were just passing through. And yet the
shortness itself resembled something strangely, across all of them.

"…But they're all so similar. No one ever asks his name, no one gets
angry, he just stays close by."

"…They are similar. That part's true."

---

"…Isn't this a big discovery?" I asked.

"…It's a discovery. Whether it's big, I don't know."

"…Why not?"

"…Let me sum up what I've told you." He counted on his fingers, one at
a time. "First, a nameless character appears in all six records.
Second, that character's behavior resembles itself across the six.
Those two things are facts. I've confirmed them."

"…And?"

"…But I still don't know what this proves."

I felt a little disappointed by that. *Looking back, there was nothing
to be disappointed about. He'd told me exactly as much as he knew. Not
more than he knew. That was his way.*

---

I looked at the six sheets again, wanting to find something more.

"…Has this person's name really never come up? Not once?"

"…Not once. Nowhere in the six records. I've read all of it, so I'm
certain."

"…Isn't that strange? Six different people wrote six different
records in six different cities, and every single one of them, without
exception, never wrote down this person's name."

Histor thought for a moment and answered.

"…It could be strange, or it might not be."

"…That answer again?"

"…Sorry. But it really is true. It's a common thing not to ask a
stranger's name, especially someone just passing through."

I had to admit that explanation made sense. Not many people ask a
stranger's name every single time.

---

Still, something kept bothering me.

"…But it's all six of them. Not one of the six asked — I find that a
little strange."

Histor stopped for the first time at that. His expression turned
thoughtful.

"…What you're saying isn't entirely wrong. But I have trouble calling
it proof. For something to be proof, every other possibility has to be
ruled out, and I haven't managed that yet."

"…What other possibilities?"

"…For example — stories that travel tend to resemble each other
anyway. Similar stories can arise independently in different places on
their own. Or the recorders could have read each other's records and
been influenced by them. Until I've checked all of those
possibilities, I can't say this is special."

I fell quiet, hearing that. *He'd already foreseen, at this very
moment, the rebuttal he'd lay out in the next chapter. I didn't know it
then. I was only just beginning to understand how careful a person he
was.*

That day we also went back through the rest of the bundles for any
more scenes like these six. Histor found a few more. That person
brushed past once more later in Haven's record, and there was one more
short line in the Reckoning's record too. In the Equity's record, it
really was exactly four times, just as Histor had said in advance.

"…The Equity really is short," I said.

"…Yes. It has the least of any of the six books. Every time, three
sentences or fewer. Whether Hora simply didn't write much about him, or
whether that person really only stays briefly wherever he goes — I
don't know."

The word "briefly" caught in my ear.

"…If it's uncertain, how do you count it?"

"…I don't count it. I only count what's certain. That's why I only
told you he appears for certain in all six records. How many times
he appears in each — I haven't finished sorting that out."

*I felt again, in this moment, how careful he was. He didn't inflate
what was uncertain. He worked only with what was sure. I thought that
was honesty. It was honesty. Only, honesty wasn't everything.*

I found myself wondering where this carefulness had come from. Why had
a child who'd picked up scraps of paper since he was small grown into
someone who spoke only of what was certain? I didn't put that question
into words that day. Instead I set it aside in my mind. On the list of
questions to ask someday.

---

That afternoon, I copied the six sheets separately. In order. Haven,
the Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity, Loop.

As I copied, I kept imagining that person. A hand pulling weeds by the
flower bed, a face that didn't get angry even when shoved to the end of
the line, feet walking among bones in the valley. All different scenes,
and yet they kept overlapping into one in my mind.

The Keeper passed by and stopped for a moment, seeing what I was
copying.

"…What are you copying?"

"…There's a person who appears in all six records. No name, but his
behavior is all similar."

The Keeper turned the six sheets over one by one. The expression didn't
change much.

"…Did you know about this?" I asked.

"…Vaguely. Stay here long enough and you naturally learn it."

"…Then why doesn't anyone talk about it? Isn't it remarkable?"

The Keeper set the papers back down.

"…Maybe because nobody knows what "remarkable" would even mean here.
Everyone knows there's someone. What it means, nobody can say for
sure."

I thought that answer sounded similar to Histor's. *There was a
difference too. Histor didn't speak because he didn't know. The Keeper
seemed to know and still hold back from speaking. I couldn't pin down
that difference precisely then.*

"…I really want to know who this person is," I said.

"…Me too," Histor answered. A short answer, but it sounded genuine.

*I felt a little relieved hearing that. That he was curious too. I
thought that curiosity would lead somewhere. In the next chapter I'd
see that it didn't.*

I bundled the six sheets together and set them aside, labeled. I wrote
"The Man with No Name" on the outside. Histor saw it and said,

"…You're naming him."

"…It's not a name, just a category. I didn't know what else to call
it."

"…That's a kind of name too."

Something jolted in me at that. He was right. I was giving the man with
no name the name "The Man with No Name." It was ironic.

"…Then what should we call him?"

"…I just call him 'that person.' It feels more accurate not to give
him a name."

I thought that sounded careful. *Looking back, it was more than
careful. He was a person who didn't casually name what he hadn't
confirmed. That principle held to the very end. Nowhere in this whole
book does he, or I, or anyone, manage to give that person a name.*

That evening, tidying up, I stacked the six sheets back in order.
Haven, the Reckoning, the Harvest, the Preserve, the Equity, Loop. And
laid my copies on top, in the same order. Seeing the original and the
copy laid out in the same order eased my mind a little. At least this
was something I was good at.

---

On the way home I passed the water channel again. Listening to the
sound of the water, I ran through the six scenes once more in my mind.
The flower bed, the end of the line, the field, the valley, the water's
edge, the front gate. Six different places, one overlapping shadow.

I wanted to know who that shadow was. But even in that same moment, I
knew something else — how long this curiosity of mine could hold up
against Histor's caution. I felt that by tomorrow I'd find out exactly
how much of this discovery he'd admit to, and how much he'd retreat
from. That was a sixteen-year-old's expectation.

That evening I wrote on my paper. Laid the six sheets side by side and
saw the same person, the same story, in six different places. Histor
said that wasn't proof. That also seemed true.

---

At home, I took out the six copied sheets again and read them once
more. Flower bed, end of the line, field, valley, water's edge, front
gate. I copied those six words onto a separate piece of paper. There
wasn't any particular reason. I just wanted to set the words together
in one place and look at them.

*I held that paper a long time. The six places seemed to have nothing
to do with each other. Different cities, different seasons, different
people appearing. And yet, lining those six places up in one row,
something like a road seemed to appear. I didn't know where the road
went. It just looked like a road.*

I folded the paper and put it in the drawer with the others. Closing
the drawer, I had a passing feeling that these six sheets would be
needed again someday. It was a feeling with no basis. I wasn't someone
who trusted feelings like that, but that day I just felt it.

*Whether that feeling turned out right or wrong, you'll learn by
reading on in this record. For now I'll only set down the feel of my
hand closing that drawer.*
