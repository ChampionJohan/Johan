# The Unsigned Book — Translation Guide

Book 6 of the **Loop Chronicles**. Korean source: 『저자 없는 책』.
This guide is authoritative. Where it conflicts with a translator's
instinct, the guide wins — consistency across 48 chapters matters more than
any single elegant line.

Books 1–5 use the same conventions; see their guides under
`../../루프-연대기/english/`, `../../울음-장부/english/`,
`../../사십일-조항/english/`, `../../이삭-줍는-자리/english/`, and
`../../마른-골짜기/english/`.

## 4. Register and voice

- **First-person retrospective.** Hora narrates after the fact and knows how
  it ends. Past tense throughout.
- **Italic passages** marked `*...*` are her later commentary — the
  "지금은 안다" voice. Keep them italic in English. They are the series'
  signature device; never merge them into surrounding narration.
- **Dialogue opens with an ellipsis**: `"…네."` → `"…Yes."` Keep the
  ellipsis exactly. Do not convert to "Well—" or drop it.
- **Hora reads to the end.** That is her characterizing habit, as counting
  was Anemos's in Book 5. Wherever the Korean notes what was at the bottom,
  on the last page, or in the last line, keep that placement explicit in
  English — never move the detail earlier in the sentence for flow.
- **Short paragraphs, spare prose.** Resist smoothing Korean's clipped
  sentences into flowing English periods.
- **The Secretary speaks without a subject.** "그렇게 됐습니다",
  "그런 방향으로 정리됐습니다" → **"That's how it came out."**,
  **"It was put in order that way."** English resists subjectless
  sentences, so use the passive and the dummy subject — never insert
  "I" or "we" for him. In chapter 47 he says "제가" exactly once; that
  single **"I"** is the whole arc of the character and must stand out
  against every other line he speaks in the book.
- **No Scripture epigraphs** per chapter. Each chapter opens with a
  `> *이전 이야기*` recap block only → render as `> _Previously_ — ...`

## 5. Scripture

Use the **King James Version** only (public domain). Book 6's seed text is
Esther, and its diction underlies the book **without ever being quoted**:

**Esther 4:14** — *"…and who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom
for such a time as this?"*

**Esther 4:16** — *"…and if I perish, I perish."*

Two hard consequences:

1. **No verse appears anywhere in the novel.** Chapter 40's title is
   『죽으면 죽으리이다』 and its English title is **If I Perish** — the title
   only. No character says it, and the chapter does not quote Esther.
2. Temel gives Esther 4:14's structure in his own flat words in ch.31,
   naming no god. Render it as the guide's phrase table gives it, and do
   **not** raise the register toward the KJV. The plainness is the point.

Esther is the one book of the Bible in which God's name never appears.
This novel is built on that fact, and the English must never explain it.

## 6. Load-bearing phrases (must be exact and consistent)

| Korean | English | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 호라야, 나는 네가 무슨 일을 하든 상관없다. 다만 네가 끝까지 읽는 사람이 되면 좋겠다. 내가 그러지 못해서 놓친 게 많다. | **Hora, it doesn't matter to me what work you do. I only hope you'll be someone who reads to the end. I missed a great deal because I wasn't.** | ch.1, her mother's last line; recurs in ch.38 |
| 저는 전달만 했는데요. | **I only passed it on.** | ch.17–18, and every time it returns. Identical wording every occurrence — the repetition is the chapter. |

| …뜻이 있었으면 좋겠다는 건 알아요. 저도 그래요. 그런데 있었으면 좋겠다는 게 있다는 근거는 아니잖아요. | **…I know you want there to be a meaning. So do I. But wanting there to be one isn't evidence that there is.** | Tyche, ch.29 |
| …네가 여기 앉은 게 그냥 번호 때문일 수도 있어. 그런데 그냥 번호 때문인 자리에서도 할 수 있는 일은 있어. | **…Maybe you're sitting here only because of a number. But there are still things you can do in a seat you got only because of a number.** | Temel, ch.31 — the Esther 4:14 beat |

| …적을 칸이 있으니까. | **…Because there's a box to write in.** | Temel, ch.27 |
| …달라지는 건 없을 수도 있어요. 그런데 비어 있지는 않게 돼요. | **…Maybe nothing changes. But it won't be empty.** | Hora, ch.39 |
| …제 이름이 그 뜻인 것도 우연이겠죠. 그렇죠? | **…I suppose my name meaning that is a coincidence too. Isn't it?** | Tyche, ch.45 — she leaves without waiting for an answer |
| 나는 그를 한 번도 못 만났다. 그런데 이 모든 일이 우연이라고는 도저히 못 믿겠다. | **I never once met him. And I cannot bring myself to believe all of this was chance.** | ch.48 — the last line of the book |

The final line must land on **chance**, because that is what Tyche's name
means and the reader has known it since chapter 44. Do not substitute
"coincidence," "accident," or "random" there. In ch.45 Tyche says
*coincidence* about herself; in ch.48 Hora says *chance* about everything.
Keep that one-word difference.

## 9. Rules that must not be broken

1. **The Consensus hides nothing.** Not once in this book. Every full text
   was public the whole time; nobody blocked Hora; nobody else read it.
   Never translate in a way that hints at concealment, redaction, a
   cover-up, or a person behind it. **There is no conspiracy and no
   villain.** The horror is that nothing was hidden.
2. **Nobody lies.** Every person Hora confronts in ch.17–18 tells the exact
   truth and feels no shame, because none is owed. Never give them a
   defensive, guilty or hypocritical note the Korean does not have.
3. **The summary is accurate** (ch.15). It is merely short. Never imply
   otherwise.
4. **The Equity is a good city and the English must let the reader feel it.**
   No one is wronged there, no one queues, no one needs a favor. Hora likes
   it and means it, and never comes to hate it. Do not add irony.
5. **The Secretary is not a villain.** He is kind, answers everything, and
   sincerely believes he is nothing. He has no guilt because he has no
   subject. He never apologizes and is never redeemed — ch.47's single
   **"I"** is all he gets, and that is deliberate.
6. **Tyche is kind, not cynical**, and the book never refutes her. She is
   right about most things; the coincidences really were coincidences. She
   does not convert. Ch.45 ends with her question and no answer, and the
   English must leave it unanswered.
7. **Never gloss the names before chapter 44.** Not in a footnote, not in a
   chapter title, not in a recap block. In ch.44: **Hora = ὥρα, "the hour,"
   the appointed time.** **Tyche = τύχη, "chance."** Tyche laughs and then
   has nothing to say; preserve that silence.
8. **He appears four times, for eight sentences.** Never lengthen them,
   never let another character remark on him, never let the narration point
   at him. The text never calls him 걷는 사람 and neither may the English.
9. **The ending is partial.** The new deliberation reverses only part of the
   Seventh Classification. What is not reversed stays unreversed and the
   book does not erase it. There is no gallows, no reversal of fortune, no
   triumph. Do not let the English supply one.
10. **No declared faith.** No character says the English equivalent of
    "이제 믿습니다." The book ends on Hora's last written line and nothing
    else — no explanation of who he was, no summary of the theme.
