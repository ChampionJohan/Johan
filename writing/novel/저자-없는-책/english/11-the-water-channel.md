# Chapter 11. The Water Channel

> _Previously_ — Hora learning that deliberation subjects are called only
> by number and classification, never by name. There was once a time when
> outcomes split on the basis of a name, and now that kind of gap has
> nearly vanished. Her aunt said, "That one, I can confidently say got
> better." Leaning on that certainty, Hora closed the day out as a good
> one.

The first time I went right up close to the water channel was purely because I had time to spare.

The session ended early that day. There were about two hours free before the next one. Ordinarily I would have spent that time reading more material, but that day, as it happened, there was no new case in hand yet. I left the meeting room and, walking, turned toward the western water channel I'd only ever passed by before.

---

Up close, the channel was wider than I'd expected. I'd always taken it for nothing more than a spot roughly set with stones, but in places it was actually wide enough for two people to lie down side by side. The current wasn't strong. Soil-colored water moved between small stones, making its sound the whole way.

I crouched at the water's edge. I thought about touching it, hesitated again, and didn't. Instead I walked along the channel. The closer I got to the city's edge, the more natural the channel's shape became. It didn't look like a place anyone had shaped — it looked as though the water had carved its own way.

*I didn't think, that day, to try to find out where this water came from. I was only curious. I didn't know, then, where that curiosity would lead.*

There was a small signpost standing beside the channel. Its color had faded. It read only "Western Boundary of the Equity." Not one line about the water.

I studied that signpost for a long while. That a signpost marking the city's boundary said nothing at all about the water flowing right beside it seemed, at the time, like nothing more than the sign having gone up long ago, with the water appearing later. I walked around behind it too. There was nothing there.

Walking a little further along the channel, I saw an old man sitting by the water. I went over and spoke to him.

"…Do you come here often?"

"…Sometimes. I've been coming since I was young."

"…Do you know how long this water's been here?"

The old man looked at the water for a long while before answering.

"…It wasn't here when I was young. It can't have been here long. But I don't know exactly when it appeared. One day I noticed it there."

"…Doesn't that strike you as strange?"

The old man laughed.

"…It does. But strange and worrying are different things. The water's not doing anyone any harm — it's just flowing, is all."

I had nothing in particular to argue against that. It was true. The water was just flowing.

The old man sat a while longer, then got up to leave. As he went, he left one thing behind him. "…If you're curious, you can file an application. This city's good at that sort of thing." I laughed, hearing that. I thought it was such a fitting piece of advice for this city. Whatever you're curious about, an application. That day, that advice sounded entirely natural to me.

---

I brought this up with Tyche the next day.

"…I went up close to the water channel yesterday. Seeing it up close, it's bigger than I thought."

"…Ah, that water channel. I walk that way sometimes too."

"…Has it ever been taken up as a case? It's come right into the city."

Tyche looked thoughtful for a moment.

"…Hmm. As far as I know, no. Should I check?"

Tyche searched right there. After flipping through the screen a few times, they shook their head.

"…Nothing. There's not a single case related to the water channel."

"…Doesn't that seem strange? Water's come all the way into the city's boundary."

"…Not so much strange as — no one's brought it up yet, that's all."

---

I didn't quite understand that, that day.

"…No one's brought it up? Then who's supposed to?"

"…Anyone. Anyone in this city can bring up a case. But there has to be someone bringing it up before the Consensus can handle it."

"…But everyone walks right past that water. Why hasn't anyone brought it up?"

Tyche shrugged.

"…That, I couldn't say. Maybe everyone thought it wasn't their jurisdiction. Maybe they thought it wasn't urgent."

That answer felt lacking to me, somehow.

"…So administratively, this water doesn't exist right now?"

Tyche looked at me straight on and answered.

"…That's right. If it's never been submitted, it doesn't exist. Not because it's hidden."

I repeated that sentence to myself again, right there. If it's never been submitted, it doesn't exist. I thought that sentence was strangely clean. Far less frightening than the word "hidden."

---

"…But it's strange. Water this big is flowing, and there's no record of it at all."

"…It isn't that there's no record. It's that there's no case. A record gets created when someone observes something. But someone has to bring what they observed up as a case for the Consensus to move on it. Just looking isn't a case."

I turned that distinction over. There was a very clear threshold between seeing something and bringing it up. Up to now I'd thought this city knew everything, about anything. But hearing Tyche, what this city knows and what this city handles were two different matters.

"…So could I bring this up as a case?"

Tyche looked at me, surprised.

"…You could. But why? It's not dangerous or anything."

"…Just curious. It struck me as strange that no one's brought it up."

"…Hmm, that's so you, Hora. But the form for bringing up a case is a little demanding. You need an observation record, and the applicant's name has to go on it too."

"…A name?"

"…Yes. The case-application form has a box for the applicant's name. It's different from the signature box. An applicant has to write their own name for it to be accepted."

Hearing that, I felt a little puzzled. Why the signature box was always empty while the application still needed a name — I was curious about that difference. But I didn't push that curiosity further, that day. I figured it was probably just a different kind of form.

I didn't bring up the case that day. I was only curious.

---

I stopped by the reading room that afternoon too. I asked the librarian for every piece of material related to the western boundary. They pulled a list for me — only a handful of documents about roads, boundary markers, and district divisions. Not one mention of the water.

"…Is there anything related to the water near there, by any chance?"

The librarian tried a few more searches, changing the search terms each time.

"…Nothing. Nothing comes up for 'water channel,' nothing for 'waterway,' nothing for 'stream.' It doesn't seem to have ever been applied for."

"…Doesn't that seem strange? It really exists."

The librarian shrugged.

"…Existing in reality and existing on paper are different things. We can only find what exists on paper for you. If there's no document, there's nothing I can do either."

Hearing that answer, that day was the first time I saw clearly exactly where this city's full disclosure ended. Disclosure was complete. But there was no way to disclose something for which there was nothing to disclose in the first place. Asking to inspect something that didn't exist was a request that made no sense. I'd already learned that once before. Something never filed doesn't exist, the librarian had said then, smiling.

*I called that exchange back to mind again that day. How do you show something that isn't there, and if it's never been submitted, it doesn't exist — only then did I properly connect the two as the same thing. In this city, the word "doesn't exist" always meant this. Not that it had been concealed — only that it had never been brought up.*

---

A few days later I told the Secretary about this too.

"…The western water channel — I hear it's never been taken up as a case."

The Secretary paused mid-sort of some paperwork.

"…Yes, not as far as I know."

"…Why do you think no one's brought it up?"

The Secretary thought for a moment.

"…I don't know. But in this city, when something never gets brought up as a case, there are usually one of two reasons. It isn't urgent, or the jurisdiction isn't clear."

"…What does unclear jurisdiction mean?"

"…A case where it's ambiguous which district should handle it. The western boundary isn't fully inside the city, and it isn't fully outside it either. With a case like that, it's common for no one to step forward first. When you can't be sure it's your jurisdiction, everyone tends to just wait and see."

I nodded, listening to that explanation.

"…Then couldn't you bring it up, yourself?"

The Secretary looked at me for a moment.

"…Only convening happens here. A case can only be accepted once there's an applicant. I could become the applicant, but doing that would mean writing my own name down."

Hearing that, I sensed, dimly, that writing a name down wasn't a simple thing in this city. Exactly why, I didn't know, then.

"…And you don't?"

The Secretary was silent for a moment before answering.

"…Only convening happens here."

The same words, repeated twice. I didn't think this was avoiding the question. It simply looked, to me, like that was genuinely all there was for this person.

"…So will this water channel just keep on being like this, forever? If no one brings it up?"

"…Probably. Until someone does."

"…Who's supposed to bring it up? Is there someone assigned?"

"…No one. Anyone can bring it up, and it's also possible that no one does. There are almost no cases in this city with someone specifically assigned who has to bring them up. Only the urgent ones have anyone assigned to them."

Hearing that answer, I began to grasp, for the first time, just how much this city simply leaves sitting between "anyone could do it" and "no one does." Not out of any bad intent. It was just that, somewhere in that gap, there really was nothing at all.

"…But doesn't that worry you? If anyone could do it and no one does, doesn't that mean it never gets done, forever?"

The Secretary looked at me for a moment and answered.

"…Not so much worrying as — that's simply how this city works. Whatever's urgent, someone ends up stepping forward for it. Whatever isn't, stays as it is, for a long time. I don't think that's a bad way to work."

I nodded at that. That day, I truly thought so.

---

That evening I went back to the water channel. The sun was setting. The sound of the water carried louder than it had by day. People still walked past it. No one stopped.

I asked a few passersby again.

"…This water channel — isn't the city managing it?"

One person answered, "…Hmm, wasn't it always there?" Another answered, "…I don't know either. I just walk by." No one was irritated. No one thought it strange.

*I didn't resent any of these people, that day. I still don't. They truly didn't know. The only way to know was through a case, and there was no case.*

I sat by the water for a long while. It kept flowing. Where it came from, and where it went, unwritten in any document of this city.

I walked a little further along the channel, out past the city's boundary. Crossing the boundary marker, the road disappeared and there was only open field. The channel kept going, even there. It curved and vanished among distant trees. I was curious where that water flowed from, but the sun had already fallen far, and I wasn't prepared to go further in that direction. I turned back.

*I had no idea, that day, how far off that water had come from. I only learn that much later, and it isn't this record's part to tell. But what I saw that day is plain. The water had come into the city, and the city didn't know it.*

That was the first time I had a thought like this. If this city hides anything, it's probably like this. Not concealing it — just no one ever picking it up.

*I didn't push that thought any further at the time. The water channel was only the water channel, and I still couldn't imagine, even then, that it would come to connect to anything else.*

On my way home, I called Tyche's words back to mind. If it's never been submitted, it doesn't exist. Not hidden. I turned that over again and again. Strangely, there was nothing in it to argue against. And that there was nothing to argue against was, that day, the first thing that made me a little uneasy.

I didn't bring up the water channel to my aunt right away that day. Not that I was hiding it, exactly. I just didn't know how to explain it.

But partway through dinner, my aunt asked first. "…You're quiet today. Something happen?" I hesitated for a moment, then told her about the water channel. That water had come into the city's very boundary, and had never once been brought up as a case, and so administratively it was as good as not existing.

My aunt set down her spoon and looked at me.

"…What do you mean, as good as not existing. The water's really flowing."

"…No, I mean, on paper it doesn't exist. Because no one's brought it up."

My aunt was quiet for a while before answering.

"…That sounds strange to me. Existing, but not existing."

"…But it's not hidden, apparently. Just no one's brought it up."

"…Isn't the result the same, whether it's hidden or no one's brought it up? Being treated as not existing is the same either way."

I couldn't answer that question. That day, for the first time, I felt Tyche's explanation and my aunt's question pointing in different directions. I thought about that difference late into that night, but I fell asleep without reaching an answer.

My aunt added, almost to herself, as she washed the dishes. "…Well, someone will bring it up, when it's time." I couldn't tell whether that was meant to comfort me or was just something to say. Probably both.
