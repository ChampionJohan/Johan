# Chapter 39. Tyche's Question

> _Previously_ — Hora faces the real reason she has always read to the
> end. Not a sense of justice, but that she once missed her mother's
> last sentence. She has become, not an accuser, but someone who wants
> to fill a box.

Once the third day ended, I started reading again.

I didn't read documents at random, the way I used to. From that day on I
held to exactly one document. The Seventh Classification. I read it for
the twenty-first time, having already read it about twenty.

*Reading it for a different reason didn't change how I read it. I still
read all forty-some pages from beginning to end, skipping no line. Only
this time, as I read, I began to think about what I would do.*

My aunt hardly said anything to me those few days. She made meals, she
returned my greeting when I left for work, and that was all. I was
grateful for that. If someone had kept asking me about it, I probably
would have found it even harder to decide.

*The decision comes later. At that point I was still standing at that
threshold. I was getting closer each day, but the approach wasn't at a
speed anyone could see. From the outside, I would have just looked like
a girl reading the same document over and over.*

---

Tyche came often, around then. During work hours, and after work too.
No one had told her to. In her own words, it was "just because I was
curious."

*I only found this strange later. When Tyche is curious about
something, she usually finds the materials and works out the answer
herself. Coming to visit a person, over and over, wasn't like Tyche. At
the time I just thought she was worried about me. Maybe the worry was
real too. The two things could be true at once.*

The way Tyche came to see me was worth noting too. She always brought
something in her hands. Papers laying out evidence, statistical tables,
past deliberation cases. But she didn't lay them out in front of me. She
just sat holding them, and only took them out once I asked about
something.

"…You look at that one for a long time, whenever."

"…Yeah."

Tyche was quiet a moment, then asked.

"…Are you going to write it?"

I didn't answer right away. She didn't press me either. For a while we
just sat there looking at the empty box together.

---

*I've revisited this moment many times since. It was the first time
Tyche had ever asked me something directly. Up until then Tyche had been
the one observing me. The one explaining things. This time she was the
one asking.*

"…If you write it, what changes?"

The question seemed simple, but it wasn't. I already felt its weight at
the time. Tyche was asking me about the result. And I didn't know the
result.

*I truly didn't know. I still don't know exactly, even now. That the new
deliberation would only be reversed in part — I only came to know that
later. What kind of thing review was — I only learned that in my body
once I reached that chapter. At that point I stood in front of this
question without having gone through any of it.*

*I thought of Temel just then. I could have asked him the same thing.
What changed, once he wrote it. In Temel's case there's a little more of
an answer. A few of his cases really were reclassified. But some others
weren't. I knew this, and still didn't bring it up to Tyche. I didn't
want to borrow a result that hadn't yet been confirmed.*

I looked into Tyche's eyes. She was truly waiting for an answer. Her
face wasn't the usual one, where she offers her own answer first and
only asks for confirmation.

The office was quiet. Outside, the sound of the water channel could be
heard, very faintly. I listened to that sound while I tried to find an
answer. I wasn't trying to find a perfect answer. I just didn't want to
lie.

I answered slowly.

"…Maybe nothing changes. But it won't be empty."

---

Tyche was quiet for a long time after hearing that.

"…Is that all?"

"…Yeah."

"…I don't see what meaning that has."

I didn't argue back. I also had no argument to make.

"…I don't know either. But that's everything I have, right now."

*I wanted to render this exchange exactly. I wasn't dressing it up to
persuade Tyche. I had no logic that day that could have persuaded her.
If I'd had one, I would have used it. I didn't, so I said what I had,
as it was.*

*That night, turning this answer over, I understood that it connected
to something I'd learned earlier. Reading the same document side by
side, Tyche saw the accuracy of the procedure, and I saw the empty box.
We were both reading it straight. That same difference was in this
conversation now. Tyche was looking at the fact that filling it changes
nothing. I was looking at the fact that even so, it stops being empty.
Both of us were looking at the same box and seeing different things.*

*I felt, for the first time that day, that these two sentences didn't
contradict each other. Maybe nothing changes, and even so it won't be
empty — these two things could stand together. I had thought, until
then, that they collided. They didn't.*

---

Tyche kept turning my words over in her face.

"…I still don't really understand why it matters that it won't be
empty. Why is being empty a bad thing? Nobody filling it just means
nobody filled it. It's not empty because someone did something wrong."

"…That's right."

Tyche fidgeted with the papers in her hand for a moment. That wasn't
like her, usually.

"…I've seen a lot of deliberations, this whole time. Quite a few I sat
in on as an assistant. I've never once seen the signature box filled.
I thought that was just how it was. I thought it was a rule. But finding
out it wasn't — it felt strange."

"…What did?"

"…Why nobody had filled it — that felt strange. It's not that you're
not allowed to. I thought at first everyone just didn't know. But
everyone knows. Look it up in the rulebook, it's right there. Nobody
had just done it."

"…Is that what you think the probability is?"

"…No. This is the part probability can't explain. If it were
probability, somebody should have filled it at least once by now. But
once in eleven years — that's too rare to explain by chance."

*I remember this part for a long time. It was the moment Tyche herself
admitted there was a part that probability couldn't explain. But she
didn't move from that admission to a different conclusion. She just
admitted it and stopped there. That was Tyche.*

*I felt, at that moment, the temptation to press the point with her.
See, it can't be explained, so there must be meaning in it — I wanted
to say that. I didn't. It felt wrong to take something Tyche had
admitted herself and use it to serve my own logic. She wanted to leave
that fact exactly as it was. I wanted to respect that way of hers.*

"…And you still want to fill it?"

"…Yeah."

"…Why?"

I couldn't answer this question right away either. I could have said
the answer I'd found before. That I'd missed something once. But that
wasn't an answer to Tyche's question. She wasn't asking about my
circumstances — she was asking about the meaning of the act itself.

"…I don't know. But does there have to be a reason?"

Tyche gave a small laugh at that. It wasn't a mocking laugh.

"…That's so like you."

"…What do you mean?"

"…Someone who just does something without a reason. I'm not good at
that. I always look for the reason first. If the reason doesn't turn up,
I can't move. So sometimes I can't do anything at all."

"…Is that a bad thing?"

"…No. But sometimes I envy it."

I thought about that for a long time. It was the first time Tyche had
ever said she envied me.

---

*I was a little surprised, hearing that. I learned, then, for the first
time, what kind of person Tyche saw me as. Someone who moved without a
reason. To Tyche, that was probably a kind of person that couldn't be
explained. Tyche is a person who deals in things that can be explained.
Probability, procedure, a vacancy. All of it can be explained.*

*I was about to do something that couldn't be explained. In front of
Tyche.*

I asked her something.

"…Why do you work here, Tyche? As a deliberation assistant."

"…Because my number came up right. And also because it suits me. It
fits."

"…What does fitting mean?"

"…I like explaining things. Figuring out why something turned out the
way it did."

"…Then explain this too. Why I'm doing this."

Tyche looked at me for a moment, steadily.

"…That one, I still can't explain either. You're one of the few things
I can't explain, Hora."

I found it strange that this came out of Tyche. She wasn't a person who
said comfortably that she couldn't explain something.

"…Doesn't it bother you, not being able to explain it?"

"…It does. But it's better than forcing an explanation onto it anyway.
I dislike that even more."

I liked that answer. Tyche didn't force herself to patch over a hole in
her own logic. If there was a hole, she just let it stay a hole.

---

Tyche asked again. This time her voice was a little more careful.

"…Aren't you scared?"

"…I am."

"…And you're doing it anyway?"

"…Yeah."

"…I don't understand it."

"…I don't fully understand myself either. But it turns out you can do
something even without understanding it."

---

Tyche didn't answer that. Instead she got up and went to the window. The
one that faced the water channel. That day the sound of the water was
unusually loud.

"…I still think all of this is probability. That you're sitting here,
that those people became the Seventh Classification — all of it is a
vacancy and a number and a procedure lining up. I think putting meaning
onto that is something people do because they're lonely."

"…I know. You've said that before."

"…But I don't want to stop you from filling that box, either."

The sound of the water broke off for a moment, then picked up again.
Probably the wind.

I was surprised by what she'd said.

"…Why?"

Tyche answered, looking out the window.

"…I don't know. There's just no reason to stop you, that's all."

---

*This was an answer that sounded like Tyche. She hadn't stayed by my
side because she'd been persuaded. She stayed because there was no
reason to stop me. I think that difference is the most important part
of this chapter.*

*Tyche never changes her position after this. Not for a long time, not
even by the time this record ends. But from that evening on, she kept
staying by my side. Without ever being persuaded.*

*I count this among the most important discoveries in this book. Two
people didn't have to reach the same conclusion in order to stay
together. Tyche believed, to the end, in chance. I believed, to the end,
that there was something more. And yet, that night, we sat in the same
room, with the same document between us.*

*I think that was Tyche's own way. Staying beside someone while
believing it's chance. Being kind while believing there's no meaning. It
was nowhere in any proposal document, but I want to call that Tyche's
faith. She'd laugh if she heard me say that.*

That night I asked her one more thing.

"…If I write it and nothing changes at all, then what does that make
me?"

This time it took Tyche a while to think.

"…Just someone who wrote it. That's all."

"…Is that all?"

"…Yeah. There's no such thing as the world taking notice of you or
anything. The Consensus doesn't do that. The Consensus just receives it
and processes it, that's all."

I didn't find that cold. I found it, instead, precise.

"…So I become someone who amounts to nothing."

"…No. You just become someone who wrote it. That's better than
amounting to nothing, I think."

*Tyche probably wasn't saying this to persuade me. She was just saying
the fact. But it was the first time I felt comforted by her words. The
comfort I got from Tyche always took this shape. Not warm words —
precise ones. I understood, that day, that this was Tyche's own kind of
kindness.*

---

We sat together in the office late into the night. We didn't talk much
more. Tyche looked over her own materials, and I read the document
again. The signature box was still empty.

The caretaker came to put out the lights, saw the two of us, and just
left. The same as he had, the very first night.

*The two scenes overlapped for me. Two hundred and some days before,
the night I sat alone reading forty pages. And now, the night with
Tyche beside me. The only thing different was that one more person had
been added beside me. That one person made quite a large difference to
me, that night.*

We packed up our things without saying anything. The silence wasn't
awkward.

Coming out late, Tyche asked.

"…I'll come again tomorrow. Is that all right?"

"…Yeah."

"…No question about why?"

"…I told you, you're allowed to do things without a reason too."

Tyche laughed. Then she nodded once and walked off. I watched her back
for a moment, then walked the other way.

That was how we parted. The night air was cold, and the sound of the
water channel followed behind me the whole way.

*Tyche hadn't come out that night to persuade me. When I asked her
about it later, she said she'd simply been curious what I was thinking.
That that was all it had been. I think that was true too.*
