# Chapter 47. The Secretary

> _Previously_ — Hora's name appeared on documents from three other
> cities. She wrote none of them. Who did, she never managed to find
> out. This city hides nothing, but this time there simply was no way
> to know, to begin with.

The Secretary came to find me around the end of the workday.

It was within working hours. Unlike that night, long before. That time
we'd first met outside of working hours; this time it was an ordinary
weekday afternoon. He came carrying a single folder. Nothing about him
seemed different from usual.

"…Hora, do you have a moment."

"…Yes."

This meeting was unlike any review I'd had before. No one had called me
in. He had come to find me on his own. That had never happened before.
It was an unusually quiet afternoon. The other seats were already
empty, and the late sunlight was coming in long through the window. I
watched that light and thought, briefly, that the light must have
looked something like this the very first time I'd walked into this
building.

---

We sat in an empty meeting room. The Secretary set the folder down on
the table. But he didn't open it.

"…I'm not here about paperwork today."

I was a little surprised. He had never come to find me without a
matter of business before.

"…Then what is it?"

"…It will soon be fifteen years in this post."

"…That long?"

"…Yes. Since before Hora was born."

His expression barely changed as he said this. It was the same face as
always.

"…How many people have passed through this seat, in that time? Assistant
members, like Hora."

"…No count was ever kept, but it's probably over a hundred."

"…Are all of them remembered?"

"…Faces are hazy. But the matters each one handled are remembered.
The matter comes to mind before the person."

I found that answer strangely sad. That the matter comes to mind before
the person.

"…And me? Am I remembered as a matter too?"

The Secretary took a moment before this question.

"…No. Hora is remembered by name."

I couldn't say anything back to that answer. That single line —
remembered by name — stayed with me longer than the whole rest of the
conversation.

---

"…Fifteen years, and only convening was done. Setting meeting dates,
distributing materials, writing minutes, passing on results. That was
all."

"…I know."

"…Everything that's happened in this seat since Hora arrived has been
watched, all of it. The Seventh Classification matter, review, the new
deliberation, and now this reexamination."

"…Yes."

"…What was done, across all of that, can be said exactly. It was
convened. It was passed on. That's all."

I couldn't tell why he was bringing this up again, so I just listened
quietly.

---

"…Hora once asked what happens if you write your name. The gaze was
averted, then."

"…I remember."

"…The answer wasn't unknown, then, and that isn't why the gaze was
averted. The answer was already known. It had been seen, eleven years
before."

*I stopped breathing for a moment, hearing this. The words eleven
years before overlapped with something he'd said earlier. I convened
that one too.*

"…And at the very start, when the wrong paperwork was filed. Hora
asked how something that isn't there could be confirmed."

"…You remember that?"

"…Yes. That was thought about for a long time."

The Secretary nodded briefly.

"…That was a fair thing to say, then too. There was no way for Hora to
confirm a notice that didn't exist. But every time that was said, no
distinction was drawn between something not existing because it truly
wasn't there, and something not existing because no one had made it
exist. Even though the two are different."

---

"…The person who wrote a name, back then — what became of that person
was watched to the very end. A new deliberation was opened, part of it
was overturned, the rest wasn't, that person kept being called in, and
then one day, vanished from the roster."

"…Vanished, meaning—"

"…Where that person went isn't known. The record simply stops there."

"…Do you remember that person's name?"

The Secretary nodded.

"…It's remembered. But it won't be spoken here."

"…Why not?"

"…That's that person's name. Not mine. It isn't a place to bring it up
carelessly."

*I was a little startled by that answer. The Secretary wasn't someone
who hid names. Nowhere in this city was there a hidden name. And yet,
in that moment, for the first time, he chose to know something and not
say it. That was different from hiding it. I felt that difference
immediately, right there.*

Saying this, the Secretary lowered his gaze for the first time.

"…That matter was only convened. But in the eleven years since, no one
wrote a name again. That wasn't thought to be anyone's fault. There was
only convening done."

---

"…But?"

The Secretary looked at me again.

"…But then Hora came. And wrote it."

"…Are you saying that's your fault?"

"…No. That's not what's meant."

He took a moment to choose his words.

"…It's true that, for those eleven years, only convening was said to
have been done, over and over. But across those eleven years, that
record was reopened, every single time. No one asked it to be."

"…Why?"

"…That isn't known. It was simply kept being looked at."

"…What was being looked for?"

"…At first, where that person had gone. Later, not even that. It just
became a habit."

"…What was found?"

"…Nothing was found. Not in eleven years."

I found that answer strangely sad. Eleven years of finding nothing. I
tried to imagine those years. Sitting in the same seat every day,
doing something no one had asked for, with no result, over and over.

"…And it was still kept up?"

"…Yes. It's strange. Even I can't fully explain why."

---

I didn't understand right away what this meant. The Secretary went on.

"…Reopening that record, over and over, wasn't part of the work here.
No one ever instructed that it be checked."

"…Then why was it done?"

The Secretary was silent for a long while. Only the sound of the
meeting room clock could be heard.

"…I was curious."

---

Something different passed through me, hearing that. I thought of
every sentence he'd spoken, all this time. It was put in order. It was
confirmed. It was convened. Sentences that had gone fifteen years
without a subject.

"…You just said, 'I was curious.'"

The Secretary paused for a moment.

"…Yes."

"…Are you going to correct it?"

This time he didn't correct it.

"…No. This time it's the right way to say it. It won't be corrected."

---

*I'll remember this moment for a long time. Once before, he had said
"I" exactly once, and corrected it right away. At the time I thought it
had simply been a slip of habit. I know now that it wasn't a slip. He
must have known it, even then. He simply hadn't allowed himself to say
it, that time.*

I didn't know how to answer him. It wasn't something to congratulate,
and it wasn't something to comfort. I just listened to those words, as
they were.

*I thought, in that moment, of the people I'd met earlier in this
record. "I only passed it on." Twelve people had all said exactly that.
None of them had lied. I'd known, even then, that all those words were
true, and still felt some kind of emptiness. Only now do I think I
understand, a little, what that emptiness had been. Being true didn't
mean it was the whole of it. The Secretary, too, was only saying today,
for the first time, that there was a self standing behind that truth.*

---

"…So what is it you wanted to say?"

The Secretary looked at the folder again. Still unopened.

"…Nothing. This was simply what needed saying to Hora."

"…Is that all?"

"…Yes. That one line, just now. That's the whole of it."

I waited for him to say more. An apology, perhaps, or regret. No such
words came.

*Writing this chapter, I felt the temptation to correct him. To write
him as if he'd suddenly become a different person, as someone who,
from this chapter on, had reclaimed a subject. I decided against it.
He was still the Secretary, even after saying this. The very next
sentence came out without a subject again. I wasn't disappointed by
that. If anything, it left that one sentence standing out all the more
clearly.*

---

"…But Secretary, what about that folder? It was never opened."

The Secretary looked down at it.

"…Ah, that was just a pretext. Coming empty-handed felt awkward."

"…What's in it?"

"…Nothing in particular. Next week's meeting schedule. It could have
been handed over any time."

I laughed a little at that. That even the Secretary needed a pretext,
sometimes, felt strangely human to me that day. I thought it had been a
long time since I'd laughed like that.

"…This will go handle the next agenda item now."

The Secretary said that and stood. Gathering the folder, his usual way
of speaking returned.

"…What's been confirmed today will be passed along the approval
line."

I laughed a little, hearing that. It wasn't a sad laugh.

"…Secretary."

"…Yes."

"…Thank you."

"…Nothing to thank. It was only said because it wanted saying."

He said that and left the room.

---

I sat alone for a long while. Outside the window the sun was setting.

*This whole record was written trying not to write this person as an
accusation. He wasn't a bad person. He was simply someone who believed
he was nothing at all. But today, in exactly one sentence, he said, of
his own accord, that he wasn't nothing.*

*I won't write that this changed him completely. Tomorrow, too, he will
convene, and pass things on, and confirm them. Without a subject. I
only set down, exactly, this one thing that happened today. He said, "I
was curious." He didn't correct that sentence. That's the whole of
what happened to this person.*

Turning off the light and leaving the meeting room, I thought, on
impulse, of a stamp I'd once seen. The one with only a window number
engraved on it. A mark that belongs to a seat, not a person. I'd long
thought of the Secretary as a person like that stamp. I didn't fully
give up that thought today. Only, I learned, for the first time, that
even a stamp sometimes has a hand.

*I'm not certain this comparison is exact. But it's the only way I
could put it into words. The Secretary was a seat, today too, and at
the same time, today alone, he wasn't only a seat.*

---

The next day I told Tyche about this. She said little the whole time
she listened, and once I'd finished, asked.

"…Is it really that big a deal? He just didn't correct one word."

"…I thought the same at first. But I keep thinking about it."

"…Why?"

"…Because he said, today, something he hadn't said once in fifteen
years."

Tyche thought for a moment, then spoke.

"…Even looked at through probability, that's not so strange. A person
speaks in the same pattern for fifteen years straight, and then one
day, by chance, the pattern breaks. That kind of thing happens."

"…But why today, of all days? Why to me, of all people?"

"…That, I can't explain either."

*I noticed that Tyche was, once again, admitting a part she couldn't
explain. She didn't bother hiding it. I confirmed again that this was
Tyche's own kind of honesty.*

"…But Tyche, what do you think of the Secretary?"

"…I think he's a good person. Fifteen years doing that work is
impressive too."

"…Have you ever once thought he was a bad person?"

Tyche shook her head.

"…Not once. There's no reason to. You said my parents did something
similar too. I can't find a reason to think badly of people like
that."

I agreed with her. I'd never thought badly of the Secretary either.
Only, after today, I could no longer see him in exactly the same way as
before. Not worse, and not better, either. Just a little more exactly.

---

At home, I told my aunt this story. She listened quietly, then asked.

"…So do you think he'll change now?"

"…I don't know. I don't think he will."

"…Then why does it matter so much to you?"

I couldn't answer that right away. After thinking for a long while, I
said.

"…Because it was one sentence. But that one sentence was the first in
fifteen years."

My aunt nodded at that. She didn't press further.

"…But Hora, was that person a bad person, or was he just a seat?"

I had to think about that question for a long while.

"…He wasn't a bad person. But it's hard now to call him just a seat,
either."

"…Then what is he?"

"…I don't know, Aunt. Sometimes it feels like both. Sometimes it
feels like neither."

My aunt didn't press further at that. She just nodded.

*Writing this chapter, I felt the temptation, many times, to reach one
conclusion about the Secretary. That he was, in truth, a good person —
or, that he was merely a part of the system. I decided to write
neither. I close this chapter without concluding anything about him. I
think that's the most honest thing I can do for him.*

*That night I opened the drawer and put nothing into it. I felt this
wasn't something to write down on paper. I decided to leave it only in
this record.*

*That person went on being the Secretary, after that. As far as I
know, he still is.*
