# Chapter 12. Cast Out

> _Previously_ — On the night of the third day, exhausted past bearing,
> Pheugon poured out words that might have been prayer or might have been
> curse. What began with "I was right" ended with "So why am I here." He
> released that question into the water and lost consciousness. What he
> remembers next is the sensation of his body moving. He was not the one
> moving it.

The current lifted my body.

I thought it was a dream. Three full days in that water — it wouldn't have been strange to dream of water lifting me.

*It wasn't a dream. I only learned that later. The sluice gate had opened. On its own schedule. Not for me. Things had simply gone back to normal. The same as when it had closed.*

*I turned this fact over many times since. That door hadn't shut to trap me, and it hadn't opened to let me out, either. A set hour had simply come. I could have died inside that indifference, and I was also saved by that same indifference. That the thing that saved me and the thing that had trapped me were the same kind of thing — I still find that strange, even now.*

Before I came fully to my senses, my body reacted first. I think I heard the sound of water rushing hard through the narrow gap past the gate. Or maybe that's a memory I've pieced together since. What's certain is that, at some moment, the water where I'd been sitting suddenly pulled me in.

---

My body was swept up in the current.

I couldn't resist. I had no strength left to resist. I struck the wall a few times. I don't remember clearly whether it hurt. I only flowed.

*I don't remember that moment in detail. What the body remembers and what the mind remembers are different things. My body still, now and then, recalls the force of that current, the shock of striking the wall. My mind kept nothing at all. I wasn't there to keep it.*

The current carried me on a long while. Through narrow stretches, through wide ones, narrow again. I don't know how many times I breathed, carried along in that flow. Whenever my head broke the surface for a moment, I drew breath by instinct.

Once my body struck the tunnel wall hard. At the shoulder. In that instant I came briefly back to myself. Pain had woken me. I opened my eyes and saw nothing. Still in the dark. I closed them again. I already knew it made no difference, open or shut.

*In that brief moment I had a strange thought. If I died like this, no one would ever know. The supervisor would believe I was somewhere on the road to the Reckoning; my father would assume I'd arrived safely. No one would ever search this waterway. Strangely, that thought didn't frighten me. I had no strength left even to feel afraid.*

The current turned once more. I gave my body over to it entirely. There was no reason left to resist, and no strength left either.

---

Light appeared.

Faint, at first. Flickering every time the current shook my body. I couldn't tell whether it was real or something I'd imagined.

*I've gone back over that moment many times since. Was I swimming toward that light, or was I just letting the current carry me. I still can't say for certain. Maybe it was both. My body moved toward the light, apart from my will.*

At first the light was only a matter of brightness. But as it grew closer, color began to appear. Gray shifting into a hazy gold. Only seeing that color again did I realize how completely I'd forgotten, in three days, that color existed at all.

The light kept growing. The current slowed. I felt myself come out into some wide, open space. It felt as if the ceiling had disappeared. Wind brushed over my head.

Wind. It took a moment to recognize the feeling. Three days without ever feeling it. There's no wind, underwater. I felt that wind on my face, and something in my body responded. Something like a sign that I was still alive.

The current pushed me upward. A narrow vertical passage. My fingertips brushed the wall. Smooth stone. A familiar touch. I had no room left to think about what it meant.

The water gave me one great push. And I struck something solid. Stone. Round, mossy. I only learned later — it was the coping stone of a well.

---

I think I lost consciousness there. I don't know if it was brief or long.

The first thing I felt, opening my eyes, was light. So bright my eyes hurt. I closed them again, and opened them, little by little.

Sky.

*The sky, seen for the first time in three days. I don't know how to describe that color. Blue wasn't enough of a word. I understood, in that moment, for the first time, how enormous a thing color was. In the dark there had been no color. Not light, not dark. Simply nothing at all. Seeing the sky, I was overwhelmed by the fact that the world held color at all.*

*Looking up at that sky, for one brief moment, I couldn't tell whether this was a sky I was seeing dead, or seeing alive. Strangely, that distinction didn't seem to matter, in that moment. The sky was simply there. Not waiting for me, not turning me away. Just there.*

The touch of sunlight on my face was unfamiliar too. For three days my skin had known only the temperature of water. Now there was a different temperature. Stinging, drying, falling from above. It took me a while even to recognize it as sunlight.

My body was soaked. I lay sprawled on stone ground. My arms and legs had no strength left at all. Moving even a single finger, opening my eyelids the whole way, was hard.

I had no strength to look at my own fingertips, but I could tell by feel alone that my fingertips had gone wrinkled and swollen. My mouth tasted of salt and iron, mixed together. A low ringing wouldn't stop in my ears. Every blink made the light look slightly different.
A chill I hadn't felt in the water came over me now. As my soaked body began to dry, I felt colder, somehow, than I had underwater.

I could feel the warmth of the stone beneath me. Which meant the sun had been heating that stone a while already. Daytime. I still didn't know what time it was, or exactly how many days I'd been trapped. The wall bore three scratched lines, but I had no way to count how much time had passed since I'd drawn the last one.

*In that moment, for the first time in my life, I realized I had completely lost track of the days. It was one of the very few moments in this whole book where I lost count of a number. Strangely, it left me feeling clear. Before something uncountable, I no longer needed to count at all.*

Sound came back slowly too. At first just a ringing hum. Then, one by one, it broke apart into separate sounds. Far off, a cart wheel rolling over paved stone; a bird's cry; someone bargaining in a low voice. To ears that had known nothing but water for three days, those sounds were too many, too loud. I lay there with my eyes open, trying to pick them apart one by one, and soon gave up. This city was simply living out its day, as if nothing at all had happened.

---

That's when a voice spoke.

"…You're not from here, are you?"

I only rolled my eyes toward the sound, not knowing where it came from. I had no strength to lift my head.

*I heard that question first, and only after, lifted my eyes and saw the person. That was the order it happened in. The voice came first.*

A child's voice. I managed to turn my head, just a little. Beyond the well's coping stone, someone was looking down at me. Backlit, so I couldn't make out the face.

Bare feet. Dust on the tops of them. One toe was tapping idly at a pebble. It might have been getting ready to run, or it might just have been bored.

I tried to answer. What came out of my throat sounded like water. It wasn't words.

*For three years I'd made my living by voice. And in that moment, what came out of my throat wasn't a human sound at all. That fact was, strangely, humiliating to me.*

*I still think about it, why that first question wasn't "Are you all right?" but "You're not from here, are you?" Usually, when someone's pulled out of the water, don't you ask first if they're all right? But that child asked first whether I belonged to this city or not. I'd learn the reason later. In this city, which side you belonged to had to be confirmed before who was hurt. I didn't know that yet. I only felt, then, that the order of the question was strange.*

The owner of that voice said nothing for a moment. Seemed to be looking me over. I felt that gaze on me and still had no strength to lift my head any further.

"…You're not dead?" the child asked again. The voice had come a little closer than before. Sounded like they'd crouched down.

I answered by trying to move my fingers, just a little. That was all I could do, in that moment. The child looked down at that small movement for a long while.

---

*I remember those few seconds very slowly. My body had completely broken down, and yet some strange corner of my mind stayed oddly clear. In that brief moment I thought: where am I. Starting from that well, three days ago — how far had I drifted, carried along, to end up here.*

*And right after, another thought came. I had run to avoid going to the Reckoning. The opposite direction, toward somewhere I knew no one. But this current had flowed regardless of my will. I had never once chosen where this water would take me.*

*I wrote, in earlier chapters, that at every fork I chose a direction with no basis for it at all. Looking back now, those choices had never meant anything to begin with. Where the sluice gates would open and close, which way the water would push me — I had decided none of it. I had simply been carried along by the water. To the very city I had tried so hard to avoid, no one but the water itself had brought me.*

*I've stood a long time, before this fact, between the word coincidence and some other word. Calling it coincidence was easier. Calling it anything else was not. I would only come to accept that other word much later, in a different chapter of this book. Here, I'm still not ready to use it.*

I gathered my strength and lifted my head. And I looked, not toward where the child was pointing, but somewhere beyond it.

*Those few seconds are the moment I've returned to, in memory, more than any other in this entire book. In that moment I still knew nothing. Who I would meet in this city, how long I would end up staying here — none of it. I only lifted my head. And with that single motion, the next chapter of my life had already begun.*

---

Beside the well's coping stone, a bronze plate was set into a low post. Rusted. An old plate.

I strained my eyes to read the letters. Blurred. I strained again.

The two characters slowly came into focus.

**The Reckoning.**

---

I looked at those letters. For a long time.

The plate was cold. My fingers hadn't even touched it, and still, I believe, even now, that I felt that cold.

*I want to say that no thought came to me, in that moment. But that isn't true. Thoughts came. Only there were too many of them, all at once, and none of them ever became a sentence.*

*The first thought was this. This is a mistake. I've misread some other sign. I held onto that thought and looked at the letters again. The blur didn't clear, and yet those two characters didn't change at all.*

*The second thought was this. A little further on might be a different city. The Reckoning is large; this well might just be on its outskirts. I couldn't lean on that thought for long either. I had no strength left to move my body and confirm it.*

*By the third thought, I could no longer make any more thoughts at all. I simply accepted the fact. I had arrived exactly where I had run from.*

*I remember what my body did, in that moment. My heart beat slowly, but hard. One long breath came in and didn't go back out for a long time. I only understood later that it was shock. At the time I just felt that something was wrong with my body.*

*I had run. Away from the Reckoning. Fled into the waterway, trapped for three days with no idea which way I was going. And the place the water had spat me out was, of all places, this city.*

*I didn't even have the strength, then, to think about whether that was coincidence or not. I only watched those two characters, and felt my whole body say, no. No. It has to be no. And still, there before my eyes, those letters stood. Blurred, and yet unmistakable.*

I lay on the stone ground, unable to look away from those letters. Above me the child said something more. I couldn't make out the words.

*I've thought about this moment many times since. That someone who fled should be washed back up in the very place they fled from. I didn't know, then, how common a story that is, in this world. I thought it was some especially cruel coincidence, meant only for me. I see it differently now. Maybe it wasn't coincidence at all — maybe there had simply never been so many places left to flee to, from the start. Every waterway leads somewhere, in the end. The places a person can be carried to are narrower than they think.*

*I'm placing this chapter at the end of Part One. That noon, I lay sprawled at the wellside of the Reckoning. A boy who had run, been trapped three days in the dark, was cast out into the very heart of the place he'd run from. Where the current had carried me, and the moment it cast me out — I had chosen neither. That was the whole of what Part One taught me.*
