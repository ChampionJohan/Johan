# Chapter 47. Were You There Before

> _Previously_ — He came up the hill to find Pheugon. "Doest thou well to be angry." Pheugon couldn't answer. He gave no answer in his place, either — only, "You'll know that answer by living." The two of them sat a long while without speaking.

The sun was drawing close to noon.

We were still there. He hadn't left yet. I hadn't gone anywhere either. Between us lay the dry stem, an unanswered question, and — strangely — a comfortable silence.

In that silence I thought again about this person. In forty days I'd only seen him a handful of times. Standing beside someone unfairly cornered, in the square. Knowing a person's weight before the Scales did. Never getting angry even when I provoked him on purpose. Even gathering all those pieces together, I couldn't say who this person was. The one thing I could be sure of was that he wasn't from this city. Exactly where he was from, I didn't know.

Fiddling with the dry stem, I suddenly remembered a question I'd been holding back for a long time. I hesitated a long while over whether to say it.

*I know now why I'd held that question back so long. It was because I already knew there would be no answer. And still, in the end, I asked it. That day I learned there are questions worth asking even without an answer.*

---

"…Can I ask you something." I said.

"…Go ahead."

I looked toward the city. It was hard to ask, looking straight at him.

"…You." I said. "Have you been in a city like this before?"

He didn't answer.

I kept going. Once I'd started, I couldn't stop.

"What I mean is, is this the first time for you? Being someone who doesn't get angry, someone who knows before the Scales does, someone who sits with a person like this and asks questions? Is this your first time, or has this happened before? In other cities? With other people?"

I poured all that out without pausing to breathe. It felt as if, if I didn't ask now, I might never ask again.

---

He looked at me.

And smiled.

*I still don't feel confident describing that smile precisely, even now. Nowhere in this book had I ever seen him smile before. I'd known he was someone who never got angry. That he was also someone who never smiled — I hadn't known that either, until that moment. When he smiled, for a second I wondered if I'd asked something wrong. But it wasn't a mocking smile. It wasn't teasing, either. I never fully worked out, that day, what that smile really was.*

"…That's an interesting question." he said.

"…I didn't ask to be interesting."

"…I know."

He kept smiling, watching me. I didn't look away. There was something in that smile. I searched his face for a long while, trying to find what it was. It wasn't only pleasure. There was something like a very old weariness mixed into it too. And yet that weariness didn't feel like exhaustion — it felt like the kind of weariness only someone who has endured something for a very long time can carry.

*In that moment, for the first time, I wondered. How much silence had this person passed through, to arrive at this one smile. I didn't know the answer. I still don't. But I could tell that this one smile had revealed, just barely, the weight of everything he'd swallowed down until now.*

---

"…You're not going to answer, are you." I asked.

"…If I" he said, still smiling a little, "answer that question, what happened on this hill won't stay on this hill."

I didn't understand what he meant.

"…What does that mean?"

"…Some questions" he said, "give you the next question once you hear the answer. And answering that one gives you another. You're knocking on the first door right now."

"…So you won't open it?"

"…Not today." he said.

"…Then someday?"

"…That's not for me to decide." he said.

"…Then who decides?"

He gave no answer to that question either. In that silence, I felt a third kind of silence — different from the other two I'd learned from him before.

*That was the first time I met this third silence. It wasn't a silence that knows the answer and withholds it. It wasn't a silence waiting for me to reach it myself, either. It was simply a silence that said, now is not the time. I couldn't find the right words to explain it then. I still can't, fully, now. But before that silence, I felt two things at once — that the answer to this question surely existed somewhere, and that I wasn't the one meant to hear it today.*

---

I thought I should have been angry at that answer. Strangely, I wasn't.

*I didn't know why, then. I know a little more now. There were several questions in this book that never got answered. Why the clause never activated. Who wrote the clause. Why he never got angry. Before all of those questions, I'd always felt only frustration. But before this question, something else came first. In that moment I felt certain he was hiding something. And that certainty — that he was hiding something — was itself proof that something was there.*

"…I think that's the answer." I said. "You not answering."

He looked at me. A little of the smile still remained.

"…Think whatever you like."

---

"…Just one more, then." I said. "Have you seen this city before, or is this the first time?"

"…Same question." he said. "Just dressed differently."

I couldn't argue with that. He was right.

"…Then let me ask it differently." I said. "I thought this was the first forty days this city had ever had to face. That the clause had activated for the first time too. The Adjudicator waited forty years for it. But you. Is this your first time too?"

He was quiet for a moment.

"…I" he said, "make a distinction between saying something is the first time, and saying it isn't familiar."

I turned that sentence over several times.

"…What's the difference?"

"…Whether something's the first time is a matter of time." he said. "Whether you've been through it before or not. Familiarity is a different matter. There are things you can go through many times and never grow familiar with."

"…So which is this? How many times has it been? Are you used to it, or not?"

"…Same question again." he said. This time he didn't smile. But there was still gentleness left in his voice. "You've put a third set of clothes on it."

I felt a little embarrassed at that. I didn't give up, though.

---

I held on to what he'd said.

"…So are you used to it? Things like this?"

He didn't answer. Instead he looked toward the city. The roofs down below were shining in the sunlight.

"…Pheugon." he said. It was the tone of someone changing the subject. "Do you know what that city is doing right now?"

"…I don't know. I'm up here."

"…Living." he said. "It's just living. It lived yesterday, it's living today, it'll live tomorrow. That's the most important thing there is."

*In that moment I knew he was changing the subject. I didn't press him on it. I already knew I wasn't going to get an answer. But in that moment, I also felt that the way he changed the subject wasn't a retreat. It was closer to a hand, gesturing to take me somewhere else.*

---

We went quiet again.

*I never heard the answer to that question, that day. I still haven't heard it. Even now, writing this, I don't know what city he was in before, how many cities he's watched through forty days like these, whether this really was the first time.*

*And yet I've never been able to let go of that question, since that day. I've held on to it the whole time I've been writing this book. The thought that someday, in some other city, someone else passing through their own forty days, might go through something very close to what happened in this one. That thought unsettled me. And, strangely, it comforted me too.*

I saw Trope, far off, climbing the hill. She had the water jug slung over her shoulder again, today too. He saw her, and stood without a word.

"…I'll come again." he said.

"…When?"

"…When you need me."

He left with those words and walked down the hill. I watched, as he passed Trope, the two of them bow their heads briefly to each other. Then he kept walking, and I never did see which direction he vanished in.

---

Trope only really looked at me once she'd finished climbing all the way to the top.

"…That person just now — do you know him?" she asked. She was still out of breath. "As he passed me, he nodded, and it was a face I'd never seen before, but somehow it felt familiar."

I stopped for a moment at that.

*I didn't know, then, what to tell Trope. How do you explain a person you don't fully understand yourself? In the end I chose the most honest answer I had.*

"…I don't really know him either." I said. "But he's helped me the whole forty days."

Trope didn't press further. Instead she sat down beside me and unwrapped her bundle.

"…The Adjudicator said you really have to eat something now." she said. Two loaves of bread and a few boiled potatoes came out.

I took them. My hands were shaking. I only truly admitted, then, how hungry I was.

---

We sat side by side next to the dry stem and shared the bread.

We were quiet for a long while. A bell sounded faintly from the city. It was ringing noon.

"…What did you two talk about earlier?" Trope asked again. This time she was asking about the content, not the person.

I stopped, halfway through splitting a potato in two.

"…I asked him." I said. "Whether he'd been in a city like this before."

"…What did he say?"

"…He smiled." I said. "And didn't answer."

Trope listened to that quietly.

"…That's strange." she said. "I've never seen someone smile and dodge a question at the same time."

*Hearing her say that, I felt reconfirmed in what I'd just seen — that it really had been strange. In forty days I'd watched this person never get angry. I'd watched him never smile, either. And today, just once, both of those broke at the same time. I don't think that was a coincidence. I still don't.*

"…I think it's strange too." I said. "But it's better than getting angry."

Trope laughed a little at that.

---

We sat quietly again. The sun climbed fully overhead. There was still no shade. The dry stem lay between us, casting not even a shadow.

"…What are you going to do with this now?" Trope asked, pointing at the dry stem.

"…I don't know."

"…Are you going to pull it out?"

"…No." I said. "I'll just leave it."

*I couldn't explain, then, why I answered that way. I understand a little now. I wanted to leave that stem behind as evidence. Evidence of what, I didn't know exactly. I only wanted whoever came up this hill again someday to know that there had once been shade here.*

Trope nodded. She didn't ask anything more.

"…The Adjudicator asked again." she said, a while later. "Whether you'll come back."

"…What did you say?"

"…I answered differently this time." Trope said. "I told him you'd be back soon."

I looked at her.

"…I never said that."

"…I know." she said. "But I wanted to say it."

*I had no answer to that. I still hadn't decided when I'd go down. And Trope had already decided the answer for me. I should have felt burdened by that. Strangely, I didn't.*

---

As the sun began sinking toward the west, Trope left the water jug and the remaining bread beside me and stood.

"…I'll come tomorrow too." she said.

"…You don't have to."

"…I know." she said. "But I will."

She went down the hill. I watched her back the whole way. Partway down the field path, she stopped once again, then walked on.

*I could recognize that pause now. It wasn't a pause to check anything. It was just her, taking one more moment to hold me — left behind on this hill — in her heart, before walking on.*

*I set the question he'd left me and the pause Trope had left me side by side, and looked at them together. Neither one demanded anything of me. Both were simply waiting for me to move on my own. I wondered how long that waiting could go on. I didn't know the answer. But that day, for the first time, I felt I didn't want to test how long that waiting could last, any longer.*

---

Alone again on the hill, I opened the water jug and drank. It was lukewarm. But this time I didn't want to refuse it.

The sun sank completely. Stars began rising, one by one. I sat with my back against the dry stem. I remembered counting stars in this same spot a few nights before, and giving up. I didn't count them today either.

*That night I kept going back over his smiling face. The old weariness inside that smile. I still didn't know what that weariness had endured. But since seeing it, I began to see him a little differently. Until then I had thought of him only as some strange person who had appeared in this city. Now he seemed like someone who had passed through something even before this city. What he had passed through, I still didn't know. But knowing that he had passed through something at all was enough to make me see him differently.*

*That night, for the first time, I pictured concretely the day I would go down this hill. Whether it would be days away, or tomorrow, I didn't decide. But that the day was coming — for the first time, in that moment, that felt certain.*
