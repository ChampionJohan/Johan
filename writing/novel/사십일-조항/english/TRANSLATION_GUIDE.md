# The Forty-Day Clause — Translation Guide

Book 3 of the **Loop Chronicles**. Korean source: 『사십 일 조항』.
This guide is authoritative. Where it conflicts with a translator's instinct,
the guide wins — consistency across 48 chapters matters more than any single
elegant line.

Books 1 and 2 use the same conventions; see
`../../루프-연대기/english/TRANSLATION_GUIDE.md` and
`../../울음-장부/english/TRANSLATION_GUIDE.md`.

## 4. Register and voice

- **First-person retrospective.** Pheugon narrates after the fact and knows
  how it ends. Past tense throughout.
- **Italic passages** marked `*...*` in the Korean are his later commentary —
  the "지금은 안다" voice. Keep them italic in English. They are the book's
  signature device; do not merge them into the surrounding narration.
- **Dialogue opens with an ellipsis**: `"…네."` → `"…Yes."` Keep the ellipsis
  and the space conventions exactly. Do not convert to "Well—" or drop it.
- **Short paragraphs, spare prose.** Resist the urge to smooth Korean's
  clipped sentences into flowing English periods. The bareness is the style.
- Pheugon does not weep or emote openly. His feeling shows in what he counts,
  what he avoids, what he refuses to look at. Never add emotional glossing.
- **No Scripture epigraphs** per chapter. Each chapter opens with a
  `> *이전 이야기*` recap block only → render as `> _Previously_ — ...`

---

## 5. Scripture

Use the **King James Version** only (public domain). Book 3 quotes Scripture
only indirectly; the Jonah material is woven into dialogue rather than
quoted. The one line that must echo KJV phrasing:

**Chapter 46** — `"네가 성내는 것이 옳으냐."`
→ **"Doest thou well to be angry?"**

This is Jonah 4:4 in KJV. Use it verbatim, archaic form intact. It is spoken
by the walking man as a genuine question, never a rebuke — do not add a
question-softening tag like "he asked gently."

| …너 여기 사람 아니지? | **…You're not from here, are you?** | ch.12/13 |
| 도망치다 | **to flee** | ch.44 name reveal |
| 돌이킴 | **turning** | ch.44 name reveal |
| 이 사람은 끝까지 설명하지 않았다 | **This one never explained himself.** | Mena's original line |
| 이 사람은 끝까지 뉘우치지 않았다 | **This one never repented.** | the database's misquote — note "repented" appears HERE and only here, which is the point |

contrast must be visible at a glance in English: *explained himself* vs.
*repented*.

## 8. File format

Output filenames: `NN-kebab-case-title.md`, e.g. `01-reading-aloud.md`,
`27-the-one-who-proclaimed.md`, `48-the-way-down.md`.

Each file:

```
---
title: Chapter N. English Title
series: The Forty-Day Clause, Part One · The Proclamation and the Flight
---

# Chapter N. English Title

> _Previously_ — two to four lines translated from the Korean
> `> *이전 이야기*` block.

Body, preserving every `---` scene break and every *italic* retrospective
passage.

*(Continued in Chapter N+1.)*
```

End markers:
- Chapters 1–11, 13–23, 25–35, 37–47 → `*(Continued in Chapter N+1.)*`
- Chapter 12 → `*(Continued in Part Two.)*`
- Chapter 24 → `*(Continued in Part Three.)*`
- Chapter 36 → `*(Continued in Part Four.)*`
- Chapter 48 → `*(The End.)*`

## 9. Rules that must not be broken

1. **The Scales is never wrong.** Not one verdict in the book is an error.
   Never translate in a way that hedges this ("seemed correct," "as far as
   he could tell") unless the Korean hedges.
2. **He never gets angry.** Never render the walking man's lines with
   irritation, sternness, or reproach.
3. **Never gloss the names before chapter 44.** Not in a footnote, not in a
   chapter title, not in a recap block.
4. **돌이킴 is "turning," never "repentance"** — except inside the database's
   misquotation, where "repented" is the deliberate wrong word.
5. **No declared faith.** No character says the English equivalent of
   "이제 믿습니다." The ending stays open.
6. Chapter 39's confirmation is never explained. If the Korean does not say
   why he said it, the English must not either.
