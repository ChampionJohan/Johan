# Chapter 43. Overnight

> _Previously_ — Pheugon climbed the hill east of the city and waited for it to fall. Nothing happened. He fell asleep against a cold rock, with no shade and no blanket.

It wasn't light that woke me.

It was something touching my body. Soft, and a little cold. I reached out on instinct. A leaf.

I sat up.

For the first few seconds I thought I was still dreaming. Green filled my whole field of vision. To eyes still half-asleep, I couldn't tell whether it was sky or leaves. I rubbed my eyes with the back of my hand. The green didn't go away.

*I still remember those few seconds precisely. That short gap between dream and waking, where nothing needs to be judged yet. I made no calculations at all in that gap. I just looked at the green in front of me. Looking back, those few uncalculated seconds were the cleanest moment of that whole day.*

---

Something had grown overnight.

Between the rocks, right above where I'd slept, a vine had stretched out. Not just stretched — it had climbed the rock and curved upward like an arch, its leaves spread wide. Beneath it lay a solid patch of shade, directly over the spot where I'd slept all night.

I couldn't move.

*I tried to understand what I was seeing. I couldn't. Vines don't grow like this. It normally takes months, at least a few weeks. But this had grown overnight — while I was asleep.*

The leaves were broad and thick, dark green. The stem was as thick as my forearm. Yesterday there had been nothing here but dry grass and rock. I remembered that clearly, because I'd spent the whole day before sitting right on this hilltop.

---

I searched for the vine's roots.

They began in a crack in the rock. Almost no soil there. I crouched down to look. No sign of a seed. No trace of anyone having planted it. The stem simply began in that crack in the rock. As if it had always been there.

*I looked at that root for a long time. Where this vine had come from, where its seed had blown in from, who had planted it — I never found an answer. I still don't know. But looking at that root, for a moment, an unfamiliar sense passed through me. A feeling like I'd seen something like this before. Like I'd seen something grow from a nameless seed once before. I didn't know then where that feeling came from, and I still can't explain it. I only note here that the feeling was there.*

The wind blew, and the leaves shook. Their shadow trembled on my feet along with them.

I followed the vine's whole shape with my eyes. The stem began in the rock crack and split into three branches, two of which twisted together and formed the arch overhead. The third was still short — as though it hadn't yet decided which way to grow, hanging in midair.

Among the leaves, not yet buds, small white points had formed here and there. I couldn't tell whether these were about to bloom, or just markings on the stem.

*I looked closely at those white points, and then took my hand away. Sometimes if you look at something too closely, instead of learning what it is, you end up knowing it less. I decided, that day, not to try to find out what those points were. I decided just to accept that they were there.*

I stood beneath that arch and looked toward the city. Morning sunlight came through the vine's leaves in fragments. Roofs and walls trembled beyond those fragments. Only then did I reckon that I'd been on this hill barely a day. It felt much longer. The forty days I'd spent in the city, I could count precisely, in numbers. The time on this hill kept blurring.

Dew had gathered at the edges of the leaves. It sparkled in the morning light. Thirsty, I gathered a few drops on my fingertip. Only a few. I touched them to my lips. Cold and clear.

*A few drops of dew couldn't quench my thirst. But in that moment, for the first time, I felt that this hill hadn't abandoned me completely. My stomach was still empty, my throat still dry. But something wasn't leaving me to simply pass through.*

---

I sat back down beneath that shade.

The sunlight was completely blocked now. After the sun I'd taken on my whole body the whole day before, its absence made my body strangely light. That lightness felt unfamiliar.

*I didn't know, in that moment, how to receive it. I hadn't come here to be comfortable. I had come here to be punished. And yet overnight, untouched by anyone's hand, shade had come to me.*

*At first I wanted to reject it. I even thought about going and sitting back out in the sun. But my body wouldn't move. The shade felt strangely good. Its being good made me angry.*

I touched one of the leaves. Smooth on top, a little rough underneath. Some kind of scent rubbed off on my fingertips. It was different from the smell of this hill's dry grass. I searched my memory for where I might have smelled it before. Nothing came.

*Smelling that scent, I can say now — for a moment, with no context at all, an unfamiliar image flickered past. A seed, fallen on some dry ground, a seed no one knew the name of. I have never seen a scene like that. Not in this body, at least. And yet in that instant the scene passed through me like a memory. I can't explain it. I've decided not to try. Some things stay truer left unexplained.*

I tried to hold on to that unfamiliar image. Like water slipping through a fist, it vanished faster the harder I tried to grasp it. Within a few seconds nothing remained. Only a scent, a green light, and an unexplainable sense of familiarity.

I ran my hand along the vine's stem. Each joint was a slightly different thickness. Some places had the hard grain of an old tree; others were as tender as something freshly sprouted. That all these textures had appeared overnight kept striking me as unbelievable.

*Out of trained habit, I tried to calculate the vine's age. By its thickness, the stem should have been at least a few years old. But yesterday nothing had stood here at all. The arithmetic didn't work. I had grown up counting one hundred twelve cases. Faced with arithmetic that didn't work, I didn't know what to do. In the end I gave up counting and simply felt the stem with my fingertips.*

---

Past noon, I was still beneath that shade.

My stomach was still empty. My throat still dry. But it was more bearable than yesterday. I found it strange, how much difference one patch of shade could make.

*I thought about the Scales again that day. How would it have weighed this shade? Shade itself carries no weight. It isn't stolen, it isn't a lie, it isn't neglect. Nowhere among the items the Scales measures is there a column for shade. And yet that day I felt, for the first time, how much this weightless thing was actually lifting off my body.*

*I already knew there was much the Scales couldn't measure, even inside that city. What isn't stolen, what isn't struck, what isn't lied — those too carried no weight. But that day, for the first time, I learned that what carries no weight isn't necessarily light. The shade had no weight, and it saved me.*

---

I looked down the hill again.

Far along the path between the fields, very small, I saw a human figure walking. Too far to tell who it was. I watched for a while, trying to tell whether the figure was coming this way, or just heading out to work the fields. It grew smaller and smaller, and soon vanished among the fields.

*I have to admit I felt disappointed, in that moment. I had been hoping someone would come find me. And at the same time, I had no idea what I'd say if they did. How would I explain the vine that had grown? How would I explain why I was still here? I had no explanation ready at all.*

I moved back beneath the vine. The temperature of the shade stayed strangely constant. However the sun's angle shifted, the leaves seemed to bend slightly toward it, in turn. I watched from beneath it for half a day, trying to tell whether that was real or my imagination. It wasn't imagination. The leaves really were shaping the shade to follow me.

*Watching that, I thought — so there really are things in this world that move this precisely for exactly one person. The Scales was precise too. But the Scales' precision was a precision that measured me. This shade's precision was a precision that covered me. I put that difference into words, clearly, for the first time that day.*

---

I was still waiting for the city to fall.

More precisely, using the word "waiting" had begun to feel a little embarrassing now. Yesterday's waiting had had an edge to it. Today's waiting simply remained, out of habit. I looked down at the city. Still there. Roofs, walls, smoke.

*That afternoon I had a strange thought. Who had this vine grown for? Not for the city — the city had already lived. So it had grown for me. But was I someone who deserved to receive it? Just the day before, I had climbed this hill resenting the fact that the city lived. That shade should come to someone like that — I still didn't understand it.*

*Now I know. Not understanding was normal. This shade couldn't be explained by a logic of deserving. That was the Scales' way of doing things. I already knew, in my body, that there was a way other than the Scales' way — I just hadn't yet been able to accept it in my mind.*

I reached a hand out past the shade. The sun was hot. I drew my hand back inside the shade. The coolness came immediately. I went back and forth over that line a few times. That one hand could feel heat and coolness by turns felt, strangely, like something I couldn't put down that day.

*Crossing that line back and forth, I thought of Trope. She had drawn a line between the city's survival and her own forgiveness. "It was the city that the Scales called void. Not me." I thought of that again, touching the boundary of the shade. There was a line like that too, between what lay inside this vine's shade and what lay outside. Except this line, unlike Trope's, had no condition for crossing it. You only had to move your body.*

Far off, a bird called. I listened to it for a long time. It was the first birdsong I'd heard since arriving on this hill. The bird landed briefly on a branch above the vine, looked at me, then flew off again. I followed the direction it flew with my eyes. Toward the city.

---

The sun began to set again.

I sat under the shade and greeted my second night there. This time I lay down beneath the vine's shade instead of against the rock. The leaves made a sound in the wind. Low and soft.

*Listening to that sound, I was able to fall asleep comfortably for the first time since arriving on this hill. I found that strange, and yet I didn't stop it. I was still angry. But beside that anger, something else was growing, very small. I didn't know, then, what it was.*

I closed my eyes and went back over what had happened the day before. Shouting at him. The substitute herald's even congratulations. The whole day I'd spent waiting for the city to fall. None of it was erased by this one vine. But it all began to settle at a slightly different weight. I couldn't explain why. It simply felt that way.

*I don't want to write that my anger disappeared. It didn't disappear. But that night, I learned for the first time that I could hold another feeling alongside anger at the same time. For three years I had lived as someone who allowed only one feeling at a time. Kind, or angry. Holding it in, or letting it burst. Under this shade, for the first time, I felt two things at once. Still angry, and at the same time, feeling that someone was looking after me.*

Starlight came through the leaves in fragments. I began counting the fragments, then gave up. I didn't need to count them. I only needed to look.

*I didn't know, that night, whom to thank. I didn't know who had sent this vine. And yet I said one thing, very quietly, where no one could hear.*

"…Thank you."

*Who those words were meant for, I still don't know exactly. Maybe for the vine. Maybe for whatever had made the vine exist. I was a little surprised at myself, saying it. In forty days — no, in three years — I had never once said something like that so easily.*

Before falling asleep, I ran my hand once over one of the vine's leaves. I wanted to remember that feeling. I don't know why I wanted to. I just did.

*Falling asleep that night, I didn't reckon how long this shade would last. It wasn't that I'd given up the habit of reckoning. Just that, this once, I chose not to reckon, and simply receive it instead.*
