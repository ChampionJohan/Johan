A boy whose job is to read sentences aloud is sent to proclaim the demolition of a city. He runs. Not because he is afraid — because he is afraid the city might be spared.

Pheugon has read a hundred and twelve verdicts without once stopping. The Scales that calculates them has never been wrong, not in a single case, and he has checked. Then he is handed a sentence for an entire city — the Reckoning, the city that took his family from him — and finds, in small print at the bottom of the page, a clause that has never once been invoked: if the city turns within forty days, the sentence is void.

The trouble is that the Scales weighs what people do. It cannot weigh which way they are facing.

The Forty-Day Clause is Book Three of the Loop Chronicles, a Christian fantasy series for readers who would rather be asked an honest question than handed an answer. It is about a boy who cannot forgive, a girl who cannot believe forgiveness would ever reach her, and a quiet man who knows exactly what everyone weighs and forgives first anyway.

Like the book of Jonah, it ends on a question.
