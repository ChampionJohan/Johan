# Chapter 46. A Moment Like a Father

> _Previously_ — Temel brought word from outside. Haven is still
> quiet, but more people are refusing COMPLETE. "We just ran, but
> something's growing in every place we ran through."

Autumn faded fully away. As winter drew near, a cold wind began to
blow through by the well.

Nearly all the leaves had fallen. A thin frost had begun settling on
the stone wall around the well. I picked up a new habit that autumn —
rubbing that frost with my fingertip, every morning.

Father seemed to love winter, of all things. I didn't know why. He
simply breathed the cold air in deep and wore a look of pleasure on
his face. I thought that expression was familiar. Father's face had
looked like that in winter, back in Brick Hollow, too.

I'd been getting by on one thin coat. One morning, Father took off his
own coat and wrapped it around my shoulders.

"…You looked cold," Father said. As if it were nothing at all.

I could feel Father's own warmth still left in that coat. It was warm.

Speon, watching from the side, smiled quietly. I felt like I
understood why. Speon must have seen countless small gestures like
that, here by the well.

"…Your father's come a long way," Speon said.

"…I think so too," I said. "But lately I'm not sure what 'coming a
long way' even means. He can do something like that even without all
his memory back."

Speon nodded at that.

"…Maybe that's what real recovery is," Speon said. "Not memory —
love that's stayed in the body."

I couldn't answer for a long while after hearing that. I carried that
line in my heart a long time after. I didn't know then that it would
follow me through this whole winter.

---

I couldn't say anything in that moment. It was such a small gesture.
But inside that gesture was something that had come before name or
memory.

*I understood then. Being a father isn't about remembering — it's
about noticing the cold first. Father didn't know that about himself.
But he was already doing it.*

Wrapping the coat around myself, I thought of Brick Hollow. Every
winter there, Father had always given us his own clothes first. Back
then I'd simply thought that was how things were. Now I know how far
from ordinary that was.

"…Are you cold?" I asked.

"…I'm fine," Father said, smiling — while looking cold himself.
Strangely, I was grateful for that lie.

I glanced sideways at Father while the coat sat around my shoulders.
There was faint gooseflesh on his arms. I pretended not to see it.

---

That day the wind was fierce from morning to night. Brother was out
late with Speon, working on the bucket rope. I watched them through
the window.

Brother had seen the same thing. That evening he said to me,

"…I still don't know if Father remembers me fully. But watching that
today, I understood something. He doesn't have to remember everything
completely. He's already Father."

"…How do you know that?"

"…This morning he tried to take the water jar from me before I could
lift it," Brother said. "He used to do that too, remember? Whenever
we picked up something heavy, he'd take it from us."

I nodded, hearing that. I'd seen similar moments myself, more than
once.

"…You can just tell, watching what he does," Brother said. "The
actions were Father before the words were."

Listening to that, I remembered that Brother never used to talk this
way. The old Brother had always wanted proof. This Brother was saying
that a single gesture was enough.

"…I think I feel that too," I said. "Lately there are things I
believe even without proof."

Brother glanced at me at that. He said nothing. But something in his
face seemed to agree.

The night wind slipped in through the window gap. I pulled the
blanket a little closer and sat pressed against Brother's side. He
didn't bother pushing me away.

---

Once the fire was lit, the room grew warm. That night, Father sat the
two of us down on either side of him and asked,

"…Will you tell me some old stories? About me. What kind of person I
used to be."

Brother hesitated a moment at the question, then slowly began to
talk. The days in Brick Hollow, the stories Father used to tell us
every evening, the calluses on Father's hands.

I added a few things of my own. The song Father used to hum while
carrying bricks. The way his hand would smooth our hair every night.
The face that never once showed anger, no matter how hard things got.

"…And he always went to sleep after we did," I said. "He'd check
that we were both asleep before he'd let his own eyes close."

"…That's right," Brother added. "He never once went to sleep first."

Father smiled faintly at that. It was a bashful sort of smile.

"…I think that's still true now," Father said. "Even these days, my
eyes don't close until I've seen you two asleep."

Father closed his eyes more than once, listening to us.

No one spoke for a long while. In that silence, I felt Father was
fitting the pieces of his own life together, one by one.

"…Will you tell me more?" Father asked, eyes still closed.

Brother went on with the story at that. The days they'd had to hide
from the taskmasters, and how, even so, Father's voice would tell us
old stories every evening. Listening to it, I felt as though I could
almost smell Brick Hollow again.

"…Wasn't that man frightening?" Father asked. "The taskmasters, I
mean."

"…He was," Brother said. "But it was less frightening with Father
beside us."

"…I don't remember it," Father said, "but hearing that, my heart
feels strangely at ease."

"…Why?" I asked.

"…It means I was useful," Father said. "Even back then."

Something stung at the tip of my nose, hearing that. Father still
didn't fully know how large a presence he had been to us.

"…He sounds like he was a good person," Father said. Listening to
his own story as if it belonged to someone else, there was still
something moved in his voice.

"…That person is you, Father," Brother said quietly.

Father nodded at that. Slowly, but plainly.

Father wrapped both arms around the two of us. It was a clumsy
gesture. But the strength in it was unmistakable.

"…Thank you," Father said. "For giving my own story back to me."

Tears welled in my eyes at that. Brother must have felt the same,
because he buried his face against Father's shoulder.

In that moment I thought of the last night in Brick Hollow, of the
way Father had held us that final time. That moment and this one
seemed to overlap. Different place, different season — and yet the
same warmth.

The fire slowly burned down. We stayed like that a while, holding one
another. No one moved first.

*I set down that line as the last sentence of this chapter. I
understood, that day, that Father was learning his own story all over
again. And the two people who knew that story best were the ones
sitting right beside him now.*
