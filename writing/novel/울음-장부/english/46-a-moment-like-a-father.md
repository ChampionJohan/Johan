# Chapter 46. A Moment Like a Father

> _Previously_ — Temel brought word from outside. Haven is still quiet, but more people are refusing COMPLETE. "We just ran, but something's growing in every place we ran through."

As winter drew near, a cold wind began to blow through by the well.

I'd been getting by on one thin coat. One morning, Father took off his own coat and wrapped it around my shoulders.

"…You looked cold," Father said. As if it were nothing at all.

Speon, watching from the side, smiled quietly. I felt like I understood why. Speon must have seen countless small gestures like that, here by the well.

---

I couldn't say anything in that moment. It was such a small gesture. But inside that gesture was something that had come before name or memory.

*I understood then. Being a father isn't about remembering — it's about noticing the cold first. Father didn't know that about himself. But he was already doing it.*

"…Are you cold?" I asked.

"…I'm fine," Father said, smiling — while looking cold himself. Strangely, I was grateful for that lie.

---

Brother saw the scene too. That evening, he said to me,

"…I still don't know if Father remembers me fully. But watching that today, I understood something. He doesn't have to remember everything completely. He's already Father."

"…How do you know that?"

"…You just have to watch what he does," Brother said. "The actions were Father before the words were."

---

That night, Father sat the two of us down on either side of him and asked,

"…Will you tell me some old stories? About me. What kind of person I used to be."

Brother hesitated a moment at the question, then slowly began to talk. The days in Brick Hollow, the stories Father used to tell us every evening, the calluses on Father's hands.

Father closed his eyes more than once, listening.

"…He sounds like he was a good person," Father said. Listening to his own story as if it belonged to someone else, there was still something moved in his voice.

*I set down that line as the last sentence of this chapter. I understood, that day, that Father was learning his own story all over again. And the two people who knew that story best were the ones sitting right beside him now.*
