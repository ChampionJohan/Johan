# Chapter 13. The First Day in the Wilderness

> _Previously_ — Marked as candidates for Reassignment, the brother and sister left Haven and crossed the boundary stones. Realizing that Haven, too, had only been another kind of fence, they set out walking into the unshaped fields beyond. "This fear is mine. No one gets to resolve it for me."

From that day on, we had to learn all over again what hunger was.

In Haven, the moment you thought you were hungry, bread was already set before you. Not here. Here your stomach just stayed hungry.

The ground underfoot was different too. Haven's paths were always even stone. Here it was rough and uneven. I stumbled more than once, and every time, my brother reached out without a word and caught me.

The sun was already high. The shadows were growing shorter. That day I learned for the first time how to judge the time by the length of a shadow. In Haven I never had to count time on my own — the Ledger always told us.

*I felt something strange then. The hunger itself was exactly the same as in Brick Hollow. But the way we had to bear it was different. In Brick Hollow, hunger came together with the whip. Here, hunger was only hunger. No one was handing it to us as punishment.*

As I walked, I touched the back of my hand. The scar from Brick Hollow was still there. While I was in Haven I had forgotten about it — too caught up in everything the Ledger gave us. Only after coming out into the Wilderness did the scar come back into my sight.

*Looking at the scar, I had a strange thought. This body remembered Brick Hollow. However perfect Haven was, it had never been able to erase even this much.*

Even as he walked, my brother kept scanning in every direction. I could tell it had become a habit. In Haven no one ever looked around like that. Here, it would have been strange not to.

"…Scared?" my brother asked.

"…Yes," I said. "But strangely, I don't hate it."

He looked at me for a moment at that. His face said he understood something.

"…Me too," he said after a while. "Maybe it's because, scared as I am, this fear is mine."

Hearing that, I looked at his face again. In Haven, his face had always looked like it was enduring something. Now it was just his face.

---

We went looking through the field for anything we could eat. My brother dug up some kind of root and chewed it.

"…Bitter," he said, wincing. "But not so bad you can't eat it."

I chewed some too. It really was bitter. But we laughed. We hadn't laughed much back in Haven.

"…Why is this funny?" I asked.

"…I don't know," he said. "It's just — we found this ourselves. Maybe that's why."

*Hearing that, I understood something for the first time. The bread the Ledger gave us had been perfect, but it had never been ours. This bitter root was wretched, but it was ours.*

After that, he pulled up a few more kinds of plants and smelled each one. Some he threw away right away. Others he tucked carefully into the fold of his shirt.

"…How do you tell them apart?" I asked.

"…I don't know," he admitted. "I'm just going by feel. I could be wrong."

Strangely, that answer felt more reassuring than frightening. In Haven, no one ever said "I could be wrong." The Ledger was always right.

We sat for a while, sharing the root between us. My stomach was still empty. But strangely, I liked that moment. My brother and I, both chewing the same thing, wearing the same face.

Far off, I heard a bird call. I was hearing that sound for the first time. Haven had no birds. Or maybe it did — I couldn't say. But I had no memory of ever having heard birdsong there.

"…What kind of bird is that?" I asked.

"…I don't know either," he said. "But you don't have to know its name. If it sounds good, that's enough."

I liked that thought. That you could like something without knowing its name. That was an idea I had never learned in Haven.

"…Do you think Father ever ate something like this?" I asked.

He was quiet for a moment before he answered.

"…I don't know. But if he's alive somewhere, I think he's probably about as hungry as I am right now."

*Hearing that, I imagined Father through the feeling of hunger for the first time. Strangely, even not knowing the answer, it felt like it brought me a little closer to him.*

We sat there a good while longer. Every time the wind blew, the dry grass made a rustling sound. I listened to it quietly. In Haven, even when the wind blew, it made no sound. It was as if every wall had something in it that swallowed sound.

*I learned then, for the first time, that there were kinds of sound. Sound that got swallowed, and sound that simply rang out. I liked the ringing kind better.*

---

Toward evening, we saw smoke far off in the distance. A sign that someone lived there.

"…Should we go look?" I asked.

He hesitated for a moment.

"…Carefully," he said. "It could be another place like Haven."

We crept carefully toward the smoke. There were a few small tents. People sat around a fire. There were children among them, and old people too.

*Seeing that, I felt strangely at ease. Every face there was different. Some people were laughing, some were crying. It was a sight I had never once seen in Haven.*

My brother slowed and stepped in front of me.

"…Stay behind me," he said.

I obediently fell in behind him. But his own steps, I noticed, were trembling a little too. I understood that he was frightened and still leading the way.

---

A few of the tents were worn and patched here and there. Looking at the mended places, I understood that these people had held on here a long time. Haven's buildings had always looked new. There was never a chance for anything to wear out before it was fixed.

A woman noticed us first.

"…Who are you?" she asked. Her eyes were wary, but not hostile.

"…We're lost," my brother said. "Could you spare us something to eat?"

The woman studied us for a moment, then waved us toward the fire.

"…Come sit. There isn't much, but we can share."

The people sitting around the fire saw us and shifted, each making a little more room. No one said much of anything. They simply moved their bodies to the side and made a space.

*I still haven't forgotten that small movement. In Haven, no one ever had to make room. There was always plenty of space. These people, with space that was not plentiful, narrowed it anyway to make some for us.*

*Hearing that, I felt something strange. That phrase — "there isn't much." In Haven I had never once heard the word "not enough." The Ledger was always sufficient. But in the not-enoughness of these people, there was something the Ledger's sufficiency never had.*

What the woman gave us was one piece of dry bread and a mouthful of water. Truly not much. But my brother's hand trembled a little as he took it. I understood that wasn't only from hunger.

"…Thank you," he said. His voice was low.

"…It's nothing," the woman said. "Everyone comes to us like that at first."

The firelight flickered over their faces. I looked at each one in turn. Some faces were tired, and some, like mine, were studying something unfamiliar. But no one pushed us away.

What exactly that something was, I did not yet know that night.
