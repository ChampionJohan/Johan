# Chapter 37. Days by the Well

> _Previously_ — Sensing they needed a place to stand rather than an answer to hold, the two of them went back to Speon's well and settled there. Elpis planted the seed again, and Father said, "I know it's planted."

That was how we began to live by the well.

The days weren't very different from one another. In the morning we drew water, walked with Father, and in the evening I set down the record. At first the sameness felt strange to me. Haven, and the road we'd run on before that — those had always been time spent being chased by something.

*That was the first time I understood how precious an ordinary day could be. How rare a gift a day with nothing happening in it was — I hadn't known that, in Brick Hollow.*

Father got a little better, bit by bit. He still didn't know Brother's name. But during the day he started trying to do things himself, for short stretches. Drawing water. Moving stones. Without anyone telling him to.

"…It's easier when I'm doing something," Father said. "When my body's moving."

*Hearing that, I thought of Father in Brick Hollow. Those hands had always been moving too. I felt, then, that something inside Father was coming back before his name was.*

---

Brother started helping Speon tend the well. He repaired the bucket, repaired the stone wall, and at night took turns keeping watch with Speon.

"…Why are you helping with this?" I asked.

"…Speon did this alone for a long time," Brother said. "I think I get it now. Why waiting can become a job."

*Hearing that, I knew Brother had started to understand Speon's life. It wasn't an understanding he'd gotten from an answer. It was an understanding he'd gotten from doing the work alongside him.*

Speon, while teaching Brother how to repair the bucket, would sometimes let slip a little of his own past. How once, out of fear, he'd run and left someone behind. Brother didn't press him about it. He just listened.

"…Why don't you ask more?" Speon asked.

"…I've learned lately that it's better to talk about something when you want to," Brother said.

---

One night, there was a sound at the well.

The four of us ran to it at the same time. A woman had just come up out of the bottom of the well and was shaking. Her eyes had nothing in them.

Brother was the first to go to her.

"…It's all right." Brother said it in the same voice we'd once heard from Father. "Breathe slowly. You're safe here."

*Watching that, my breath caught. Brother was standing, now, most calmly of all of us, in the exact place he'd been most afraid of only months before.*

---

The woman stayed by the well for a few days, and then remembered part of her name. Not all of it. But even with just that much, she wept and laughed.

That night, Brother said to me,

"…I still don't know who that man is. I can't even say I believe in God. But watching that woman today, I knew something. I know how to be beside someone now. That much is certain."

*I set that down as the last sentence of this chapter. I knew Brother still couldn't use the word faith. But he was already doing what faith does. I decided not to think that order was strange.*
