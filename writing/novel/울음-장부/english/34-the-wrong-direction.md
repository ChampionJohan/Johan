# Chapter 34. The Wrong Direction

> _Previously_ — A Haven search party actually came after the siblings. After the danger passed, Jeteo asked the night sky, "You don't show up for this, but you showed up back then?" — but only silence answered.

The next day, we started walking again, as quietly as we could.

We'd all barely slept, and it showed in our hollow eyes. I flinched at every footstep sound the whole way. Even the snap of a twig made me jump, more than once.

The sky was just as clear as the day before. That clarity felt strangely foreign to me. The world went on as if it had no idea at all what had happened to us the night before.

*For the first time, I thought about the world's indifference. Even the night we held our breath and came so close to something like death — to the sky, it was just one more night passing. That indifference felt unfair to me, and at the same time, strangely comforting. Because it also meant the world wasn't singling us out.*

Jeteo barely spoke all that day. I didn't want to disturb him, so I just walked beside him.

*I understood that his silence was tied to last night's question. I learned, that day, that a question with no answer stays on as silence, in place of an answer.*

Every so often I saw Jeteo glance up at the sky. He looked like he was searching for something, and also like he was resenting something. I didn't ask.

Father was unusually bright that day, of all days. I didn't know why. He just said he liked walking. I envied that brightness. Since Father remembered nothing, by morning even last night's terror seemed almost entirely forgotten.

Father found a small insect by the road and crouched there watching it for a long while. That sight struck me as strange, and, somewhere in it, lovely too. Father looked like someone entirely present in this one moment.

Jeteo watched him from a little way off, then said to me, quietly,

"…Father seems a lot freer than we are," Jeteo said. "Because he has no memory."

I didn't know how to answer that. His words felt both right and wrong to me.

*For the first time, I thought forgetting might not only be a curse. It was a strange thought. I didn't hold on to it for long.*

As he walked, Father tapped stones along the road with the toe of his shoe. A childlike gesture. Watching it, I'd laugh, and then, right after, some corner of my heart would ache.

I didn't understand that ache until late that evening. Father wasn't becoming more and more of a stranger. He was growing up again, alongside us. It felt strange to me that both of those things could be happening at once.

---

In the afternoon, we met a merchant coming from the opposite direction. He spoke to us first.

He carried a large pack on his back — the kind of man who sold goods, moving from one area to another. I could see, in his clothes, the fatigue of a long-traveling life.

"…This area was in an uproar last night," the merchant said. "I heard the search party was chasing someone and ended up completely off track."

"…Off track?" Jeteo asked, ears pricking up.

His voice cracked, faintly. I knew that hearing this, he'd gone straight back to last night.

"…Yeah. Some old man supposedly pointed them the wrong way. And when they went to look for him afterward, he'd disappeared too, they say. It's going around as a strange story among their people."

*The moment I heard that, I looked at Jeteo. He looked at me too. Neither of us said anything, but I knew we were thinking the same thing.*

The merchant, not noticing the change in our faces, went on talking.

"…Funny thing is, there wasn't a single trace where the old man vanished. Not even a footprint." The merchant shrugged. "People are saying all kinds of things about it."

"…What did the old man look like?" Jeteo asked, unable to hold back.

The merchant shrugged.

"…I only heard it secondhand, so I don't know. Just that he was wearing worn-out clothes, that's all I heard."

That description was so plain it made me even more certain. Nothing flashy, nothing special about it. It was exactly how the man we knew looked.

*That answer alone was enough. I could see in Jeteo's face that he thought so too. For the first time, I felt some corner of my heart warm at the thought of that man in worn clothes, moving on our behalf. I learned, that day, that fear and gratitude could live in the same place at once.*

Before the merchant left, he added one more thing, to us.

"…You take care too. People around here have been on edge lately."

We nodded. That parting word didn't sound like something tossed off in passing to me — it sounded like real concern.

---

After the merchant left, Jeteo was quiet for a long time before he spoke.

"…Was it him?"

His voice held both certainty and resistance at once. I felt that he wanted to admit it, and at the same time, feared that admitting it might change something.

"…I don't know," I said. But in my heart, I was already almost certain.

"…Then why doesn't he just say so from the start?" Jeteo asked, his voice mixed with frustration. "Then I wouldn't have to keep getting angry like this, every time."

*I didn't know the answer to that. But a strange thought came to me, then. Maybe telling us ahead of time would be worth less than walking through the fear together.*

"…And," Jeteo added, low, "being angry is exhausting too. But if I don't even get angry, I don't know what I'm supposed to do instead."

I couldn't answer that. I just walked quietly beside him. I learned again, that day, that some silences are better than any answer.

---

Father, listening quietly to the conversation, asked,

"…Who is he?"

Jeteo couldn't answer the question right away. After a long while, he said,

"…I don't really know either," Jeteo said. "But… it seems like he's always around us somehow. Without ever explaining."

He looked startled by his own words, even as he said them. That was the first time I'd heard him admit it like that.

Father gave a small laugh at that.

"…Then he's a bit like me," Father said. "I can't explain who I am either, and yet here I am."

Jeteo stopped, hearing that. I watched him start to say something and swallow it back.

"…Yeah," Jeteo said at last. His voice was low. "A bit like you."

Father, hearing that answer, gave Jeteo's shoulder a light pat. He didn't say anything more. But that one gesture was enough for the moment.

In that short answer, I felt that Jeteo had set something down, a little. He hadn't let go of the anger entirely. But it seemed like some other place had opened up beside it.

We walked late into that evening. We didn't stop until the sun had gone all the way down. That night, I noticed Jeteo spent a little longer looking up at the sky than he had before.

*I set down those words as the last line of this chapter. In that moment I saw an expression on Jeteo's face I'd never seen before. It wasn't anger. It wasn't certainty either. It was simply the face of someone who had opened his heart, just a little.*
