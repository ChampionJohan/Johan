# Chapter 12. Outside Haven

> _Previously_ — Jeteo and Elpis were both classified as candidates for Reassignment. The two of them decided not to stop asking, and left Haven by the unwatched southern road the man at the flowerbed had shown them. He left them with these words: "I'm staying here. But that doesn't mean you're going alone."

We walked all through the night.

The dirt underfoot was nothing like Haven's straight, even streets. It was rough and uneven, and now and then a stone snagged my foot. Strangely, I welcomed that unevenness. It meant this road had never been smoothed by anyone.

There was no one else on that road. It was a different quiet from Haven's. That city's quiet had felt like something someone had ordered into being. This road's quiet was simply the quiet of no one being there.

*I kept thinking about that difference as we walked. That was the first time I understood there were different kinds of quiet.*

Every time the wind blew, dry grass rustled. Even that sound was welcome to me. In Haven, even the wind blew quietly, carefully, so as not to disturb anyone. This wind blew however it pleased.

I stumbled and nearly fell more than a few times as we walked. Every time, my brother caught my arm. We kept each other up like that, again and again, the whole way.

Once, we stopped for just a moment and shared what water we had. The canteen was small. I noticed my brother pouring more into my cup than his own. He said he hadn't, but I knew better.

Once, my brother looked back. I turned and looked too. Haven's lights still burned, straight and even, far behind us. The Administrator, that old woman — they'd still be there.

I wondered whether the old woman was sitting on that bench today too, looking at the flowerbed. I wondered whether she'd notice the empty seat beside her.

"…Do you think the Administrator will be all right?" I asked.

"…I don't know," my brother said. "He seemed trapped in that city too. Like us."

*Hearing that, I prayed for him for the first time. I didn't know exactly what I was praying toward. But I know that, in that moment, I prayed.*

My brother closed his eyes for a moment too. I didn't ask what he was doing. But I could tell he was doing something like what I was doing.

As I walked, I kept thinking of what the man at the flowerbed had said. That we weren't going alone. I couldn't begin to guess, then, what those words meant.

---

Just before dawn, we reached a low pile of stones.

The sky was growing bright, little by little. In that faint light I made out the shape of the stones. My heart suddenly began pounding hard.

In that same light, I could finally see my brother's face clearly too. There were dark rings under his eyes. A face that hadn't slept a wink all night. Mine must have looked the same.

I stopped walking at the sight of that shape. It looked exactly like the boundary stones of Brick Hollow.

My brother stopped there too. I saw, in his face, that he'd recognized the same thing.

"…Are these boundary stones too?" I asked.

My brother touched the stone.

"…Looks like it," my brother said. "So Haven was inside some boundary too. We didn't leave one boundary behind us at all. We just crossed one boundary into another."

"…And if we cross this one?" I asked.

"…Somewhere else again, I'd guess," my brother said. "But this time, we're the ones choosing to cross it. That's the difference."

*I understood something strange, hearing that. We'd thought we "left" Brick Hollow, all this time. But Haven had been a fence too. How many boundary stones are there in this world? I didn't know the answer, then.*

My brother tapped my shoulder lightly. "…Let's go," he said. His voice was steadier than before.

I laid my hand on the stone. It was cold. I couldn't quite remember whether Brick Hollow's boundary stones had been this cold too. That fading memory made me sad.

I found myself trying to recall Father's hand, in that moment. The warmth of it, the exact place of the calluses — all of it had gone hazy now. That frightened me. I hadn't known forgetting could come this quietly.

---

We crossed the boundary stones, and the sun came up.

Light poured in all at once. I raised a hand to shield my eyes for a moment against the glare. Haven's light had always been constant. This light kept changing, moment to moment.

A wide field opened before us. It was nothing like Haven's straight streets. The grass grew however it pleased. Some tall, some short. Nothing looked as though anyone had ever tended it.

I smelled grass. It had been a long time since I'd smelled anything like it. Not brick dust, not Haven's clean scent. Just the smell of something alive.

*Looking at that grass, I felt, strangely, like I might cry. I understood, then, for the first time, that a thing left untended could itself be proof of being alive.*

I sat down right there for a moment. My legs had given out a little, and I also wanted to see that field a little closer. When I touched it with my hand, it felt rough and prickly.

My brother stood in the middle of the field and slowly turned in a full circle. I thought he was searching for something. But he was only looking.

"…Brother, what are you doing?" I asked.

"…Nothing," my brother said. "It's just been a while since I've seen something like this."

I understood what he meant. There had been no field like this, not in Brick Hollow, not in Haven.

I took the flowerbed leaf out of my pocket and looked at it. It hadn't wilted. I pressed it back into the earth of the field. It had no roots. But I wanted to do it anyway.

I couldn't know whether that leaf could take root in this soil. But strangely, once I'd planted it there, my mind was at ease.

Far off, at the edge of the field, something like a mountain came into view. I couldn't be sure whether it was truly a mountain or something else. Low clouds hung above it.

That mountain was a color I'd never once seen in Haven. Deep blue mixed with gray. That color frightened me, and it was beautiful too.

"…Should we head that way?" my brother said, pointing.

"…We don't know what's there," I said.

"…That's exactly why we go," my brother said. There was a playfulness in his voice I hadn't heard in a long time.

"…And if there's nothing?" I asked.

"…If there's nothing, we just keep walking somewhere else," my brother said. I liked that answer. It sounded like it meant it was fine not to have the right answer.

We started walking toward that mountain. I didn't look back. This time, there was no reason to.

---

My brother sat down beside me. We settled onto a broad, flat rock in the middle of the field. The sun was fully up now.

"…Where do we go now?" I asked.

"…I don't know," my brother said. "But at least now, where we go is something we get to decide. In Haven, the Ledger decided everything for us."

*Hearing that, I finally understood, for the first time, what Haven's peace had cost. Not having to choose. But also — not being allowed to choose.*

"…Are you scared?"

"…I'm scared," my brother said. "But this fear… it's mine. No one's going to say they'll resolve it for me."

"…I'm scared too," I said. "But hearing you say that, I think I understand it a little."

"…Understand what?" my brother asked.

"…Why this fear is different," I said.

My brother nodded quietly at that.

"…Why is that a good thing?" I asked.

"…It's not that it's good," my brother said. "But at least it's real. Haven's peace was fake. This fear is real. I think I'd rather have a real fear than a fake peace."

My brother looked up at the sky after saying that. Not a single cloud in it. I thought he was searching that sky for something.

I turned that thought over in my mind a long while. I didn't fully agree with it. But I couldn't find a way to argue against it either.

I moved a little closer to my brother's side. He leaned toward me too, just a little.

The wind blew. All the grass lay down at once, and rose again. I watched that movement for a long time. My brother was watching the same thing.

*I set this down as the last line of this Part. We had gotten free of Haven. We still hadn't found Father, and we still didn't know what the COMPLETE stamp meant. Not one answer had come to us. And yet that morning, for the first time since leaving Brick Hollow, I saw something in my brother's face standing alongside the fear. I didn't know, then, that its name was hope.*
