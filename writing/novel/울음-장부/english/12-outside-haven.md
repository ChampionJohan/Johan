# Chapter 12. Outside Haven

> _Previously_ — Jeteo and Elpis were both classified as candidates for Reassignment. They decided not to stop asking questions, and left Haven by the unwatched southern road the man at the flowerbed had told them about. He had left them with these words: "I'll be here. But that doesn't mean you go alone."

We walked all through the night.

The ground under our feet was nothing like Haven's even paths. It was rough, and now and then a stone caught at my foot. Strangely, I was glad of the discomfort. It meant no one had smoothed this road for anyone.

There was no one else on it. It was quiet in a different way than Haven was quiet. That city's quiet had always felt like something someone had ordered into being. This road's quiet was just quiet, because no one was there.

*I kept turning that difference over as I walked. I understood then, for the first time, that there was more than one kind of quiet. One kind erased people. The other was simply a quiet where nothing was happening.*

Every time the wind blew, the dry grass made a sound. I was glad even of that. In Haven the wind itself had always blown quietly, carefully, as if it didn't want to be in anyone's way. This wind blew however it pleased.

The night air was cold. I tucked my hands into my sleeves as we walked. My brother saw and tried to take off his coat for me. I wouldn't take it. He was cold too. I knew that.

For a while I kept thinking I heard something behind us, and kept looking back. There was never anything there. I looked back anyway, again and again.

"…No one's following us," my brother said.

"…How do you know?"

"…This city doesn't chase people," he said. "It doesn't need to. People walk in on their own."

I found that both frightening and, somehow, a relief.

The sound of our two sets of footsteps was a comfort to me all that night. When one quickened, the other quickened with it. When one slowed, the other slowed too. Listening to that rhythm, I forgot to be afraid, a little.

I even tried breathing to that rhythm. Matching my breath to my brother's steps made it strangely easier. I didn't know what made that true. But that night, it was small things like that which carried me through.

I stumbled more than once and nearly fell. Each time, my brother caught my arm. We walked that whole way holding on to each other, over and over.

We stopped once, only for a moment, to share the water. The bottle was small. I saw my brother pour more into my cup than his own. He said he hadn't. I knew better.

I weighed the bottle in my hand. Less than half was left. I didn't tell him that. He already knew.

Once, my brother looked back. I turned and looked too. Haven's lights still burned, straight and even, far behind us. The Administrator would still be there. So would the old woman.

I wondered whether she was sitting on that bench today too, looking at the flowerbed. I wondered whether she'd notice the empty seat beside her.

It struck me as strange that the farther those lights fell behind, the more beautiful they looked. From inside, they had never looked beautiful at all.

"…Do you think the Administrator's all right?" I asked.

"…I don't know," my brother said. "He seemed like someone trapped in that city too. Like us."

*Hearing that, I prayed for him for the first time. I didn't know exactly what I was praying toward. But I prayed, right then.*

My brother closed his eyes for a moment too. I didn't ask what he was doing. But I could tell he was doing something close to the same thing.

As we walked, I kept thinking about what the man at the flowerbed had said. That we weren't going alone. I couldn't begin to guess, then, what that meant.

"…Remember the day we crossed the boundary stone at Brick Hollow?" I asked.

"…I remember," he said.

"…I was really scared that time. I'm less scared today."

"…Why?"

"…That time we were running away. Today we're going somewhere," I said.

He looked at me for a moment at that. Then he nodded, without saying anything.

*I understood that difference for the first time that night. Running has a behind to it. Going has an ahead. In Brick Hollow, we ran. From Haven, we went.*

---

Just before dawn, we came to a low heap of stones.

The sky was brightening by degrees. In that thin light I made out the shape of the rock. My heart lurched.

In that same light I saw my brother's face clearly for the first time. Dark under his eyes. A face that hadn't slept at all. Mine was probably the same.

I stopped walking at the sight of the shape. It looked exactly like the boundary stone at Brick Hollow.

My brother stopped too, in the same spot. I saw on his face that he'd recognized the same thing.

"…Is this a boundary stone too?" I asked.

He touched the rock.

"…Looks like it," he said. "Haven was inside some boundary too, then. We didn't leave a boundary behind. We just crossed one, into another."

"…So is there nowhere without a boundary?" I asked.

"…I don't know," he said. "But if there is one, I don't think it would be a place no one guards. I think it would be a place that doesn't need guarding."

"…So what happens when we cross this one?"

"…Somewhere else again, probably," he said. "But this time we're the ones choosing to cross it. That's what's different."

*Hearing that, I realized something strange. All this time I'd thought we'd "left" Brick Hollow. But Haven had been a fence too. How many boundary stones were there, in this world? I didn't know the answer then.*

*Reading this chapter over again, I want to add one thing. For a long time afterward, whenever we reached somewhere new, I got in the habit of looking for the boundary stone first. And every time, I'd touch the stone and ask myself the same question. This time, where does the fence end?*

My brother tapped my shoulder. "…Let's go," he said. His voice was steadier than before.

I laid my hand on the stone. It was cold. I couldn't quite remember whether the boundary stone at Brick Hollow had been this cold. It saddened me, that the memory had already gone blurry.

My brother kept his hand on the stone a long time too. I didn't ask what he was doing.

"…Do you think Father ever stood in front of a stone like this?" he said.

"…He must have," I said. "But he couldn't have crossed it."

At that, my brother drew his hand back.

In that moment I tried to picture Father's hand. The warmth of it, where the calluses were. Both had already gone hazy. That frightened me. I hadn't known forgetting could come this quietly.

---

The moment we crossed the boundary stone, the sun came up.

Light poured in all at once. I threw a hand up over my eyes against the glare. Haven's light had always been constant, unchanging. This light was different from one instant to the next.

A wide field opened out ahead of us. Nothing like Haven's straight, tidy roads. The grass grew however it wanted. Some of it tall, some short. It looked as though no one had ever trimmed it.

Dew sat on every blade, and each drop caught the sunlight differently. I noticed that every glint pointed a different way. Haven's light had always shone in exactly one direction.

I could smell the grass. It had been so long since I'd smelled anything like it. Not brick dust, not Haven's clean, scentless air. Just the smell of something alive.

*Looking at that grass, I felt strangely close to tears. I understood then, for the first time, that what hadn't been trimmed and ordered might be exactly the proof that it was alive.*

I sank down where I stood, partly because my legs had given out, and partly because I wanted to see that grass up close. When I touched it, it was rough under my fingers.

Just then something burst up out of the grass in a flurry of wings. I flinched back. A bird. Small and brown.

Only then did I realize I'd never once seen a bird in Haven. That city's sky had always been empty. It had never even occurred to me to find that strange.

"…Brother, a bird," I said.

"…Yeah," he said. He was following it with his eyes too.

The bird filed no request with anyone. It simply flew. I found that, absurdly, wonderful.

I watched the direction it went for a long time. It didn't seem to have any plan at all. It just flew wherever it wanted to fly. I envied it that freedom.

There was the sound of insects moving through the grass too. Small, broken-off sounds. I sat still a long while just to listen to them. Only then did I understand how large and heavy Haven's silence had actually been.

My brother stood in the middle of the field and slowly turned in a circle. I thought he was looking for something. But he was only looking.

"…Brother, what are you doing?" I asked.

"…Nothing," he said. "It's just been a long time since I've seen something like this."

I thought I understood what he meant. There had been no field like this in Brick Hollow, or in Haven either.

I took the flowerbed leaf out of my pocket. It hadn't wilted. I planted it back in the field's soil. It had no roots. But I wanted to do it anyway.

I didn't know whether that leaf could take root in this ground. But strangely, once I'd set it there, something in me settled.

"…That's not going to live," my brother said. Not scolding — just saying it.

"…I know," I said.

"…Then why plant it?"

I thought about it for a moment.

"…It seemed too strange to leave nothing behind," I said. "This isn't something I left behind, though. It's something I brought. So I'm leaving what I brought, here."

My brother said nothing to that. But when we started walking again, he looked back at that spot once more.

Far off, at the edge of the field, something like a mountain rose up. I couldn't tell for certain whether it was a mountain or something else. Clouds hung low over it.

It was a color I'd never once seen in Haven. Deep blue mixed with gray. I found that color frightening and beautiful, both at once.

"…Should we head that way?" my brother said, pointing.

"…There might not be anything there," I said.

"…That's exactly why we go," he said. There was mischief in his voice, for the first time in a long while.

"…What if there's nothing?" I asked.

"…Then we just keep walking somewhere else," he said. I liked that answer. It sounded like it meant it was all right not to have the right one.

We started walking toward that mountain. I didn't look back. This time, there was no reason to.

---

My brother sat down beside me. We settled onto a flat rock in the middle of the field. The sun was fully up by then.

"…Where do we go now?" I asked.

"…I don't know," he said. "But at least now, where we go is something we get to decide. In Haven, the Ledger decided everything for us."

*Hearing that, I understood for the first time what Haven's peace had cost. There had been no need to choose anything. But that also meant we hadn't been able to.*

"…Are you scared?"

"…I'm scared," he said. "But this fear is… mine. No one's going to step in and resolve it for me."

"…I'm scared too," I said. "But hearing you say that, I think I understand it a little now."

"…Understand what?" he asked.

"…Why this fear is different," I said.

He nodded quietly at that.

We didn't say anything for a while. The sun kept climbing. I could feel the rock slowly warming beneath us.

"…Why is that a good thing?" I asked.

"…It's not that it's good," he said. "But at least it's real. Haven's peace was fake. This fear is real. I think I'd rather have a real fear than a fake peace."

He said that, then looked up at the sky. Not a single cloud. I thought he was searching for something up there.

"…What are you looking at?" I asked.

"…Nothing," he said. Then, after a moment, he added, "…But every time I see something like this, it makes me a little angry."

"…Why?"

"…If something out there knows how to make this," he said, "then why did it leave Brick Hollow the way it was?"

I couldn't find anything to say to that. I just sat there beside him.

*I've come back to that moment many times since. I had no answer for him then. But from that day on, I came to understand something. Having no answer to that question was never the failure. The failure was letting no one sit beside it.*

I turned his words over for a long time. I didn't fully agree. But I couldn't find anything to argue back with either.

I moved a little closer to him. He leaned toward me too, just slightly.

"…Brother," I asked. "What do you think that man meant?"

"…That we're not going alone?"

"…Yeah."

He shrugged.

"…I don't know," he said. "Maybe it was just something to say, to comfort us."

"…I don't think it was that," I said.

"…Why not?"

"…Something meant to comfort you usually comes out softer," I said. "That wasn't soft. It sounded like he was just telling us a fact."

He didn't answer for a long while.

"…Then what's supposed to be coming with us?" he finally said. He wasn't mocking me. He sounded like he actually wanted to know.

"…I don't know either," I said.

*I think that was the most honest answer I had, at the time. I didn't know. But I waited anyway, not knowing. I think that was the only thing I knew how to do.*

The wind blew. All the grass lay flat at once, then rose again. I watched that movement for a long time. My brother was watching the same thing.

"…Brother," I said. "Are we going the right way?"

He took my hand. "…I don't know," he said. "But at least we're going together."

I didn't think that was a perfect answer. But that morning, it was enough.

*I set those words down as the last line of this Part. We had gotten out of Haven. We still hadn't found Father, and we still didn't know what the COMPLETE stamp meant. Not one answer had come to us. But that morning, for the first time since we'd left Brick Hollow, I saw something in my brother's face standing alongside the fear. I didn't know then that its name was hope.*
