# Chapter 38. Mena Came

> _Previously_ — Life settled in at the well. Helping Speon tend it, my
> brother learned how to be beside someone. "I can't say I believe in
> God. But I know how to be beside someone."

About a month in, one day, a familiar figure came walking from far off.

It was Mena. She had her record box slung over her shoulder, the same
as always.

The record box had grown much thicker than before. Paper stuck out past
its edges. Mena's shoes were caked with mud. It showed, how far she'd
walked.

The wind stirred the grass around the well lightly. I was the first to
see a shadow appear beyond that grass. At first I didn't know who it
was. Only once the gait grew familiar did I know it was Mena.

*I ran the moment I saw her. Mena remembered us. And then she saw Father
and stopped walking.*

"…Did you come to bring news to Speon?" I asked.

"…That too," Mena said. Her face colored a little. "And I wanted news
of you all."

"…How long did it take you to get here?" I asked.

"…About ten days," Mena said. "I had to stop by a few places along the
way. But I didn't feel tired. I was curious what I'd find here."

"…What did you think you'd find?" I asked.

Mena thought for a moment.

"…I don't know," Mena said. "I just wanted to see with my own eyes how
you all were living here. There's something a written name alone
doesn't give you."

I didn't understand right away what she meant. But over the next few
days, I came to understand. For Mena, a name was only a beginning.
Seeing how that name went on living — that was what Mena really wanted
to do.

I took Mena's hand. Her fingertips were cracked. I could guess, from
that, how many names she had written on her way here.

---

"…Is this…" Mena couldn't finish.

"…Yes," my brother said. "We found him. His name hasn't all come back
yet."

Mena's eyes filled with tears. I had never seen Mena show emotion like
this before. She had always been someone who wrote names down calmly. In
that moment, for the first time, I understood that calm hadn't been
indifference.

Mena carefully took Father's hand. Father didn't pull away. Even though
it was an unfamiliar hand, he stayed still.

"…It's very rare for one of the names I've recorded to be met again like
this," Mena said. "Thank you, for not giving up."

"…It's thanks to you, Mena," my brother said. "If you hadn't recorded
his name, we would have kept wandering for nothing."

Mena shook her head.

"…I only wrote it down," Mena said. "You're the ones who walked here."

My brother shook his head at that.

"…If you hadn't written it down, we wouldn't even have known where to
go," my brother said. "I don't think that's a small thing."

Mena went quiet for a moment at that. I saw something in her face come
loose, something that had been held down a long time.

Father stood beside them, looking quietly at Mena. His face didn't quite
remember who she was. But he wasn't wary of her, either.

"…Do you know me?" Father asked carefully.

"…We crossed paths once, a long time ago," Mena said. "You didn't know
me then. I didn't know you well either. But I wrote your name down."

Father nodded at that. It wasn't a face of understanding so much as one
that simply accepted it.

*I watched that short exchange and thought: how many of the names Mena
has carried ever get to see this face-to-face meeting again? I didn't
know the answer. But looking at Mena's eyes, I knew she was someone who
would keep writing regardless of the answer.*

---

Speon heard the news and came running out of the well. The moment he saw
Mena, he stopped.

The two of them said nothing for a while.

In that silence, I watched Speon's face. He always kept a calm face at
the well. Not in that moment. I saw something old shift there.

"…You said you were still waiting," Mena spoke first.

"…Yes," Speon said. "That still holds."

Mena laughed at that answer. I couldn't tell if the laugh was relief or
something else. Speon laughed along too. Their two laughs looked alike.

*In that short exchange, I felt how much time had passed. I still didn't
know what had happened between the two of them. But that day, for the
first time, I decided not to ask. I had learned that some stories
deserve to be respected without explanation.*

Mena stayed at the well for several days after that. I caught myself
secretly watching, more than once, how she and Speon sat side by side
filling water jars. The two of them didn't say much. But the silence was
unlike any silence I'd seen before.

During the day, Mena still opened her record box and wrote things down —
names of newly met people, the things they'd lost. I peeked at those
pages beside her. Short single lines were written next to the names.
"Looking for a daughter." "Hurt her hand, can't use it yet." I understood
that these short sentences were how Mena saw the world. Not resolution.
Remembrance.

In the evenings, Mena sometimes showed the box to Speon. He turned the
pages one by one, and when he found a name he knew, he tapped it with a
fingertip. That looked nothing like any scene we'd seen in Haven. The
Ledger erased names from a screen. Mena left names on paper.

One evening, I sat beside Mena and turned through the papers in that box
with her.

"…Do you have all this memorized?" I asked.

"…Not all of it," Mena said. "But when you write it by hand, you don't
forget it completely. It's strange that way."

"…Isn't it hard? Writing down nothing but sad stories all the time."

Mena turned a page and was quiet for a moment.

"…It's hard," Mena said at last. "But if no one writes it down, that
name really is as though it never existed. I find that more frightening."

*I heard that and thought about it for a long time. I understood then
why Mena walked so far. She was doing what the Ledger could not. Not
forgetting.*

---

The night before Mena left, we all sat together by the fire. Speon
hummed an old song, something he hadn't done in a long while. I couldn't
make out the words. But the melody alone stirred something in me.

That night, Mena took a sheet of paper out of her record box and showed
it to Father.

"…This is your name," Mena said. "Exactly as your children told it to
me. I wrote it down and carried it with me."

Father looked at that paper a long time. Even reading his own name, his
face wasn't sure it belonged to him.

Firelight flickered over the paper. Father traced the letters one by
one with his fingertip. Very slowly. I thought that gesture looked like
a child's, learning letters for the first time.

I saw the crease marks where the paper had been folded and unfolded. It
looked as if it had been folded many times. I could guess how long Mena
had carried that paper close.

"…This is… my name?" Father asked.

"…Yes," Mena said. "It's a name your children have never once
forgotten."

*I set that down as the last line of this chapter. In that moment, I
heard Father read his own name aloud. It was still an unfamiliar sound
to him. But in that unfamiliarity, I felt something coming back, very
slowly.*
