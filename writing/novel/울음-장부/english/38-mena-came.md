# Chapter 38. Mena Came

> _Previously_ — Living by the well had begun. Brother helped Speon tend the well and learned how to be beside someone. "I can't say I believe in God. But I know how to be beside someone."

About a month later, on a certain day, a familiar figure walked toward us from far off.

It was Mena. She had her record box, the one she always carried, slung over her shoulder.

*The moment I saw her, I ran to her. Mena remembered us. And then she saw Father and stopped walking.*

"…Did you come to bring news to Speon?" I asked.

"…That too," Mena said. Her face colored a little. "I wanted to know how you two were doing, as well."

---

"…Is this—" Mena couldn't finish the sentence.

"…Yes." Brother said. "We found him. His name hasn't all come back yet, though."

Mena's eyes filled with tears. It was the first time I'd seen her show feeling like that.

"…Of all the names I've recorded, meeting one again like this is really rare," Mena said. "Thank you. For not giving up."

"…It's thanks to you, Ma'am," Brother said. "If you hadn't recorded his name, we'd have just kept walking for nothing."

Mena shook her head.

"…I only wrote it down," Mena said. "You were the ones who walked and found him."

---

Speon heard the news and came running out from the well. The moment he saw Mena, he stopped walking.

The two of them said nothing for a while.

"…You said you were still waiting," Mena said first.

"…I did," Speon said. "That still holds."

*I felt, in that short exchange, how much time had passed between them. I still didn't know what had happened between the two of them. But that day, I decided for the first time not to ask. I'd learned that some stories deserve to be respected without explanation.*

Mena stayed by the well for several days after that. I caught myself watching, more than once, the sight of her and Speon sitting side by side filling water jars. They didn't say much to each other. But that silence was nothing like any silence I'd seen before.

---

That night, Mena took a piece of paper out of her record box and showed it to Father.

"…This is your name," Mena said. "Exactly as your children told it to me. I wrote it down and carried it with me."

Father looked at the paper for a long time. Even reading his own name, his face wasn't sure it belonged to him.

"…Is this… my name?" Father asked.

"…Yes," Mena said. "It's the name your children never once forgot."

*I set that down as the last sentence of this chapter. I heard Father read his own name aloud in that moment. It still came out sounding unfamiliar. But inside that unfamiliarity, I felt something coming back, very slowly.*
