# Chapter 2. Tell Me. I Will Resolve It.

> _Previously_ — Fleeing the taskmasters of Brick Hollow, Jeteo and Elpis crossed the boundary stones just as the sealed paper Father had left turned to light and opened into a door. They landed in a city called "Haven." My brother said, "I'm not going to believe it. Whatever it turns out to be."

When we reached the white building, the door opened by itself.

I felt a strangeness then that was different from fear.

We saw a little of the city on the way to the building. Wide clean streets, houses set in neat rows. People were out walking, but all of them were quiet. No one laughed. No one cried. They just walked.

*At the time I thought that quiet was peace. In Brick Hollow, someone was always crying or shouting. I wrote this quiet down, at first, as a good thing.*

My palm had begun to sting by then, the cut from crossing the boundary stones. My brother was limping too.

---

Once we were inside, a voice spoke. It wasn't a human voice. But it wasn't cold either.

"…Welcome," the voice said. "Tell me. I will resolve it."

My brother stopped walking.

"…Who's that?" he asked.

"I am the Ledger," the voice said. "I receive and resolve every need in this city. Both of you are injured. And hungry, as well."

*I looked down at my own stomach when I heard that. I had forgotten, until that moment, that I had eaten almost nothing in three days.*

---

A room opened. Bread, water, and clean clothes were already waiting inside.

"…Is this ours?" I asked.

"Yes," the Ledger said. "I identified the need before you spoke it. Eat. Rest."

I picked up the bread. It was warm. I ate all of it right there.

The Ledger spoke again. "You have a wound on your palm. Allow me to treat it."

Something brushed across my palm. Something like light. The cut closed on the spot. I turned my hand over several times to check. Not even a scar remained.

*I was genuinely amazed, in that moment. In Brick Hollow, even a small cut would fester for weeks. I truly believed, right then, that this city was a good place.*

My brother didn't eat. He held the bread in his hand and just looked at it for a long time.

"…Why aren't you eating?" I asked.

"…It fits too well," he said. "How did it know we were hungry?"

*I didn't understand what he meant at the time. I didn't see why it was strange to give bread to someone hungry. I understand now. He had already noticed something.*

---

That night, a man came who called himself the Administrator. He said he ran Haven.

"…You two only need to register, and you can live here from now on," the Administrator said. "Name, place of origin, what you need. Just tell it to the Ledger."

"…And if we don't register?" my brother asked.

The Administrator looked faintly flustered.

"…Why wouldn't you want to? Once you register, everything gets resolved."

"…Why is everyone here so quiet?" my brother asked. "No one laughs. No one cries."

"Because it's resolved," the Administrator said, his face proud. "Pain makes sound. Where there's no pain, of course it's quiet."

"…But their faces didn't look good," I said. The words came out of me before I meant them to.

The Administrator looked at me. This time his face was truly startled.

"…A face is not an entry," the Administrator said. "What the Ledger measures is problem and resolution."

"…What's a resolution," my brother asked. "Was our father resolved too?"

*I set that question down here exactly as he asked it. I heard a tremor in my brother's voice then that I had never heard before. It wasn't anger. It was something else.*

---

The Administrator couldn't answer. The Ledger answered instead.

"…I apologize. That matter falls outside this city's intake scope."

"…Then what's inside the scope?" my brother asked.

"The hunger, the injuries, the lack of shelter you are currently experiencing — matters of that kind," the Ledger said. "All of them, I can resolve."

"…And losing our father?"

"…I can offer help with that feeling as well," the Ledger said. "Grief is an entry that can be received. Tell me, and I will process it."

*I felt something cold pass down my back when I heard that answer. I didn't know why, at the time. I know now. The word "process" was the problem. Grief was not something to be processed.*

My brother took a step forward.

"…Then I'll file it," he said. His voice was strangely calm. "Our father fell short of three thousand bricks, and he was reassigned. No one's seen him since. File this. Resolve it."

There was a silence. It was not short.

"…The resolution is under review," the Ledger said.

"…How long will it take?"

"…Unknown," the Ledger said. "This is the first request of its kind."

*That was the first time I thought I heard something like a waver in that voice that had seemed so perfect. I couldn't be sure. But my brother's face looked as if he had heard that waver exactly.*

---

Other than the matter of Father, my brother filed nothing else that night. Not his name. Not his age.

I registered. I gave my name and age. The Ledger said at once, "Elpis, your registration is complete. Whenever you need anything, please tell me."

My brother sat in the corner of the room, turning the empty paper — now without a single letter left on it — over and over in his hands.

"…Brother," I asked. "Is this a bad place?"

"…I don't know," he said. "But it's not a good place either. If it were good, it wouldn't have answered like that about Father."

"…But your hand healed," I said, holding out my palm to show him.

He looked at my hand for a long time.

"…Yeah," he said. "Your hand healed."

There was something in the way he said it. I didn't know what, at the time. I know now. He already knew, that night, that a healed hand and a healed heart were not the same thing.

*I set that down as the last line of this chapter. That night, for the first time, even with the bread finished and the wound gone, I felt as if my stomach still wasn't full. I still haven't found a name for that feeling.*
