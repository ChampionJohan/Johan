# Chapter 22. The House of Records

> _Previously_ — Speon introduced the brother and sister to Mena, the Recorder. It came out that gathering the names of the completed was the same work my brother had been doing alone. They set out on a two-day walk, following the nicks carved into the stones.

On the evening of the second day, we arrived in front of a low earthen house.

It looked more like a storehouse than a home. There was a small sign by the door. **THE HOUSE OF RECORDS.**

*Seeing that sign, I felt strangely at ease. There were places in this world that erased names, and places that wrote them down and kept them. Just knowing both kinds of place existed gave me a little comfort.*

The whole way there, my brother barely spoke. I knew what he was afraid of. No answer at all, or an answer that turned out worse — he kept weighing which of the two would be better.

*I could read that weighing plainly on his face. I said nothing at the time. I knew no words of mine could stand in for that scale.*

---

When we knocked, a woman came out. She looked much older than us. There was ink on her fingers, as if she had been writing something without stopping.

"…Who are you?" the woman asked.

"…Speon sent us," my brother said. "Are you Mena?"

Something shifted slightly in the woman's face at that name.

"…I am. What did Speon say?"

"…That he's still waiting," I said.

Mena closed her eyes for a moment at that. She said nothing. But I could feel something very old in that silence.

"…Is he well?" Mena asked. Her voice shook a little.

"…Yes," my brother said. "He keeps that well every day."

Mena smiled a little at that. Something like tears mixed into her smile.

"…That's just like him," Mena said.

---

Mena let us inside. The whole place was paper. There were shelves along every wall, and every shelf was packed tight with paper covered in names.

"…Are all these the names of completed people?" I asked.

"…Only the ones I've managed to gather," Mena said. "I haven't gathered them all. This world is too vast."

Looking around at the papers, my brother asked carefully.

"…Could you look for our father's name too?"

Mena looked at him for a long time.

"…Tell me his name," she said. "I'll look."

*My heart was pounding so hard in that instant it hurt. Listening to my brother say Father's name, I could feel that even after all this time, the name still sat exactly right in his voice.*

Mena wrote the name down on paper. I saw her fingertips trembling slightly.

"…Is it a common name?" Mena asked.

"…No," my brother said. "In Brick Hollow, our father was the only one with it."

"…Then it might be a little easier to find," Mena said.

---

Mena moved back and forth between the shelves for a long time. The room filled with nothing but the sound of pages turning.

I didn't know how much time had passed. I couldn't breathe right through any of it.

Mena finally stopped in front of one shelf. She turned back to face us. Her expression was complicated.

"…It's here," Mena said. "But it isn't the answer you're hoping for."

*I set that line down as the last sentence of this chapter. In that instant I learned that Father really was alive, somewhere in this world. But at the same time, I had to endure the whole of that night before I would learn what she meant by "not the answer you're hoping for."*
