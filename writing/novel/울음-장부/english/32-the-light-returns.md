# Chapter 32. The Light Returns

> _Previously_ — Father, without meaning to, hummed a fragment of Mother's lullaby as he walked — the first piece of his earliest memory left in his body. Jeteo, for the first time, said aloud to himself: "Maybe there's something in me too that hasn't been erased completely."

Some nights later, that low light appeared again in the sky.

We were crossing a broad open field that day. There was no good place to take cover. The moment I realized it, a chill ran down my spine.

The grass in that field rose to our knees. By day, that openness had felt refreshing. By night, that same openness turned into pure defenselessness. There was only, here and there, far apart, a single tree, a single boulder.

I found myself, out of habit, counting hiding places as we walked. One, two. I couldn't count a third. I realized, that day, that this habit had formed after we'd left Haven.

We had all but forgotten that light in the meantime. Caring for Father, walking, simply surviving each day — we'd been consumed by all of it. I understood, that night, all over again, how dangerous that forgetting had been.

*Even now, I find that strange. People forget fear too. There isn't enough strength in the heart to be afraid every single day. I used to think that forgetting was laziness. Now I think it was also a way of staying alive.*

My brother spotted the light first and stopped walking. I watched his body go rigid in an instant. The same reaction as weeks before. But this time, the speed of it had grown even faster.

Watching that speed troubled me. That my brother had gotten better at sensing danger was a relief. But at the same time, it meant he was carving that much more fear into his body.

"…Searching light," my brother said, low. A familiar fear had returned to his voice.

The light moved very slowly, over near the horizon. Unhurried. That unhurriedness frightened me more. A hurried thing might miss you. That light looked like it had no intention of missing anything.

The insect sounds cut off abruptly right then. I couldn't tell if that was coincidence. But it felt like the whole field was holding its breath.

*The instant I saw that light, I checked on Father first. He had no idea what it was — he was simply gazing up at the sky.*

I envied that ignorance. One glimpse of that light and our hearts dropped, while Father just stood there admiring something curious in the night sky. I thought, in that moment, that Father having been "completed" might, in some way, be a kind of shield.

*I was ashamed of that thought later. If ignorance was a shield, it was a shield built by taking something from Father first and covering the wound with it. I wanted Father to come back to being someone who could feel afraid. Knowing how to fear is part of being alive, too.*

"…It's pretty," Father said, innocent. Something in me ached to hear it. Father had no idea at all what that light meant.

Father even lifted a hand and pointed at the light. A gesture as pure as a child pointing at a star. I found that purity unusually precarious that night.

"…It's not pretty," my brother said. His voice was harder than usual.

"…No?" Father asked. "Then why does it shine like that?"

My brother didn't answer. I think it wasn't that he couldn't answer — it was that he didn't want to. To explain what that light shone for, he'd have had to explain, first, why we were running.

"…Father, quickly," my brother said urgently, tugging Father's arm. "We'll explain later."

Father let himself be pulled along, still with no idea what was happening. I found that compliance both a relief and a grief. The old Father would have looked after us first and led the way himself.

---

"…We need to hide," my brother said. We ducked quickly behind a low boulder. Father crouched down with us. He probably didn't know exactly why. But he followed without resistance.

The boulder was small for three people. My brother pushed his own body toward the outer edge. I knew that wasn't an accident. He always positioned himself that way.

"…Brother, get in," I whispered.

"…I'm fine," my brother said.

*I don't know how many times I've heard those two words over the course of this journey. My brother always said he was fine. I know he was never once fine.*

That compliance struck me as strange. Two strange children suddenly pulling at his arm, and Father crouched down without a moment's resistance. It was as if some sense for danger was still alive, somewhere in his body.

As Father lowered himself, he spread both arms toward us. A sheltering posture. He himself didn't seem to notice he was doing it.

*I remember those arms even now. Father didn't know who we were. But his body knew how to protect us. That night, I understood for the first time that what is lost and what is erased are not the same thing.*

The light drew closer. I held my breath.

The boulder's cold surface pressed against my back. Strangely, that cold helped me focus. Fear, I learned that night, sharpens every sense in the body.

A damp smell rose from the earth. Beside me I heard my brother's breath, very shallow. Father's breathing, by contrast, was even and deep. I found that difference strange enough to glance his way. His eyes were closed.

In that moment I thought of Haven's quiet corridors. We had held our breath there too. But back then, we hadn't even known what we were hiding from. Now I know. I still don't know whether knowing is better.

*A strange thought crossed my mind in that moment. Whether that light was searching for us, or for Father. Father was already a "completed" person. Does someone completed still get come looking for?*

A single blade of grass tickled my cheek. I couldn't brush it away, couldn't move at all. Enduring that small itch was the only resistance I had left in that moment.

---

The light stopped, briefly, right over our heads.

The light made no sound while it hovered there. That was the most frightening part. If it had made a sound, I could at least have guessed where it was. That light only shone.

Wherever the light touched, the grass cast long shadows. I turned my head, very slightly, trying to gauge how far our shadows stretched out past the boulder. My brother pressed down on my wrist. A signal not to move.

That moment felt eternal. My brother gripped my hand tight. Father took my other hand too. He didn't know the reason, but he seemed to sense that something was dangerous.

The color of the light shifted, just slightly. The white flickered, for a moment, into a pale yellow. I didn't know what that meant. I only watched that flicker and held my breath.

That yellow tinge lasted only an instant. But in that instant, a strange thought crossed my mind. That maybe this light was wandering too, unable to find what it was looking for.

*I told my brother that thought later. He was baffled. "That's a machine," he said. I thought he was right, and still thought that not finding what you're looking for must be just as frustrating for a machine as for a person.*

*In that moment I prayed, in my mind, though I didn't know to whom. I only asked for one thing — that the light would pass by.*

The light moved again. This time, not toward us, but off in a different direction.

I followed that direction with my eyes. The light was heading toward the path we'd come from, toward the spot where we'd rested earlier. I couldn't tell if that was pure chance, or if something had turned it aside.

*I've gone back over that night many times since. That light had been directly above us. We were three people crowded onto one small rock, and Father's coat had spilled out past its edge. There was no reason we shouldn't have been seen. I still can't explain, to this day, why we weren't found that night.*

*I couldn't breathe properly until that light had disappeared completely. What came before relief was a strange guilt. The thought that this light might be searching for someone else right now.*

In the stillness of that night, I heard only my own heartbeat, unusually loud. It took a while for that sound to settle.

"…Are you all right?" my brother asked me. Only then did I realize I was trembling.

"…Yes," I said. It was a lie. My brother's face showed he knew I was lying. But he only squeezed my hand once more.

"…That was scary," my brother said. Not a question.

*In that moment I found myself thinking, strangely, of the man from the flowerbed. If he'd been here right now, what would he have said? I thought he probably would have just sat beside us in silence.*

"…Weren't you scared?" I asked.

My brother was quiet for a moment.

"…I was," he said. "But I'm not someone who's allowed to be scared."

"…There's no such person," I said.

My brother didn't answer that. He just looked up at the sky once more.

Father sat quietly between us. He clearly had no idea what had happened, but he seemed to understand it had been something serious. Father carefully laid a hand on my shoulder. I still remember, even now, how great a comfort the weight of that hand was, in that moment.

---

We stayed crouched there a good while longer. My legs began to cramp, but no one spoke first about moving. I didn't think that silence was only about fear. All three of us, I think, were being careful, afraid that something would change if this moment stretched even a little longer.

By the time my legs had gone fully numb, Father shifted position first. He asked me, "…are you all right?" I nearly laughed at that question. The person we'd thought we needed to protect, just moments before, was the one looking after us first.

Once the light had disappeared, my brother spoke low.

"…We really do need to get far from Haven now. This place must still be inside that light's reach."

"…Where do we go?" I asked.

"…Anywhere," Father cut in carefully. "As long as I'm not a burden to you."

Father said that while watching our faces closely. I found that carefulness unfamiliar. Father had never been the kind of person to watch others' faces like that — at least, not the Father I remembered.

My brother thought for a moment.

"…I don't know. But we have to keep walking. Father, and both of us — staying here is dangerous."

"…How much further until it's safe?" I asked.

"…I don't know," my brother said. "That's the whole problem. No one tells you how far you have to go."

Father looked up carefully at the sky at that.

"…I don't know the direction," Father said, "but the constellations somehow look familiar to me. Would that help?"

My brother and I looked at each other. It was an unexpected thing to hear from a father who claimed to remember nothing.

*I thought about that for a long time. Neither when we left Brick Hollow, nor when we left Haven, did we know how far we had to go. We had always walked without knowing the end. I think that not-knowing is the name of this whole story.*

I saw, on my brother's face, the confident look he used to wear before. He'd always led the way with a face like that, leaving Haven, leaving Brick Hollow. That face was welcome to see again, and at the same time it worried me — that he might be trying to shoulder everything alone once more.

"…You don't have to decide alone," I said.

My brother looked at me. A little surprised.

"…I know," he said. "But someone has to say which way to go."

"…Then let's say it together," I said.

My brother smiled faintly at that. There was relief in that smile, and also a strange, unfamiliar lightness.

"…Me too," Father said carefully. "I don't know anything, so I can't decide the direction. But I can help say it, at least."

My brother looked a little startled at that. Then he laughed.

"…Yes," my brother said. "That's enough."

Father heard that and asked quietly.

"…Am I a burden?"

Father's voice was calm. But behind that calm there was something braced, as if for the worst. My heart sank, seeing that expression.

My brother looked at Father, startled.

"…No," my brother said firmly. "You're not a burden. We're going together."

I took Father's hand too.

"…That's right," I said. "Don't ever say *burden* again."

Father looked back and forth between the two of us, then nodded, small. Not a face that fully believed it. But in that moment, at least, he didn't question it further.

The night wind blew and stirred the hems of our three coats. I felt that even in that wind, the hands the three of us held onto each other never wavered.

Somewhere far off, I heard something like a dog barking. It meant people lived nearby. I started to feel relieved at the sound, then remembered — where there are people, there's a searching light too — and tightened my guard again.

*From that night on, I learned to see the world in two layers. Behind every welcome thing, there was always some danger attached. I thought that was what it meant to grow up. It was early to learn it, at thirteen.*

Father heard all that and laughed quietly. A laugh I hadn't seen from him before. A laugh with both relief and shyness mixed into it.

"…Thank you," Father said. A short sentence. But I could feel the weight carried inside it.

"…For what?" I asked.

"…I don't know," Father said. "I just felt thankful. That word came first."

My brother heard that answer and laughed quietly too.

"…I have that too," my brother said. "Words that come out without a reason. It keeps happening to me lately."

"…Such as?" Father asked.

My brother hesitated a moment.

"…The word *it's all right,*" my brother said. "Even when it's really not all right, that word comes first."

I liked that answer. It meant that words coming out without a reason were still alive somewhere inside Father.

*I set that line down as this chapter's last line. I saw my brother look surprised at himself as he said those words. As he said them to Father, he seemed to be saying them to himself, at the very same time.*
