# Chapter 32. The Light Returns

> _Previously_ — Father hummed Mother's lullaby without even realizing it, as he walked — the first fragment of memory left in his body. For the first time, Jeteo said aloud, of himself, "Maybe something inside me hasn't been completely erased either."

A few nights later, that low light appeared in the sky again.

We were crossing a wide-open field that day. There was nowhere fit to hide. The moment that fact struck me, a chill ran down my spine.

We'd nearly forgotten that light in the days since. We'd been consumed with caring for Father, with walking, with simply getting through each day. I realized again that night just how dangerous that forgetting had been.

Jeteo spotted the light first and stopped walking. I watched his whole body go rigid in an instant. The same reaction as weeks before. Only faster this time.

Watching that speed, my heart tangled. It was a relief that Jeteo had gotten better at sensing danger. But at the same time, it meant he was carving that much more fear into his body.

"…The searching light," Jeteo said, low. A familiar fear had returned to his voice.

*The moment I saw the light, I checked on Father first. He was just looking up at the sky, not knowing at all what it was.*

I envied that ignorance. That single light was enough to drop our hearts through the floor, while Father simply gazed at something curious in the night sky. I thought, in that moment, that being marked COMPLETE might, in some strange way, be a kind of shield.

"…It's pretty," Father said, innocently. Something about that ached strangely in my chest. Father had no idea at all what that light meant.

Father even lifted his hand and pointed at it. It was as pure a gesture as a child pointing at a star. That purity felt unusually precarious to me, that day of all days.

"…Father, hurry," Jeteo said, pulling urgently at his arm. "Explanations later."

---

"…We have to hide," Jeteo said. We ducked quickly behind a low rock. Father crouched down with us too. He couldn't have known the exact reason. But he followed along without question.

That readiness struck me as strange. Two children he'd only just met suddenly seized his arm, and he crouched without a moment's resistance. It was as if some sense for danger, somewhere in his body, was still alive.

The light drew closer and closer. I held my breath.

The cold surface of the rock pressed against my back. That cold gave me something to focus on. I learned, that day, that fear sharpens every single sense in the body.

*A strange thought struck me right then. Whether that light was searching for us — or for Father. Father was already someone marked COMPLETE. Did they come searching even for someone already complete?*

---

The light paused for a moment right above our heads.

That moment felt like forever. Jeteo gripped my hand tight. Father took my other hand too. He didn't know why, but he seemed to sense something dangerous.

I saw the light's color shift, just a little. The white flickered, for a brief instant, into a pale yellow. I didn't know what that meant. I just watched that flicker and held my breath.

*I prayed, in my heart, right then. I didn't even know who I was praying to. I just prayed for that light to pass — that one thing, and nothing else.*

The light moved again. This time, not toward us — it went off in another direction.

I followed it with my eyes. The light was heading back the way we'd come — toward the spot where we'd rested earlier. I couldn't tell whether that was pure chance, or whether something had turned it that way.

*I couldn't breathe properly until the light had completely disappeared. What came before relief was a strange guilt — the thought that the light might be searching for someone else right now instead.*

In the stillness of that night, all I could hear, unusually loud, was my own heartbeat. It took a while for it to quiet down.

"…Are you all right?" Jeteo asked me. Only then did I realize I was shaking.

"…Yeah," I said. It was a lie. Jeteo's face showed he knew it was a lie too. But he just squeezed my hand once more.

Father sat quietly between us. He didn't know exactly what had happened, but his face knew it had been something serious. He set a careful hand on my shoulder. I still remember, even now, how much comfort the weight of that hand was, in that moment.

---

We stayed crouched there a good while longer. Cramps started in our legs, but none of us was the first to say we should move. I don't think that silence was only fear. All three of us, I think, were being careful — afraid something might change if this moment stretched on a little further.

After the light was gone, Jeteo said, low,

"…We really do need to get far away from Haven now. Even out here is inside that eye's reach, it seems."

"…Go where?" I asked.

"…Anywhere," Father put in carefully. "As long as I'm not a burden."

Father watched our faces carefully as he said it. That wariness felt strange to me. Father had never been the kind of man to watch for other people's moods like that. Not the Father I remembered, at least.

Jeteo thought for a moment, then said,

"…I don't know. But we have to keep walking. You, us — staying here is dangerous for all of us."

I saw the old, certain look on Jeteo's face again. That was the face he'd worn leading us, back when we left Haven, back when we left Brick Hollow. I was glad to see it, and worried, too, that he was trying to shoulder everything alone again.

"…You don't have to decide this by yourself," I said.

Jeteo looked at me. His face was startled, briefly.

"…I know," Jeteo said. "But someone has to say which way to go."

"…Then let's say it together," I said.

Jeteo looked at me and smiled, small. That smile carried relief, and mixed in with it, a lightness that felt a little unfamiliar.

Hearing that, Father asked quietly,

"…Am I a burden?"

Father's voice was calm. But behind that calm was the look of someone bracing for something. Seeing his face, my heart dropped.

Jeteo looked at Father, startled by the question.

"…No," Jeteo said firmly. "You're not a burden. We're going together."

I took Father's hand too.

"…That's right," I said. "Don't ever say the word 'burden' again."

Father looked from one of us to the other and gave a small nod. It wasn't quite the face of someone who fully believed it. Still, in that moment, he didn't press any further.

A night wind blew, stirring all three of our clothes. Even inside that wind, I felt that the hands we held onto each other stayed perfectly still.

Father smiled quietly, hearing it. A smile I hadn't seen before — relief and shyness mixed together.

"…Thank you," Father said. It was a short thing to say. But I could feel the weight carried inside it.

*I set down those words as the last line of this chapter. I saw that Jeteo looked startled by his own words, even as he said them. It seemed like he was saying them to himself, at the same time as he said them to Father.*
