# Chapter 21. In Search of the Recorder

> _Previously_ — Speon briefly revealed why he kept the well every day, and a piece of his past — that he hadn't gone looking, but had run, and was now learning how to wait. When Speon said he'd seen the man at the flowerbed in more than one place, Elpis began, faintly, to feel it was no coincidence.

"…Go find someone," Speon said the next morning. "Her name is Mena. She's the Recorder."

"…What does she record?" my brother asked.

"…The names of the completed," Speon said. "She's someone who gathers, from every well scattered across this world, as many of the names of the people who come out as she can. I send her word myself, sometimes."

*Hearing that, I understood for the first time, truly, that there were far more people like us scattered across this vast Wilderness. It wasn't only Speon.*

"…Who is the record for?" my brother asked.

"…At first, I didn't know either," Speon said. "But I know now. It's for the day someone who lost their name wants to hear it again. Someone has to be holding on to that name when that day comes."

*Hearing that, I thought of the paper stamped COMPLETE that my brother carried. He had already been doing that work, alone. Mena was someone doing that same work, only far larger, and far longer.*

---

"…Why does Mena do this?" I asked.

"…By her own account, she used to be someone who kept records, once," Speon said. "I heard she recorded some great story somewhere, once. I don't know exactly what it was myself."

*Something about that pulled at me strangely. I was recording this story too. I didn't know then that someday I would come to share that very thing with Mena.*

---

Before we left, Speon packed us dry bread and a water flask.

"…Where Mena lives is two days from here," Speon said. "There are markers on the way. Small nicks cut into the stones. I don't know who carved them. But follow those nicks, and you won't lose your way."

"…Aren't you coming?" I asked.

"…I have to stay here," Speon said. He looked back at the well. "Someone might come out. I never know when."

*Hearing that answer, I understood that Speon might never be able to leave this place for the rest of his life. I couldn't tell, then, whether that was a sad thing or a beautiful one.*

---

As we left, Speon said one last thing to us.

"…Tell Mena that Speon is still waiting. She'll understand what that means."

"…What does that mean?" my brother asked.

Speon smiled a little.

"…That's something only Mena and I know," he said. "Someday you two will have words like that too. Words you never have to explain to anyone."

*I set that line down as the last sentence of this chapter. That morning we left the well and began walking, following those nicks carved into the stones. I didn't know then that those nicks were the trace of someone's footsteps — someone very old, carved all across this world.*
