# Chapter 21. In Search of the Recorder

> _Previously_ — Speon briefly revealed why he kept the well every day, and a piece of his past — that he hadn't gone looking, but had run, and was now learning how to wait. When Speon said he'd seen the man at the flowerbed in more than one place, Elpis began, faintly, to feel it was no coincidence.

The morning fog lay low around the well. In it, I could see Speon already up and pacing near the water. He didn't look like he'd slept at all.

"…Go find someone," Speon said the next morning. "Her name is Mena. She's the Recorder."

"…What does she record?" my brother asked.

"…The names of the completed," Speon said. "She's someone who gathers, from every well scattered across this world, as many of the names of the people who come out as she can. I send her word myself, sometimes."

*Hearing that, I understood for the first time, truly, that there were far more people like us scattered across this vast Wilderness. It wasn't only Speon.*

"…Is Mena a Well-Keeper too?" I asked.

"…No," Speon said. "Mena doesn't keep a well. She keeps names instead. Everyone keeps watch in their own way."

Listening to that, I found myself thinking that this world must be full of far more people, keeping and watching over someone in ways we didn't yet know, than we could imagine.

"…Who is the record for?" my brother asked.

"…At first, I didn't know either," Speon said. "But I know now. It's for the day someone who lost their name wants to hear it again. Someone has to be holding on to that name when that day comes."

*Hearing that, I thought of the paper stamped COMPLETE that my brother carried. He had already been doing that work, alone. Mena was someone doing that same work, only far larger, and far longer.*

At that, my brother pressed a hand briefly to the paper in his shirt, checking it was still there. I'd lost count of how many times I'd seen him do that by now. It had become almost a part of his body.

"…I guess I should be doing something like that too," my brother murmured. It sounded less like it was meant for Speon than like he was talking to himself.

"…You're already doing it," Speon said. "That one piece of paper is where it starts."

Listening to them, I felt my brother becoming, little by little, a different person. When we left Brick Hollow, he had been someone trying to leave evidence behind. Now he was becoming someone trying to keep something safe. I understood that the difference looked small, but was in fact enormous.

---

"…Why does Mena do this?" I asked.

"…By her own account, she used to be someone who kept records, once," Speon said. "I heard she recorded some great story somewhere, once. I don't know exactly what it was myself."

*Something about that pulled at me strangely. I was recording this story too. I didn't know then that someday I would come to share that very thing with Mena.*

From that moment on, I found myself curious about this person named Mena. I tried to imagine what she looked like, what her voice sounded like. In my imagination, she was always sitting among stacks of paper.

"…A great story — what kind?" I asked.

"…I don't know exactly either," Speon said. "But every time Mena talks about it, something changes in her eyes. It's the look of someone who's kept something for a very long time, and very carefully."

I held on to that. Something told me I would hear that story myself, someday.

"…Has Mena been out here a long time too?" I asked.

"…Longer than I have, I'd guess," Speon said. "When I first came here she was already doing this work. She hasn't stopped, not once, from then until now."

"…That's remarkable," my brother said.

"…It's not remarkable. It's just going on," Speon said. "The two look alike, but they're different."

---

Before we left, Speon packed us dry bread and a water flask.

"…Where Mena lives is two days from here," Speon said. "There are markers on the way. Small nicks cut into the stones. I don't know who carved them. But follow those nicks, and you won't lose your way."

He tied the water flask securely to my hand as he passed it over. I found that touch strangely familiar. It resembled the way Father used to pack our things.

"…Aren't you coming?" I asked.

"…I have to stay here," Speon said. He looked back at the well. "Someone might come out. I never know when."

*Hearing that answer, I understood that Speon might never be able to leave this place for the rest of his life. I couldn't tell, then, whether that was a sad thing or a beautiful one.*

I looked back at the well one last time. The water was still clear. The bottom was still out of sight. I found it hard to walk away from that spot, thinking that someday Father might rise up out of that water.

"…Elpis," my brother called. "We have to go."

I ran my hand once more along the stone wall before I finally took a step.

My brother turned back to look at the well once more.

"…If our father comes out here," my brother said, "could you tell him we went looking for him?"

Speon caught my brother's shoulder briefly at that.

"…I will," he said. "I promise."

---

As we left, Speon said one last thing to us.

"…Tell Mena that Speon is still waiting. She'll understand what that means."

"…What does that mean?" my brother asked.

Speon smiled a little.

"…That's something only Mena and I know," he said. "Someday you two will have words like that too. Words you never have to explain to anyone."

We walked a few steps and turned to look back. Speon was still standing there in the same spot, watching us. I held that image in my eyes. A single figure standing on a hill, beside a well. I already knew then that the shape of it would never leave me.

---

The first nick was on a boulder not far from the well. My brother found it first and traced it with his fingertip.

"…Here," my brother said. "Look."

It looked something like a small arrow, or maybe just a scratch. I stared at the shape a long while and still couldn't be sure. But my brother was already turning his body in that direction.

"…How do you know?" I asked.

"…I don't know," he said. "But I think this is the right direction."

We walked all that day, finding the marks one after another. On boulders, sometimes at the base of a dead tree, the nicks appeared at even, steady intervals. Proof that someone, a very long time ago, had walked this road, steadily, again and again.

*Watching those marks, I felt a strange comfort. That someone, long before us, had already found their way through this vast, empty Wilderness. We weren't entirely alone.*

My brother kept checking the marks as we walked, as if he mustn't miss a single one. I found that carefulness of his unfamiliar. In Brick Hollow, he had always been someone who walked looking only straight ahead. Now he was watching the ground and the boulders and the dead tree stumps in turn. I thought this must be a new way of walking he had learned in this world.

As the sun went down, I traced one of the nicks with my fingertip. It was cut deep. Not hurried — it looked like something worked over, more than once, with care. I found myself wondering whose hand it had been.

*I set that line down as the last sentence of this chapter. That morning we left the well and began walking, following those nicks carved into the stones. I didn't know then that those nicks were the trace of someone's footsteps — someone very old, carved all across this world.*
