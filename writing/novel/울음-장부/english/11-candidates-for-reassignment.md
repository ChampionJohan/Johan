# Chapter 11. Candidates for Reassignment

> _Previously_ — Elpis filed her brother's same questions herself, choosing to share the risk. Learning this, Jeteo wept without a sound for the first time since leaving Brick Hollow. Brother and sister confirmed to each other that they weren't alone.

A few mornings later, the Administrator came in holding a single sheet of paper. His face was more grave than I had ever seen it.

The instant I saw that paper, I had a bad feeling. The Administrator had never once, until then, come to us holding paper. Everything in this city was delivered by voice alone. The very fact of paper in his hand felt like a sign that something couldn't be undone.

"…I am formally notifying the two of you," the Administrator said. His voice shook. "Jeteo, Elpis — both of you have been classified as candidates for Reassignment."

*The strength went out of my legs the instant I heard that. I didn't know exactly what those words meant. But my body already knew.*

My brother said nothing. I saw his hand brush lightly against the edge of the paper tucked in his shirt. The one stamped COMPLETE. I knew that gesture was him guarding it.

---

"…What does 'candidate' mean?" my brother asked. His voice was calm, but his hands were shaking. I could tell he was working hard to hide that tremor.

"…It isn't finalized yet," the Administrator said. "But those who reach this stage… mostly, it becomes final."

"…If it's mostly, does that mean some don't?" I asked.

The Administrator shook his head. "…Not as far as I know." His eyes wavered for a moment as he said it.

"…How much time do we have?"

"…I don't know," the Administrator said. "I truly don't know. This time, I really don't know."

My legs went weak hearing that answer. My brother caught my arm.

*I knew then that the Administrator wasn't lying. There was the same fear in his face that was in ours.*

I felt something like pity for the Administrator then, for the first time. He wasn't trying to frighten us. He too was trapped inside this city's rules. I thought he might have been trapped inside them longer than we had.

"…Is there any way to stop it?" I asked.

The Administrator shook his head. I saw his hand tremble a little. A tremor the Administrator, as I'd always known him, would never have shown.

"…If you stop filing, it may be reconsidered," the Administrator said. "If you stop asking questions."

My brother looked at me at that. I knew what he was thinking. Stopping might mean living. But there was already an answer in my brother's eyes.

Even then, I wanted to stop him. But the words wouldn't come. I already knew, too. My brother wasn't going to stop.

"…That's not an option," my brother said. "That paper's name, our father — if we just stop asking, they really do disappear."

I didn't argue, hearing that. I felt the same way. Disappearing frightened me more than stopping did. That was when I first understood I had come to share the same fear as my brother.

---

That was the first day my brother spoke to me of running.

We sat facing each other in a corner of the room. It was daytime, but we'd drawn the curtains. I knew that was my brother's own kind of caution.

"…I think we need to leave this city," my brother said.

"…Can we leave?" I asked. "Is there something like the boundary stones here too?"

"…I don't know," my brother said. "But staying here is definitely dangerous. If we leave, at least it's a danger we don't know."

"…There might be something out there too, though," I said. My voice shrank smaller than I meant it to.

"…We still have to go," my brother said. "If we stay, we can't even find an answer at all. If we go, at least there's a chance."

*That distinction felt strange to me. My brother always chose the unknown danger over the certain one. I didn't know, then, that this was my brother's own kind of courage. Now I do.*

I looked out the window. Haven's lights were still burning in their straight, even lines. That straightness felt suffocating to me that day, of all days.

"…I'm coming too," I said. My brother looked at me, worried. "No need to even ask. I'm a candidate too, either way."

My brother smiled, small, at that.

"…All right," my brother said. "Let's go together."

My brother took my hand. In that moment we looked at each other like two people who had already made up their minds to leave.

---

We packed that night. There wasn't much to call luggage. The paper stamped COMPLETE. And a single small leaf I'd torn from the flowerbed I'd watered every day.

The house was dark. We packed without turning on a light. In that darkness, listening to my brother's breathing, I thought it was exactly like the night we'd left Brick Hollow.

Before I went out the door, I turned back once to look at the room. Even though we'd only lived there a few days, it already felt unfamiliar. That unfamiliarity made me sad.

On our way out, we stopped by the flowerbed. The man was there that night. As if he'd known we would come.

"…You're leaving," he said. Not a question — a tone that already knew. I was startled all over again at that. We hadn't told anyone yet.

"…Yes," I said. "I'm scared."

My brother set a hand on my shoulder. He said nothing, but I knew, from the weight of that hand, that he felt the same.

"…You're right to be scared," he said. "But you're not going alone."

"…Are you coming too?" I asked.

"…I'm staying here," he said. "But that doesn't mean you're going alone."

"…What does that mean?" my brother asked. There was a slight impatience in his voice. I knew he was holding that impatience back.

He answered with a quiet smile instead. I couldn't tell whether that smile was sad, or gentle.

My brother shrugged at that smile. He seemed to decide not to press further.

I thought what he'd said didn't quite add up. My brother's face said the same. And yet, strangely, hearing those words made me a little less afraid.

*At the time I thought he meant himself, staying behind. Now I know. He meant something else entirely.*

He set a hand on each of our shoulders, once. His hands were surprisingly warm. I carried that warmth with me for a long time afterward.

---

He told us of a low road heading south out of the city. A route with no watch, he said.

"…How do you know something like that?" my brother asked him directly, for the first time.

I knew that question was the first doubt my brother had ever put to him, directly. I waited for the answer with a knotted heart.

He looked at my brother and smiled a little.

"…Because I've been here longer than this city has," he said.

My brother thought the answer strange, but he didn't press further that night. There wasn't time.

Before we left, I looked once more at the flowerbed. The sprout had become a proper stem by now. I wondered whether it would keep growing without us.

The sky was still dark. A few faint stars showed. I thought those stars must be far older than any of Haven's lights.

*I set the last line of this chapter down here. We left Haven that night. I looked back once at that flowerbed as I went — the flowerbed where the sprout had grown just a little more. I didn't know it then. That he would go on staying there, in that place no one had planted, even after we were gone.*
