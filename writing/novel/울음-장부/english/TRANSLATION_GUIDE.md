# Translation Guide — *The Weeping Ledger* (울음 장부)

Translate Korean Christian YA fantasy chapters into literary English. This
guide is the single source of truth for names, terms, Scripture wording, and
formatting. Follow it exactly so all 48 chapters read as one consistent book
translated by one hand. This is Book 2 of the *Loop Chronicles* series —
match the register and conventions already established in Book 1's own
`루프-연대기/english/TRANSLATION_GUIDE.md` wherever the two overlap (e.g.
Speon, Mena, Temel are the SAME characters, now adults).

## 1. Title and series

- Series title: **Loop Chronicles**
- This novel's title: **The Weeping Ledger**
- Full title as it will appear on the cover / KDP listing: **The Weeping
  Ledger: A Novel of a Father Who Forgot His Name** (subtitle may be
  shortened later; use the plain title alone inside chapter front-matter)
- Part titles (these are the exact strings found in each chapter's
  `series:` front-matter line — match them verbatim by Korean text, not by
  chapter number, in case of any mismatch):
  - 울음 장부 1부 · 벽돌골 → **The Weeping Ledger, Part One · Brick Hollow**
  - 울음 장부 2부 · 빈들 → **The Weeping Ledger, Part Two · The Wilderness**
  - 울음 장부 3부 · 북쪽 길 → **The Weeping Ledger, Part Three · The Northward Road**
  - 울음 장부 4부 · 우물가 → **The Weeping Ledger, Part Four · By the Well**

## 4. Register and voice

- Narrator: **Elpis**, first person past tense, writing a retrospective
  record as she goes — she frequently addresses the fact that she is
  writing ("I set this down," "I record this exactly"). Prose is spare,
  short paragraphs, frequent one-line paragraphs for emphasis. This is a
  YOUNGER narrator than Book 1's Mena (13, growing to roughly 14 across the
  book) — keep sentences a little simpler and more direct than Book 1's
  translation, but do not make her sound childish; she is plainspoken, not
  naive.
- *Italicized* passages are Elpis's present-tense (i.e. "now, writing this
  down") reflections inserted into the record — keep them italicized in
  English, exactly as the Korean does.
- Blockquoted `>` passages at the top of each chapter are the
  `*이전 이야기*` ("Previously") recap ONLY — this book does NOT use a
  separate Scripture-epigraph blockquote per chapter the way Book 1 did
  (the one exception is the Exodus 3:7 / Psalm 34:18 epigraphs on the
  `기획안.md` planning document itself, which is not a chapter and does not
  need translating for the ebook). Translate `*이전 이야기*` as **_Previously_**
  (italicized, its own line inside the blockquote).
- Dialogue lines beginning with "…" (ellipsis, marking a hesitant or quiet
  opening) — preserve that ellipsis at the start of the quoted line in
  English, e.g. `"…아빠."` → `"…Father."` Keep it as a stylistic tic
  throughout, don't normalize it away.
- Scene breaks marked `---` in the Korean must remain `---` in the English.
- Keep sentences short. Do not smooth Elpis's clipped, documentary style
  into flowing literary prose — the choppiness is intentional
  characterization, and it is even more pronounced than Book 1's Mena.
- Numbers: 삼천 (bricks) → **"three thousand"** consistently (chapters 1,
  23, 27). 이천구백구십칠, 이천구백구십팔 (ch.27, Father's counting) →
  **"two thousand nine hundred ninety-seven," "two thousand nine hundred
  ninety-eight"** — write these out as words, not digits, to match the
  spoken/murmured quality of the scene.

## 5. Scripture — use the King James Version (KJV) ONLY

The KJV is public domain worldwide, which matters for commercial
publication — never substitute NIV, ESV, NASB, or any other copyrighted
translation. Only ONE verse is directly quoted in the body of this novel:

- **Exodus 3:7** — "And the LORD said, I have surely seen the affliction of
  my people which are in Egypt, and have heard their cry by reason of their
  taskmasters; for I know their sorrows." *(quoted in full inside chapter 1
  — the father's sealed paper. The Korean text quotes only part of the
  verse: "내가 애굽에 있는 내 백성의 고통을 정녕 보고 그들이 그 감독자로
  말미암아 부르짖음을 듣고 그 우고에 관심하고" — this maps to "I have
  surely seen the affliction of my people which are in Egypt, and have
  heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters; for I know their
  sorrows." Quote exactly this much, matching the Korean's boundary — do
  not add "And the LORD said" if the Korean excerpt doesn't include an
  equivalent lead-in; check the exact clause boundary against the Korean
  in ch.1 before finalizing.)*
- In chapter 1, Jeteo then echoes fragments of the verse aloud in anger
  ("…봤냐? 정녕 보고, 정녕 듣고…") — render this as him quoting back the
  words **"surely seen"** and **"surely... heard"** from the verse he just
  read, in his own bitter, broken delivery — not a fresh translation, an
  echo of the KJV wording above.

No other formal Scripture quotation appears anywhere else in the book's 48
chapters. If you encounter a paraphrase of Scripture content elsewhere
(e.g. the Ledger's four-point theodicy in ch.4, or the Administrator's
lines), translate it as ordinary prose in your own words — do NOT format it
as or attribute it to a KJV quotation; it is the Ledger's own generated
argument, not Scripture.

## 6. Recurring phrases — translate identically every time

| Korean | English (use every time) |
|---|---|
| 말씀해 주세요. 해결해 드리겠습니다. | **"Tell me. I will resolve it."** |
| 얼굴은 항목이 아닙니다 | **"A face is not an entry."** |
| 처리 방안을 검토 중입니다 | **"The resolution is under review."** |
| 이런 유형의 접수는 처음입니다 | **"This is the first request of its kind."** |
| 해결과 들음은 다르다 (or close paraphrases of this idea) | **"Resolving and listening are not the same thing."** |
| 답할 권한이 없습니다 / 제가 답할 수 있는 게 아닙니다 | **"I don't have the authority to answer that."** / **"That isn't mine to answer."** — use the first for the Ledger/Administrator's formal register, the second for the Administrator's more personal, off-script moments |
| 이건 울음을 그치게 하는 장부가 아니다. 이건 울음을 기억하는 장부다. | **"This is not a ledger that stops the weeping. This is a ledger that remembers it."** *(ch.48 — the book's closing thesis; render exactly this way, it is the title's payoff)* |
| 나도 잃어봤어 | **"I've lost someone too."** *(ch.41 — the man's central line; keep it this plain, do not elevate the diction)* |

## 7. File format — match exactly

Each translated chapter file must keep this structure:

```
---
title: Chapter N. <English Chapter Title>
series: The Weeping Ledger, Part <One/Two/Three/Four> · <Part English Title as mapped in section 1>
---

# Chapter N. <English Chapter Title>

> _Previously_ — <translated recap>

<translated body, preserving every `---` scene break and every *italic*
reflection>

*(Continued in Chapter N+1.)*
```

For chapter 48 (the final chapter), end with `*(The End.)*` instead of a
"Continued in" line, matching the Korean's `*(끝)*`. Chapters 12, 24, and 36
additionally close each Part — keep their existing `*(Part Two continues in
Chapter N+1.)*`-style Korean markers (`(2부에 계속)`, `(3부에 계속)`,
`(4부에 계속)`) translated as **"(Continued in Part Two.)"**,
**"(Continued in Part Three.)"**, **"(Continued in Part Four.)"**
respectively, in place of the ordinary "Continued in Chapter N+1" line.
