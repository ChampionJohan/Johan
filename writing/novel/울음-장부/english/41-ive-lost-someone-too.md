# Chapter 41. I've Lost Someone Too

> _Previously_ — For the first time, Brother went to find the man at
> the flowerbed on his own. Asked "Why do you still not trust me?"
> Brother finally said the real reason: "If I believe, and it ends up
> like it did with Father again… I don't think I could bear it this
> time. Not really."

I still remember the air of that afternoon. It was warm. But
Brother's voice made that warmth feel heavy.

I had thought I already knew Brother's fear. But hearing it that day,
straight from his own mouth, was an entirely different thing.

Brother's words seemed to hang there in the air. I could tell, just
from the shake in his voice, how long that fear had been pressed
down inside him.

He was silent a long while after hearing Brother's words.

*I watched that silence from a distance. I knew it wasn't a dismissive
silence. It was a silence that was weighing something.*

I held my breath and waited to see how long that weight would go on.

Evening was drawing near. The sky had begun to take on a pale orange.
I watched that light touch Brother's face. His expression was far more
open than usual.

The bucket rope swayed in the wind and creaked softly. I heard that
sound unusually loud.

I stood a little way off. Speon came quietly and stood beside me. He
said nothing. We both just watched that spot.

The wind stirred the trees by the well. Brother sat through that
silence and stayed right there. He didn't run. That alone told me how
far Brother had come.

I saw that Brother's feet, this time, didn't move even a little. The
old Brother would already have turned and walked away. Watching those
feet, I understood how many moments Brother had learned, one by one,
to bear.

Only then did I notice my own hand was gripping the edge of my
clothes. I'd been tensing up without knowing it.

*I realized then, for the first time, that I was afraid of this moment
almost as much as Brother was. If Brother broke, I felt I would break
too.*

"…I know that feeling," he said at last.

The answer felt strangely simple to me — strange enough to feel wrong.
Brother must have felt the same, because his brow creased slightly.

"…How would you know that?" Brother asked. There was still an edge in
it.

I heard that edge still left in Brother's voice. But underneath it, I
could also feel — for the first time — that there was a real question
there.

There was no trace of anger on his face. Watching that, I thought he
must be someone who had already taken that sharp tone of Brother's
more times than could be counted. Not someone who simply didn't get
hurt by it — someone who looked past it, to what lay beneath.

Watching that, I thought of the first time we met Father. Father, too,
must have been able to come this far only because there had been
someone who accepted our anger exactly as it was.

Thinking that, I felt strangely at ease. We weren't the only ones who
needed a place like that.

---

He said nothing for a while. In that silence, I felt he was working to
bring something out that had been pressed down a long time. The wind
by the well stirred the leaves once more and passed on.

"…Because I've lost someone too," he said.

Brother's face showed surprise at that.

I watched Brother's expression change. He'd always been someone who
thought his own grief was the largest thing in the world. But in that
moment, for the first time, Brother was facing someone else's loss.

Brother's lips moved, as if to say something, then closed again. I
felt, in that, that Brother was for the first time thinking of the
other person before his own question.

"…What did you lose?"

That question hung in the air a moment. I couldn't tell whether he was
avoiding an answer, or truly choosing one.

He looked off into the distance for a moment.

I watched his eyes. Even looking off into the distance, they seemed to
hold something less like grief than an old resolve.

*In that moment I thought of everything we had lost. Mother. The old
man who had fallen in Brick Hollow. People we'd parted from without
even learning their names. I hadn't known, before that, exactly how
heavy the word losing could be.*

"…It would take a long time to explain," he said. "But I can tell you
one thing. I'm not going to promise you I won't lose you again. That's
a promise I couldn't keep. But I can promise you one thing instead.
When it happens, I won't leave you alone."

*The moment I heard that, tears came to me, strangely. I didn't
understand it then — why he didn't say it wouldn't hurt. I understand
it now. He wasn't someone who gave false comfort.*

I wiped my tears and looked at Brother. His eyes were red too. Neither
of us could say exactly why those words had shaken us so much.

"…Aren't you going to tell me who you lost?" Brother asked.

He closed his eyes for a moment and opened them again. Even in that
short moment, something heavy seemed to pass across his face.

"…Someday," he said. "Not now. Right now is your story's time, not
mine."

Brother nodded. He didn't ask further. I thought the way Brother
accepted that answer was a way he never could have imagined, once.

*Strangely, that answer settled well with me. It was the first time I
felt that he wasn't trying to cover our grief with his own.*

In that moment I thought of the Ledger back in Haven. The Ledger
always gave the answer first. But this man gave his own silence first,
instead of an answer. I began, dimly, that day, to feel that this
difference was the most important one in the whole of this book.

I looked at Brother in that moment. He didn't press any further. The
old Brother would surely have asked again. I felt that Brother was
learning, on his own, how to stop.

I glanced up at Speon's face beside me. Something like an old grief
was passing across it.

"…Is this the first time you're hearing it, Speon?" I asked him,
almost in a whisper.

"…No," Speon said. "I already know. But it's just as heavy, no matter
how many times I hear it."

"…How do you bear it?"

"…I don't bear it," Speon said. "I just stay beside it."

Hearing that answer, I felt I understood again why Speon had been able
to keep this well for so long. Not enduring — staying beside. I set
that difference into my heart.

---

Brother sat there in silence for a long while. I knew he was choosing
his next question. It was a question he had held back for a very long
time.

The sky was turning redder and redder. Watching that red light touch
Brother's face, I knew he was finally about to bring out the heaviest
question of all.

"…Then why did our father end up like that?" Brother asked. His voice
shook. "Why didn't you stop it?"

"…I can't give you that answer either," he said. "If I could give it
to you, I already would have."

"…Then what can you give me?"

"…This, right now," he said. And he held out his hand. "Sitting
beside you."

Brother looked at that hand for a long time. From a distance, I saw
his shoulders shaking.

Brother took the hand.

*I set down that answer as the last sentence of this chapter. I knew,
then, that the answer this whole book had been searching for had never
existed as a sentence to begin with. That answer was shaped like the
hand he held out. And Brother, that day, answered by taking it.*
