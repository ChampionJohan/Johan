# Chapter 41. I've Lost Someone Too

> _Previously_ — For the first time, Brother went to find the man at the flowerbed on his own. Asked "Why do you still not trust me?", Brother said, for the first time, the real reason: "If I believe, and it ends up like it did with Father again… I don't think I could bear it this time. Not really."

He was silent for a long time after hearing Brother's words.

*I watched that silence from a distance. I knew it wasn't a dismissive silence. It was a silence that was weighing something.*

The wind shook the tree by the well. Brother stayed right there through that silence. He didn't run. That alone told me how far Brother had come.

"…I know that feeling," he said at last.

"…How would you know that?" Brother asked. There was still an edge in it.

---

"…Because I've lost someone too," he said.

Brother's face showed surprise at that.

"…What did you lose?"

He looked off into the distance for a moment.

"…It would take a long time to explain," he said. "But I can tell you one thing. I'm not going to promise you I won't lose you again. That's a promise I couldn't keep. But I can promise you one thing instead. When it happens, I won't leave you alone."

*The moment I heard that, tears came to me, strangely. I didn't understand it then. Why he didn't say it wouldn't hurt. I understand it now. He wasn't someone who gave false comfort.*

"…Aren't you going to tell me who you lost?" Brother asked.

"…Someday," he said. "Not now. Right now is your story's time, not mine."

*Strangely, that answer settled well with me. It was the first time I felt that he wasn't trying to cover our grief with his own.*

---

"…Then why did our father end up like that?" Brother asked. His voice shook. "Why didn't you stop it?"

"…I can't give you that answer either," he said. "If I could give it to you, I already would have."

"…Then what can you give me?"

"…This, right now," he said. And he held out his hand. "Sitting beside you."

Brother looked at that hand for a long time. From a distance, I saw his shoulders shaking.

Brother took the hand.

*I set down that answer as the last sentence of this chapter. I knew, then, that the answer this whole book had been searching for had never existed as a sentence to begin with. That answer was shaped like the hand he held out. And Brother, that day, answered by taking it.*
