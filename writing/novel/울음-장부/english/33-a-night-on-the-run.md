# Chapter 33. A Night on the Run

> _Previously_ — The searching light appeared again. It passed them by, thankfully, but guilt lingered behind that relief. When Father asked, "Am I a burden?" Jeteo answered, "We're going together."

Two nights later, this time it wasn't a light that came, but people.

That day had been unusually peaceful. Father even picked a small wildflower growing by the roadside and handed it to me. It was only later that the fact that such peace had given no warning at all of that evening felt more frightening still.

Jeteo had been unusually on edge from early that evening. He stopped and looked back, again and again. I thought it was just habit. Looking back on it now, I think he must already have sensed something.

Father, too, had been unusually quiet that day. I still wonder, even now, whether that was pure coincidence, or whether all three of us had somehow already sensed it coming.

Footsteps. Several of them. We hurried to hide ourselves behind dry brush.

*I gripped Father's hand as hard as I could, right then. I wanted him to understand that we had to stay quiet. But the unfamiliar sound frightened him instead, and his body began to shake.*

The brush's dry branches brushed our clothes and rustled. Even that small sound felt too loud to me, and I even breathed carefully.

Voices came to us. Low, businesslike.

Their shadows stretched long under the moonlight. I counted them. Three.

"…They said there was a reading around here?"

"…Two candidates for Reassignment review. And there's a report that one COMPLETE record has strayed."

*My body froze the moment I heard that. I understood that "one COMPLETE record has strayed" meant Father. For the first time, I felt in my whole body that even Father, to this city, was not a person but an entry.*

I glanced sideways at Father's face. He didn't understand the meaning of that conversation at all — he simply held our hands tight. I thought his not knowing was, if anything, a mercy. If Father had known he was being called a "strayed record," he wouldn't have been able to bear it.

---

"…Shh." Jeteo carefully covered Father's mouth with his hand, as gently as he could. "You have to be quiet. Please."

Father nodded, eyes wide. He didn't know why, but he seemed to sense the danger.

Father's hand kept gripping mine tight. I felt a faint tremor in it. A body that had forgotten its name, forgotten faces, still remembered fear whole and entire.

I wanted to calm that trembling. But I was shaking too, and I couldn't tell any more who was calming whom.

The footsteps drew closer and closer. Three people in Haven uniforms passed by. Each carried some bright instrument in hand.

Their pace wasn't hurried at all. That very unhurriedness frightened me more. It was the walk of people certain they would find us in the end.

*I didn't know what the instrument was. But I knew it was meant to find us.*

Jeteo gave me a slight push, tucking me behind him. Even in that moment, it was a gesture that put me first. I was used to that gesture, but that day it ached in a way I hadn't felt before.

From behind Jeteo's back, I gripped Father's hand tighter too. The three of us were strung together in a line like that. I understood, in that moment, that there was nothing at all left for us to do except hold on to each other.

*I knew Jeteo was afraid too. But he was trying to carry all of that fear by himself. The way he always had.*

I looked at Jeteo's back. His shoulders, holding his breath, trembled faintly. Seeing that, I reached out and lightly caught the hem of his shirt. I didn't say anything. I just wanted him to know I was here too.

---

One of them stopped right in front of the brush where we hid.

Time seemed to stop right there. I was afraid the sound of my own heartbeat would carry outside my body. I could feel sweat rising on Jeteo's hand too.

I squeezed my eyes shut in that moment and thought of our last night in Brick Hollow — the night we held our breath, hiding from the taskmasters' footsteps. That night and this one felt exactly the same. We were still surviving the same way.

I thought my breath would stop. Jeteo pulled Father closer, holding him tighter.

The person shone the instrument toward the brush. Light swept past right beside us.

I even had the illusion that the light was brushing my skin. It wasn't hot or cold — but every hair on my body stood on end.

*I squeezed my eyes shut in that moment. For the first time in my life, I prayed something — not in words, just in my heart. I didn't even know whether it was a prayer. I just prayed.*

In that brief instant, every one of them came back to me at once — the nights in Brick Hollow, the alleys of Haven, all the nights we'd ever run. We had always been hiding from something. I couldn't have imagined, in that moment, when it would ever end.

*I understood something strange, then. We had always been running from something. But even inside that running, we had kept holding on to each other, all along. For the first time, it occurred to me that maybe that wasn't running at all. That maybe it was something else.*

The person soon turned and walked off in another direction.

Even then, I couldn't open my eyes. Only after the footsteps had faded completely did I open them, carefully. My fingertips were numb and tingling. I'd been gripping too hard, for too long.

---

Even after the footsteps had completely faded, we couldn't move from that spot for a long while.

My legs had gone numb, and I couldn't stand. Jeteo seemed the same — he didn't get up right away either, just sat there among the brush. Father was the first to pull himself together, and he helped the two of us to our feet.

Father's hand felt unusually steady in that moment. He had no memory left. But his body still knew how to lift his children up.

Jeteo finally let out a breath and looked up at the sky.

"…Where are you," Jeteo said, low but clear. I knew who he was speaking to. "You don't show up for this, but you showed up back then?"

There was no answer. The night sky was just the night sky.

Father watched him quietly. He couldn't have understood any of it. And yet he came and stood beside Jeteo and set a quiet hand on his shoulder. Jeteo didn't shake it off.

Watching that hand, I felt a strange comfort. A man who had lost his name, lost his memory, still knew how to set one hand beside someone breaking under an answer that never came. I thought that might be the deepest thing left inside Father.

I watched the two silhouettes for a long time. A son who'd gotten no answer, and a father who couldn't even understand the question, standing side by side. And strangely, that picture didn't feel only sad to me.

*I set down that silence as the last line of this chapter. I understood how fair Jeteo's question was. I couldn't give him an answer. No one could. But even inside that silence, I held on to one single fact — that we were still alive.*
