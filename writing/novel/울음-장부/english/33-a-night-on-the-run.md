# Chapter 33. A Night on the Run

> _Previously_ — The searching light appeared again. It passed them by, thankfully, but guilt lingered behind that relief. When Father asked, "Am I a burden?" Jeteo answered, "We're going together."

Two nights later, this time it wasn't a light that came, but people.

Footsteps. Several of them. We hurried to hide ourselves behind dry brush.

*I gripped Father's hand as hard as I could. I wanted him to understand that we had to stay quiet. But the unfamiliar sound frightened him instead, and he began to shake.*

Voices came to us. Low, businesslike.

"…They said there was a reading around here?"

"…Two candidates for Reassignment review. And there's a report that one COMPLETE record has strayed."

*My body froze the moment I heard that. I knew "one COMPLETE record has strayed" meant Father. For the first time, I felt in my whole body that even Father, to this city, was not a person but an entry.*

---

"…Shh." Jeteo carefully covered Father's mouth with his hand, as gently as he could. "You have to be quiet. Please."

Father nodded, eyes wide. He didn't know why, but he seemed to sense the danger.

The footsteps came closer and closer. Three people in Haven uniforms passed by. Each carried some bright instrument in hand.

*I didn't know what the instrument was. But I knew it was meant to find us.*

Jeteo gave me a slight push, tucking me behind him. Even in that moment, it was a gesture that put me first. I was used to that gesture, but that day it ached in a way I hadn't felt before.

*I knew he was afraid too. But he was trying to carry all of that fear by himself. The way he always had.*

---

One of them stopped right in front of the brush where we hid.

I thought my breath would stop. Jeteo pulled Father closer, holding him tighter.

The person shone the instrument toward the brush. Light swept past right beside us.

*I squeezed my eyes shut in that moment. For the first time in my life, I prayed something — not in words, just in my heart. I didn't even know whether it was a prayer. I just prayed.*

The person soon turned and walked off in another direction.

---

Even after the footsteps had completely faded, we couldn't move from that spot for a long while.

At last Jeteo let out a breath and looked up at the sky.

"…Where are you," Jeteo said, low but clear. I knew who he was speaking to. "You don't show up for this, but you showed up back then?"

There was no answer. The night sky was just the night sky.

*I set down that silence as the last line of this chapter. I understood how fair Jeteo's question was. I couldn't give him an answer. No one could. But even inside that silence, I held on to one single fact — that we were still alive.*
