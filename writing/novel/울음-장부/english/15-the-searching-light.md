# Chapter 15. The Searching Light

> _Previously_ — At a tent camp in the Wilderness, the brother and sister met Noa and her children. Noa was someone who had left Haven of her own will rather than have her sorrow "resolved." When they showed her the paper stamped COMPLETE, it came out that the resistance of remembering names was already underway all across the Wilderness.

At dawn, one of the children woke us.

"…Look, there's a light," the child said, pointing at the sky.

I opened my eyes. Low in the sky, a small light moved slowly. It looked like a star, but it wasn't. It hung too low, and it moved too evenly.

The night air was cold. I sat wrapped in my blanket, watching that light. I couldn't tell whether my fingertips were trembling from the cold or from fear.

*The instant I saw it, a chill ran down my back. I didn't know what it was. But I knew, from the look on Noa's face, that it was nothing good.*

My brother saw it too. I felt his whole body go rigid, all at once. Without a word, he took my hand and pulled me low. His hand was cold as ice.

Around us, one by one, the other adults were already getting up, gathering the children in. No one shouted. Everyone moved quietly, practiced, as if this weren't the first time.

*That quiet frightened me more. A danger that came with shouting wasn't unfamiliar to me — that had been Brick Hollow, every day. But a danger this quiet, that no one raised their voice against, was something entirely new.*

Noa called the children's names quietly, one by one. Not loudly. Just a calling to check where each of them was. Even in that brief, frightened moment, hearing their names called put me strangely at ease.

*I understood then. Being called by name in a moment of fear, and having your case received in a moment of comfort, were two entirely different kinds of care.*

Rachel came up quietly beside me and gripped my arm. I could feel the small tremor of her hand against mine. I gripped it back. I said nothing at all. But holding that hand, somehow, I was a little less afraid.

---

"…It's a searching light," Noa said in a low voice, quietly pushing the children into the tent. "Haven sends those out sometimes. When it's looking for Reassignment candidates."

"…Is it because of us?" my brother asked.

"…I don't know," Noa said. "But there's no need to find out. Put out the fire."

We doused the fire quickly and crouched behind the tent. I gripped my brother's hand tight. He gripped mine just as tight.

*In that instant, the night we crossed the boundary stones of Brick Hollow came back to me exactly as it had been. In place of the crack of whips, there was that silent light. I found it more frightening. Because it made no sound.*

I lifted my head just slightly and looked at the light again. It moved without a sound, slowly crossing the sky. The motion of it looked as if something were being combed through, very carefully, inch by inch.

*Watching that motion, I felt something strange. There was no hurry in that light at all. And the fact that it wasn't hurrying was somehow more chilling than if it had been. Not hurrying was the ease of something already certain it could find whatever it was looking for.*

---

The light passed slowly over our heads once. It didn't stop. It went on past and vanished into the dark.

Only after a long while did Noa let out her breath.

"…It's gone," she said.

Only then did I let out the breath I'd been holding, long and slow. When I opened my hand, there were little half-moon marks where my nails had dug into my palm. Only then did I realize how hard I'd been gripping my brother's hand.

"…Does this happen often?" I asked.

"…Sometimes," Noa said. "Not a few of the people living out here came from that city. So we're always careful of it."

*Hearing that, I understood that the Wilderness was not, as Haven seemed to believe, empty. This was a place where the people Haven had missed had gathered to live.*

My brother asked quietly.

"…So does that light keep circling this area?"

"…There's no pattern to it," Noa said. "Sometimes it won't come for months. Sometimes it comes several days running. So we just stay ready, always."

"…Ready how?"

"…Putting the fire out fast. Keeping the children quiet. That's about it," Noa said. "It isn't much. But it's all we can do."

"…What happens if it catches you?" I asked.

Noa's face went thoughtful, choosing her words.

"…It might be better not to know," she said. "But not asking might be more frightening, so I'll tell you. If it catches you, you're just taken back to Haven. And after that… no one has ever heard about that person again."

At that, my brother's hand closed into a fist.

"…So going back means becoming a Reassignment candidate all over again?" my brother asked.

"…Probably," Noa said. "I don't know exactly, myself. But the fact that word of the people who go back stops completely — that can't mean anything good."

Listening to that, I thought of the day the phrase "candidate for Reassignment" had first been spoken to us. That phrase had always worn a gentle face, then and now, hiding something terrible underneath it.

*Hearing that, I thought of the COMPLETE stamp again. I understood then that the people of the Wilderness lived carrying the same fear we did, every single day.*

I looked at my brother's face. He was still watching the spot where the light had disappeared. I didn't ask him what he was thinking. I already knew, by then, that some thoughts only grow heavier once you put them into words.

---

That night, my brother asked Noa something for the first time.

"…If we stay here, will we be putting the rest of you in danger?"

Noa looked at him for a long moment.

"…A little," she said honestly. "But sharing danger is just how it works out here. We all came here the same way."

"…What do you mean, you all came here the same way?" I asked.

"…The first time I stepped into someone else's tent, I was a danger to them too," Noa said. "But they didn't drive me out. So that's what I learned to do too."

*Hearing that, I felt that this tent camp was like one long chain. Someone had taken on the danger and let it in, and because of that, the next person could take on the danger too, and let the next person in after that.*

"…Still, wouldn't it be better if we left?"

"…That's for you to decide," Noa said. "But as for me, I'd choose to carry the danger together over driving away someone dangerous. That's what's different between us and Haven."

My brother said nothing to that. But I saw something like a decision cross his face.

"…Thank you," my brother finally said. "We don't want to make you carry more danger because of us. But… let us stay a little longer, for now."

Noa smiled a little at that.

"…That's all I need to hear," she said. "Out here, even the words 'let us stay' are a good thing to say."

*I set that line down as the last sentence of this chapter. Haven was a place that got rid of danger. This Wilderness was a place that shared it. For the first time, I understood that which place was safer might matter less than which place was more alive.*
