# Chapter 27. Before the Firelight

> _Previously_ — The brother and sister met Temel. Along with his words — "There's no wasted step in the search" — they spotted, on the fourth night, a small light far off in the distance.

We crept toward that light with careful steps.

The grass brushed quietly underfoot. I set each step down heel to toe, trying to kill even that sound.

A faint scent of smoke was mixed into the night air. The instant I caught it, my heart reacted before my mind did. There are things the body knows faster than thought.

A small campfire. Someone sat beside it. His back was to us, so I couldn't see his face. But his hand kept moving — a motion of counting something in the air.

The back was bent. The set of the shoulders was unfamiliar. And yet I couldn't tear my eyes away from that silhouette.

The hand moved with a steady rhythm, like a motion carved into the body long ago.

It never stopped, never hurried. That regularity seemed, somehow, less like a person than a clock.

There was no wind that night. The flame burned straight up, hardly flickering at all. I found that stillness strangely frightening.

A few dry branches lay neatly beside the fire. Not piled carelessly — laid side by side, matched for length.

That neatness troubled me. I couldn't understand why someone alone would keep things arranged so carefully.

I slowed my steps. My brother did too. We both tried not to make a sound. Even the sound of grass underfoot felt like something to be careful of.

We stopped every few steps. The wish to get closer and the wish to draw this moment out a little longer were both in me at once. I felt those two wishes fighting each other as I walked.

Temel walked half a step behind us. He didn't lead. It was the distance of someone who knew this walk belonged to us.

*The moment I saw that hand, I stopped walking. I felt my brother stop at the same instant. We gripped each other's hands tight, right there.*

The closer we came, the more a small sound reached us. A voice made almost without moving the lips.

"…two thousand nine hundred ninety-seven, two thousand nine hundred ninety-eight…"

*Hearing those numbers, the strength went out of my legs. I knew exactly where that number stood. It was nearly at three thousand. I understood, for the first time, that Father had not once, not a single time since that night, made it all the way to three thousand.*

I tried to measure the distance between those two numbers — two thousand nine hundred ninety-eight, and three thousand. Barely two. And yet Father had failed to cross those two for years.

I stood there and folded down my fingers. One, two. That was all. That short distance had become, for Father, a wall he could not climb.

A moment later, the count started over from the beginning. One, two, three.

I listened to that return and couldn't say a word.

*I understood then. Father hadn't failed to reach three thousand. Every time he came close, he was dragged back to that night, and made to start again from the beginning, every time.*

His voice was low and cracked. And yet the accent — the accent alone — was exactly the one I remembered from Brick Hollow.

I felt years of time collapse in an instant, hearing that single accent.

Temel stopped too, and listened to the sound in silence. He said nothing. I understood that silence as the silence of someone who had already lived through moments like this many times before.

Even Temel's breathing went very quiet. I understood that quiet as a kindness meant for us.

Temel's hand rested on my shoulder for just a moment. No words. But the weight of that hand alone told me: nothing should be rushed now.

---

Temel moved ahead of us, quietly.

"…Excuse me," Temel said, low. "Are you all right?"

The voice was very small. And yet it carried clearly through that stillness.

The man's hand stopped. The counting stopped with it. Even that stillness felt as sharp as a sound.

The man slowly turned his head.

The movement was slow, like a neck creaking.

That short instant felt very long to me. For those few seconds, I forgot how to breathe.

*The moment I saw that face, my breath caught. It was a face that hadn't aged, hadn't changed. I saw that face for the first time since the last night in Brick Hollow. It was Father.*

I reached for my brother's hand in that instant. He was already reaching for mine. Our hands found each other exactly, in the dark.

The firelight flickered faintly across that face. The eyebrows, the nose, the lips. I ran my eyes over each of them again, checking, as if confirming.

My brother had frozen in place too. He couldn't say a word. I saw years' worth of feeling pass across his face all at once — anger, relief, fear, and fear again.

His two hands opened weakly at his sides, then closed again. Hands that wanted to grab hold of something but couldn't find what.

Temel turned quietly to look at us. He seemed about to say something, then simply stepped back instead. A step back that left this moment to us.

Even so, Temel didn't disappear completely. He stood still a few steps off in the dark. A place from which to watch without stepping in.

I still remember that step back. That there are moments when giving someone space matters as much as staying beside them — I learned that for the first time then.

The campfire crackled once. In that moment, it sounded like the loudest sound in the world.

A single spark leapt up and vanished into the dark.

I followed that spark's vanishing point with my eyes for a long time. Where it rose to, nothing remained.

The night sky hung low. The stars looked unusually close. I felt that night stepping quietly aside for us.

I felt my own heart beating along with that sound.

---

Father looked at us. But there was nothing in that gaze. It was the gaze one gives a stranger.

Seeing that gaze, my breath caught. We had walked for years to get here, and what we met was strangeness.

Father's clothes were worn. A different cloth from what we knew. His shoes had split soles too, just like ours. That long walk had settled itself into his body, exactly the way it had settled into ours.

A thin layer of dried dirt sat on the backs of his hands. Hands that hadn't been washed in a long time.

"…Who are you?" Father asked. His voice was exactly the same. And yet in that voice there was no weight toward us at all.

I couldn't say a word in that moment. My fingertips were trembling.

Heat rose in my throat. No words came out past it. I just stood and looked at Father's face.

I looked at his face again. The shape of his eyebrows, the small scar on his left cheek — all the same. But the look held in that face belonged entirely to someone else.

My brother stepped forward instead, one step. Dry leaves crunched underfoot.

"…Do you… know us?" he asked. His voice had completely broken down.

Father shook his head. His face was apologetic. But there was strangeness in that apology too.

The shake of his head was slow, careful — the movement of someone unsure even of that much.

My brother stood motionless for several seconds. I was watching only his back. His shoulders sank, just slightly.

There wasn't a breath of wind that night. Only the campfire's smoke rose straight up.

I followed that smoke with my eyes. Somewhere at a certain height, it scattered without a sound.

I thought, that night, for the first time, that some things don't disappear — they simply travel to where they can no longer be seen.

I wanted to reach out and touch that shoulder. But I couldn't move. Neither my brother nor I had the strength, in that moment, even to touch each other.

"…I don't know," Father said. "I'm sorry. I… don't really know who I am, either."

Father's voice trembled slightly at the end of that sentence. Something other than apology was mixed into that tremor. I didn't know what it was, then.

I hesitated, again and again, over whether to reach out my hand. It felt like taking a stranger's hand. It also felt like taking the most familiar hand in the world.

My fingertips paused in midair for a moment. Father waited quietly for that hand. He didn't hurry it.

Only then did I reach out and take Father's hand. It was trembling. It carried the exact same warmth as the hand that had held mine the night we crossed the boundary stones, long ago.

I crossed years of time on that single warmth.

Father's hand was cold at first. It warmed, little by little, while I held it.

I couldn't tell whether that warmth was mine or Father's. I didn't want to tell the difference either.

"…I'm Elpis," I said. My voice cracked. "I'm Elpis. I'm your daughter."

Only after I'd said it did the tears finally come.

"…I'm Jeteo," my brother managed to say. His voice shook. "I'm your son."

My brother said it with his fists clenched tight. I knew that was the hand that was holding back tears.

Father quietly tried the name out too. "…Jeteo." Slowly, like someone learning an unfamiliar sound.

My brother heard that pronunciation and couldn't say anything. He just watched the shape of Father's mouth.

The firelight lit only half my brother's face. The other half stayed sunk in darkness. I couldn't see what expression he was wearing in that dark half.

Father's hand gave a very faint twitch in that moment. His eyes still saw nothing they recognized. But that hand alone was responding to something.

*I watched that tremor exactly. I didn't know, then, whether it was memory or just a habit left in the body. I still don't know exactly, even now. But I decided to hold onto that single tremor.*

My brother must have seen it too. He said nothing, but his eyes never left that hand.

I stroked that hand gently. The knuckles were thicker than they used to be. A hand that had held something and walked for a long time.

There were new calluses in the palm. I didn't know what road they'd been made on. But I wanted to feel each of them out with my fingertips all the same.

*I thought those calluses were a list of the days Father had passed through. I couldn't read that list. Still, I didn't lift my fingertips away from them.*

I held that hand a little longer. Father didn't pull it back.

Somewhere far off, a night bird cried once, briefly, and stopped. Besides that, there was no other sound at all.

My brother came quietly closer too and took Father's other hand. Father didn't shake that one off either. We sat like that for a while, saying nothing, just holding hands in front of the fire.

The firelight lit each of our three faces in turn. No one broke that silence first.

The campfire was slowly dying down. No one added more wood. Even that fading felt like something none of us wanted to disturb.

*I set that answer down as this chapter's last line. In that moment I learned, for the first time, that finding and losing could happen at once. Father was there before us. And Father was, at the same time, farther from us than anyone.*
