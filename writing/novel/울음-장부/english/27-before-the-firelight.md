# Chapter 27. Before the Firelight

> _Previously_ — The siblings met Temel. Along with his words — "There's no wasted walking in the finding of someone" — on the fourth night they spotted a small light far off in the distance.

We crept carefully toward the light.

A faint smell of smoke was mixed into the night air. The moment I caught it, my heart reacted before I did. There are things the body notices before the mind catches up.

It was a small campfire. Someone sat beside it. His back was to us, so I couldn't see his face. But his hand kept moving — the gesture of counting something in the air.

The gesture moved in a steady rhythm. Like something carved into the body long ago.

It was a windless night. The flame burned straight up, barely flickering at all. That stillness felt strangely frightening to me.

I slowed my steps. So did Jeteo. Both of us tried hard not to make a sound. We were careful even of the grass under our feet.

*The instant I saw that gesture, I stopped walking. I felt Jeteo stop at the same moment. We stood there, gripping each other's hands.*

The closer we came, the more we could hear a small sound — made with lips that barely moved.

"…two thousand nine hundred ninety-seven, two thousand nine hundred ninety-eight…"

*My legs went weak the moment I heard those numbers. I knew exactly where in the count that was. It was almost, almost three thousand. And I understood, for the first time, that since that night, Father had never once made it all the way to three thousand.*

His voice was low and cracked. But the cadence of it — that alone was exactly the same as the one I remembered from Brick Hollow.

Temel stopped too, listening. He said nothing. I sensed that his silence was the silence of someone who had already lived through moments like this, more than once.

For just a moment, Temel's hand rested on my shoulder. No words. But the weight of that hand alone told me: from here on, nothing should be said in a hurry.

---

Temel went ahead of us, quietly.

"…Excuse me." Temel spoke low. "Are you all right?"

The man's hand stopped. The counting stopped with it. Even that stillness was distinct enough to seem almost audible.

He turned his head, slowly.

That brief moment felt very long to me. For those few seconds, I forgot how to breathe.

*My breath stopped the instant I saw that face. It hadn't aged, hadn't changed much at all. I was seeing that face for the first time since our last night in Brick Hollow. It was Father.*

Jeteo froze right where he stood too. He couldn't say a word. I watched years' worth of feeling pass over his face all at once — anger, relief, fear, and then fear again.

Temel glanced back at us, quietly. He seemed about to say something, then simply stepped back — a step that left this moment to us.

I still remember that step back, even now. I learned then, for the first time, that there are moments when someone stepping out of the way matters just as much as someone staying beside you.

The campfire crackled. In that moment it sounded like the loudest sound in the world.

I felt my own heart beating in time with it.

---

Father looked at us. But there was nothing in his eyes. The eyes of someone looking at strangers.

Father's clothes were worn — a different cloth from what we remembered. His shoes, like ours, had soles split open. That long walking had left its mark on his body too, exactly as it had on ours.

"…Who are you?" Father asked. The voice was exactly the same. But there was no weight in it toward us at all.

I couldn't say anything at all in that moment. My fingertips trembled.

I looked at Father's face again. The shape of his eyebrows, the small scar on his left cheek — all exactly the same. But the look inside that face belonged, completely, to someone else.

Jeteo stepped forward in my place.

"…Do you… know us?" Jeteo asked. His voice had come apart completely.

Father shook his head. His face was apologetic. But even in that apology, there was strangeness.

Jeteo couldn't move for several seconds. I was watching his back — nothing else. His shoulders dropped, just slightly.

Watching those shoulders, I wanted to reach out. But I couldn't move. In that moment, neither Jeteo nor I had even the strength to touch the other.

"…I don't know," Father said. "I'm sorry. I don't… even know who I am, myself."

I hesitated, again and again, over whether to reach out. It felt like taking a stranger's hand, and also like taking the most familiar hand in the world.

Only then did I reach out and take Father's hand. It trembled. It held the exact same warmth as the hand that had held mine, long ago, the night we crossed the boundary stones.

"…I'm Elpis," I said. My voice cracked. "I'm Elpis. I'm your daughter."

"…I'm Jeteo," Jeteo managed to say. His voice shook. "I'm your son."

Father repeated that name back, quietly. "…Jeteo." Slowly, like someone learning to shape an unfamiliar sound.

Jeteo said nothing, hearing it. He just watched the shape of Father's mouth, quietly.

Father's hand gave the faintest twitch, right then. His eyes still recognized nothing at all. But that hand — that alone was responding to something.

*I saw that tremor clearly. I didn't know, then, whether it was memory or just a habit left over in the body. I still don't know exactly, even now. But I decided to hold on to that one tremor.*

I stroked that hand, gently. The knuckles were thicker than before. It was the hand of someone who had been walking a long time, holding something all the way.

I held that hand a little longer. Father didn't pull it away.

Jeteo came quietly and took Father's other hand. Father didn't shake that one off either. For a while, we sat there in front of the fire in silence, just holding hands.

*I set down that answer as the last line of this chapter. I understood, in that moment, for the first time, that finding and losing could happen at once. Father was right in front of us. And Father was farther from us than he had ever been.*
