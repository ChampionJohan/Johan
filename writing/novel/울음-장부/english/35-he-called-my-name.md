# Chapter 35. He Called My Name

> _Previously_ — From the merchant they heard about an old man who had led the Haven search party the wrong way. Without a word, the siblings were both certain who it was. At Father's offhand remark, an expression appeared on Jeteo's face for the first time that was neither anger nor certainty.

A few days later, we were resting by a small stream.

The water ran low and quiet. Sunlight broke into small pieces across its surface. I watched that shimmer for a long time. It was the first peaceful afternoon we'd had, after days of walking as though something were chasing us.

Jeteo sat with his feet in the water, eyes closed. Watching him, I saw the tension leave his face for the first time in a long while. Father was washing his hands a little way off.

Father, washing his hands, suddenly called out to me.

"…Elpis."

My whole body froze in that instant. Jeteo's did too.

The water kept flowing. But even that sound seemed, to me, to stop for a moment. It felt as if every sound in the world had given way to that one name.

We'd told Father my name many times over these days. But that had only ever been introducing myself. This was different. Father had called me. The difference might not have looked like much, but to me it was as large as the whole world.

Jeteo stood frozen too, looking at me. Neither of us could say a word. Only the sound of the water filled the silence between us.

Inside that silence, I watched Jeteo's eyes waver, little by little. His eyes hadn't decided yet whether to be glad, or to be tangled up over the fact that his own name still hadn't come.

---

Father looked startled at his own words, as if they'd surprised him too.

Father stayed frozen there, hand still raised. Water dripped from his fingertips, drop by drop. He didn't even seem to notice.

"…What… did I just say?" Father asked.

"…My name," I managed to say. My voice shook. "You called my name."

My voice was shaking as much as Father's was. I didn't try to hide it. There was no reason to.

Father touched his own mouth with his hand, as if trying to check where the word had come from.

"…How did I know that," Father murmured. "It just… came out on its own."

His eyes held bewilderment, and mixed in with it, something that seemed almost too much to hold. I thought that expression looked like the face of someone whose long-locked door had opened just a crack.

*I looked at Jeteo in that moment. He stood there, unable to say a word. I saw tears well up in his eyes for the first time.*

I went to Father and took his hand.

I looked into Father's eyes. They were still strange eyes. But inside that strangeness, something very small seemed to be nestled there — something that knew me.

"…Say it again," I said.

"…Elpis." Father said it again. This time with certainty. Tears filled his eyes. "Elpis, Elpis…"

Father repeated that name over and over, like a song he'd just learned. His voice trembled more with every repetition. Inside that trembling, I thought I heard something like a very old lock turning open, little by little.

*That name reached past being just my name — it sounded like something Father was saying to himself. As if he were gripping that one name and struggling to find his way back to himself.*

I wrapped Father's hand in both of mine, right then. It was still wet, and cold. I welcomed even that cold. It was a living hand.

Father closed his eyes as he said my name. Behind those closed eyes, I sensed him struggling hard to hold on to something — something like the fraying, almost-severed end of a thread of memory.

---

I closed my eyes for a moment, right there. A memory rose up — our last night in Brick Hollow, Father lifting me up, calling my name. I felt that memory and this moment lay over each other.

"…What about me?" Jeteo asked, his voice trembling. "Do you know my name too?"

Father looked at him for a long time. His face was that of someone reaching for something just out of grasp.

Father's lips moved, several times. No sound came. He looked like someone who had opened a drawer, only to find it empty.

"…I'm sorry," Father said. "That one… hasn't come yet."

I could hear how truly sorry his voice was. I hoped that sorrow might offer Jeteo some small comfort.

Jeteo's face fell. I watched his disappointment plainly. In that brief moment, I couldn't read every feeling passing through his eyes — disappointment, something like shame, and the effort of trying hard to press both of them down.

But strangely, he smiled.

"…It's all right," Jeteo said. "My name can come later. Right now… it's enough that Elpis's name came out."

His voice shook, but it didn't waver. I could tell that steadiness wasn't something he'd forced out of himself — he really did feel that way.

*I was startled to hear that. For the first time, I watched Jeteo accept someone else's joy before his own. In Brick Hollow, in Haven, he had never been someone who looked out for his own share first. But that wasn't because he couldn't take care of himself — it was because he was someone who took care of others first. I confirmed that fact again, that day.*

Father went to Jeteo and wrapped an arm around his shoulders. It was an awkward gesture. But Jeteo didn't shy away from that awkward embrace.

---

That night, Jeteo said to me quietly,

"…I've been gathering evidence this whole time. To protect the name, to remember. But today I understood. What's coming back inside Father — it wasn't because I gathered evidence. It was just… because we were beside him."

"…So evidence doesn't matter anymore?" I asked.

"…No," Jeteo said. "It still matters. But now I know. Evidence alone wouldn't have been enough. Being beside him had to be there too."

I thought of the worn scraps of paper he'd carried close to his chest all this time — the evidence he'd gathered since Brick Hollow. I felt that he was learning to hold something else alongside them, without ever letting them go.

I moved a little closer and sat beside him. He put his arm around my shoulders. A familiar weight I hadn't felt in a while.

I sat quietly beside him. Night insects called somewhere in the distance. Listening to them, I felt anew how enormous a day this had been for us.

The campfire's embers crackled and faded, little by little. Listening to that sound, I felt the day's afterglow still lingering somewhere in my body.

"…Jeteo," I said.

"…Yeah?" he answered.

"…I believe Father will call your name too, someday," I said.

Jeteo looked at me and smiled quietly. He said nothing. But that smile was his answer.

*I set down those words as the last line of this chapter. That night, I watched Jeteo hold two things together for the first time — keeping, and staying beside. I didn't know exactly, then, that this was his first step toward trust.*
