# Chapter 35. He Called My Name

> _Previously_ — From a merchant, we heard about an old man who had led Haven's search party the wrong way. Neither of us said it aloud, but we both knew, without a doubt, who that had to be. At one careless word from Father, something crossed Jeteo's face for the first time — not anger, not certainty, but something in between.

Days later, we were resting by a small stream.

The water ran low and quiet. Sunlight broke into small pieces on its surface. I watched that glimmer for a long time. It was the first peaceful afternoon we'd had after days of walking like we were being chased.

The stream wasn't wide. Anyone could have jumped across it. But the water was clear enough that I could see every stone on the bottom, one by one. I started counting them and gave up. There were too many.

A few small birds, ones I didn't know the name of, landed at the water's edge and flew off again. No one chased them away. I couldn't remember the last time I'd watched something that wasn't being chased.

*I still remember that afternoon with unusual clarity, even now. It felt like an afternoon where nothing would happen. I learned that day that the biggest things happen on afternoons exactly like that.*

Jeteo sat with his feet in the water, eyes closed. Watching him, I saw, for the first time in a long while, the tension gone from his face. Father was a little way off, washing his hands.

Jeteo scooped up a handful of water and washed his face with it. Then he looked up at the sky, his face still wet. I didn't bother checking whether what was on his face was only water.

Father washed his hands for a long time. Longer than he needed to. I understood that he wasn't really washing — he was just touching the water. He kept dipping his hands in and pulling them out, staring at them the whole time.

"…The water's cold," Father said, half to himself.

No one answered. He didn't seem to be waiting for an answer either.

Then, still washing his hands, Father called out to me, without warning.

"…Elpis."

I froze where I sat. So did Jeteo.

He said it completely plainly — the way you'd say "hand me that" or "look over there." That's how people say a name when they're just calling someone. That plainness undid me more than anything else could have.

The stream kept running. But to me, even that sound seemed to stop for an instant. It felt like every sound in the world had made way for that one name.

We had told Father my name many times by then. But that had only ever been an introduction. This was different. This time, Father had called me. The difference looked small. To me, it was as large as the whole world.

*I still find that difference hard to explain, even now. An introduction is information. Being called is a relationship. Information can be written on paper. Being called has to come out from inside a person. That day, that name came out from inside Father.*

Jeteo stood frozen too, watching me. Neither of us could say anything. Only the water filled the silence between us.

In that silence, I watched something waver, little by little, in my brother's eyes. He hadn't decided yet whether to be happy, or whether his heart should tangle over the fact that his own name hadn't come yet.

In that moment I felt sorry for him. Glad, and sorry, at once. That day I learned for the first time that both those feelings could live in one body together.

---

Father looked startled by his own words.

He stood frozen, hand still raised. Water dripped from his fingertips. He didn't seem to notice even that.

I watched the small ripples those drops made as they fell into the stream. One, two, three. Ever since, whenever I remember this moment, those ripples are the first thing that comes back to me.

"…What did I just say?" Father asked.

"…My name," I managed. My voice was shaking. "You called my name."

My voice shook as much as his did. I didn't try to hide the shaking. There was no reason to.

Father touched his own mouth with his hand, as if checking where the words had come from.

Even the feel of the stream against my ankles felt strange in that moment. Every sense in my body had gathered around that one name.

"…How did I know that," Father murmured. "It just… came out on its own."

"…On its own again," I said. I was laughing and crying at the same time. It didn't even strike me as strange, laughing and crying together like that.

"…Again?" Father asked.

"…The song came out on its own too," I said.

Father's face went thoughtful at that.

"…Then it's still in me," Father said. "I just can't bring it out. It's not gone."

*I copied that sentence down exactly, that night, into this ledger. It was the truest thing Father ever said about himself.*

There was bewilderment in Father's eyes, mixed with something close to overwhelm. I thought his expression looked like someone whose long-shut door had opened just a crack.

*In that moment I looked at Jeteo. He stood there unable to say anything. I saw tears gather in his eyes for the first time.*

I went to Father and took his hand.

I looked into his eyes. They were still unfamiliar eyes. But inside that unfamiliarity, something very small seemed to live there now — something that recognized me.

"…Say it again," I said.

"…Elpis." Father said it again. This time with certainty. Tears filled his eyes. "Elpis. Elpis…"

He repeated the name over and over, like a song he'd just learned. His voice trembled more each time. In that trembling, I thought I heard something — a very old lock, turning open a little at a time.

Jeteo turned his back on that sound. I knew he didn't want to be seen crying. Without letting go of Father's hand, I reached my other hand out toward him. He didn't come. But a moment later, his hand settled on top of mine.

*That name sounded, to me, like it went past my own name — like Father was saying something to himself. Like he was holding onto that one name and trying to work his way back to himself.*

In that moment I wrapped both my hands around Father's. It was still cold and damp. Even that cold felt welcome to me. It was a living hand.

There was an old scar on the back of Father's hand. A scar from carrying bricks. I touched it with my fingertip. Father didn't know why it was there.

The scar was shaped like a small crescent moon. I'd seen that shape many times back in Brick Hollow — Father rubbing that spot absently every evening as he washed his hands.

"…Do you know where you got this?" I asked.

"…No idea," Father said.

"…I know," I said. "I'll tell you later."

Father didn't press for more. He just nodded and looked down at the back of his hand once more.

*I didn't tell the story of that scar that day. To tell it, I would have had to start with why Father became what he became in Brick Hollow. I wanted to tell it once Father could bear hearing it himself.*

Father closed his eyes, saying my name. Behind those closed eyes, I could feel him straining to hold onto something — the frayed, still-unbroken end of a thread of memory.

---

The sound of the stream seemed unusually loud in that moment. I listened to it and tried, for a while, not to think anything at all. I wanted to feel this moment with my body alone.

I closed my eyes there for a moment. A memory rose up — the last night in Brick Hollow, Father lifting me up while calling my name. I felt that memory overlap with this one.

That night, lifting me up, Father had said, "…Don't be scared, Elpis." Even then, I'd known it was a lie. But that lie was what kept me alive that night.

"…What about me?" Jeteo asked, his voice shaking. "Do you know my name too?"

Father looked at Jeteo for a long time. His face held something that seemed almost within reach, and yet wasn't.

His mouth moved several times. No sound came out. He looked like someone who'd opened a drawer only to find it empty.

Seeing Father struggle made it hurt more. Father didn't want to disappoint Jeteo, so he was trying to force something into being that wasn't there.

"…It's okay. You don't have to force it," Jeteo said first. But his voice was already wet when he said it.

"…I'm sorry," Father said. "That one… hasn't come yet."

I could hear that Father's sorrow was real. I hoped that sorrow could be some small comfort to Jeteo.

Jeteo's face fell apart. I watched him fall into disappointment, right there. In that brief moment, too many feelings crossed his eyes for me to read them all. Disappointment, shame, and the strain of trying to hold both down.

In that moment, I wanted to be angry on my brother's behalf. Not at Father — at someone, I didn't even know who. I wanted to demand why only one name had come back, why not both.

*I think, in that moment, I understood a little of what it had felt like for Jeteo, every day, looking up at that sky. Something that comes back only partway can sometimes be harder to bear than something that never comes back at all.*

But Jeteo smiled, strangely.

"…It's okay," Jeteo said. "My name can come later. Right now… it's enough that Elpis's did."

His voice shook, but it didn't waver. I could tell that steadiness wasn't something he'd forced out — he really felt it.

*I was startled to hear that. That was the first time I ever saw my brother take someone else's joy before his own. Back in Brick Hollow, and in Haven too, Jeteo had never been someone who looked out for himself first. But that wasn't because he couldn't look after himself — it was because he was someone who looked after others first. That day, I confirmed that fact all over again.*

"…Jeteo," I said. "You don't have to force yourself to be okay."

"…I'm not forcing it," Jeteo said.

"…So a little, then?"

Jeteo looked at me and laughed, briefly.

"…A little," he said.

Father went to Jeteo and put his arms around his shoulders. It was an awkward gesture. But Jeteo didn't pull away from that awkward embrace.

Father patted his back a couple of times. The rhythm was clumsy. But somehow that clumsiness made it feel more real.

"…I don't know your name," Father told him. "But I know your face. I see it every day."

At that, Jeteo leaned his forehead against Father's shoulder for a moment. Just a moment.

I watched the two of them from a little way off. The stream still ran low and quiet. I thought that sound was carving this moment into something, somewhere.

---

That night, Jeteo spoke to me quietly.

"…I kept collecting evidence, this whole time. To protect the name, to remember it. But today I learned something. What's coming back inside Father — it wasn't because I gathered evidence. It was just… because I stayed close."

He took a bundle of paper scraps from inside his coat and spread them on his lap. In the firelight, the writing on them had all faded. Some of it was hard to make out at all anymore.

"…What is all this?" I asked.

"…Everywhere Father's name might turn up, I wrote it down," Jeteo said. "Work-crew numbers, ration slips, taskmasters' names. I thought it might have survived somewhere."

I looked at those papers one by one. Only then did I understand how long he'd been carrying this alone.

"…So you don't need the evidence anymore?" I asked.

"…No," Jeteo said. "I still need it. But now I know. Evidence alone wouldn't have been enough. Staying close had to happen too."

"…You can do both," I said.

Jeteo looked down at the bundle of papers for a moment at that. He ran his fingertips along the edges, then looked at me again.

"…Can you, though," he said.

"…You did today," I said.

Jeteo couldn't answer that for a moment. Then he folded the papers carefully again and tucked them back inside his coat. His hands moved more carefully than before.

I thought of the worn scraps of paper he'd carried deep in his coat all this time — evidence gathered all the way back from Brick Hollow. I felt that he was learning how to hold onto those without letting go, while also holding something new at the same time.

I sat down a little closer to him. He put his arm around my shoulder. It was a familiar weight I hadn't felt in a long time.

Father was already asleep a little way off. Even asleep, his lips moved very slightly now and then. I thought it might be my name. I didn't go check.

I sat quietly beside Jeteo. Somewhere far off, night insects were singing. Listening to them, I felt all over again how enormous a day this had been for us.

The embers of the fire were dying down with small crackling sounds. Listening to that, I could feel the day's afterglow still lingering somewhere in my body.

I tried to write down what had happened that day, then gave up. My hand was shaking too much to write properly. In the end, I wrote down only one name. My own. The name Father had called.

"…Jeteo," I said.

"…Yeah?" he answered.

"…I believe Father will call your name too, someday," I said.

"…Why do you believe that?" he asked.

"…I don't know," I said. "I just do."

"…That's not based on anything," he said.

"…Yeah," I said. "Nothing at all."

*I didn't know exactly what I meant by that, back then. I understand what it means to believe without grounds far better now than I did then. It's not that you believe because there's no evidence. You believe before all the evidence has gathered.*

Jeteo looked at me and smiled quietly. He didn't say anything. But that smile was his answer.

The last embers sparked faintly and scattered into the dark. I watched those fragments of light glimmer and vanish for a long while. I thought that today, too, would stay somewhere inside us, like a small light like that.

*I set that thought down as the last line of this chapter. That night, I watched Jeteo hold two things together for the first time. Keeping, and staying close. I didn't know then, not exactly, that it was his first step toward trust.*
