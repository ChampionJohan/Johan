# Chapter 45. News Temel Brought

> _Previously_ — Father revealed the meaning of our names. Jeteo means
> "to seek, to search"; Elpis means "hope." It came out that every way
> the two of us had lived had already been inside our names.

Around the start of autumn, Temel stopped by the well again.

It was a time when the leaves had just begun to turn. I thought this
color was different from the autumn I remembered in Brick Hollow.
Brick Hollow's autumn had been the color of dust. Here, autumn was red
and gold.

The trees by the well were beginning to drop their leaves too, one by
one. Every morning I gathered up a few fallen leaves. There was no
real reason. It just seemed a shame to let them go.

"…It's been a while," Brother greeted him warmly. His face was far
more at ease than it had been months before.

Temel's walk looked lighter than before, too. I wondered where that
lightness came from. His pack looked lighter as well.

Temel saw Father and smiled.

"…You look much better," Temel said.

Father looked at Temel with something like curiosity.

"…You're the one who first came looking for me, aren't you?" Father
asked. It wasn't an exact memory. But something seemed to have stayed
with him.

"…That's right," Temel said, his face showing surprise.

Temel knelt down to bring himself level with Father's eyes.

"…You didn't even have a name, back then," Temel said quietly. "And
now, look at you — you know me."

Father smiled faintly at that. It was the smile of someone who found
it a little strange himself.

"…When you first found me, Temel — what was I like?" Father asked.

Temel took a moment to choose his words at that question. The
firelight cast shifting shadows across his face.

"…There was nothing there," Temel said honestly. "No name, no
expression. But your eyes were looking for something. It was because
of that look that I didn't give up on you."

Father listened to that and fell silent for a long while. I felt that
he was carving those words somewhere deep inside him.

---

That evening, we all gathered around the fire. Speon threw a few more
logs on. The flames grew and lit our faces red.

Temel gave us what news there was.

"…You want to hear about Haven?" Temel asked.

Brother nodded. There was none of the old anger in it. Just curiosity.
I noticed that difference all over again, watching from beside him.

"…Still quiet," Temel said. "But I've heard that quiet's gotten some
cracks in it lately. More and more people refusing COMPLETE, not
filing at all."

*Hearing that, something in me stirred strangely. We hadn't gone
there to change that city. But it seemed like something had been left
behind, in the place we walked away from.*

"…Aren't those people in danger?" I asked.

"…They are," Temel said plainly. "But they don't stop, apparently.
Once someone starts first, it seems to get a little easier for the
next one."

"…Who started it, first of all?" Brother asked.

"…No one knows," Temel said. "They started without even leaving a
name behind, they say. All they did was not file."

Brother nodded quietly at that. I felt Brother was carving that
nameless person into his heart.

Father was listening to all this too. His expression was complicated.
I could feel that something in Father stirred, faintly, every time he
heard the name Haven. It wasn't a complete memory. But it seemed his
body recognized it before his mind did.

"…Are you all right?" I asked Father.

"…Yes," Father said. "It's just… an unfamiliar name that doesn't feel
unfamiliar."

Brother moved closer and sat beside Father.

"…You don't have to talk about it, if you don't want to," Brother
said.

"…No," Father said. "I want to know. I want to understand my own
story a little more too."

Hearing that, I thought Father had grown so much braver than before.
The way he faced his own past instead of fearing it — I admired that.

---

"…What about the Administrator?" I asked.

"…Still there," Temel said. "But not long ago, there was a rumor —
that someone secretly made a room where they keep remembering the
names of the people marked COMPLETE. They say the Administrator knows
about the room and looks the other way."

*In that moment, I thought of the Administrator's face. The face that
used to say, "That isn't mine to answer." I thought that maybe he was
still resisting, inside that city, in his own way.*

"…Does the room have a name?" I asked.

"…No," Temel said. "It's just a room, they say. But people have taken
to calling it 'the remembering room.'"

I turned that name over in my mind several times. The remembering
room. I thought that phrase resembled the very thing we were doing.
Somewhere in the middle of Haven, someone was secretly doing the same
work we had learned to do here.

Speon spoke up then, for the first time in the conversation.

"…And Mena — how is she?" Speon asked. His voice made a plain effort
not to give anything away.

Temel answered, holding back a smile.

"…Doing well. Still out walking, these days. But I hear she's been
setting her steps this way more often than she used to."

Speon said nothing to that. But I saw the tips of his ears turn faintly
red.

Watching that, I couldn't help but laugh. Even someone who had lived
so many years turned out to be, in a moment like this, no different
from anyone else — and I found that strangely dear.

---

"…And Noa?" Brother asked.

"…Doing well. Her tent camp has gotten bigger, they say. More and
more people are showing up who remember names."

I pictured Noa's face. Along with it came the lights of that tent camp
that had first taken us in. And the song the children used to sing
under those lights. I could still hum that song, even now.

"…The children must have grown, too," I said.

"…A lot," Temel said. "Some of them are looking after children younger
than themselves now. That's how time passes."

Brother laughed quietly at that.

"…It's strange," Brother said. "We just ran. But something's growing
in every place we ran through."

Temel looked at Brother quietly at that.

"…That's just how seeds are," Temel said. "They only really start
growing once they leave the hand that planted them."

Hearing that, I thought of my own flowerbed. That sprout, smaller than
a fingernail, was probably growing a little more, even now, out of
sight.

Temel stayed with us late into that night. The next dawn, he set out
on the road again. We saw him off as far as the edge of the well.

"…You'll come back?" I asked.

"…Yes," Temel said. "This place — it's become an important place for
me too, now."

Temel's back grew smaller and smaller, until it vanished over the
hill. I waved until he was completely out of sight.

Speon, too, watched that retreating figure a long while. I saw
something in his eyes that held both a wistfulness and an anticipation
at once.

Brother walked over to Speon and clapped him on the shoulder.

"…Next time, try holding on to her first," Brother said, teasing.

Speon cleared his throat and looked away. I couldn't hold back my
laughter, watching him.

*I set down that line as the last sentence of this chapter. I
understood, then. A seed grows even without the one who planted it
seeing all of it. We were learning that fact in our bodies.*
