# Chapter 31. Humming

> _Previously_ — When Father woke in the night in violent confusion, the man from the flowerbed appeared again and calmed him. Jeteo asked him outright who he was, but the only answer that came back was, "I've always been here. You're the ones who didn't recognize me."

By the next morning, the man had vanished without a word.

The moment I opened my eyes, I looked first at the spot where he'd sat. Only a faint press in the grass remained. Not even a clear footprint. So little trace was left that it made me doubt he'd really been there at all.

I put my hand to that spot. It seemed like a little warmth might still be left there. I couldn't be sure.

Jeteo glared at the spot for a long while, then turned away. I felt that he was angry at that absence of any trace, and at the same time, hoping for something — that next time, a little more would be left behind.

"…He just left again," Jeteo said, his voice empty. "Every single time. He shows up, and then — no explanation at all."

*I had nothing to say against that. But strangely, his disappearing felt different to me from being abandoned. I couldn't have explained exactly why, then.*

Father didn't even remember that the man had come. That saddened me. The very voice that had calmed all of us so completely last night — Father himself didn't hold even a trace of it.

"…Did someone come, last night?" Father asked casually, over breakfast.

Jeteo and I traded a look. We didn't know how to explain it.

"…Yes, briefly," I managed to say.

"…I see," Father said, and nodded. He didn't press further. That indifference was a relief to me, and sad, both at once.

*I thought, then — maybe there are things that stay even when they can't be remembered. Like that song.*

---

That afternoon, Father hummed something low as he walked.

Sunlight fell in dappled patches through the shade of the trees. The wind blew gently. It was that kind of ordinary afternoon. I think it was exactly because it was such an ordinary afternoon that the sound reached me so clearly.

The moment I heard it, I stopped walking. I knew that tune. It was the one Mother used to sing to put us to sleep.

"…Father, what song is that?" I asked, urgent.

Father stopped humming and looked at me. His face was flustered.

"…I don't know," Father said. "It just… came out on its own."

Father touched his own mouth and laughed, as if amazed. It seemed strange even to himself, that his body could remember something before his mind did.

*My heart felt like it would burst in that moment. I looked at Jeteo. His face showed that he'd recognized the tune too.*

Jeteo stood there, having stopped walking, and couldn't say a word. I saw his lips tremble slightly. He looked like someone who wanted to sing along, and was holding himself back for fear that something might break if he did.

---

"…Can you keep singing it?" Jeteo asked. His voice shook.

Father tried to hum it again. But he got stuck after only a few bars.

"…I can't remember what comes next," Father said, his face apologetic.

Father's eyebrows creased faintly. It was the face of someone reaching for something that kept slipping out of his hand. Watching that face hurt me even more than the night he'd shouted.

Jeteo came quietly and sat down beside Father.

"…It's all right," Jeteo said. "It might come again, another time."

Father looked at Jeteo and gave a small nod. I felt that Jeteo was saying those words to himself too.

Watching the two of them, I got up quietly and walked a little way off. I needed a moment to settle my own heart. As I walked, I looked up at the sky. A daytime moon hung there, faint.

"…It's all right," I said. I was crying. "That's enough."

I picked up where it left off and sang the rest myself, exactly the way Mother used to. Father closed his eyes, listening.

My voice shook. The pitch wavered more than once. Still I sang it through to the end. Jeteo came quietly to my side and began humming along. The three of our voices — or to be precise, our two voices and Father's silence — mixed together into one, right there.

"…It's strange, it settles something in me," Father said. "Even though I don't know whose song it was."

"…It was Mother's song," Jeteo said quietly. It was the first time he had offered that piece of it himself.

Father opened his eyes and looked at Jeteo.

"…Mother." Father repeated the word, quietly. "So there was someone like that."

*Hearing that, my heart broke a little. To Father now, Mother was just "someone like that." But even saying it, something in his face looked like longing. His words didn't know her. His heart, it seemed, still did.*

In that moment, I thought of that room in Brick Hollow, our last night there, where Mother sang us this same song to sleep. Under the low lamplight, that room where Mother's voice and Father's breathing lay side by side. I felt that memory being completed again, here, now.

*In that moment I understood again why I was still keeping this record. This song, this moment — these were things to give back to Father, someday, whenever he returned whole.*

That night I tried to write the tune down on paper. But I didn't know how to set a melody down in words. In the end I just wrote this: "Today, Father hummed Mother's song. He couldn't sing it to the end. But he sang it."

Under that sentence, I wrote one line more. "Someday, if Father reads this page, he won't remember today — but he'll know that today happened." Writing that line, I felt certain, for the first time, that this record was not only for me.

Father fell asleep unusually early that night. I couldn't tell whether singing had worn him out, or whether, for once in a long while, he'd simply felt at peace. I watched his sleeping face for a long time. Asleep, if only for those hours, Father looked like a man who had lost nothing at all.

---

That night, Jeteo said to me quietly,

"…I still don't really know. Who that man is. Whether I'm supposed to believe in God."

The firelight's shadow flickered over his face. I could tell he was choosing his words more carefully than usual.

"…But?" I asked.

"…But listening to that humming today, just for a moment, I thought this: maybe, the way something still remains inside Father, something remains inside me too. Something not completely erased."

I said nothing and just listened. I felt like if I interrupted, he'd close up again.

"…I don't know what it is yet," Jeteo went on. "But I can feel that it's there. Strange, isn't it."

"…Not strange," I said. "I have that too."

I really did feel that way. I couldn't say exactly what it was, but that certainty — that something in me couldn't be erased either — felt unusually clear to me that day.

Jeteo looked at me. He looked a little surprised.

"…What is it?" he asked.

"…I don't know either," I said, smiling. "But it's there."

Jeteo laughed out loud at that. It was the first time in a long while I'd heard him laugh.

*I set down those words as the last line of this chapter. I understood how enormous it was that Jeteo had said that out loud, for the first time — to himself, and to me.*
