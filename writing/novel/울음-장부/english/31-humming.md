# Chapter 31. Humming

> _Previously_ — When Father woke in the night in violent confusion, the man from the flowerbed appeared again and calmed him. Jeteo asked him outright who he was, but the only answer that came back was, "I've always been here. You're the ones who didn't recognize me."

By the next morning, the man had vanished without a word.

"…He just left again," Jeteo said, his voice empty. "Every single time. He shows up, and then — no explanation at all."

*I had nothing to say against that. But strangely, his disappearing felt different to me from being abandoned. I couldn't explain exactly why, then.*

Father didn't even remember the man had been there. That saddened me. The very voice that had calmed us all so completely last night — Father himself didn't have even a trace of it left.

*I thought, then — maybe there are things that stay even when they can't be remembered. Like that song.*

---

That afternoon, Father hummed something low as he walked.

The moment I heard it, I stopped walking. I knew that tune. It was the one Mother used to sing to put us to sleep.

"…Father, what song is that?" I asked, urgent.

Father stopped humming and looked at me. His face was flustered.

"…I don't know," Father said. "It just… came out on its own."

*My heart felt like it would burst. I looked at Jeteo. His face showed that he'd recognized the tune too.*

---

"…Can you keep singing it?" Jeteo asked. His voice shook.

Father tried to hum it again. But he got stuck after only a few bars.

"…I can't remember what comes next," Father said, his face apologetic.

"…It's all right," I said. I was crying. "That's enough."

I picked up where it left off and sang the rest myself, exactly the way Mother used to. Father closed his eyes, listening.

"…It's strange, it settles something in me," Father said. "Even though I don't know whose song it was."

*In that moment I understood again why I was still keeping this record. This song, this moment — these were things to give back to Father, someday, whenever he returned whole.*

That night I tried to write the tune down on paper. But I didn't know how to write down a melody in words. In the end I just wrote this: "Today, Father hummed Mother's song. He couldn't sing it to the end. But he sang it."

---

That night, Jeteo said to me quietly,

"…I still don't really know. Who that man is. Whether I'm supposed to believe in God."

"…But?" I asked.

"…But listening to that humming today, just for a moment, I thought this: maybe, the way something still remains inside Father, something remains inside me too. Something not completely erased."

*I set down those words as the last line of this chapter. I understood how enormous it was that Jeteo had said that out loud, for the first time — to himself, and to me.*
