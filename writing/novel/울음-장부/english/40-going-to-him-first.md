# Chapter 40. Going to Him First

> _Previously_ — A nameless boy came up out of the well. Brother passed
> on to him, exactly as it was, the way of being beside someone. And he
> realized, on his own, that he was already carrying in his body words
> he'd learned from the man at the flowerbed, without ever having
> memorized them.

The man appeared and vanished at no set time.

For the first few days I tried marking on a calendar which days he
came. Then I gave it up. I couldn't find any pattern at all. And yet
he never once came on a day he wasn't needed.

Some days he touched the soil by the well. Some days he sat quietly
beside someone newly come up. No one ever knew when he would come.

His clothes were always about the same — plain, the kind you'd see
anywhere. At first I thought that was strange. Later I came to think
it was simply like him. A man who didn't want to be noticed.

I once tried to trace back the places he'd appeared. When someone
newly arrived couldn't lift the water jar. When the boy woke alone at
night and cried. When Speon, rarely, looked worn through. He had stood
quietly in those exact places. I didn't think that could be
coincidence. And yet he never made it look as if he'd known in advance
and come looking.

*It was around then that I began to notice that irregularity was, in
fact, the rule. He was always there at the moment he was most needed.*

There was something neither Speon nor Mena ever asked him. When he
would come. Why now. Living by the well, I came to understand that
their silence wasn't resignation. It was an ease that only someone who
had watched him a long time could have.

I asked Speon about it once.

"…Where does that man live?"

Speon answered without stopping his work on the bucket.

"…I don't know," Speon said. "Haven't known for decades. But I've
never once gone without. He's come when he was needed."

"…Have you never once wanted to know?"

"…I've wanted to," Speon said. "But at some point, that wanting
stopped turning into a fret. Some things you can just leave as things
you wonder about."

"…Elpis, you want to know too, don't you?" Speon asked.

"…Yes," I said. "But lately that wanting doesn't scare me."

"…Why not?"

"…I learned that it's all right not to know," I said.

I carried that answer in my heart a long while. I didn't know then
that it would reach Brother too, someday.

The boy — the nameless one — flinched the first time he saw him too. I
understood that. A body tenses first, when a stranger suddenly
appears. But after a few days the boy started to feel at ease with
him. Whenever the man appeared, the boy would edge over and sit down
beside him. He said nothing. He just sat. Watching that, I understood
that the boy, too, was already learning how to be beside someone
without needing to speak.

He never tried to teach the boy anything. He simply touched the soil
alongside him whenever the boy fidgeted with the dirt. When the boy
stacked stones, he watched without knocking them down. I felt
something in that, something like the way Father had been with us,
the first time he came to us.

I thought back to the first time I met him. In an alley in Haven, he
had looked like nothing more than a passerby. I hadn't known then that
he would stay beside us this long. Now, on the days he didn't come, I
felt an odd emptiness. I couldn't say exactly when that emptiness had
started.

---

One afternoon, he sat alone in the shade of the tree by the well.

Brother walked over to him. I didn't follow. I knew that was Brother's
place to go alone.

It was late afternoon. Sunlight slanted in through the leaves.
Brother's shadow stretched long and reached toward him. I watched that
shadow shorten, little by little, as he walked. Brother's steps were
slow. But they didn't stop.

"…Can I talk to you for a bit?" Brother asked. It wasn't the accusing
tone from before.

*I watched it from a distance. For the first time, I saw Brother go
looking for him. It had always been the man who appeared first. This
time Brother went to him first.*

Watching his back, I thought of the last night in Brick Hollow. The
night Brother had shouted up at the sky. Back then he'd gone toward
him in anger. Now he was walking toward him. I knew that difference
was a large one.

---

The man motioned with his hand to the seat beside him. Brother sat
down.

I watched the way Brother lowered himself into that seat. The old
Brother would have crossed his arms, or turned his body away. That
day, he simply sat side by side. Legs stretched out, hands resting on
his knees. I understood, then, how much a single small posture could
say.

For a long while, neither of them spoke. Even from a distance, I could
feel that the silence wasn't from awkwardness.

I sat down beside the bucket and watched the two of them. Speon came
over quietly and sat beside me. He said nothing either. We sat there,
side by side, and watched the two figures in the distance.

Every time the wind stirred the leaves, the shadow shifted a little
across them. I tried to guess, from that shadow, how much time had
passed. But I couldn't quite manage it. That silence didn't seem like
something you could measure that way.

*I understood, then, that some conversations have already begun even
without sound. Brother and the man hadn't said a single word yet. But
I could feel that something was already passing between them.*

The bucket rope swayed lightly in the wind and made a small sound. A
bird landed on the well's stone wall, then flew off again. I found
myself counting those small sounds, one by one. If I didn't do
something like that, the silence felt too heavy to bear.

"…I still don't really know," Brother said at last. "Who you are. Why
you keep showing up. Whether I'm supposed to believe in God."

"…I know," he said.

"…Don't just say you know — tell me what you know," Brother said.
There was a trace of an old irritation in his voice.

He laughed a little at that. It wasn't an angry laugh.

"…I know you still want an answer," he said. "And I know you're
sitting here even when that answer doesn't come. That's a beginning."

"…But there's one thing I do know," Brother said. "I know how to be
beside someone now. But when I think about where I learned it… in the
end, it's from you."

He said nothing to that. But even from a distance I saw his shoulder
move, just slightly. I didn't know what that movement meant. But I
could tell it wasn't a bad one.

---

At that, he turned and looked at Brother straight on for the first
time.

I felt that his eyes were different from usual. Eyes that were always
gentle were, in that moment, meeting Brother's straight on.

"…Then let me ask you one thing," he said. "Why do you still not
trust me?"

The well seemed to go suddenly, entirely quiet. Even the sound of the
bucket, even the birds, seemed to pause for a moment. I held my breath
in that stillness.

Brother was silent for a long time. In that silence, I saw his hands
tighten into fists on his knees.

"…Because I'm afraid," Brother said finally. "If I believe, and it
ends up like it did with Father again… I don't think I could bear it
this time. Not really."

*I set down that answer as the last sentence of this chapter. I heard
Brother, for the first time, say out loud the real reason for his
doubt. It wasn't logic. It was fear. I knew, in that moment, that
Brother had come a very little bit closer.*
