# Chapter 40. Going to Him First

> _Previously_ — A new child who had lost his name came up out of the well. Brother passed on to him, exactly as it was, what he knew about being beside someone. And he realized, on his own, that he was carrying in his body the words he'd learned from the man at the flowerbed, without ever having memorized them.

The man appeared and vanished with no set time.

Some days he touched the soil by the well. Some days he sat quietly beside someone newly come up. No one ever knew when he would come.

*It was around then that I began to notice that irregularity was, in fact, the rule. He was always there at the moment he was most needed.*

Neither Speon nor Mena ever asked him certain things when they were with him. When he'd come, why now. Living by the well, I came to understand that their silence wasn't resignation. It was an ease that only someone who had watched him a long time could have.

---

One afternoon, he was sitting alone in the shade of the tree by the well.

Brother walked over to him. I didn't follow. I knew that was Brother's place to go alone.

"…Can I talk to you?" Brother asked. It wasn't the accusing tone from before.

*I watched from a distance. For the first time, I saw Brother going to find him. It had always been the man who appeared first. This time Brother went to him first.*

Watching his back, I thought of the last night in Brick Hollow. The night Brother had shouted up at the sky. Back then he'd gone toward him in anger. Now he was walking toward him. I knew that difference was a large one.

---

The man motioned with his hand to the seat beside him. Brother sat down.

For a long while, neither of them spoke. Even from a distance, I could feel that the silence wasn't from awkwardness.

"…I still don't really know," Brother said at last. "Who you are. Why you keep showing up. Whether I'm supposed to believe in God."

"…I know," he said.

"…But there's one thing I do know," Brother said. "I know how to be beside someone now. But when I think about where I learned it… in the end, it's from you."

---

At that, he turned and looked at Brother straight on for the first time.

"…Then let me ask you one thing," he said. "Why do you still not trust me?"

Brother was silent for a long time.

"…Because I'm afraid," Brother said finally. "If I believe, and it ends up like it did with Father again… I don't think I could bear it this time. Not really."

*I set down that answer as the last sentence of this chapter. I heard Brother, for the first time, say out loud the real reason for his doubt. It wasn't logic. It was fear. I knew, in that moment, that Brother had come a very little bit closer.*
