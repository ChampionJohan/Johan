# Chapter 42. The Three of Us, Sitting

> _Previously_ — To Brother's confession of fear, the man answered not
> with an argument but with the fact that he too had lost someone.
> "When it happens, I won't leave you alone." And he held out his
> hand. Brother took it.

That evening, for the first time, I sat with Brother under that same
shade tree.

What had happened that day was still vivid before my eyes. Brother
holding out his hand — Brother taking that hand. I called that scene
back to mind again and again. It was a scene no words could fully
explain.

I was already turning over, that same evening, how I would set that
scene down in the record. It seemed like no word I chose would be
enough.

He was already gone. Vanished quietly, as he always did. But something
like warmth from where he had been still remained in that spot. I
touched my hand to the grass. It felt as if the shape of where he'd
sat was still pressed into it.

*I didn't know what to call that warmth. I still can't find the exact
word for it now. I just set it down as it was.*

The evening air was turning cool. The last of the sunlight fell in
scattered pieces through the leaves. I watched those pieces of light
tremble across Brother's face.

Brother sat under that tree for a long while. I asked nothing, sitting
beside him. I had learned, by then, that some moments suit silence
better than questions.

I folded my hands in my lap and simply waited. In Brick Hollow, I
would surely have asked something by now. Not that day.

The crickets started up, one by one. I thought that sound seemed to
make the quiet well grow even quieter. It was a strange kind of quiet
— not an empty quiet, but a quiet full of something.

Brother finally shifted a little. I recognized that movement as a
sign he was ready to say something.

Speon sat far off in front of the hut. He wasn't looking our way. But
I could feel he was keeping his ears open toward us. That was his way
of being present.

---

The longer the night wore on, the more the stars came out. I looked
at Brother's face under that starlight. It looked more at ease than
it had by day.

"…Do you believe now?" I asked.

Brother couldn't answer right away. I waited without rushing that
silence.

"…I don't know," Brother said. "But when I took that hand a little
while ago… something changed. I can't explain exactly what."

I thought that answer was so like Brother. He never dressed up a
certainty he didn't have.

"…Is the anger gone?"

"…No," Brother said. "The anger's still there. What happened to
Father still makes me angry. But now I know I don't have to hold that
anger alone."

I could hear both anger and relief in Brother's voice at once. I
learned, in that moment, for the first time, that those two things
could live inside the same sentence.

*I thought that answer was strangely honest. Brother didn't say, "It's
all fine now." I thought that honesty was so like him.*

Brother looked up at the sky for a moment. Starlight caught in his
eyes.

"…Elpis, when did you start believing?" Brother asked.

I thought about that question for a long time. I knew how much that
question mattered to Brother. So I didn't want to answer carelessly.

"…I don't know," I said. "At some point, I just started waiting.
Before I even had the words to say I believed."

Brother nodded at that answer. It was a face that seemed to understand
something. I was grateful Brother didn't press me further.

"…You believe in such a strange way," Brother said. It wasn't teasing.

"…And you're angry in such a strange way," I said back.

Brother laughed at that. A small laugh. But it was the first laugh
I'd seen in days.

"…You know what?" Brother said. "Lately I think it's a good thing
we're so different, the two of us."

"…Why?"

"…Because you kept waiting, I think that's how I made it this far,"
Brother said.

I didn't know what to say to that, so I just leaned my head against
his shoulder.

*I remember that laugh for a long time. I understood, then, that we
had come to the same place by two different roads.*

---

We sat like that under the starlight for a while. It was a comfortable
silence, even without a word.

Father walked toward us then. He had a few small stones in his hand.

Father's footsteps were familiar to us by now. Not careful the way
they used to be. I felt, in those steps, how much at ease Father had
become at this well. When we first came here, Father had tried not
to even make the sound of his own footsteps.

"…What were you two doing?" Father asked.

"…Just sitting," Brother said.

Father sat down beside us naturally. That day was the first time the
three of us sat side by side.

I was struck by how natural it was. A few months ago, Father would
have found even sitting beside us unfamiliar.

Father's shoulder brushed lightly against mine. I felt the warmth of
it. I thought that warmth was different from, and yet somehow like,
the warmth the man had left behind earlier.

*I record that moment exactly. Father didn't know what we'd been
talking about. But in that moment, something felt complete, just from
the three of us sitting there together.*

No one spoke for a long time. I felt that this silence was a different
kind from the one we'd shared that afternoon. This wasn't a silence
that was weighing something — it was simply a comfortable silence.

Brother leaned his body a little toward Father. Father seemed to feel
it too, and shifted his own posture slightly. I felt the distance
between the two of them narrowing, little by little.

---

The stones in Father's palm were each a different shape. Round ones,
flat ones, one with a faint shimmering grain. I took each stone in
with my eyes, one at a time.

Moonlight fell softly over those stones. I thought that light made
them look even more special than usual.

"…Why do you collect these stones?" I asked.

Father set each stone on his palm and looked at it, one by one. His
fingertips slowly traced over each one.

"…I don't know," Father said. "I just thought they looked nice. And…
I feel like I want to give them to someone. I don't know who."

Brother heard that and looked at Father quietly. He said nothing. But
I saw tears well up faintly in Brother's eyes.

"…Could you give one to me?" I asked. The words came out before I
knew it.

Father looked at me, surprised, then chose one stone and pressed it
into my hand. The smallest, smoothest one there.

"…So it was this," Father said quietly. "This is who I wanted to give
it to."

Relief crossed Father's face at last. Watching that, I thought Father
must have been quietly troubled, all this time, not knowing the
reason.

I held that stone tight in my hand. It was warm. The warmth of
Father's hand still lingered in it. I didn't open my hand for a long
while, not wanting to lose that warmth.

*I still have that stone. I think it stands in for everything Father
couldn't say in words.*

*I set down that line as the last sentence of this chapter. I nearly
cried in that moment. Father didn't know. But Father's body knew.
Gathering something for someone — that had always been the thing
Father did.*
