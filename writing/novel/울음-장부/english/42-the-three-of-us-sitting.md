# Chapter 42. The Three of Us, Sitting

> _Previously_ — To Brother's confession of fear, the man answered not with an argument but with the fact that he too had lost someone. "When it happens, I won't leave you alone." And he held out his hand. Brother took it.

That evening, for the first time, I sat with Brother under that same shade tree.

He was already gone. Vanished quietly, as he always did. But something like warmth from where he had been still remained in that spot.

*I didn't know what to call that warmth. I still can't find the exact word for it now. I just set it down as it was.*

Brother sat under that tree for a long while. I asked nothing, sitting beside him. I had learned, by then, that some moments suit silence better than questions.

---

"…Do you believe now?" I asked.

Brother couldn't answer right away.

"…I don't know," Brother said. "But when I took that hand a little while ago… something changed. I can't explain exactly what."

"…Is the anger gone?"

"…No," Brother said. "The anger's still there. What happened to Father still makes me angry. But now I know I don't have to hold that anger alone."

*I thought that answer was strangely honest. Brother didn't say, "It's all fine now." I thought that honesty was so like him.*

"…Elpis, when did you start believing?" Brother asked.

I thought about that question for a long time.

"…I don't know," I said. "At some point, I just started waiting. Before I even had the words to say I believed."

---

Father walked over to us then. He had a few small stones in his hand.

"…What were you two doing?" Father asked.

"…Just sitting," Brother said.

Father sat down beside us naturally. That day was the first time the three of us sat side by side.

*I record that moment exactly. Father didn't know what we'd been talking about. But in that moment, something felt complete, just from the three of us sitting there together.*

---

"…Why are you collecting these stones?" I asked.

Father set each stone on his palm and looked at it, one by one.

"…I don't know," Father said. "I just thought they looked nice. And… I feel like I want to give them to someone. I don't know who."

*I set down that line as the last sentence of this chapter. I nearly cried in that moment. Father didn't know. But Father's body knew. Gathering something for someone — that had always been the thing Father did.*
