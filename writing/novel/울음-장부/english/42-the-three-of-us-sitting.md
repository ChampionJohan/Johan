# Chapter 42. The Three of Us, Sitting

> _Previously_ — In answer to Jeteo's confession of fear, the man didn't offer an argument but the fact that he too had lost someone. "When you lose someone, I won't leave you alone." And he held out his hand. Jeteo took it.

That evening, I sat with Jeteo in that tree's shade for the first time.

What had happened that day still played vividly before my eyes. Jeteo holding out his hand; the moment that hand was taken. I replayed that scene again and again. It was a scene words couldn't fully explain.

Every time I replayed that scene, my fingertips tingled. As if I had been the one to take that hand. Only then did I realize that all that afternoon, I had been breathing in Jeteo's place.

I was already thinking about how to write that scene into tonight's record. It felt like no word would be enough.

He was already gone. Vanished quietly, as always. But the spot still held something like the warmth of his having been there. I laid my hand on the grass. It felt like the mark of where he'd sat was still there.

I traced my fingertips along the flattened blades of grass. I tried to imagine, from the shape of that mark, how he had been sitting — how his legs had been positioned, where his hands had rested. That imagining was strangely enjoyable.

*I didn't know what to call that warmth. I still can't find the exact words for it now. I'm just setting it down like that.*

The evening air was cooling. The last light fell in pieces through the tree's leaves. I watched those pieces tremble on Jeteo's face.

Every time the wind blew, those fragments of light shifted position. I followed that movement with my eyes, and felt, that way, the day settling into evening. In the place where the heavy conversation of the afternoon had happened, only the quiet of evening was settling now.

Jeteo sat there a long time under the tree. I asked nothing, beside him. I had learned, by then, that some moments suit silence better than questions.

I gathered my two hands on my knees and just waited. In Brick Hollow I would surely have asked something. Not now.

Strangely, in that waiting, I wasn't bored. In the old days, sitting still like that would have been hard to endure. Now, that stillness felt at ease instead. I couldn't say exactly when that had changed.

The sound of night insects began, one by one. I thought that sound made the quiet well-yard even quieter. A strange quiet. Not an empty quiet, but a quiet filled with something.

Far off, the faint creak of the bucket rope carried. Speon must have been finishing drawing the evening's water. Hearing that familiar sound, I felt that today, like any day, was drawing to a close. But today was a little different from any other day.

Jeteo finally shifted a little. I recognized that movement as a sign he was ready to say something.

Speon sat far off, in front of the hut. He wasn't looking our way. But I felt that he had his ears open toward us. That was how he stayed close.

Speon's silhouette stood with the hut's light behind him. I thought that sight looked like a quiet sentinel standing between us and the world. Blocking nothing, but simply being there — a presence that steadied all the same.

I wondered how many people in this world were like that. People who never came close, yet never drifted far either. That distance didn't upset me. If anything, thanks to that distance, this time between Jeteo and me could be entirely our own.

*That day, I learned for the first time that there is more than one way to be beside someone. There is the beside of holding a hand, and there is the beside of standing with your back turned. Both were still beside.*

---

As the night deepened, stars rose one by one. Under that starlight, I looked at Jeteo's face. It looked more at ease than it had during the day.

"…Do you believe now?" I asked.

Jeteo couldn't answer that right away. I didn't rush that silence, and waited.

"…I don't know," Jeteo said. "But when I took that hand earlier… something changed. I can't say exactly what."

"…Then what didn't change?" I asked.

Jeteo laughed a little at that question.

"…That's a strange question," Jeteo said. "But a good one."

Jeteo thought for a moment and went on.

"…I still don't know who he is," Jeteo said. "That hasn't changed. But strangely, it feels like it's okay not to know."

I thought that answer was very Jeteo. He never dressed up a certainty he didn't have.

"…Is the anger gone?"

"…No," Jeteo said. "The anger's still there. What happened to Father still makes me angry. But now I know I don't have to carry that anger alone."

I heard both anger and relief in Jeteo's voice at once. I understood, for the first time, that those two things could sit together in one sentence.

I thought, hearing that, of the Ledger of Haven again. The Ledger always gave exactly one answer, once a question was filed. Being angry and relieved at the same time — that was a state that had no entry in the Ledger. I felt again, in that moment, how much more complicated a person's heart was than a ledger.

*I thought that answer was strangely honest. Jeteo hadn't said "everything's fine now." I thought that honesty was very much like Jeteo.*

Jeteo looked up at the sky for a moment. Starlight reflected in his eyes.

"…Elpis, when did you start believing?" Jeteo asked.

I thought about that question for a long time. I knew how much it mattered to Jeteo. So I didn't want to give it a careless answer.

"…I don't know," I said. "At some point, I was just already waiting. Before I even used the word believe."

Jeteo nodded at that answer. His face looked like he understood something. I was grateful Jeteo didn't press me for more.

"…You believe in a really strange way," Jeteo said. Not a teasing tone.

"…Is strange a bad thing?" I asked.

"…No," Jeteo said. "It's something I envy."

I looked at Jeteo, startled by that. He wasn't looking at me — he was still looking at the sky. I tried to guess how much courage it must have taken for him to say that.

"…You get angry in a really strange way too," I said back.

"…How's it strange?" Jeteo asked.

"…You get angry, and you don't leave," I said. "You used to always walk off somewhere when you got angry."

Jeteo said nothing for a moment at that.

"…True," Jeteo said quietly. "When did I stop walking off."

I didn't answer that question. It seemed like a question Jeteo needed to find on his own.

Jeteo laughed at that. A small laugh. But the first laugh I'd seen in days.

"…You know what?" Jeteo said. "Lately I think it's actually a good thing that the two of us are so different."

"…Why?"

"…I think I only got this far because you kept waiting," Jeteo said.

I didn't know what to say to that, so I just leaned my head against Jeteo's shoulder.

Jeteo's shoulder had grown firmer than before. Muscle from helping with the well. That firmness, strangely, put my mind at ease. Jeteo was growing sturdier now, in body and in heart both.

*I remember that laugh for a long time. I knew, then, that the two of us had arrived at the same place by different roads.*

---

We sat like that for a while under the starlight. A silence that was comfortable even without a word.

Father came walking toward us then. He held a few small stones in his hand.

Father's footsteps were familiar now. Not careful, like before. I felt, in that walk, how much at ease Father had grown at this well. When Father had first come here, he'd tried not to make even the sound of his own footsteps.

Remembering that change, I thought of that first night we'd arrived. Father had hesitated at the doorway, again and again. As if asking permission to come in. Now he just strode straight in. I thought that difference was the greatest gift this well had given Father.

"…What were you doing?" Father asked.

"…Just sitting," Jeteo said.

Father sat down naturally beside us. It was the first time the three of us had sat side by side like that.

I was surprised by that naturalness. A few months earlier, Father would have found it unfamiliar even to sit beside us.

Father's shoulder touched mine lightly. I felt that warmth. It was warm. I thought that warmth was different from, yet somehow similar to, the warmth of that man from earlier.

I set those two warmths side by side in my mind. One was a father's warmth, the other a nameless guest's warmth. I found it strange that the two didn't compete with each other. If anything, they seemed to fill each other in.

*I set down this moment exactly as it was. Father didn't know what we had talked about. But in that moment, just the three of us sitting side by side, I felt something being completed.*

No one spoke for a long while. I felt that this silence was a different kind from the one we'd shared earlier in the day. This wasn't a silence weighing something — it was simply a comfortable silence.

I set those two silences side by side in my mind. One had been the silence before a heavy question; the other, the silence after passing through it. I found it strange that we had gone through both silences in a single day.

Jeteo leaned his body slightly toward Father. Father, sensing it, shifted his own posture a little too. I felt the distance between the two of them narrowing, bit by bit.

I measured that distance with my eyes. About a hand's width. A few months earlier, that hand's width would never have closed. I knew how many evenings it had taken to make that hand's width.

The sound of night insects wrapped around the three of us. I thought that sound was filling in our silence for us. No one spoke, and yet the space didn't feel empty.

---

The stones in Father's palm were each a different shape. Round ones, flat ones, one with a shining grain. I took in each stone with my eyes, one at a time.

One of them especially caught my eye. A gray stone with a thin white stripe running across it. I thought that pattern looked familiar somehow. It resembled a stone I'd seen by the stream in Brick Hollow. I couldn't tell if that connection was coincidence or not.

Moonlight fell softly over those stones. I thought that light made the stones look more special than usual.

I noticed that every one of those stones bore the trace of having been handled by Father's hands all day. Their surfaces were unusually smooth. I understood that the smoothness hadn't happened by itself — it was the mark left by Father's fingertips.

"…Why do you collect these stones?" I asked.

Father laid the stones out one by one in his palm and looked at them. His fingertips slowly stroked each one.

"…I don't know," Father said. "They just seem pretty. And… I feel like I want to give them to someone. I don't know who."

"…When did that start?" Jeteo asked.

Father tilted his head.

"…I don't remember," Father said. "But it feels like my hands knew before I did. My hand just reached for it, every time I saw a stone."

Hearing that, I felt I dimly understood what it might mean for the hand to know before the heart. Father's body had lost memories, but not the habit of loving.

Jeteo heard that and looked quietly at Father. He said nothing. But I saw tears well up faintly in his eyes.

"…Would you give me one?" I asked. The words came out before I knew it.

Father looked at me, startled. And chose a stone, pressing it into my hand. The smallest, smoothest one.

Father's fingertips lingered for a moment as he set that stone in my palm. In that brief touch, I felt something like a gesture prepared long in advance. As if Father had been choosing that stone for a long time, for this very moment.

"…So that was it," Father said quietly. "Who I wanted to give it to."

Relief crossed Father's face only then. Watching that expression, I thought Father must have been frustrated all along, not knowing why.

I held that stone tightly in my hand. It was warm. Father's hand's warmth still lingered in it. I didn't want to lose that warmth, so I didn't open my hand for a while.

Jeteo watched that beside us and laughed quietly.

I thought Jeteo's laugh had both envy and warmth mixed into it. Jeteo had always been someone who put his own share off to later. I hoped that this time, he wouldn't put it off.

"…I want one too," Jeteo said, playfully.

Father, flustered at that, looked back through the remaining stones. I thought that flustered look was endearing.

"…This one," Father said, handing Jeteo a slightly larger stone. "This one… somehow feels like yours."

Jeteo laughed, taking the stone.

"…Why is this one mine?"

"…I don't know," Father said. "It just feels that way."

I thought that answer was very Father. Father was beginning to trust a feeling now, even without knowing the reason.

I suddenly thought of the things we'd been given in Haven. Everything given there had a reason attached. Why this coat, why this bowl. There was no object without a reason. Only then did I realize — this stone was the first thing I'd ever been given with no reason at all. Strangely, that made it feel even more precious.

*I still have that stone. I think that stone stands in for everything Father couldn't say in words.*

I set the stone in my palm and looked at it for a long time. The white stripe glinted faintly in the moonlight. I traced that stripe with my fingertip. It was a pattern with no beginning and no end, just continuing on. I thought it looked exactly like the three of us. Not knowing where it started or where it would end, but going on all the same.

*I set down those words as the last line of this chapter. In that moment, I nearly cried. Father didn't know. But Father's body knew. Gathering something for someone — that had always been what Father did.*
