# Chapter 26. The Man Who Finds People

> _Previously_ — The siblings found footprints they didn't recognize. A traveler confirmed that the person had been walking along, counting numbers, and told them the name of a man who finds people — Temel.

Two days later, we actually met Temel.

He wasn't a big man. He carried his pack light. His eyes never stopped moving, scanning everything around him.

His walk was unusual. Not hurried, not slow. It was the walk of someone who already knew exactly where he was going. Watching it, I felt strangely at ease. I thought — a man like this wouldn't lose his way.

"…Are you the one who finds people?" Jeteo asked.

Temel stopped and looked at us. His eyes flickered, just for a moment.

"…Something like that," Temel said. "Who are you looking for?"

*There was something strange in Temel's eyes right then. The look of someone who'd done the same thing, over and over, for a very long time. Not tired — worn smooth by repetition.*

Temel's hands caught my eye too. Dirt was packed under his nails in layers, the kind that wouldn't wash off in one try. They were the hands of someone who'd been walking a long, long time.

"…Our father," Jeteo said. His voice trembled a little. "He walks along counting numbers. He's forgotten his own name too."

Temel closed his eyes for a moment at that. He seemed to be remembering something.

"…Finding someone like that can take a long time," Temel said. "Are you still all right with that?"

"…Yes," Jeteo answered right away. No hesitation at all.

"…We met Speon and Mena," Jeteo said. "They never mentioned you — we came looking for you on our own."

Something shifted, faintly, in Temel's face at those two names.

"…Are the two of them well?" Temel asked. His voice held both gladness and caution together.

"…Yes," I said. "Each of them is doing well, where they are."

Temel nodded. He didn't ask anything more. In that silence, I sensed there was some old story among the three of them that we knew nothing about.

"…Is Speon still keeping his well?" Temel asked, out of nowhere.

"…Yes," I said. "People keep coming to find him, I hear."

Temel gave a small laugh at that. It looked like a laugh he hadn't given in a long while.

"…That sounds like him," Temel said. He didn't say anything more after that.

---

Jeteo told him about Father — the habit of counting bricks, the footprints, the numbers.

Temel listened, nodding.

"…I've seen people like that a few times," Temel said. "People who've lost their names — mostly they keep repeating something, with their hands or their mouths. Whatever they used to do stays behind in the body."

"…Why does that happen?" I asked.

"…I don't know exactly, myself," Temel said. "But it seems like the body remembers longer than the mind does. The mind lets go of the name. The hands keep counting bricks."

*Hearing that, I thought of Father's hands. I tried to imagine what they might be doing right now. Just imagining it hurt.*

"…If he finds his name," I asked, "does he go back to how he was?"

Temel shook his head.

"…Not always," Temel said. "Even finding the name back, he might not be exactly the way he was before. But it's better than having no name at all."

*That answer wasn't much of a comfort. But strangely, I was grateful for the honesty in it. Temel didn't hand us a false hope.*

"…Then can you find him?" Jeteo asked, urgent.

"…I'm a man who finds people," Temel said. "But I'm not a man who carries answers around with him. I'm a man who goes looking."

*I didn't understand that difference, then. Now I do. Temel wasn't someone who handed you certainty. He was someone who walked alongside you.*

"…What if we find him and he's not Father?" I asked. I was afraid to ask it. But it had to be asked.

Jeteo turned and looked at me. His face said he hadn't expected me to be the one to raise it. I didn't look away from his eyes. I thought someone had to say that possibility out loud.

"…Then we find that person, whoever he turns out to be, in his own right," Temel said. "There's no wasted walking in the finding of someone. Whoever you're looking for, every step of it means something."

"…Has that happened to you? Finding someone, and it not being who you wanted?" Jeteo asked.

Temel nodded.

"…It has," Temel said, briefly. He didn't say more. I sensed a long story hiding inside that shortness.

*Strangely, that answer comforted me. For the first time, I understood that this journey might not be a complete failure even if we never found Father.*

Jeteo said nothing back to that. But I saw his expression ease, just a little. He'd always been someone who measured a journey only by its outcome. That day, I felt something like a different standard beginning to take shape inside him, little by little.

---

"…Why do you do this?" I asked.

Temel looked off into the distance for a moment.

"…A long time ago, there was a place where I was supposed to guide someone, and I ran instead," Temel said. "I was afraid, back then. Now I'm still afraid, sometimes — but I don't run. That's the only repayment I know how to make."

*Hearing that, I thought it sounded like something Speon had once said. I didn't know, then, that the two of them had truly come out of the same story.*

"…Weren't you afraid? Moments when you wanted to run again?" I asked.

"…Every single time," Temel said honestly. "Even now, sometimes. But once you run because you're afraid, you find yourself running from then on, over and over. So I just walk, afraid."

*I held on to those words for a long time. I didn't understand their full weight then. Now I do. It was something we had to learn, again and again, for the whole length of this journey.*

"…Do you still see him now — that person?" I asked.

Temel smiled briefly. There was a touch of loneliness mixed into that smile.

"…Sometimes," Temel said. "Not as often as you'd think. But every time we meet, it feels exactly like it did before."

---

Temel led us walking north for several more days. The whole way, he kept studying the ground — which way the dry grass lay bent, scratches on the stones, even the depth of every footprint.

Temel's stride looked far more unhurried than ours. But look closely, and his eyes never rested, not for a single moment. Walking behind that stride, I felt anew how exhausting the work of searching really was. It wore out the eyes before it ever wore out the body.

At night, Temel always kept his fire small. "Too big, and you can't find anyone," Temel said. "A person who's searching can't afford to be too bright. Or the one you're looking for won't be able to see you." I felt like I both understood that and didn't.

Through those four days, Jeteo slept badly every night. I lay awake listening to him turn over, and slept badly along with him. Temel never tried to stop us. Every morning he just packed up his things without a word and started walking again.

On the third day, the path began to narrow, and trees started appearing here and there. Temel's steps slowed, little by little — the walk of someone examining something more carefully.

"…We're getting close," Temel said. His voice wasn't full of certainty. But my heart started pounding at those words anyway.

"…Found it," Temel said quietly, on the fourth night. Far off, a single small light was visible.

*The moment I saw that light, I set down the last line of this chapter here. I gripped Jeteo's hand tight again. I couldn't know whether that light was Father. But for the first time, I felt with my whole body that it was possible to walk on without knowing the answer.*
