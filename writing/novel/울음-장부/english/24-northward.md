# Chapter 24. Northward

> _Previously_ — Mena delivered word of Father. A month earlier, he had come out alive from a well in the north, but having forgotten his name and faces, he counted three thousand bricks and then vanished somewhere. For the first time since leaving Brick Hollow, the brother and sister wept together.

The next morning, my eyes were swollen. So were my brother's. But strangely, our bodies felt lighter.

*I understood for the first time then. Tears didn't erase anything. They only lifted away, by their own weight, whatever had been piling up.*

The moment my brother opened his eyes, he checked the paper stamped COMPLETE again, as he always did now, out of habit. But that day was a little different. He looked at the paper for a long time, then folded it carefully and tucked it into his innermost pocket.

"…It's not proof anymore. Not for keeping a name, either," he said, watching my face. "Now this is… proof that we made it this far."

---

As Mena packed our breakfast, she asked.

"…What will you do now?"

"…We'll go north," my brother said. "To that well, and then we'll follow the road Father walked after."

Mena nodded. She didn't try to stop us.

"…I thought you'd say that," she said.

"…Won't it be dangerous?" I asked.

"…It will be," Mena said honestly. "But you two already knew that much when you set out."

My brother smiled at that for the first time. The first smile I'd seen from him in days.

---

Before we left, Mena called me aside.

"…I hear you've been keeping a record," she said. "Speon told me."

"…I just write down what happens," I said. "It's nothing special."

"…That's exactly what makes it special," Mena said. "I started the same way too. Just writing down what happened."

Mena handed me a worn writing tool.

"…Keep writing," she said. "Someday, even your father might need this record."

*Taking it, I felt strangely close to tears again. I understood, for the first time, that what I had been doing was no different from what my brother had been doing. He had tried to keep names. I had tried to keep the story. We had only been holding the same thing with different hands.*

"…Doesn't it frighten you?" Mena asked. "Keeping a record doesn't mean writing down only the good. You have to write down days like today too."

"…It does," I said. "But if I don't write it down, today will just disappear. And that frightens me more."

Mena looked at me for a long time at that. And then, for the first time, she smiled — a real smile.

---

We left Mena's house that afternoon.

"…Tell Speon," Mena said, one last thing. "That I'm still waiting too."

My brother said he'd remember it exactly.

*I set down the last sentence of this Part here. We started walking north. Whether we could find Father, and whether he would know us even if we did — nothing was certain. But that day, for the first time, I saw something close to certainty in my brother's face. Not the certainty of someone who has every answer. Just the certainty of someone who has a reason to keep walking.*
