# Chapter 19. Those Who Lost Their Names

> _Previously_ — After four days of travel, the brother and sister found the well. A man named Speon, who kept it, was deeply shaken by the paper stamped COMPLETE. "I know this stamp," he said. He promised to tell them everything he knew the following night.

The next night, Speon began to speak by the fire.

The fire burned low. Speon studied it for a long while before he opened his mouth. I could tell he was choosing his words — the face of someone who had spent the whole day thinking about where to begin.

*I remember my brother standing there that night, unable even to sit down. I thought then that he was ready to hear it. Looking back now, I don't think anyone can ever really be ready for a story like that one.*

From the well came the faintest sound of water moving. I hadn't known, until I heard it, that the well made any sound at all.

"…This well is connected to the Completion Houses in the cities," Speon said. "I don't know exactly how it's connected. But some of the people who are completed — they come up out of the bottom of this well."

*My breath stopped the moment I heard that. I understood at once — if that were true, it meant Father might not be dead at all.*

My brother shot to his feet right there.

"…Are you sure?" he asked. "They really come out alive?"

"…Every one I've seen with my own eyes, yes," Speon said. "Not many. But every one of them was alive."

My brother's hands were shaking. It was the first time I'd seen them shake.

I wanted to take that hand in mine. But I couldn't bring myself to reach for it. In that moment my brother looked like someone who needed no comfort from anyone — only Speon's next words.

Speon watched him a moment, then motioned for him to sit back down.

"…Sit and listen," Speon said. "If your legs give out, you won't hear the rest of this either way."

Only then did my brother sit. I saw that his knees were still trembling.

---

"…They come out? Alive?" my brother asked. His voice trembled.

"…Yes, alive," Speon said. "But not whole."

"…What do you mean?"

My brother asked it again. I had a feeling he would ask it a second time, and a third. It wasn't an answer a person could take in all at once.

Speon closed his eyes for a moment.

"…They come out having lost their name," Speon said. "And along with it, they lose who they loved. The most painful memory is erased, and along with it, it seems, the names and faces bound up in that memory disappear too."

*Hearing that, I thought of the old woman at the flowerbed. That blurred place where she had forgotten her daughter's name but "having loved her" alone remained. For the first time, I understood that the two things worked by the same principle. Haven's "resolving" and the Completion House's "completing" only differed in degree — they were looking in the same direction.*

"…So nothing at all is left?" I asked. My voice had gone small.

"…I said something is left," Speon said. "It isn't erased completely. But what that something is differs from person to person. For some it's a habit of the hands. For some it's a line of a song they liked."

"…How do you know what's left?" my brother asked.

"…You only find out by watching them, after they come out," Speon said. "That's why I'm here. To watch."

"…What are they like, when they first come out?" I asked. "Aren't they afraid?"

"…They're afraid," Speon said. "They don't know who they are, or why they came up out of the water. But strangely, at first, they're all quiet. They don't even cry. They just sit still and look around."

"…Why don't they cry?" I asked.

"…To cry, you have to know you've lost something," Speon said. "But those people don't even know what they've lost. So there's no way for them to cry."

*Hearing that, I felt a chill run over my skin. I understood then, for the first time, that forgetting wasn't only a sad thing — it was a thing that made you unable to even grieve.*

---

"…So could our father come out somewhere here too?" I asked.

"…He could," Speon said. "But it might not be this well. Each Completion House seems to connect to a different well. I only know this one."

"…Then how many wells are there?"

"…I don't know," Speon said. "I'm still looking too."

*I could feel, in that answer, that Speon had been doing this for a long time. And that even after all that time, he still hadn't found every answer.*

"…Then we'll have to find the other wells too," my brother said. His voice already sounded decided.

"…It won't be easy," Speon said. "This world is wide, and the wells are hidden. It took me years just to come to keep this one."

"…We'll do it anyway," my brother said.

There was no wavering in his voice. Beside him I felt that steadiness, and at the same time accepted that this journey might turn out to be far longer than we'd thought. When we left Brick Hollow, I'd believed it would be over in a matter of days. Now, for the first time, it occurred to me that it might take years.

Speon looked at my brother a long time after that answer. I felt something like pity in that look, and something a little larger than pity — a kind of respect.

---

"…Why do you do this?" my brother asked. "What does it have to do with you?"

Speon was quiet for a long time.

"…I once made someone else wait, a long time ago," he said finally. "Back then, I couldn't wait, and I handed someone else over instead. Waiting here, at this well, now… it's doing the thing I couldn't do then."

*I didn't fully understand what he meant. But seeing my brother offer no argument at all to that, I knew there was something heavy and true in it.*

I understood then, all over again, that Speon had reached this spot far, far before us. We thought we'd walked four days. Speon had not moved an inch from this place in years. I could only guess, then, at what kind of walking that was.

The fire had burned down low. Speon fed in another branch. The flame caught again and lit his face. For the first time, I really saw the old exhaustion that had settled into it.

"…What does 'handed someone else over' mean?" I asked carefully.

Speon looked at me. He didn't answer. But the silence said more than an answer would have. I decided not to press further.

Speon went on.

"…I've told you everything I know. But there's one more thing. The people who come out here — even having lost their name, they don't become an entirely new person. Something remains. I help them hold on to what remains."

The fire kept sinking lower. No one spoke again. In that silence I felt my brother settling something inside himself. His eyes weren't on Speon anymore — they were on the distant horizon.

That night, lying down to sleep, I couldn't close my eyes. I could hear my brother tossing beside me too. The sky was full of stars. Looking up at them, I thought of Father — sitting somewhere, perhaps, without a name. Was he looking at these same stars right now? And if he was, did he even know they were stars?

*I set that line down as the last sentence of this chapter. That night I learned, all at once, that Father might be alive somewhere, and that he might not remember us at all. I didn't know then which of the two was harder to bear.*
