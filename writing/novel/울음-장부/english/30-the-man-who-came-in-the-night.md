# Chapter 30. The Man Who Came in the Night

> _Previously_ — The brother and sister gave Father a placeholder name, Waiting. Parting from Temel, they heard: "Losing a name doesn't have to mean losing the place where you're loved."

That night, Father woke suddenly, screaming.

We had made camp under a small tree that day. It had been a long walk, so everyone fell asleep early. The last thing I remember is counting stars and drifting off.

That day's walk had been unusually long. For the first time, Father let a little of his tiredness show. "…Should we walk a bit more?" he'd asked, in a voice that watched our faces even as he said it. I thought that carefulness was another habit that had stayed with him.

Moonlight filtered through the leaves and trembled across Father's face. I remember it was the last thing I saw before falling asleep. He'd looked peaceful.

My brother was already sound asleep beside me. Days of built-up tension had made him fall asleep unusually fast. I remember listening to his breathing and feeling my own eyes drift shut, slowly.

Insects called low in the grass. I fell asleep to that sound like a lullaby. I never imagined that peace would be shattered completely before the night was through.

*I've gone back to that night many times since. That evening, nothing had been wrong with any of us. Father had smiled twice; my brother had tended the fire; I had counted stars. I learned then that the nights that come apart never announce themselves first.*

"…Where am I! Why am I here!" Father shouted. His eyes were wide open. Looking straight at us, and recognizing nothing at all.

My brother tried to go to him and Father recoiled. The movement was so fast that, for an instant, I was terrified he'd turned into an entirely different person.

Father's back struck the tree trunk. He didn't seem to feel it at all. Both hands were thrust forward, pushing us away.

A branch shook and a few dry leaves fell. Even that small sound made Father flinch and curl in on himself. I understood, then. It wasn't us Father was afraid of. It was this whole night.

"…No one's here but us," my brother said. "It's just the three of us."

"…Three?" Father asked back. "Who are the three?"

My brother couldn't answer that. I saw his mouth open, then shut without a word. He must have understood, right then, that saying our names would do no good at all.

*I froze with fear in that moment. What frightened me more than the thought of Father attacking us was the sight of that gaze — a gaze that saw us as complete strangers.*

"…It's all right, it's all right," my brother said, holding up both hands, keeping his voice as low as he could. "We won't hurt you."

Father didn't calm at that either. He kept gasping and looking around wildly. Watching him, unable to do anything, only made it more terrifying.

His eyes darted here and there. As if trying to recognize something, or as if just searching for a hole to run through. I couldn't find, in that gaze, a single trace of the gentle Father I'd seen by day.

I half hid behind my brother and watched Father. Under the moonlight his shadow looked unusually large and unsteady. I thought that shadow looked like it belonged to someone else entirely.

*I understood, for the first time then, that getting Father back wasn't a single event. The Father who had held my hand last night, and the Father who didn't recognize us tonight, being the same person — that was what frightened me more.*

My brother stood there, hands still raised, not moving. I saw him frozen in place, unable to move forward or back. The stance of someone who didn't want to fight but couldn't run either.

I hated myself in that moment for being useless. All I could do was stand off at a distance, looking back and forth between my brother and Father.

*That night I mimicked something like prayer for the first time. I didn't even know who I was praying to. I just kept repeating, in my mind, "please." I didn't know if that counted as a prayer.*

I had my fists clenched so tight my nails dug into my palms. I didn't even feel the pain. I only noticed the marks it had left afterward.

"…It's all right, no one's getting hurt," my brother said again, low. His voice shook, unlike his words.

Father still looked around with eyes that didn't recognize us. I found, in that moment, that I was afraid even to call Father "Father" — those eyes didn't look like his at all.

---

Then, quietly, someone stepped out of the dark.

"…It's all right," said a familiar voice. "Breathe slowly."

I recognized that voice. The man from the flowerbed. Or — no, just the man, now. The one who'd been standing where no one had planted anything, the one who'd also been seen at the well.

"…What are you doing here…" My brother couldn't finish the sentence, too startled.

I could tell there was relief mixed into my brother's shock. In that moment he said neither *scared* nor *glad*. But he must have been both.

He'd made almost no sound as he walked. I had no idea how long he'd been nearby. He appeared as quietly as if he'd always been part of the darkness itself.

I looked again toward the direction he'd come from. No trees, no brush that way. Just darkness. I had no time to think it strange — I was only relieved that he had come.

He said nothing, and quietly sat down in front of Father.

There was no hurry at all in his movement. Strangely, that unhurriedness seemed to be settling Father down even faster. As if the not-rushing itself were a kind of comfort.

He didn't touch Father. Didn't grip him, didn't pat his shoulder. He simply sat at a distance where an arm might just barely reach, and looked in the same direction Father was looking.

*I only understood later how exact that distance was. Too far, and it's leaving someone alone. Too close, and it's pushing them. He knew that single hand's-width between the two, exactly.*

I stood beside them, holding my brother's hand. His hand was still shaking. I gripped it tighter. He gripped mine back. We endured that moment together, leaning on each other.

Moonlight lit the man's back. A blurred outline showed through his worn clothes. I thought that silhouette wasn't unfamiliar. At the flowerbed, by the well, and now here. I found that repetition strange, and at the same time, strangely comforting.

Leaf-shadows trembled across his back. I noticed that shadow brushed against his body without changing him at all. Wind, darkness — to him, they were only things passing through.

I had a strange thought in that moment. That we had never called him here. It was Father who had shouted; no one had asked anyone for help. And yet he had come.

*Looking back, I think I already heard one answer that night. That there are those who come even when unsummoned. I didn't understand what that meant, then.*

---

"…Who am I?" Father asked him. A question he hadn't asked us, he asked him.

*A strange hurt passed through me in that moment. A question he hadn't asked us, he was asking a stranger. Looking back now, that wasn't because Father distrusted us. It was, I think, simply that there was nothing he needed to prove to this man.*

I looked at my brother in that moment. He was looking at me too. We confirmed the same small hurt in each other's expressions. Neither of us said a word. But something passed between us anyway, in that look.

Father was leaning slightly toward the man. I thought that posture looked far too at ease, for a posture toward a stranger.

My brother was watching quietly too, beside me. I could feel him wanting to step in and holding himself back. His hand twitched, more than once, and returned to stillness.

"…You don't need to know right now," the man said. "You don't have a name, but you're here right now. That's enough."

Father's breathing gradually settled.

I watched that shift happen beside them. Watching the shoulders of someone who'd been screaming minutes ago slowly lower — it was like watching a storm die down.

"…Aren't you afraid of me?" Father asked.

"…I'm not afraid," he said. "I've met many people who lost their names."

There was no exaggeration at all in his voice. He spoke plainly, like stating a fact. I found that plainness, somehow, more trustworthy than anything else.

"…Did they all find their names again?" Father asked. There was longing in his voice.

"…Some of them," he said. "Some are still looking. But either way, they weren't lost while they were looking."

Father sat quietly, turning that answer over. I watched his shoulders lower, bit by bit. He didn't seem to understand the full meaning of it. But something was calming him.

"…Will I find mine too, someday?" Father asked again. His voice was much calmer than before.

"…Yes," he said. "But I don't know when. No one does."

"…And you wait, even so?" Father asked.

"…Yes," he said. "Waiting is a little easier, too, when there's someone staying with you."

"…Is that what you're doing here with me right now?" Father asked.

"…Yes," he said simply. No hesitation at all.

"…Who are you waiting for?" Father asked.

I held my breath, listening to that question. I'd wondered it myself, badly.

He was quiet for a moment.

"…It's less waiting," he said, "than going out to search."

"…For whom?"

"…For those who haven't come back yet," he said.

Father nodded slowly at that. Not the nod of someone who understood completely. But the nod of someone trying to understand.

*Hearing that, I looked at my brother. His face was complicated. Suspicion, and something he couldn't quite overcome, both there at once.*

He went on speaking to Father, low, for a while. I didn't catch all of it. But that tone was strangely like the one our mother had used to sing us to sleep in Brick Hollow.

*I couldn't explain that resemblance then. I still can't, exactly, now. But from that night on, I learned that I could feel familiarity even in an unfamiliar voice.*

I edged a little closer, trying to hear that voice more clearly. But strangely, the closer I got, the fainter the words became. As if those words weren't meant for me at all, only for Father. I stepped back quietly at that.

Father's breathing slowed, little by little. I sat beside him and listened to that rhythm. It was hard to believe it was the same person who'd been shouting like the world was ending, only minutes before.

---

After Father had fallen back asleep, my brother went up to the man.

"…Who exactly are you?" my brother asked. There was an edge in his voice. "Brick Hollow, Haven, and now here too… why do you keep showing up?"

He looked at my brother for a long time.

"…I've always been here," he said. "You just didn't recognize me."

"…What does that mean?"

"…In Brick Hollow, in Haven, and here too, now," he said. "I've never once left you."

My brother let out a hollow laugh at that.

"…Then why can't you help Father?" my brother asked. His voice rose. "Why don't you give his memory back? You just said you were always here."

He didn't answer, and my brother stepped closer.

"…If you were really always here," my brother said, "were you there when we fell carrying bricks? When Mother was sick? Were you there then too?"

He nodded at my brother.

"…I was."

"…Then why didn't you do anything!" my brother shouted. At that shout, Father stirred in his sleep. My brother clamped his mouth shut instantly. Then, in a much lower voice, he said it again. "…Why didn't you do anything."

He smiled quietly. He didn't answer.

I couldn't bear that silence and wanted to say something in his place. But nothing came to mind. All I could do was lightly grip my brother's arm.

He simply rose and looked up at the night sky for a moment. Starlight passed across his face like a brush. In that brief instant, I thought I saw something like grief cross his features. I couldn't be certain.

*I still can't describe that expression exactly, even now. It wasn't anger. It wasn't grief either. But it was, unmistakably, the face of someone who had endured something for a long time.*

Then he turned once to look at us, and walked back into the darkness he'd come from.

Wind blew from the direction he'd disappeared into. I caught no scent at all in that wind. If a person had passed through, something should have been left behind. There was nothing.

Somewhere in the grass, the night insects began to call again. Only then did I realize the sound had cut off completely, just moments before. It felt like the world had held its breath and now let it go.

I went and stood beside my brother, watching that retreating back. He didn't say anything. He just stood there for a long time.

"…Brother," I said quietly.

"…I'm not angry," my brother said. But his voice shook, unlike his words. "I just… don't know."

I took his arm. He didn't pull away.

"…Brother, I'm cold," I said. It wasn't true. But I thought if I said it, he might sit down.

Only then did he sit. We sat side by side beside the dying fire and listened to Father's breathing for a long time. It was even, and slow.

*I set that unanswered silence down as this chapter's last line. That night I saw something in my brother's face deeper than anger. I thought, then, that it was fear. I know now. It was my brother's certainty, beginning to crack — just a little.*
