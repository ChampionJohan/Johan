# Chapter 30. The Man Who Came in the Night

> _Previously_ — The siblings gave the nameless Father a temporary name — Waiting. Parting from Temel, they heard him say, "A person can lose his name and still not lose the place where he's loved."

That night, Father suddenly woke, shouting.

We had made camp under a small tree that day. We'd walked a long way that afternoon, and everyone fell asleep early. The last thing I remember is counting stars before sleep took me.

Moonlight, filtering down through the leaves, shifted over Father's face. I remember that was the last thing I saw before I slept. He looked peaceful.

Jeteo was already deep asleep beside me. Days of unbroken tension had put him under unusually fast. I remember listening to his breathing before my own eyes slid shut.

"…Where is this! Why am I here!" Father shouted. His eyes were wide open. Even looking straight at us, they recognized nothing at all.

When Jeteo tried to move closer, Father backed away. The motion was so sudden that, for a moment, I was afraid Father had turned into someone else entirely.

*I was so frightened in that moment that my body froze. It wasn't Father attacking us that scared me most — it was that look in his eyes, seeing us as complete strangers.*

"…It's all right, it's all right," Jeteo said, raising both hands, keeping his voice as low as he could. "We won't hurt you."

Even so, Father couldn't calm down. He kept gasping for breath, looking wildly around him. Watching him, unable to do anything at all, frightened me even more.

I watched him from half-hidden behind Jeteo. In the moonlight, Father's shadow looked unusually large and unsteady. I thought that shadow looked like it belonged to someone else.

*For the first time, I understood in my whole body that getting Father back wasn't a single event. The fact that the Father who'd held our hands last night and the Father who couldn't recognize us tonight were the same person frightened me even more.*

Jeteo stood still in front of Father, his hands still raised. I watched him frozen there, unable to move forward or back. It was the posture of someone who didn't want to fight, and couldn't run either.

In that moment, I hated how useless I was. All I could do was stand back and look from Jeteo to Father and back again.

---

That's when someone came quietly out of the dark.

"…It's all right," said a familiar voice. "Breathe slowly."

I recognized the voice. It was the man from the flowerbed. No — he was just the man now. The one who'd been there in a place no one had planted, the one they said had been seen at the well too.

"…Why are you here…" Jeteo couldn't finish, too startled.

I could hear both surprise and a kind of relief in Jeteo's voice. In that moment he said neither that he was afraid nor that he was glad. But it was probably both.

He made almost no sound as he walked. I had no idea how long he'd been nearby. He appeared as quietly as if he had always been part of the darkness itself.

Without answering, he sat down quietly in front of Father.

There wasn't a trace of hurry in his movements. Strangely, that unhurriedness seemed to settle Father faster than anything else could. As if not hurrying was, itself, a kind of comfort.

---

"…Who am I?" Father asked him — a question he hadn't asked us.

*A strange hurt came over me right then. He was asking a stranger a question he'd never asked us. Thinking back on it now, it wasn't that Father didn't trust us. It was simply that, with this man, there was nothing at all Father had to prove.*

Jeteo was watching quietly beside me too. I could feel him wanting to step in and holding himself back. His hand twitched, again and again, then returned to his side.

"…You don't have to know, right now," he said. "Even without a name, you're here, right now. That's enough."

Father's breathing slowly settled.

I watched that change from where I stood. Seeing the shoulders of someone who had been shouting minutes before slowly settle was like watching a storm pass.

"…Aren't you afraid of me?" Father asked.

"…I'm not afraid," he said. "I've met a great many people who've lost their names."

There was no exaggeration in his voice at all. He said it plainly, the way you'd state a fact. Strangely, that plainness made him feel all the more trustworthy.

"…Did all of them get their names back?" Father asked. There was longing in his voice.

"…Some of them," he said. "Some are still looking, even now. But neither kind was ever truly lost, not even while they were still searching."

Father sat still, seeming to turn the words over. I watched his shoulders lower, little by little. Even without understanding all of what it meant, something in those words was settling him.

*Hearing that, I looked at Jeteo. His face held a complicated look — suspicion, and something that couldn't overcome that suspicion, both at once.*

He kept speaking to Father, low, on and on. I didn't catch all of it. But the way he spoke was strangely like the way Mother used to speak, putting us to bed back in Brick Hollow.

*I couldn't explain that resemblance, then. I still can't explain it exactly, even now. But since that night, I've known that a stranger's voice can carry a kind of familiarity too.*

Father's breathing slowly grew slower. I sat beside him, listening quietly to its rhythm. It was a breath so peaceful you'd never have believed it belonged to someone who, minutes before, had been shouting as though the world were ending.

---

After Father had fallen back asleep, Jeteo went up to the man.

"…Who are you, really?" Jeteo asked. His voice had an edge. "Brick Hollow, Haven, now all the way out here… why do you keep showing up?"

He looked at Jeteo for a long moment.

"…I've always been here," he said. "You're the ones who didn't recognize me."

"…What does that mean?"

"…In Brick Hollow, in Haven, and here, now," he said. "I've never once left you."

Jeteo let out a hollow laugh at that.

"…Then why can't you help Father?" Jeteo asked, his voice rising. "Why won't you give him his memory back? You say you've always been here."

He smiled quietly. He didn't answer.

He simply rose and looked up at the night sky for a moment. Starlight passed lightly over his face. Even in that brief instant, I thought I saw something like sorrow in it. I couldn't be certain.

*Even now, I can't describe that expression exactly. It wasn't anger. It wasn't sorrow either. But it was, without question, the face of someone who had endured something for a very long time.*

Then he turned once to look back at us, and walked back into the darkness he'd come from.

Watching him go, I came and stood beside Jeteo. He said nothing, and stood there a long time.

"…Jeteo," I said quietly.

"…I'm not angry," Jeteo said. But his voice shook, unlike his words. "I just… don't know."

*I set down that not-quite-answer as the last line of this chapter. That night, I saw something on Jeteo's face deeper than anger. I thought it was fear. Now I know. It was his certainty, beginning, ever so slowly, to crack.*
