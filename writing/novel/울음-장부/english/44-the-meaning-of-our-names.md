# Chapter 44. The Meaning of Our Names

> _Previously_ — Father called Brother's name for the first time. And
> he remembered that, the night before he was taken, Mother had left
> with him, ahead of time, the words she'd wanted to say to Brother.
> "You're a child who's always going to be asking. I'm proud of that."
> That night, for the first time, Brother cried out loud.

The seasons by the well went on passing quietly. They were days when,
on the surface, nothing seemed to have changed at all.

A few days later, Father called us over with a somewhat clearer look
on his face.

That morning, Father had woken unusually early. His eyes were clearer
than usual. I found that clearness both unfamiliar and welcome.

"…I remember who named you two," Father said.

I stopped what I was doing at that. The bucket handle nearly slipped
from my hand. I could tell, just from Father's face, that this wasn't
a question that would simply pass by.

Brother felt it too. From Brick Hollow to now, we had never once asked
what our names meant. A name was just a name. Something you were
called by — that was all.

I felt strange in that moment. We had called each other's names
hundreds of times over. And yet it had never once occurred to us that
something might be held inside them.

"…Have you ever wondered what your name meant?" I asked Brother.

"…No, never," Brother said. "I always thought a name was just a
name."

"…Me too," I said.

In that moment I saw something like fear cross Brother's face. I
understood, for the first time, that knowing your own name might be a
frightening thing.

---

Father sat us down beside the stone wall around the well. Morning
sunlight fell slanting across the three of us.

I felt Father's voice was unusually clear that day. As if this,
whatever it was, was a story he refused to let slip past him.

"…Your mother named you," Father said. "There was an old language,
back where she was from. A language that puts meaning into names.
She named you two with that language."

Hearing that, I realized I had never once asked where Mother was
from. To us, Mother had only ever been "Mother" — we didn't know
where she'd come from.

I found that fact strangely sad. There was still so much about
Mother we didn't know.

"…What does it mean?" Brother asked.

I heard Brother's voice shake faintly as he asked it.

Father searched hard in his memory for a moment.

I felt my heart pounding hard in that silence. I reached over and
took Brother's hand. He took mine back.

"…Jeteo means… 'to seek,' 'to search,'" Father said. "Your mother
told me. 'This child is going to spend his whole life asking things,
digging for the bottom of them. That's going to be his name.'"

*The moment I heard that, my breath caught. I looked at Brother.
Brother stood there, unable to say anything. That name lined up
exactly with every way Brother had lived, from Brick Hollow to now.*

I saw Brother's hand tremble faintly. That hand still carried the
calluses of the bucket rope.

"…So I just… lived out my name?" Brother asked. Bewilderment was
mixed into his voice.

"…Or maybe the name followed you," I said. "Either way — isn't it
strange?"

Brother had no answer to that. He only looked off into the distance.
I knew he would turn that question over for a long time to come.

"…Does that man know it too? What my name means?" Brother suddenly
asked.

Father tilted his head.

"…Who's that man?" Father asked back.

Brother hesitated a moment at the question, then laughed a little.

"…I'll explain later," Brother said.

I understood that Brother still hadn't told Father the whole story of
that man. But he wasn't in a hurry. Some stories only come out when
their time arrives.

I sat quietly beside Brother. I added nothing more. I already knew,
by then, that some moments are enough just by being beside someone.

---

I hesitated a long while before asking my own question. All the more
so, after hearing Brother's name.

Brother looked toward me. As though he'd read my mind, he gave a small
nod. It seemed to mean it was all right to ask.

Father looked at me too, and waited. In that waiting, I felt, oddly,
at ease.

"…And Elpis?" I asked. My voice shook.

"…Hope," Father said. "Your mother told me. 'This child is going to
keep waiting, for no reason at all. That's going to be her name.'"

*The moment I heard that, tears poured out of me. I thought of the
seed, the flowerbed, the heart that had watered it every day and
waited. My whole life I hadn't known why I was someone who waited so
well. Now I know. It was my name.*

In that moment I thought of the last night in Brick Hollow — Brother's
hand, holding out the seed pouch to me. I hadn't known the reason
then. Now I do. I had simply taken up that seed the way my own name
told me to.

"…Isn't the name Hope heavy to carry?" Brother asked.

I laughed at that question.

"…Maybe it does feel heavy," I said. "But it's not a bad kind of
heavy."

Brother quietly wrapped an arm around my shoulder. He said nothing
more. That touch alone was enough for me.

---

We sat like that in silence for a long while. The wind passed over
the grass by the well.

Speon, tidying up the well in the distance, glanced our way. Something
about his face looked as if he'd noticed something. But he didn't come
closer.

"…Do you think Mother knew we'd turn out like this?" Brother asked.

Father shook his head. His face showed he was thinking hard about the
question.

"…I don't think she knew," Father said. "But I think she knew what
kind of people you two would become. Someone who searches. Someone
who waits."

"…And what about you, Father?" I asked. "Does your name have a
meaning too?"

Father looked startled by the question. It seemed like a question
he'd never once thought about. He looked down at his own hands for a
moment, as if the answer might be written there.

Watching that expression, I felt something strange. We had called
Father's name hundreds of times over. But this was the first time
we'd ever wondered what it meant.

"…I don't know," Father said. "My own name… I don't really know it
either."

"…Then we'll find it for you," Brother said. "Someday."

Father laughed at that. There was a faint sheen of moisture at the
corners of his eyes.

Watching that laugh, I imagined the day we would truly find the
meaning of Father's name. I hoped that on that day, too, the three of
us would be together, just like this.

*I set that laugh down in this record. I understood, then. Knowing the
meaning of a name matters less than having someone beside you who
calls it.*

Brother looked at me at that. I looked back at Brother. We nodded to
each other without a word.

Father looked between the two of us, and laughed softly. It was a
laugh I hadn't seen in a long time.

*I set down that line as the last sentence of this chapter. I
understood, for the first time that day, that we hadn't lived
according to the meaning of our names. We had simply lived as
ourselves, and that life had been inside our names from the very
beginning. It took me a long time, thinking it over, to see how large
that difference was.*
