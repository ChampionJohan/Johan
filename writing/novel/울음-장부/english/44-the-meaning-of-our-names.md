# Chapter 44. The Meaning of Our Names

> _Previously_ — Father called Jeteo's name for the first time. And he remembered — the words Mother had left for Jeteo, in advance, the night before she was taken. "You'll always be a child who asks. I'm proud of that." That night, Jeteo cried aloud for the first time.

The seasons at the well passed quietly by. Days went by that looked, on the surface, as if nothing at all had changed.

Dew gathered on the grass tips each morning. I went out barefoot and liked how the tops of my feet grew wet, so I put my shoes on late on purpose. The clothes of the people coming to draw water were growing thicker, bit by bit. The changing of the seasons always announced itself that way — without anyone ever saying a word.

*I wrote those days down as "days when nothing happened." But reading it again now, those were the days right before a certain word was about to arrive. I didn't know that, then.*

During those days, I found myself calling Jeteo's name unusually often. For no reason. Just because I wanted to. Jeteo didn't find it odd, and answered every time. I thought it was like some small practice the two of us were doing together.

A few days later, Father called us with a clearer face than before.

That morning, Father had woken unusually early. His eyes were clearer than usual. I found that clarity unfamiliar, but welcome.

Father was already sitting on the stone by the door. Hands on his knees, looking toward where the sun was rising. I thought that back looked like the back of someone waiting for something.

I had seen Father like that a few times before. Every time his memory came back a little, Father grew quiet like that. As if he were holding his body still until something settled into place, inside.

"…I remember who named you two," Father said.

I stopped what I was doing at that. The bucket handle in my hand nearly slipped. I knew, just from Father's expression, that this wasn't just some question passing by.

Jeteo felt the same way. From Brick Hollow up to now, we had never once asked what our names meant. A name had just been a name. Something you were called by. That was all.

I felt something strange in that moment. We had called each other's names hundreds of times over. But it had never once occurred to me that something might be held inside those names.

"…Have you ever wondered what your name means?" I asked.

"…No, never," Jeteo said. "I always thought a name was just a name."

"…Me too," I said.

I suddenly thought of something from Haven. In that city, filing a name would instantly bring up its etymology, its origin, statistics. I hadn't once thought to ask about that, back then. Strange, thinking of it now. It wasn't that I hadn't been curious. Any answer received in that city had always felt like it belonged to someone else.

*I know now. Who gives an answer is half of what the answer contains. The meaning of a name the Ledger might have told me, and the meaning Father stumbled through to tell me, would have been the same sentence — and yet different things.*

I saw something like fear cross Jeteo's face in that moment. That knowing what your name meant might be a frightening thing — I felt that for the first time, then.

I understood that fear. Knowing the meaning of a name meant that someone had known us before we'd ever been set into the world. I found that both welcome and, somehow, unsettling.

---

Father sat us down beside the stone wall at the well. The morning sunlight fell slanting over the three of us.

The stone wall had cooled overnight, so it was chilly at first. But as we sat there, the sunlight moved across it little by little, and by the time the story ended, our backs had grown warm. From that day on, I thought of that temperature every time I passed that wall.

Before sitting, Father swept a hand once across the top of the wall. A gesture of brushing off dust. I understood that it was Father's way of tidying our seat before he sat us down. Father still did things like that without a word.

I noticed Father's voice was unusually clear that day. As if he didn't want to miss a single word of this story.

"…Your mother named you," Father said. "There was an old language, from her hometown. A language that gives names meaning. Your mother named you both with that language."

Hearing that, I realized I had never once asked about Mother's hometown. To us, Mother had always just been "Mother." We never knew where she was from.

I found that fact strangely sad. There was still so much we didn't know about Mother.

I didn't know what Mother had liked as a child, what songs she'd known, what she'd been afraid of. I only knew her as Mother. But Mother had been someone's daughter too, once.

*I still haven't filled in those blanks. But now, that emptiness isn't only sad. That there's still something I don't know also means there's still something left to ask.*

"…What does it mean?" Jeteo asked.

I heard Jeteo's voice tremble faintly as he asked that question.

Father worked hard to dredge up the memory for a moment.

I felt my heart pound loudly in that silence. I quietly took Jeteo's hand. He took mine back.

Jeteo's palm was damp. I knew from that how tense he was. His face gave nothing away. His hand had spoken first.

A gust of wind passed by and stirred Father's hair across his forehead. Father didn't brush it back. His eyes stayed closed, and his lips moved soundlessly a few times. I thought Father was rehearsing the words to himself first, inside.

"…Jeteo means… 'to seek,' 'to search,'" Father said. "Your mother told me. 'This child will spend his whole life asking. That'll be his name.'"

*My breath caught, hearing that. I looked at Jeteo. He couldn't say a word, just stood there. That name overlapped exactly with every way Jeteo had lived, from Brick Hollow all the way to now.*

I saw Jeteo's hand trembling faintly. That hand still carried the calluses of the bucket rope.

*In that moment, I remembered, all at once, every question Jeteo had ever thrown out, from Brick Hollow to this well. Why did this happen to us. If God is there, why did he just let it happen. Isn't there a real answer besides that one.*

*Haven had filed every one of those questions as an "unresolved complaint." The Administrator saw Jeteo as someone bad at intake. Even I sometimes found myself frustrated by why he kept pressing so hard.*

*But it hadn't been a flaw. It had been the name he'd been called by since the day he was born.*

Jeteo opened his mouth a few times and shut it again. I could tell he was trying not to cry. He didn't cry. Instead, he let out one very long breath.

"…So did I just… live according to my name?" Jeteo asked. His voice was mixed with confusion.

"…Or maybe the name followed you," I said. "Either way, isn't it strange?"

Jeteo couldn't answer that. He just gazed off into the distance. I knew that he would turn that question over for a long time.

"…Did she like that about me?" Jeteo asked, after a long while. "That I kept pressing so hard."

"…It wasn't so much that she liked it," Father said slowly. "I think she knew it was just who you were. She never tried to fix it."

Jeteo bowed his head at that. I saw the back of his neck flush.

*I understood, then. What Jeteo had truly feared for so long wasn't the absence of an answer. It was that he might be someone made wrong.*

"…Does that man know? What my name means?" Jeteo asked suddenly.

Father tilted his head.

"…Who's that man?" Father asked back.

Jeteo paused for a moment at the question, then laughed a little.

"…I'll explain it to you later," Jeteo said.

I knew Jeteo hadn't told Father everything about the man yet. But he wasn't in a hurry. Some stories only came out when their time arrived.

I sat quietly beside Jeteo. I added nothing more. I already knew now that some moments are enough with just being beside someone, and nothing else.

Jeteo turned the stone in his hand over, again and again. I knew that gesture was a sign he was digesting something. Words came late, but his hands always moved first.

---

I had hesitated a long time before asking my own question. Even more so, having just heard Jeteo's name.

Jeteo looked toward me. As if he'd read my mind, he gave a small nod. It seemed to mean it was all right to ask.

Father was watching me too, waiting. In that waiting, I felt, strangely, at ease.

"…What about Elpis?" I asked. My voice trembled.

The moment I asked, I grew afraid. Jeteo's name had fit him exactly. Would mine, too? Or what if I turned out to be the one whose name didn't match. I had those thoughts, in those few seconds.

Father looked at me for a long time. Then he smiled. Not the smile of someone who'd just recalled the answer — the smile of someone glad for the answer itself.

"…Hope," Father said. "Your mother told me. 'This child will keep waiting, even with no reason at all. That'll be her name.'"

*Tears poured out of me the moment I heard that. I thought of the seed, the flowerbed, the heart that had watered it every day, waiting. I had never known why I was such a good waiter. Now I know. It was my name.*

I thought, in that moment, of Jeteo's hand handing me the seed pouch, that last night in Brick Hollow. I hadn't known the reason then. Now I did. I had simply taken up that seed, according to my name.

Strangely, I wasn't ashamed of crying. In Haven, I would have filed this crying. Here, I just cried. Father clumsily patted my back. Completely out of rhythm. I liked that mismatched rhythm.

*During those days, I kept thinking about why I'd been able to wait so long. It wasn't because I was especially good. It wasn't because my faith was great. I was simply a child who waited. And someone had recognized that in me beforehand, and given it as my name.*

*I think that's proof I was loved. Because it's a name only someone who has watched you a long time could ever give.*

"…Isn't the name Hope heavy?" Jeteo asked.

I laughed at that question.

Jeteo never said his own name was heavy. Worrying about the weight of someone else's name first — that was very Jeteo.

"…It feels heavy sometimes," I said. "But not in a bad way."

Jeteo quietly wrapped an arm around my shoulder. He said nothing. That touch alone was enough.

---

We sat like that for a long while without speaking. The wind brushed over the grass at the well.

The sun had climbed fairly high by then. Someone who'd come to draw water greeted us, seeing us there. We returned the greeting. It was a morning where nothing at all seemed to have changed.

And yet, from that morning on, my name sounded a little different every time it was called. The same two syllables, but there was something inside them now.

From that day on, I paused for a moment every time someone called my name. When Jeteo called out "Elpis," I felt I could hear, inside that call, even the heart Mother had held the very first time she gave me that name. I thought it was a strange habit, but I didn't want to break it.

Far off, Speon glanced our way while tidying the well. He had a face like he'd noticed something. But he didn't come closer.

Instead, he made the sound of the bucket a little quieter than usual. I noticed that. A well-keeper was someone who cared for people by handling things quietly.

That evening, I told Speon what our names meant. He listened for a long while and then only said this:

"…Good names. Both of them."

"…What does your name mean?" I asked.

Speon laughed.

"…I don't know either," he said. "But I've been called well-keeper longer than I've been called by my name. There are more names for a person than just their given name."

"…Do you think Mother knew we'd turn out like this?" Jeteo asked.

Father shook his head. His face showed he was thinking hard about the question.

"…She probably didn't know that," Father said. "But I think she knew what kind of people you'd become. A seeker. A waiter."

"…Then what about you, Father?" I asked. "Does your name have a meaning too?"

Father looked startled by that question. It seemed like a question he'd never once thought about. Father looked down at his own hands for a moment. As if the answer might be written there.

Watching that expression, I felt something strange. We had called Father's name hundreds of times. But this was the first time we'd ever wondered what it meant.

"…I don't know," Father said. "My name… I don't really know either."

I found those words sad. Father had given us back the meaning of our names, but he still hadn't found his own. I thought that showed exactly how Father had lived, these past few years.

"…Then we'll find it for you," Jeteo said. "Someday."

Father smiled at that. There was a faint gleam of moisture at his eyes.

Watching that smile, I imagined the day we would truly find the meaning of Father's name. I hoped that day, too, the three of us would be together, just like now.

*I set down that smile in this record. I knew, then. What mattered more than knowing the meaning of a name was that the person who calls that name is there, beside you.*

Jeteo looked at me at that. I looked at Jeteo. We nodded to each other without a word.

Father looked back and forth between the two of us and smiled softly. A smile I hadn't seen in a long time.

That evening, I called the three of our names to myself, side by side, in my mind. Jeteo, Elpis, and Father's name, whose meaning we hadn't found yet. I decided to wait for the day that third name would be filled in too, someday.

*I set down those words as the last line of this chapter. I understood, that day, for the first time. We hadn't lived according to the meaning of our names. We had simply lived as ourselves, and that life had been inside our names from the very start. I only understood, after a long time thinking it over, how great that difference was.*
