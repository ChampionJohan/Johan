# Chapter 6. The Paper Stamped COMPLETE

> _Previously_ — Jeteo decided to find out for himself where the "reassigned" went, and began investigating the city. At the flowerbed, the strange man gave Elpis a nameless seed; going to that spot each day, she was learning to wait, not to fear.

The place my brother found was a low building that appeared on no map of the city.

No windows. No sign at the door. My brother said he'd walked past it for days without knowing what was inside.

Even the color of its walls matched the buildings around it exactly. I was amazed my brother had found it at all. He said he'd simply noticed something strange in the way people walked — everyone's pace picked up, just slightly, only in front of that building.

"…What's that place?" I asked.

"…I don't know," my brother said. "But I've seen people go in. I've never once seen anyone come out."

*I heard that and felt a chill go down my back. That was the first time it truly sank in that my brother was doing something dangerous.*

My brother said he'd been watching that building for days. The people always came at roughly the same time. They walked up quietly and went straight in, without even knocking. As if they'd already been summoned.

My brother lowered his voice while he told me this. I found myself glancing around the room, even though no one else was there.

"…They all walked in on their own," my brother said. "Nobody was dragging them. That was the strangest part."

"…Why would they go in on their own?" I asked.

"…I don't know," my brother said. "But they all had the same face. The same face as that old woman."

*I heard that and thought of the old woman at the flowerbed. That hollowed-out face after she'd filed away her grief. A frightening thought came to me then. Maybe that building was where people went once they'd already been "resolved" — once there was nothing left in them to file.*

---

My brother said he'd found a small window around the back of the building. Something like a low vent. He'd looked inside through the gap.

As he told the story, he acted out with his fingers the crouch he must have made, kneeling low to the ground. He seemed to want to show me just how low a spot it had been. I could guess, from that gesture, how careful he must have been in that moment.

"…What was in there?" I asked.

"…Shelves," my brother said. "The whole wall was lined with paper. Names on them. And a stamp, over each one."

"…What kind of stamp?"

My brother looked at me. His face was strange.

"…COMPLETE," my brother said. "Every single one stamped COMPLETE. It didn't say where they'd gone. Just — complete."

*I heard that word and felt gooseflesh rise. That was the first time it occurred to me that what the Ledger called "resolved" and what that building called "COMPLETE" might, somehow, be the same word.*

I asked my brother how big the shelves were. He said they looked too large even to fill an entire wall. I tried to picture the number and gave up. It came too easily.

---

My brother had slipped one sheet off that shelf and brought it back. It was old, thin paper. A name was written on it. A name neither of us knew. And above it, a red stamp. **COMPLETE.**

"…Why did you take this?" I asked.

"…Evidence," my brother said. "Evidence I can show someone — anyone — of what this city does."

"…Show who?"

My brother had no answer.

*I knew, then, that my brother hadn't thought that far ahead. He hadn't wanted evidence. He'd needed to see, with his own eyes, that this city was hiding something.*

I looked at the paper again. It was old, but it had no fold marks. I found that strange. It looked too clean for something he'd pulled out in a hurry.

"…I'm scared, holding onto this," I said. "What if you end up in there too?"

"…I won't," my brother said. "I'm not like those people. I still have questions I haven't asked yet."

*Even hearing that, I couldn't rest easy. That night, after my brother fell asleep, I looked at that paper again, more than once. A stranger's name, and a red stamp. I touched the stamp with my fingertip. The ink had already dried completely.*

---

That night, the Administrator came to our room. His face was more set than usual.

"…Jeteo," the Administrator said. "There have been recent reports of you seen near a restricted area."

My brother's hand, holding the paper, slid behind his back. I saw it. The Administrator must have seen it too.

"…I don't know what you're looking at," the Administrator said. "I'm only passing this along. That the Ledger does not accept questions about that area."

"…Why doesn't it accept questions there?" my brother asked. "You said the Ledger accepts everything. Grief, questions, everything."

The Administrator's voice had dropped lower than before. I noticed him glancing toward the door again and again while he spoke.

For the first time, the Administrator avoided the question.

"…That isn't mine to answer."

"…Then who can answer it?" my brother asked.

The Administrator glanced once at the door. As if checking whether someone might be listening.

"…Jeteo." The Administrator lowered his voice. "I've worked in this city a long time. I had a lot of questions myself, at first. But at some point, I learned that not asking was more comfortable."

"…What does that mean?"

"…Nothing." The Administrator's voice returned to normal. "It's just advice I'm giving you. Don't go near that area again."

I watched the Administrator's back a long while as he left. His steps were slower than usual. I thought there was something like hesitation in that slowness.

Once the Administrator left, my brother stared at the door for a long time.

"…I think the Administrator knows something too," I said.

"…I think so too," my brother said. "But the fact that he won't say — I think that means there's a reason he can't."

"…Because he's afraid?"

"…Or because there's something he has to protect," my brother said. There was a bitterness in his voice I hadn't heard before.

I heard that and pictured the Administrator's face again. Always so courteous, so businesslike. But today, for the first time, I thought I'd seen something else behind that face.

*I remember that moment exactly. That was the first time, since we'd come to this city, that anyone other than the Ledger had said "that isn't mine to answer." I understood, hearing it, that my brother had been right. There were things in this city that no one filed. And that was no accident.*
