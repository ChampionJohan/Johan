# Chapter 43. What Mother Never Got to Say

> _Previously_ — After taking the hand, Brother answered honestly: "The anger's still there. But now I know I don't have to hold that anger alone." Not knowing why, Father had been gathering stones because he wanted to give them to someone.

A few nights later, Father suddenly bolted upright out of sleep.

"…Jeteo." Father said it with his eyes open.

Brother startled at the sound and sat up.

"…Me?" Brother asked. His voice shook.

*I held my breath in that moment. Father had called Brother's name for the first time. But Father's face was still the confused face of someone not fully out of sleep.*

"…It's all right, Father." I said it, and the word "Father" came out of me naturally, without my meaning it to. Father, too, paused for a moment at the word. But his wasn't a face that disliked it.

---

"…Your mother…" Father said, stumbling over it. "Not long before they took me away, one night, she said something like this to me. That there was something she wanted to say to you, but she still couldn't."

Brother froze where he sat.

"…Do you remember what it was?" Brother asked. His voice cracked all the way through.

Father closed his eyes and strained a long while. I watched him strain, and clenched my hands tight.

---

"…'You're a child who's always going to be asking,'" Father said finally. Slowly, one word at a time. "'And that's not a bad thing. I'm… proud of that.' That's what I think she was trying to say."

Brother sank down where he stood when he heard it.

*I record that moment exactly. It was the first time I'd seen Brother cry like that. Out loud, hard. For the first time since we'd left Brick Hollow.*

*Hearing those words, I thought of the night we crossed the boundary stones. The night Mother called Brother's name, opened her mouth, and closed it again. "…No. Never mind. Go and live. That's enough." I understood, then, for the first time, that what Mother had tried and failed to say that night had already been left, ahead of time, with Father.*

---

"…Was Mother never ashamed," Brother asked, "of how much I kept asking?" He asked it of Father, or maybe of nothing at all.

Father put a hand on Brother's shoulder. The gesture was still clumsy. But there was something certain in it.

"…She said she was proud," Father said again. "That much, I remember exactly."

*I set down that line as the last sentence of this chapter. That night, I watched Brother receive the answer to a question he'd been asking himself for years — was it wrong of me to keep asking? — the latest it could possibly come, from the person he'd least expect to bring it.*
