# Chapter 43. What Mother Never Got to Say

> _Previously_ — After taking the hand, Brother answered honestly:
> "The anger's still there. But now I know I don't have to hold that
> anger alone." Not knowing why, Father had been gathering stones,
> wanting to give them to someone.

A few days passed like that, peacefully. Father still gathered stones
by day, and Brother helped Speon look after the well. I set down the
evening record somewhere in between.

Father had been unusually quiet these past few days. But it wasn't an
uneasy quiet. I only understood later that it was a quiet of turning
something over. At the time I simply thought Father must be tired.

That night was unusually still. Even the wind seemed to be asleep.

A few nights later, Father suddenly bolted upright out of sleep.

"…Jeteo." Father said it with his eyes open.

Brother startled at the sound and sat up.

"…Me?" Brother asked. His voice shook.

*I held my breath in that moment. Father had called Brother's name for
the first time. But Father's face was still the confused face of
someone not fully out of sleep.*

I felt that the voice calling that name sounded as if it had come out
of a dream. Both unfamiliar and familiar, all at once.

"…It's all right, Father." I said it, and the word "Father" came out
of me naturally, without my meaning it to. Father, too, paused for a
moment at the word. But his wasn't a face that disliked it.

Father's hand was trembling. I took it carefully. It was cold. I
didn't let go until that cold began to fade.

Outside, the faint cry of night insects drifted in. That sound felt
unusually loud to me. Probably because all three of us were holding
our breath.

Brother stayed sitting where he was, still watching Father. I saw
fear and hope crossing his face at once.

"…What did you dream about?" Brother asked, carefully.

Father shook his head. And shook it again.

"…I don't think it was a dream," Father said. "It felt like… a
memory."

---

Only a faint warmth remained where the fire had gone out. In that
darkness, I could barely make out Father's eyes. He stared into the
empty air for a moment, like someone trying to hold on to something.

"…Your mother…" Father said, stumbling over it. "Not long before
they took me away, one night, she said something like this to me.
That there was something she wanted to say to you, but she still
couldn't."

Brother froze where he sat. I heard his breath stop, for a moment.

"…Do you remember what it was?" Brother asked. His voice cracked all
the way through.

The moment I heard that question, I understood that Brother had been
waiting for this moment for years. A question he might never get an
answer to — and Brother had never given up asking it.

Father closed his eyes and strained a long while. I watched him
strain, and clenched my hands tight.

Father's lips moved several times, then closed again. Watching that,
I understood there was nothing left to do but wait. And, strangely,
that waiting didn't frighten me.

"…Take your time," I said. "There's no hurry."

Father nodded at that, eyes still closed.

"…I'm sorry," Father said. "For stopping again and again."

"…It's all right," Brother said. His voice had steadied a little from
before. "I'm here. I'm not going anywhere."

Father opened his eyes at those words. He looked at Brother a long
time. I felt something growing clearer, little by little, inside that
gaze.

"…Not going anywhere," Father repeated the words, quietly, to
himself. "I feel like I've heard that a lot, somewhere."

I caught my breath at that moment. I knew those were the words we had
said to Father, over and over, without number. Somewhere in Father's
body, those words had already been carved.

Brother said nothing to that. He only gripped Father's hand tighter.
I thought that silence was larger than any words could be.

---

Speon woke at the sound too, and came out. He didn't come close to us
— he stood a little way off and watched. I knew that distance was a
kindness.

It was a night so dark even the stars were dim. In that darkness, I
heard Father's voice tremble. It was a tremble I'd never heard before.

"…'You're a child who's always going to be asking,'" Father said
finally. Slowly, one word at a time. "'And that's not a bad thing.
I'm… proud of that.' That's what I think she was trying to say."

Brother sank down where he stood when he heard it.

Brother's shoulders shook hard. I knelt beside him and set my hand on
his back. He didn't push my hand away.

Father knelt down beside him too. It was a clumsy movement. But Father
didn't hesitate. I saw, for the first time that day, the three of us
tangled together like that, into one.

*I record that moment exactly. It was the first time I'd seen Brother
cry like that. Out loud, hard. For the first time since we'd left
Brick Hollow.*

*Hearing those words, I thought of the night we crossed the boundary
stones. The night Mother called Brother's name, opened her mouth, and
closed it again. "…No. Never mind. Go and live. That's enough." I
understood, then, for the first time, that what Mother had tried and
failed to say that night had already been left, ahead of time, with
Father.*

*I don't remember Mother's face clearly, from that night. But her
voice alone has stayed with me, sharp and whole. Now I know: that
voice, and the words Father just brought back to us, came from the
same person.*

I closed my eyes and tried to call that voice back once more. It
wasn't whole. But the warmth of it, at least, was still there,
unmistakably.

I thought, that night, that the whole well was listening to Brother's
crying together with us. No one came out to make him stop. They just
listened.

I thought for a long while about how I would set that crying down in
this record. In the end I decided to write it plainly: Brother cried.
For a long time, and very loudly. And no one hurried him toward the
end of it.

---

Long after, Brother's crying settled. I saw Father sitting quietly
beside him in that time. Saying nothing, simply keeping watch beside
him.

Dawn seemed to be near; the far edge of the sky had begun to lighten
faintly. Watching that light, I only then realized how long the night
had been.

Brother wiped his tears and looked straight at Father. I felt he
wanted to ask something more.

"…Did Mother think it was… shameful, that I kept asking things?"
Brother asked. Of Father, or maybe of nothing at all.

Father put a hand on Brother's shoulder. The gesture was still
clumsy. But there was something certain in it.

Brother let that hand stay. He didn't push it away. I knew how large
a change that small permission was.

"…She said she was proud," Father said again. "That much, I remember
exactly."

Brother lowered his head. Tears fell again. This time, silently.

"…Thank you," Brother managed, "for remembering it."

Father gripped Brother's shoulder a little harder at those words.
There were no words. But I thought the strength of that hand alone
was enough.

The sky was growing fully light. I only then realized that this was
the first time the three of us had stayed up through an entire night
together like this.

*I set down that line as the last sentence of this chapter. That
night, I watched Brother receive the answer to a question he'd been
asking himself for years — was it wrong of me to keep asking? — the
latest it could possibly come, from the person he'd least expect to
bring it.*
