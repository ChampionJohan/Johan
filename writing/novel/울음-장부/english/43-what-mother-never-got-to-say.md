# Chapter 43. What Mother Never Got to Say

> _Previously_ — After taking the hand, Jeteo answered honestly: "The anger's still there. But now I know I don't have to carry that anger alone." Father, not knowing why, gathered stones because he wanted to give them to someone.

Several days passed peacefully like that. Father still gathered stones during the day, and Jeteo still helped Speon tend the well. I filled in my evening record between it all.

The days at the well always flowed in the same order. It began with the sound of Speon lowering the bucket at dawn; through the day, the footsteps of people coming to draw water continued; and as the sun sank, it grew quiet again. That repetition wasn't boring to me.

In Haven, a day had always ended in "processing." Here, a day simply drew to a close. I turned that difference over for days. A day that gets processed, and a day that simply closes. The same length, but what remained was different.

*I still remember those days as days when nothing special happened at all. But strangely, that nothing-happening has stayed with me the longest.*

Around then, I started writing down sounds instead of events in my evening record. The creak of the bucket, the clink of Father's spoon, the sound of Jeteo humming and trailing off. I didn't know, then, that these were the things I'd miss the most, later.

During those days, I carried the stone Jeteo had given me in my pocket all the time. I'd check its shape with my fingertips now and then. I kept touching it, for no reason, as if it were some kind of charm.

The stone always came to resemble the temperature of my body inside my pocket. I liked that. When I'd first received it, it had held Father's warmth; after a few days, it had become my own warmth. I thought that was how warmth traveled from one person to another.

Jeteo hadn't lost his stone either. I'd caught him more than once, stopping his well work to pat his pocket and check it was there. Jeteo didn't seem to notice that I'd seen it. I didn't say anything about it.

Father had been unusually quiet these past few days. But it wasn't an anxious quiet. I only realized later that it was a quiet of turning something over. At the time, I just thought Father must be tired.

During those days, Father often closed his eyes. While walking, while eating. I couldn't tell if that was sleeping or trying hard to remember something. Jeteo must have noticed it too — he moved more quietly than usual, so as not to disturb Father.

Once, Father stopped mid-meal with the spoon still in his hand. For quite a while. When Jeteo carefully touched his arm, Father startled and opened his eyes.

"…What were you looking at?" Jeteo asked.

"…I don't know," Father said. "Something keeps coming close and then going away. When I try to catch it, it disappears."

I copied that line into that night's record exactly as it was. I didn't know, then, what it meant. I just wrote it down because Father had said it.

*I still think that's what a record is. Not writing something down because you know its meaning, but writing it down and coming to know its meaning later.*

That night was unusually quiet. Even the wind was asleep.

Not even the insects were calling. I thought later that quiet had felt like the quiet right before something happens. But that's a thought I attached afterward. That night, it was simply quiet.

A few nights later, Father suddenly bolted upright out of sleep.

"…Jeteo," Father said. His eyes were open.

Jeteo woke with a start at the sound.

"…Me?" Jeteo asked. His voice trembled.

*I held my breath in that instant. Father had spoken Jeteo's name for the first time. But Father's face was still not fully awake, still confused.*

I felt that the voice calling that name sounded like something coming from inside a dream. A sound both unfamiliar and familiar.

"…It's all right, Father," I said. In that moment, the word "Father" came out naturally, without my meaning it to. Father paused for a moment at that too. But his face didn't look like he minded.

Father's hand was trembling. I carefully took hold of it. It was cold. I didn't let go until that coldness faded.

There was still a little dirt under Father's fingernails. Dirt from picking up stones during the day. Looking at that dirt, I thought that Father's whole day was written into that hand.

Holding his hand, I felt Father's pulse racing. Fast. I matched my own breathing to it, slow, hoping Father's would follow and slow down too. I don't know if it actually did.

The faint cry of night insects drifted in from outside. That sound felt unusually loud to me. Probably because all three of us were holding our breath.

Jeteo was still sitting there, watching Father. I saw fear and hope cross his face at the same time.

"…What did you dream about?" Jeteo asked carefully.

Father shook his head. And shook it again.

"…I don't think it was a dream," Father said. "It was… a memory, I think."

I heard that word from Father's mouth for the first time. Memory. Father had been avoiding that word all this while. Probably because it felt like naming something that wasn't there.

Jeteo leaned forward at that. I felt Jeteo wanting to rush to ask something, and at the same time, holding himself back. Jeteo knew a little now, how not to hurry.

Watching that restraint beside him, I felt again how far Jeteo had come. The old Jeteo from Brick Hollow would have grabbed Father and asked the same question over and over, that night. Now Jeteo simply waited.

---

Only a faint warmth remained where the fire had burned all the way down. I could just barely see Father's eyes in that dark. Father stared into the empty air for a moment, like someone trying to grasp something.

"…Your mother…" Father said, stumbling. "Not long before they took me away, she said this to me at night. That there was something she wanted to say to you, but she still couldn't."

Father said "your mother." I stumbled on those three words. Father had never called our mother that before. He hadn't been able to. But that night, the words simply came out, with no preparation at all.

I knew that Jeteo had noticed it too. His shoulder tensed slightly, then loosened again.

*My hand shook as I wrote those three words into the record. From that night on, I came to know that for some words, where they're spoken matters more than what they mean.*

Jeteo froze in place. I heard his breath stop for a moment.

"…Do you remember what she said?" Jeteo asked. His voice had cracked completely.

Hearing that question, I knew, in that instant, that Jeteo had been waiting for this moment for years. A question that might never get an answer — Jeteo had never given up on it.

*I had a strange thought, right then. In Haven, this question would have had an answer within seconds of being filed. Under some title like "the most probable reconstruction of what the mother meant to say." And that answer probably would have been flawless.*

*But what Jeteo had been waiting for wasn't a plausible answer. Jeteo was waiting for Mother's actual words. The two looked alike, but they were entirely different things.*

Father closed his eyes and strained for a long time. I watched that effort, gripping my hands tightly.

Father's lips moved a few times, then closed again. Watching that, I knew there was nothing to do but wait. Strangely, that waiting wasn't frightening.

"…Take your time," I said. "No rush."

Father nodded at that, eyes still closed.

"…I'm sorry," Father said. "It keeps breaking off."

"…It's all right," Jeteo said. His voice was a little steadier than before. "I'm here. I'm not going anywhere."

Father opened his eyes at that. He looked at Jeteo for a long time. I felt something growing clearer, bit by bit, in that gaze.

"…Not going anywhere," Father repeated the words back, quietly. In a small voice. "I feel like I've heard that a lot."

I swallowed at that moment. I knew — that was the very phrase we had said to Father, countless times. That phrase had been carved somewhere in Father's body.

Jeteo said nothing to that. He just gripped Father's hand tighter. I thought that silence was bigger than any words.

---

Speon woke to the sound and came out too. He didn't come close to us — he stood a little apart and watched. I knew that distance was his consideration.

Speon held a lantern in his hand, but he didn't shine it toward us. He held it low, just enough to light his own feet. I knew that angle had been practiced for a long time. A well-keeper was someone who knew how to light the path without lighting the person.

*I asked Speon about it later — why he hadn't come closer, then. Speon said this: "…That wasn't my place to be in. But it was also a place that couldn't be left with no one in it." I found it strange, for a long time, that both of those sentences could be true at once.*

Even the starlight was dim that night. In that dark, I heard Father's voice tremble. A trembling I'd never heard before.

"…'You'll always be a child who asks.'" Father finally said. Slowly, one word at a time. "'That's not a bad thing. I'm… proud of it.' I think that's what she was trying to say."

Jeteo sank to the ground on hearing that.

Jeteo's shoulders shook hard. I knelt beside him and laid a hand on his back. He didn't push my hand away.

Jeteo's back kept shaking beneath my palm. I didn't try to stop that shaking. I just kept my hand there and shook along with it. I knew that was all I could do, and I knew, too, that it wasn't too little.

Father knelt down beside him too. It was an awkward movement. But Father didn't hesitate. I saw the three of us tangled together like that, in one mass, for the first time that day.

*I set down this moment exactly as it was. I saw Jeteo cry like that for the first time. Aloud, and hard. For the first time since we'd left Brick Hollow.*

*Hearing those words, I suddenly remembered that night we crossed the boundary stones. The night Mother called Jeteo's name, opened her mouth, and closed it again. "…No. Go and live. That's enough." I understood, then, for the first time, that what Mother had tried and failed to say that night had already been left with Father, in advance.*

*I don't remember Mother's face exactly, from that night. But her voice alone has stayed with me clearly. I know now that that voice, and the words Father had just given us, came from the same person.*

I closed my eyes and tried to bring that voice back. It wasn't complete. But its warmth, at least, was still there, clearly.

I thought, that night, that the whole well-yard seemed to be listening to Jeteo's crying together with us. No one came out to stop him. They were simply listening.

I thought for a long time about how to put that crying into this record. In the end, I decided to write it just like this. Jeteo cried. Very long, very loud. And no one tried to hurry that crying to an end.

---

A long while later, Jeteo's crying began to fade. In that time, I saw Father sitting quietly beside him. Saying nothing, just keeping watch.

I've hardly ever seen anyone cry in Haven. Crying got filed, and stopped within a few minutes. Even the longest crying that city had ever seen probably never passed ten minutes.

That night, Jeteo cried far longer than that. No one filed it, and no one processed it. We simply all stayed there together until that crying ended.

*After that night, I stopped seeing things that take a long time as bad. Some things are real because they take so long.*

I thought again of Haven's intake window. There, a thing taking a long time was itself the mistake. If processing took too long, an apology came first. I didn't want to turn this night into something that owed an apology. It wasn't that I was sorry it took so long — I was grateful it had taken that long.

The edge of the sky was faintly brightening, as if dawn were near. Watching that light, I only then understood how long the night had been.

Jeteo wiped his tears and looked straight at Father. I felt he wanted to ask something more.

"…Wasn't Mother ashamed, that I kept asking so many questions?" Jeteo asked. To Father, or maybe just to the empty air.

Father laid a hand on Jeteo's shoulder. Still an awkward gesture. But there was something certain in that gesture.

Jeteo let that hand stay. He didn't push it off. I knew how great a change that small permission was.

"…She said she was proud," Father said again. "That much, I remember exactly."

Jeteo bowed his head. Tears fell again. This time, silent tears.

"…Thank you, for remembering," Jeteo managed to say.

Father gripped Jeteo's shoulder a little harder at that. No words. But I thought the strength of that hand alone was enough.

"…I didn't remember it," Father said, after a while. "It just… came back. I didn't do anything."

"…Even so," Jeteo said. "It stayed with you. That's enough."

I noticed, again, in that moment, that Jeteo still called Father "sir." But that word of address no longer sounded like distance to me now. It only meant that Jeteo still wasn't rushing.

Far off, the first bird cried. Once one called, others followed. I found it strangely odd, that day, that a bird's crying and a person's crying were called by the same word.

The sky was fully brightening. I only then realized this was the first time the three of us had stayed up through a whole night together.

Watching that night give way to dawn, I strangely didn't feel any regret. There are nights that are hard because they're long, and nights you regret because they're short. I still don't know, even now, which kind that night was. I only know that, by the end of it, the three of us had become a little more one.

*I set down those words as the last line of this chapter. I saw Jeteo receive an answer that night — to a question he had asked himself for years, "Is it wrong that I keep asking?" — from the latest, and the most unexpected, person of all.*
