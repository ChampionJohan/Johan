# Translation Guide — *Loop Chronicles* (루프 연대기)

Translate Korean Christian YA fantasy chapters into literary English. This
guide is the single source of truth for names, terms, Scripture wording, and
formatting. Follow it exactly so all 48 chapters read as one consistent book
translated by one hand.

## 1. Title and series

- Novel title: **Loop Chronicles**
- Part titles (these are the exact strings found in each chapter's
  `series:` front-matter line — match them verbatim by Korean text, not by
  chapter number, in case of any mismatch):
  - 루프 연대기 1부 · 빨라진 발 → **Loop Chronicles, Part One · The Faster Feet**
  - 루프 연대기 2부 · 많아진 지식 → **Loop Chronicles, Part Two · Knowledge Increased**
  - 루프 연대기 3부 · 아무도 부르지 않은 이름 → **Loop Chronicles, Part Three · The Name No One Called**
  - 루프 연대기 4부 · 다 이루었다 → **Loop Chronicles, Part Four · It Is Finished**

## 2. Character names — do not alter

These names are already Greek-root transliterations chosen by the author to
sound natural in English. Keep them exactly as given; do not further
anglicize or translate them.

the honorific itself is the point (ch.47); otherwise just "the well-keeper."

## 3. Register and voice

- Narrator: **Mena**, first person past tense, writing a retrospective
  record. Prose is spare, short paragraphs, frequent one-line paragraphs for
  emphasis.
- *Italicized* passages are Mena's present-tense reflections written into the
  record — keep them italicized in English.
- Blockquoted `>` passages are (a) the chapter's Scripture epigraph and (b)
  the `*이전 이야기*` ("Previously") recap. Translate `*이전 이야기*` as
  **_Previously_** (italicized, as its own line inside the blockquote,
  exactly as the Korean does).
- Dialogue lines beginning with "…" (ellipsis, marking a hesitant or quiet
  opening) — preserve that ellipsis at the start of the quoted line in
  English, e.g. `"…아저씨."` → `"…Sir."` / `"…Mena?"` etc. Keep it as a
  stylistic tic throughout, don't normalize it away.
- Scene breaks marked `---` in the Korean must remain `---` in the English.
- Keep sentences short. Do not smooth Mena's clipped, documentary style into
  flowing literary prose — the choppiness is intentional characterization.
- Numbers written out in Korean (사백일흔두 명, 삼백 년, 서른 자, etc.)
  should be translated as English words or numerals consistently within a
  sentence's register — e.g. "삼백 년" → "three hundred years", "사백일흔두
  명" → "four hundred seventy-two people", "서른 자" → "thirty cheok" is
  wrong (there's no such unit in English) — convert 자 (a traditional
  Korean unit, ≈ 30.3 cm) to **"thirty feet"** throughout for readability;
  keep the number consistent across every chapter that reuses it. Likewise
  convert **근** (a traditional Korean weight unit, ≈ 600g) to **pounds**
  — never transliterate it as "geun" (KDP's spellcheck flags it and it
  means nothing to an English reader). "백사십 근" → **"one hundred
  eighty-five pounds"** everywhere it recurs (chapters 2, 5, 6).

## 4. Scripture — use the King James Version (KJV) ONLY

publication — never substitute NIV, ESV, NASB, or any other copyrighted

in-text Scripture reference used across the 48 chapters. Copy these verbatim
for the blockquoted epigraph; you may quote a shorter clause of the same
verse inline in dialogue/narration if the Korean also only quotes a clause
(match the Korean's boundary — if the Korean citation stops mid-verse, quote
KJV only that far).

## 6. File format — match exactly

Each translated chapter file must keep this structure:

```
---
title: Chapter N. <English Chapter Title>
series: Loop Chronicles, Part <One/Two/Three/Four> · <Part English Title as mapped in section 1>
---

# Chapter N. <English Chapter Title>

> <KJV epigraph text, exactly as given above>
> — <Book Chapter:Verse (KJV)>

> _Previously_ — <translated recap>

<translated body, preserving every `---` scene break and every *italic*
reflection>

*(Continued in Chapter N+1.)*
```

For chapter 48 (the final chapter), end with `*(End of Part Four.)*` instead
of a "Continued in" line, matching the Korean's `*(4부 끝)*`.
