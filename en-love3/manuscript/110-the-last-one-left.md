## The Number Goes Down

When a person is very ill or very old, the number of people around them goes
down. There is almost no exception to this.

At first many come. Word gets out and the phone rings and people arrive. After a
few months it thins. After a few years it is very few. It is not that they are
bad people. People have their own lives, and a long illness is not something the
circle can keep pace with.

And at the end there is usually one person left. Two at most.

## Who Stays Is Not Predictable

Look at who stays and it is not what you would expect.

You would think it would be whoever was closest. It is not. It is not the one who
talked most either. Quite often it is somebody who was not especially noticeable.
From around them comes the sentence that they never thought that person would go
that far.

Why that person they cannot explain either. They say it just ended up being them.
They say they did it because nobody else was. **The one who stays is not the one
who chose to. It is the one who did not leave.**

## What Happens to the One Who Stays

Look at what happens on the side of the one who stayed.

First the outside world shrinks. They cannot make plans, they cannot go far,
and then their friends thin out, and a few years into nursing someone, their
own circle has come down to one person as well. Care narrows two people at
once.

And nobody is watching. When nobody comes to the room, there is no one to see
what happens there, and the second book had a chapter on giving without a name
attached, and here nothing is hidden and still nobody sees it.

## Resentment Is Mixed In

If this is left out the chapter is false. The one who stays resents people.

They resent the ones who do not come, they resent the sibling who left it all to
them, and sometimes they resent the person lying there. And then they resent
themselves for resenting. The double layer from the fifth chapter is thickest
here.

Leave the resentment out and the chapter becomes a saintly story. Written as a
saintly story, a person who is in that position right now reads it and
concludes they are inadequate, and that is the thing this book must least do,
so it is recorded. **Staying while resenting is what staying is.**

## The Ones Who Did Not Stay

And the ones who left have to be spoken about. This is where the chapter is
stuck.

The ones who did not come have reasons. They live far away, they are ill
themselves, their children are small, there is no money. Every one of those is
true. And from the side of the one who stayed, the reasons are invisible. Only
the not coming is visible.

Both are correct. The reasons are a fact and the not coming is a fact. And the
two do not reconcile. Many siblings grow apart over it for the rest of their
lives, and both of them are right, and I cannot take a side and have no
standing to. The chapter stops here.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone stayed to the end of somebody's last few years. Think about how old they
were at the time, and what they did with their own work during those years, and
the numbers get heavier.

## Where This Chapter Came From

The order in which people around a sick person thin out is observation and
hearsay. I did not count it; I was told it, and it is written as such.

There is material on what happens to families during a long illness. I did not
use it. Put numbers in and a person currently in that position starts looking for
their own household in the table, and having found it, it starts to feel like
that is what will happen to them. I decided not to write that kind of thing.

The blank in the middle is for the author to fill.
