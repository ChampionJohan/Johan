## With Nowhere to Repay, It Goes Sideways

The last pages of the first book said what we receive goes sideways. This chapter
looks at the going.

Try to repay and the road is usually blocked. The giver is gone, or far away, or
will not take it, or has already died. Hold something unrepayable in your hands
and a person sends it somewhere. The direction is always the same. **Never
upward. Sideways or down.**

## It Goes in the Shape It Arrived

This is the clearest thing in the chapter. What was received goes to the next
person in the shape it came in.

Someone carried on a back carries someone. Someone who had a person to feed them
feeds people. Someone who had a visitor when they were ill goes when someone is
ill. They do it the way it was done to them. Other ways do not work well. They
learned one.

So watch the shape of a person's giving and you can see the shape of what they
received. Not what they give. How. The one who gives by food, the one who gives by money, the one who gives by talking, the one who gives by sitting quietly are all different from one another. What each of them received was
different.

## The Bad Parts Travel the Same Way

Writing only one side would be false. If it goes in the shape it came in, **the
bad parts go in that shape too.**

Someone who was hit comes to hit. Someone raised on comparison comes to compare.
One day you catch yourself saying with your own mouth the sentence you hated
most. That is the frightening part of this structure.

Flowing allows no selection. You cannot keep only the good. So to avoid
sending on what you received you have to put force in the middle. Some people
do. They have decided which of the things they received they will not send on,
and they live that way, and it does not happen by itself and it takes a
lifetime.

## Cutting Something Off Is Also Passing Something On

One thing to set down. Cutting off a bad thing is also an act that reaches the
next person.

Someone was hit and did not hit. That person's child grows up unhit. That child
does not know what they did not receive. **What was not received is invisible.**
So the act is known to nobody, and the person gets no credit for it as long as
they live.

This looks like the most invisible giving in the book. Not giving by doing something, but **giving by not doing it.** An act of omission cannot be
recorded.

## And People Who Received Little

The place where the second book jammed comes up again here.

If what we received is what travels, someone who received little should have
little to send. And there are people who did not. People who received almost
nothing and give a great deal. Flowing does not explain them.

The second book built two explanations and discarded both. I tried to build
one more here and it fails again. The ninth chapter looks at those people from
a slightly different angle, and no explanation there either, only the record
that there are people giving without knowing where theirs came from.

## Someone Comes to Mind

There is one way you are good to people. Think about who did that to you. If it
comes to you, that is the person.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside. Observation and things I was
told.

There is a great deal of research on bad things being handed down across
generations. I confirmed from public sources that the field exists and put no
figures in the text. Put a figure in and it reads as *people from houses like that
turn out this way.* The people who did not are the back half of this chapter. I
did not want to make them the exception, so the numbers came out.
