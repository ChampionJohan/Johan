## They Nearly All Say the Same Sentence

Ask people who have held on to someone for a long time and a similar sentence
comes out. They say they never gave up.

Watch how the sentence arrives and something is odd in it. It does not mean they
never thought about giving up. Mostly they thought about it repeatedly. Ask them
to count the days they wanted to stop and they can count them. And they still say
they never gave up.

So the sentence is not about a state of mind. **It is about the fact that they
never once stopped.**

## Wanting to Stop and Stopping

These are two different things. Mix them up and you start cutting yourself down
unfairly.

Somebody who goes every day thinks, on the way, that they do not want to go
today. They think it and they go. That person went. That they did not want to is
not in the record and only the going is. On the receiving side, that is a person
who came gladly every day.

And on the doing side, all those days of not wanting to are remembered. So they
conclude their love was insufficient. That is the saddest thing in this chapter.
**The day you went without wanting to is heavier than the day you wanted to go.**

## Giving Up Does Not Arrive in a Day

Watch the shape of giving up and it does not come at once. It thins out and one
day it is gone.

Weekly becomes monthly, monthly becomes holidays only, and then they stop going.
Almost nobody decided on a day to stop. They drift without a decision. Which is
why, asked when they stopped, they cannot answer.

Not ending has the same shape. Almost nobody decided to keep going either. They
simply go again next week. **Neither the ending side nor the continuing side is
made of a decision. Both are made of one next week.**

## So What Makes the Next Week

A thing worth knowing appears here. What is the difference between the side that
goes again next week and the side that does not.

It is hard to call it the quantity of love. The ones who stopped going did not
love less. They ran out of strength, and I am in no position to reproach them.

Look at circumstances and a little of the difference shows. People carrying it
alone hit bottom fast. People sharing it last. Near is longer and far is shorter.
Money makes it longer and no money makes it shorter. **Lasting has more to do
with circumstance than with love.** That is a deflating conclusion and it looks
true.

## The Cases That Go Past Circumstance

And there are people whose circumstances are as bad as they get who go all the
way. Alone, far, no money, and they did it for decades.

Look at them and the explanation above does not hold. So it has to be corrected,
and corrected, the other side stops holding. Explain by circumstance and those
people are unexplained. Explain by love and the people who ran out of strength
become deficient.

Neither works, so both are written down and not made into one. Make them one and
somebody gets cut down. The chapter stops here.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone never gave up on you. They too will have had days when they wanted to
stop. You do not know those days, and without your knowing them, they came.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside. Observation and things I was
told, and the passage about circumstances is my own inference, marked as such.

There is probably research on the conditions under which long-term care
continues. Finding it would have made this chapter sturdier and I did not
look. Find it and the chapter becomes four conditions for lasting, and then
anyone who is not on that list starts doubting their own part, and that was
the thing I most wanted to avoid here.
