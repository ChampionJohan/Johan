## What Gets Recorded as an Event

Stories about saving people mostly get recorded as events. Pulled a child out of
the water. Drove through the night to get there.

Those happen once, and happening once, they make a story. A beginning, an end,
tension in the middle. People like those stories.

And what actually keeps a person alive is usually not that. It is the side that
did the same thing several thousand times. This side does not make a story. No
beginning, no end, no tension, and what was done yesterday is identical to what
was done today.

## Why Repetition Is Invisible

There is a reason repetition goes unseen. **What stays the same does not catch
the eye.**

The human eye watches what changes. What does not change becomes as good as
absent. So the person who cooked every day is invisible and the one day they
did not is visible, and the person who handed over the pills every day is
invisible and the one day they forgot is visible.

Because of this, the repeating side never once gets noticed for doing well and
gets noticed only for doing badly, and do it several thousand times, miss
once, and the once becomes their record. It is an unjust accounting and it
cannot be changed, because that is how eyes are built.

## Inside the Person Doing It

Leaving aside what is invisible from outside, I looked at what happens inside the
one doing it.

At first they do it on will. Then they do it on habit. Then comes the strange
part, where at some point the act stops being their routine and becomes them. Not
doing it feels like not being themselves. People who have reached that stage have
a harder time when they stop.

That seems to be why the carer sometimes collapses after the cared-for person has
gone. It is not that they have nothing to do. It is that they have gone. The
seventh chapter looks at that place.

## How Repetition Accumulates

The tenth chapter of the second book said a small thing repeated tens of
thousands of times stacks up. One thing can be added here. **The stacking is
invisible from the receiving side too.**

A child does not know what they were given daily. They do not know when they are
grown. They find out on the day they end up raising a child and doing the same
things. So the value of this work is made known one generation late, and by the
time it is known the person who could have been told is old, or gone.

## The Word Tedious

Here is where this chapter is stuck.

Writing only well of repetition does a disservice to the people doing it. The
work is tedious. Saying so sounds like a shortage of love, so they do not say it,
and the tedium and the love are both there at once.

Then what to call the state of being bored and continuing anyway I do not
know. Call it endurance and it goes cold. Call it love and the tedium drops
out, and there does not seem to be a word in the language with both in it, and
I am not going to invent one.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone did the same thing for you several thousand times. Think about what it
was and it will probably be something extremely trivial. Trivial is correct.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside. Observation and things I was
told.

What happens to a carer after the person they cared for is gone has been
studied. I did not use it. Bring that material in and the chapter becomes an
explanation of a named syndrome, and then those people become case studies,
and that the material exists is worth recording.
