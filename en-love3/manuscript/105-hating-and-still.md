## Both Are There Together

We are taught that love and hatred are opposites. Live a while and the two are
inside the same person together. Together on the same day.

The person you hate most is often the person closest to you. There is no occasion
to hate a stranger. Hatred requires nearness. So a place with a lot of hatred in
it is also a place that had a lot of love.

That is what this chapter is looking at. **Hatred is not love gone cold. It only
grows where there is love.**

## They Cannot Bear Hating

Come to hate someone close and a person hurts twice. They hurt from the hatred,
and they hurt again from being someone who hates.

That is why people who hate a parent find it so hard to say. Say it and you are a
bad child; do not say it and you carry it alone. So sometimes a person suddenly
starts being dutiful in ways they never were. They are trying to cover the
hatred. Covered, it does not go.

And when that person falls ill they go running. They go running while hating.
How two such things live inside one person they cannot explain either.

## Not Cutting It Off Despite the Hatred

One shape of the thing this book calls not ending sits right here.

There are reasons to cut it off and they do not cut it off, and there is
hatred, there is injury, they know it would be easier not to see them and they
know nobody would blame them, and they do not cut it off, and they go again at
the holiday. They go and they fight again. And they go again.

You could call that foolishness. If it were foolishness they would stop after one
try, and they do it for decades. Foolishness does not last decades. Something
else is holding it up.

## Some Things Must Be Cut Off

Something has to be written here without fail. **Some relationships must be cut
off.**

Where there is violence, where someone keeps being injured, where the other party
has no intention of changing. Call not cutting off in a place like that love and
the sentence kills people. This chapter is not about places like that.

There is a clear line between not cutting off as love and not cutting off as
self-harm. Only, that line cannot be drawn from outside. Only the person standing
there can know, and sometimes they only know much later. I cannot draw it.

## Writing the Hatred Down

One useful thing to set down. Admitting there is hatred looks better than trying
to remove it.

Try to remove it and the hatred hides. Hidden, it comes out somewhere else. You
get angry about unrelated things and it lands on the wrong people. Admit it and
it stays where it is. Hatred that stays in place can be looked at, and looked at,
what is underneath it becomes visible too.

Underneath it there is usually something that was hoped for. It is hateful
because what was hoped for did not come. With nothing hoped for there is no
hatred either.

## Is Hatred Evidence of Love Then

Here is the stuck place. Go that far and it goes too far.

That there was love where there is hatred looks right, and turn it around and
say the measure of the hatred is the measure of the love and it is wrong.
Written that way it makes hatred into a good thing and ends up defending
people who injure others, and the two sentences look joined and joining them
is dangerous, so I am leaving them apart.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Among the people you hate, is there one you have not cut off. I will not ask why
not. It would be good if you knew the single fact that you did not.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside. Observation and things I was
told.

There is a term in psychology for holding two feelings about someone close at
once, and I confirmed from public sources that the term exists and did not use
it. Attach a term and the state becomes a symptom, and this chapter is not
looking at symptoms.

There is material on relationships that must be cut off, some of it good. Outside
the scope of this book, so it is not used. Anyone who needs that would do better
to look there than to read this.
