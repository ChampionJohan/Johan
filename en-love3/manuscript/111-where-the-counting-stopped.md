## While You Are Counting There Is an End

One thing kept catching while I wrote these three books. People who count and
people who do not are different.

Count and an end arrives eventually. Counting has an end built into it, and
while you are keeping track of how much you have done, one day that number
passes what can be borne, and passing it, you stop. That is normal and that is
what people are.

For people who do not count, that day does not come. There is no number to pass,
so nothing is passed. That was what the first chapter could not solve. Why does
it continue. **They are not counting, so there is no point at which to end.**

## And Nobody Decided to Stop Counting

Here is the odd part. Ask people whose counting has stopped when they decided to
stop and no answer comes.

I have not met anyone who decided. They all say that one day they noticed they
were not counting. So stopping the counting does not appear to be something
you do, and if it could be done by trying there would be a method, and nobody
knows one.

This is the limit of this book. That people who stop counting last longer is
visible, and the way to stop is not, and if it had been visible I would have
written it, and writing it would have made this a book that teaches a method.
That it did not become that book is not because I am good. **It is because I
could not find the method.**

## What Happened Before the Stopping

The people whose counting stopped do have one thing in common. Before it stopped,
they saw something.

Many of them had seen what they themselves received. That is the content of
the first book, and one day they come to see the things that were already
there without ever having been asked for, and after seeing it, they say, the
counting went blurry. They cannot explain why and the order is that way. First
they see what they received, then the counting thins.

This is my inference, and it seems that once you start counting what you
received, that column comes out far larger. Then counting your own giving becomes
trivial. It is not that they gave up the arithmetic. The arithmetic stopped
balancing and they let go.

## There Is Still a Place That Does Not Fit

The place that jammed twice in the second book and again in the eighth chapter
jams here too.

There are people who received almost nothing and give a great deal. The ninth
chapter said there was often one person beside them. And some of them had no one.
I have seen people like that giving enormously.

Then the explanation above does not hold. *They saw what they received and the
counting blurred* is an explanation that requires something to have been
received. What those people have did not come from anywhere I can find.

## Leaving the Place Empty

This place came up four times across the three books. Four times I built an
explanation and four times I discarded it.

The reason for discarding was the same each time. If the explanation I built is
right, those people get explained and other people stop being explained. A
sentence built to explain people that cuts somebody down is a sentence that must
not be used. So all of them went, and I am not building another.

**One explanation is missing here.** That is the most accurate sentence this book
can produce. Leaving a gap as a gap is better than pretending to fill it. The next
chapter looks into that gap once and then the book closes.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Among the people who have done something for you there is one who is not counting
it. Ask them and they will say they do not know. That *I do not know* is the most
important answer in all three books.

## Where This Chapter Came From

This chapter is observation and inference throughout. There is not a single
number in it, and the things I was told were not counted either. Writing how many
people I met would make it look like a sample, and it is not a sample, so the
number is not here.

There may be books on how to stop counting. I did not look. Find one and bring it
in and this becomes a chapter introducing a method, and then what the chapter says
about not having found one becomes a lie.
