## In Front of a Face That Does Not Recognize You

You go into the room and the person lying there asks who you are. Somebody who
comes every day, met for the first time every day.

The person sitting there takes their hand that day too, tells them what
happened that day, combs their hair and goes out, and the next day they hear
*who are you* again. There are people who do this for years.

This book starts here, because this is the place that explains least of all,
and from the giving side it goes one step further than every chapter of the
second book. Not hearing a thank you and **not having who you are get
through** are different things.

## A Place With No Receiver

Giving usually has a receiving side. That person knows they received, and
knowing, the act is recorded somewhere.

In front of someone who cannot recognize you, none of that is there. What you
did today does not accumulate inside them. Yesterday's did not accumulate and
tomorrow's will not, and what it is like to do something daily that does not
accumulate I do not know, not having done it. Ask people who have and the
answers are short.

## So Who Is It For

An uncomfortable question comes up here. Who is that act for.

If the receiver does not know, you could say it is for the one doing it. That it
is done so as not to regret it later. That feeling is probably mixed in there.
Denying it would be false.

That explanation has a place where it does not hold, though. If it were about
avoiding regret, the doer would do it better when somebody was watching. They
would do less at an hour when nobody was. And that is not how it goes. More of
it is done at hours when nobody is watching, and the work of those hours is
recorded nowhere, and what is left is an act the receiver does not know about
and nobody observes.

## Is Nothing Left Inside Them

One thing has to be written carefully.

It does not seem to be true that people who cannot recognize you feel nothing.
There are people who do not know a name and grow calmer when that person comes,
people who cannot remember a face and grip back when a hand is taken. Which
means memory and feeling do not go together.

Only, this must not be used as comfort. *They still feel all of it* is sometimes
harder to hear than anything else, because nobody can confirm it. What cannot be
confirmed should not be used as comfort.

## What This Chapter Cannot Solve

Something continues even though the receiver does not know. That is a fact. Why
it continues this chapter did not find.

Ask the people doing it and mostly they say they just go. It is not that they are
withholding an explanation; they seem not to have one. An act with no explanation
continuing for years is the first riddle of this book. The eleventh chapter looks
at it once more.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Is there someone you still remember who does not remember you. If there is, they
came to mind just now. If there is not, you might think instead about who would
remember you that way.

## Where This Chapter Came From

That people losing their memory can feel a person without knowing the name is
something I was told by people who have nursed them. It is hearsay and not
something I verified. It is written as such.

I know there is research in this direction. I saw in public sources that
emotional memory persists separately, and I could not read the material properly,
so it is not in the text. Summarizing what you have not read produces errors.
That was the thing I most wanted in this chapter, and it is not here.

The blank in the middle is for the author to fill.
