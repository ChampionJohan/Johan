## Gone and Not Gone

When a person goes you would expect what they did to go with them, and it does
not. Several things stay.

The strangest of them is **speech**. One day you notice that what came out of
your own mouth is theirs. You were not a person who said that, and you are saying
it. There is no memory of learning it and it is in there.

Habits stay too. The order you cook things in, the way you close a door, what you do first when somebody says they are ill. What that person did for decades you are doing now.

## Belongings Do Not Stay for Their Use

Objects stay as well, and objects are a little different.

The valuable things among someone's belongings mostly get dealt with. What stays
is the things with no value. The glasses they wore, a bowl worn where their hand
went, one sheet of paper with their handwriting. Those cannot be thrown away.
Being useless and unthrowable is what a keepsake is.

Thinking about why, it seems to be because that person's body touched the object.
The place their hand went is still there. The worn patch on that bowl was made by
their hand, and it cannot be made again.

## What Continues on the Remaining Side

This book is about what does not end. Here is the clearest instance of it. One
side is gone and something continues.

The nature of it is different, though. In the earlier chapters the giving side
kept going. Here the giving side is absent and the received thing keeps going.
**It is not that they stopped. It is that they are not here and it did not
stop.**

Which is why people do things for someone who is gone. Visiting a grave.
Buying what that person liked. Doing something for a receiver who is not there. The second book spent twelve chapters on going where nothing comes back, and here there is not even anyone to receive.

## Time Heals Is Half True

One thing I will state plainly. *Time heals* is half true.

The sharp parts round off. They do not disappear. Decades later a smell or a song
brings it back whole. That is not a malfunction; it is because the person was
there. What passes leaving no mark was never deep in the first place.

So saying it will be all right to someone for whom it is not yet is something
to be careful with, and the sentence is heard as *hurry up and be all right.*
I have decided not to use it.

## Where Did They Go

What this chapter cannot solve is here.

What remains plainly remains. There is a turn of phrase, a habit, a bowl. And
that is the trace they left, and it is not them. Where did they go. That is not a
question this book can answer.

I stop here. Pretend to answer what cannot be answered and every other chapter
in this book becomes false along with it, and what can be recorded is that the
question kept rising through all three books. It comes up once more in the
twelfth chapter.

## Someone Comes to Mind

There is a phrase you use that is not yours. If whose it is comes to you, that
person is still speaking inside you.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside. Observation and things I was
told.

There is a great deal of material on how grief moves, some of it good. I did not
use it. Set out the stages and a reader starts counting which stage they are in,
and then they are grading their own grief. Better left out, I thought.

Whether there is research on being unable to throw away a keepsake I did not look
for.

The blank in the middle is for the author to fill.
