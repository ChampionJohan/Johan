# What this is

Four Korean books become four English books for Amazon KDP.

**Before a single sentence gets translated, three things have to be decided,
because every page depends on them:** what the framework is called in English,
what the books are called, and what has to change for a reader who is not Korean.

This document settles all three, then gives the KDP upload pack.

# 2. The framework in English

This is the most important decision in the whole project. The five boxes are the
brand. Get the words wrong and every chapter, every exercise, and the series
name all inherit the mistake.

| Korean | English | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **다섯 칸** | **The Five Boxes** | 칸 is a compartment on a grid. *Box* keeps the spatial metaphor and — critically — keeps the instruction working: 빈칸을 채운다 → **"fill in the empty box."** *Cells* sounds biological, *squares* is vague. |
| 고객 | **Customer** | Who actually pays? |
| 가치 | **Value** | What are they paying for? |
| 과금 | **Payment** | How and when do you get paid? (*Capture* is the textbook term but too abstract for this book's plain voice.) |
| 해자 | **Moat** | Already standard English business usage. Free win. |
| 균열 | **Fault Line** | 균열 is where a structure gives way. *Crack* is one word but small; *fault line* carries the sense of a break that was always there and finally moved. Two words is worth it. |

**Series name: The Five Boxes Series.**

## Terms that recur

| Korean | English |
|---|---|
| 직접 해 보기 | **Try It Yourself** |
| 오늘 할 것 (teen) | **Do This Today** |
| 흔한 오해 | **The Common Misreading** |

| 살아남은 쪽은 무엇이 달랐나? | **What was different about the ones that lasted?** |

# 3. Titles

| | English title |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Anatomy of Money**<br>*How to Take Any Business Apart in Five Boxes* |
| 2 | **Anatomy of Money II**<br>*Moats, Fault Lines, and How to Build One That Lasts* |
| 3 | **Anatomy of Money for Teens**<br>*How Businesses Really Make Money — and How You Can Start* |
| 4 | **Anatomy of Money for Teens II**<br>*I Started — So Why Isn't It Working?* |

## Why these

**"Anatomy of Money" survives translation intact.** It is concrete, it promises
dissection rather than advice, and it is not already taken by a major title.

**The subtitles do the search work.** On Amazon the subtitle is indexed. Book 1's
subtitle carries the method; Book 2's carries *moats*, *fault lines*, and *build*,
which is what people actually type.

**The teen books drop the Korean wordplay on purpose.** 「너 그거 아니?」 is a
conspiratorial "hey, you know what?" — charming in Korean, limp in English
("Hey, Did You Know?" reads like a children's trivia book). Instead the teen
editions inherit the parent brand, which does three things at once:

- parents searching **"business books for teens"** find it
- the link to the adult series is visible on the cover
- Book 4's subtitle keeps the original hook — *I started, so why isn't it working?*

**One caution.** *Anatomy of Money* is a strong title but not a unique one; there
are unrelated books using similar phrasing. Before you commit, search Amazon for
the exact string and check the top results. If it's crowded, the fallback that
keeps the same promise is **Take It Apart: How Any Business Actually Works.**
I have not been able to check Amazon from here, so treat this as unverified.

# 4. What has to change in the text

Translation alone will not make these books work for an English reader. Five
things need editorial decisions.

## 4.1 The Korea ending — the big one

Book 2 closes with 「대한민국, 창업하는 나라」. Its argument runs:

> Of the five moats, two — switching cost and intangibles — do not require a
> large domestic market. And those are exactly the two whose value AI is raising.

**The argument is excellent. The framing is local.** An American reader hits a
chapter about Korean industrial policy and closes the book.

**The fix is not to delete it — it is to notice that the argument was never really
about Korea.** It applies to a founder in Tallinn, Lisbon, Auckland, Dublin, or
a spare bedroom in Ohio with no capital. Retitle and reframe:

> **Closing — Building From a Small Market**

Korea stays in, as the author's worked example and as the place he is writing
from. That is more honest than genericizing it, and more interesting: the reader
gets a case study instead of a slogan. The final line of the Korean edition —
*크게 시작해야 한다는 생각이 사실은 가장 오래된 오해였고, 그 오해가 시작을 가장 많이 막았다* —
generalizes without a word of strain.

## 4.2 Korean cases: keep them, gloss them

야놀자 · 골프존 · 완도 전복 · 배달의민족 · 당근.

**Keep all of them.** Swapping in Uber and Airbnb would make the book
indistinguishable from every other business book in the category. Unfamiliar
cases are an asset — they are the reason to buy *this* book rather than another.

Each gets one clause of orientation on first mention, no more:

> Yanolja, a Korean travel platform that started as a booking site and moved into
> running the hotels' back-office software…

> Wando, an island county that produces most of South Korea's farmed abalone…

## 4.3 Money and units

The adult books mostly use dollars and euros already. **The teen books use won for
illustrative amounts** (8,000원 stickers, 3,000원 materials). These are made-up
teaching numbers, not facts — convert them to round dollars ($6 sticker, $2.50
materials) so a US teenager can picture them.

**Do not convert the factual figures** in the adult books. A revenue number in
euros stays in euros.

## 4.4 The teen voice

Korean 반말 has no English equivalent. The register that does the same work:

- second person, contractions, short sentences
- **no slang** — slang dates a book in three years and these are meant to last
- the voice of an older sibling who knows things, not a teacher and not a peer

> **너 그거 아니?** → *Here's the thing.*
> **~할 거야** → *You'll…* / *What'll happen is…*
> **그거 정상이야** → *That's normal.*

## 4.5 The verification chapter — translate it faithfully

Book 2 contains 「확인은 어디까지 했는가」, where the author states plainly that he
set a primary-source rule and did not fully meet it, lists the nine things he
corrected and the four numbers he deleted.

**Translate every word of it.** In a category full of overclaiming, this chapter
is the book's strongest differentiator. Do not soften it, and do not move it to
the back.

# 5. Amazon KDP upload pack

## 5.1 What KDP wants

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Format | **EPUB** (KDP accepts EPUB directly; no need to convert) |
| Language | **English** — set in the file, not just the form |
| ISBN | **Leave blank.** KDP assigns a free ASIN. Only buy an ISBN if you also want wide distribution. |
| Series | Use KDP's **Series** field: *The Five Boxes* (adult) and *The Five Boxes: Teen Edition* |
| DRM | **No DRM** — it does not stop copying and it annoys buyers |
| Royalty | **70%** requires a list price of **$2.99–$9.99** |

## 5.2 Pricing

| Book | Price | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Anatomy of Money | **$6.99** | Full-length, dense, first in series |
| Anatomy of Money II | **$7.99** | Longer, and the sequel buyer is already sold |
| for Teens | **$3.99** | Short; impulse price for a parent |
| for Teens II | **$4.99** | Nearly double book 1's length |

**Consider $0.99 or free for Teens I as a series entry point** once all four are up.

## 5.5 Front matter English editions need that the Korean ones don't

KDP eBooks should carry these. The Korean editions omit them by design; the
English ones should not.

1. **Copyright page** — © 2026 Jaehyuk Choi, all rights reserved, edition note
2. **"Also by this author"** — the other three titles, once they exist
3. **A closing ask** — one line inviting an Amazon review. This matters more than
   anything else you can do for ranking, and it costs one sentence.

# 6. Order of work

Not alphabetical, and not by size. By **risk**.

| Order | Book | Why this position |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **for Teens** (~11k words) | Shortest. It proves the whole pipeline — voice, EPUB, KDP upload — end to end at the lowest cost. If something is wrong with the approach, you find out after 11,000 words instead of 46,000. |
| **2nd** | **Anatomy of Money** (~46k) | The flagship. Everything else references its terminology, so it must be locked before the sequel. |
| **3rd** | **for Teens II** (~21k) | Reuses the teen voice already established. |
| **4th** | **Anatomy of Money II** (~44k) | Last on purpose: it quotes Book 1 constantly, so Book 1's English must be final first. |

**Do not publish any of them until at least two are ready.** A series with one
book has no series effect; the second book is what makes the first one sell.
