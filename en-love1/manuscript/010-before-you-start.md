## There Are Three

This is the first of three.

| Book | What it looks at |
|---|---|
| **One · The One Who Was Already There** | What had arrived before I ever asked |
| Two · The One Who Never Asked | Going toward what will not come back |
| Three · The One Who Stayed | What continues even when they do not know |

The three look at one thing from three sides. You can read any one of them
alone. Read them in order and a line joins up. The line is drawn on the covers.

## Every Chapter Ends With a Box

> ## Someone Comes to Mind

**Not something to do. Someone to remember.**

This book does not tell you to do anything. All it does is point at something
that has already happened, and it happened to you, not to me. So that box is
one I cannot fill.

One person is enough. You can stop reading there. Stopping is what the box was
for.

## Every Chapter Leaves One Thing Open

A book about love wants to close neatly. To keep myself from that, I have put
one place in every chapter where I do not have the answer.

Warm writing collapses in exactly one spot: **when it pretends the painful part
does not hurt.** Readers know before the writer does. So the unresolved places
in this book are left standing.

## And This Book Does Not Finish

There is no knot tied in the last chapter. There is none in the third book
either.

That is not an oversight. **Explain everything and close it and it ends there.**
I left it open because I would rather it stayed with you after you shut it.

## How the Facts Are Handled

Each chapter ends with a section called Where This Chapter Came From. Two
things go in it: what I checked, and what I cut because I could not check it.

**There is almost no outside material in this book.** Numbers would have
propped up some of it, but attach a number to a story like this and the story
becomes somebody else's. Anything I could not verify was cut whole rather than
softened.
