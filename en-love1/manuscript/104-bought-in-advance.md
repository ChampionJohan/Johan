## The Smallest Size

Baby clothes carry sizes marked in months. Newborn, three months, six months.
The smallest of them is the one worn least. It is outgrown in a few weeks. The
thing chosen after the longest look is the thing used for the shortest time.

There is one place in the house where those clothes sit, and everything in it
is small, everything is new, nothing has been used. And the one who filled that
place has never seen the face of the one who will use it. Guessing sizes and
picking colors and thinking about which season it will be, all for a face
nobody has seen, resembles no other transaction in the world.

## Preparing Is a Bet

Preparation is another word for believing. Nobody buys in advance unless they
believe the person is coming. And the ground under that belief is thin. Nobody
actually knows, and in some houses the person does not come. They buy anyway.
They buy without having worked out what would be done with the things if the
person never arrived.

So preparing is **putting something down on what does not yet exist.** The one
who puts it down can lose. The one it was for may never learn that it
happened, and people have been doing this for thousands of years, and the
houses that do it far outnumber the houses that do not.

## The Things That Went Unused

Not everything prepared gets used. There is the outfit outgrown before it fit.
The thing that never came out because the season turned wrong. The thing bought
and then forgotten. Those stay at the bottom of the drawer.

What went unused was preparation all the same. While that shirt sat there
somebody was picturing a person wearing it, and the picturing changed those
few months in that house, and what never reached the receiver had already
happened on the giving side. This comes back in the second book.

## It Keeps Happening After They Are Grown

None of this is only about babies.

Parents go on buying things after a child has left home. They buy what the child
used to like, thinking they will come at the holiday, and when nobody comes it
sits there. Next holiday they buy it again. If a friend says they are coming,
out comes the thing that friend liked, and the house gets cleaned from the
morning of the day. The guest has not arrived and the house is already a house
with a guest in it.

People move ahead of time toward whoever they love. It is hard to explain and it
is plainly so.

## What If the Preparation Is Refused

Here is where this chapter stops.

What happens when the thing bought in advance is not wanted. More commonly, when
it simply goes unused. The one who prepared feels it, and the one who received
does not know why the air changed. What gets said is usually about the object,
and both of them know it is not about the object.

So some people prepare less. They stop buying ahead so as not to be hurt. That
looks like wisdom from one angle and like losing something from another, and I
have not settled which.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone bought something for you before you came.

Do you know where that thing is now. If you still have one of them, today would
be a reasonable day to take it out and look at it. That object has never once
asked whether you deserved it.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside.

There is probably research on what households prepare before a birth and what
it costs. I did not look. A figure turns this into a chapter about spending, and
then the unworn clothes at the bottom of the drawer become unsold stock. The
unsold stock is what the chapter is looking at.
