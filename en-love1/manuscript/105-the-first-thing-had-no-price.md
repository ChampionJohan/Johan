## Put This Down First

The first thing a person ever received had no price on it.

There is a reason to say that at the start. Nearly every exchange we know has a
price attached, so it takes a moment to call to mind an exchange that has none.
Goods have a cost. Help carries a share to be returned later. Even a gift sets
off some arithmetic on the receiving side. And the things received in a person's
first years had none of that arithmetic anywhere in them.

## Milk Comes With No Invoice

Think about an infant being fed and something odd shows.

The child pays nothing, has nothing to pay with, does not know how paying
works, and does not know it is receiving, and the one giving gets up several
times a night and sometimes wrecks their health, and writes no invoice. Not
once. And nobody writes a note to collect the same amount back once the child
has grown.

So this is not a trade. To call it a trade both sides would need arithmetic,
and only one side has any, and that side does not do it, and what the right
name for it is I still do not know. What can be set down is that the first
exchange a person meets in the world has this shape. **We meet the exchange
without arithmetic first, and then we enter a world that runs on arithmetic.**

## When Prices Start Appearing

Then one day prices appear.

Run an errand and something comes back. Do well and praise arrives. Do not and
it does not. From there the child learns to count. This much done brings that
much back. Learning it is not a bad thing. Not learning it makes the world very
hard to live in.

Only, once it is learned, what came before goes out of view. To someone who
knows the arithmetic, the years of receiving without any is neither remembered
nor explicable, and so the grown person comes to believe every exchange has a
price, and with that belief starts recalculating everything they ever
received.

## Putting a Price on It Changes It

Something curious happens when you price what you were given.

The moment it has a number it becomes a debt, and a debt sits heavy, and what
sits heavy you want to avoid. Which is how it happens that someone who received
a great deal keeps their distance from the one who gave. It is not dislike. The
sum simply got too large to look at.

The giving side mostly does not follow this. They never put a price on anything.
One side did no arithmetic and one side did, and only the side that did the
arithmetic is suffering.

## So Should We Not Count

Here is the stuck place.

Not counting at all is strange too. Someone who does not know they received has
no gratitude, and without gratitude there is nothing to pass to the next person.
But counting makes a debt, and a debt makes distance.

There ought to be a road where you know you received and never assign a figure,
and I cannot write down how that is done. I suspect the people who manage it are
doing it right now. I have never heard one of them explain it.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone has never asked you what it cost. They are still not asking. Their not
counting made things easy for you, and that it was not free is something you
only see much later.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside. It is what I have seen and
what I thought while looking at it.

There is likely research putting a number on the labor of feeding. I did not use
it. The moment it is converted into hours the chapter turns over. This chapter
is looking at the side where no price gets assigned, and bringing in a source
that assigned one would mean I did the arithmetic first.
