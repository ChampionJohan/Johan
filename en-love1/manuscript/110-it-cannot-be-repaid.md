## Has Anyone Repaid It

Has anyone repaid what they received from a parent.

No. Plenty of people have worked at it. Given money, bought a house, driven them
to hospital, stayed beside them to the end. Ask those people and not one of them
says they repaid it. They all say they could not. The harder someone worked at
it, the more firmly they say so.

The question from the first chapter comes back here. There it was whether this
is a debt. Here it is whether it can be paid.

## Why It Will Not Settle

A few reasons.

One is that the amount is unknown. As the earlier chapters showed, there is no
way to count the size of what was received. With no amount there is no paying in
full. Give whatever you like and you cannot tell whether that was all of it, so
no receipt is ever issued.

One is that the kinds do not match, and what was received was mostly time and
body, and what gets returned is mostly money or things, and the two do not
convert, so no sum however large becomes the same thing.

And one is direction. **When you received it they were young, and when you
return it they are old.** It looks like giving back to the same person, and the
one who gave and the one receiving the return are not the same person. Time went
through the middle.

## The Other Side Will Not Take It

Here is the oddest part of this place. Try to repay and the other side refuses.

Give money and it comes back into your pocket. Buy something and you are asked
why you spent that much. Say thank you and you are told it is nothing. They will
not let you repay. So there is a debt and the counter is closed.

Why they do it is guessable. Accepting repayment would make it a trade. The
giving side does not want it to be a trade. The moment they take it, the past
several decades become an invoice.

## So What Do People Do

There is one thing people do when they have received something that cannot be
repaid.

**They send it sideways.** Unable to return it to the giver, they give it to
someone else. To a child, to someone younger, to a stranger. Those people never
gave them anything, and they do it anyway.

This behavior does not explain. It makes no arithmetic sense. Receive from A and
give to B and the account with A sits exactly where it was. And people do it,
and having done it they are strangely lighter.

The next chapter is about that.

## The Regret Does Not Go

Here is the stuck place.

Sending it sideways does not remove the regret. However much you give to
someone else, what you failed to give them stays exactly as it was. Which is
why the regret of people who have buried a parent never shrinks across a whole
life, and I have not found anything to say that would shrink it, and I do not
want to write a paragraph here that pretends I have.

## Someone Comes to Mind

There is someone you did not repay.

If they are still alive, today a phone call will do. If they are not, you may
leave it unpaid. Not being repayable is the nature of the thing, and they never
meant to collect in the first place.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside.

There is certainly research, country by country, on what people give their
parents. I did not use it. Once a figure appears the chapter becomes arithmetic,
and this chapter is looking at the side where the arithmetic does not close.
