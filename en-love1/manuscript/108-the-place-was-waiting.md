## Picking Up What the Last Chapter Could Not Count

The last chapter said there is no way to count the size of what we received. Let
me take one step further from there. Part of why it cannot be counted is that
**it was already there before it arrived.**

To count something received you need a moment of receipt. You have to be able
to write down that on such a date you were given such a thing, and the
things this book is looking at have no moment of receipt. They were already in
place before I got there.

## A Place Exists Before the Person

Think of the first day at school. There was already a place. A desk, a number,
a slot for a name tag. Nobody told any of the children who had made the place
ready, and no child asked. A place looks like something that was simply always
there.

Work is the same. First morning and there is a desk, an account on the system, a
chair to sit in. Somebody set that up the night before. That somebody is usually
a stranger, and it is rare to ever find out who.

**People always walk into places that somebody arrived earlier and made.** Once
you have noticed it once, the world looks slightly different. Roads are like
that. Letters are like that. Food is like that. All of it done by someone before
I came.

## There Is Waiting Inside

A place has waiting inside it. To make a place ready is to wait for whoever is
coming.

Waiting is invisible. Once it is over it leaves no mark. The moment a person
sits down, the whole stretch during which the place stood empty disappears. So
the one who sits does not know the place was waiting for them. They think the
place was there.

The third book has a chapter that looks only at waiting. That one looks at the
times nobody comes. This one looks only at the times they do.

## It Is There for Latecomers Too

There is one good thing about the place coming first.

Arrive late and the place is still there, and to give a different place to the
punctual and the late you would have to be keeping track of who came when, and
a place made in advance does no such arithmetic. The ones preparing it do not
know the arrival time of whoever is coming.

So even someone who arrives very late comes and sits. That there exist places
where nobody asks why you are only coming now is strangely reassuring.

## And the People Who Had No Place

This is the stuck place. Some people had no place. People who walked into
somewhere nobody had prepared, who arrived and found nothing. Read this chapter
to them and it turns cruel.

I have not yet made anything I could say to them. *There must have been one
somewhere* might be a lie. Saying nothing makes this a chapter written for half
of the people. Of everywhere I got caught while writing this book, this is where
I got caught most often.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Somebody made ready the place you walked into. School or work or a house. Have
you ever tried to find out who it was. Mostly you cannot. Not being able to find
out is part of the nature of the thing.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside.

There is certainly a great deal of research on people who had no place. I did
not use it. Bring that material in here and those people become a statistic,
which is the exact opposite of what the chapter was trying to say, and I left
the place empty instead and wrote that I could not fill it.
