## The Phrase About Not Knowing How to Be Grateful

There is a phrase about people who receive and do not know how to be grateful.
It is usually said to scold someone. This chapter disagrees with it. **It is not
that they do not know how. It is that it cannot be seen.** While you are
receiving, it is built so as not to show.

There is one way to make it show, and it is not a good way. It shows when it
stops.

## You Learn by the Cut

Think of a morning with no hot water.

Nobody is grateful for hot water on an ordinary day. You turn the tap and it
comes. But on the morning it does not come you think about it all day. It
becomes vivid only once it is gone. So that gratitude arrives during the
absence, not during the having. Not while receiving. When it is cut off.

People work the same way. Someone who is always there steps out once, and only
then does everything they were doing become visible, and no meal appears, no
call comes, something is not where it should be. One day away is enough.

## So the Constant Person Loses

There is something unfair built into this.

The more constantly someone is there, the less they are seen. The steady person
is the least thanked and the occasional visitor stands out more. What the
once-a-year guest brought stays in memory, and what the daily person did becomes
background.

So the person who does the most in a house ends up the least visible. Until they
fall ill. After that it is all visible within a day. Learning it that way is late
and the price is high.

## A Way to See It Earlier

I have wondered whether there is a way to see it before it is gone.

One is imagining. Sketching out what today would be like without this person.
Try it and it comes up surprisingly clear. Only, imagining does not last, and
within days it has sunk back into background.

The other is asking. Ask what they did today and it becomes visible. Do not ask
and it does not. This works better than imagining and is harder by the same
amount.

## What Shows After a Death

The commonest case is that it shows after a death.

What people say after a funeral is nearly always the same. That now they
understand. The expression on their faces when they say it is closer to
bewilderment than to grief, and it is the face of someone finding out that
what was in front of them went unseen the whole time.

The seventh chapter of the third book is about that. It looks at what remains
after the person is gone. This one records only that it takes the going to make
it seen.

## So Am I Supposed to Watch It Every Day

Here is the place I cannot solve.

Is there anyone who lives conscious at all times of what they are receiving.
Probably not. Live like that and the day will not move. A person needs the
capacity to take things for granted in order to get on to the next thing. You
cannot breathe while attending to the air.

So the not-seeing looks like the design rather than a fault, and if that is so I
do not know when gratitude is supposed to happen. Taking it out occasionally
seems to be the only option, and how occasional that occasionally should be I
cannot write down.

## Someone Comes to Mind

There is someone always there beside you right now.

Sketch out, just once, what your day would have been without them today. It does
not take long. And having sketched it, this evening is slightly different.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside.

The business of learning the size of a thing only after it is gone may well have
a name and a body of study behind it. I did not look. The instant it has a name
this becomes a chapter that explains, and what it is looking at is that morning
with no hot water.
