## The Conclusion First

What was received goes sideways. It cannot go backward, so it goes sideways.

The last chapter said the repayment counter is closed. You would expect what
was received to pool there, and it does not, and people send what they
received off somewhere, and when the somewhere cannot be the giver they send
it to someone else. Nobody taught this and nearly everybody does it.

## It Goes Toward the Younger

The direction leans. Mostly it goes toward whoever is younger.

Someone who received from a parent gives to a child. Someone taught by a teacher
teaches a student. Someone who was shown the work on their first day shows the
new arrival. It steps down a generation at a time. So what was received does not
stay put but keeps descending, and nobody along the way recovers the principal.

The structure is strange. Nobody is repaid and it keeps circulating. Look at each
person separately and every one of them is at a loss. Look at the whole and it
does not break.

## What Is Not Returned Travels Further

There is one more strange thing.

An exchange that is returned ends between the two of them. Lend and be repaid and
that is the end; the account closes on the spot. An exchange that is not returned
does not end. The one who received sends it to another, and that one sends it
again. **Because it is not repaid, it travels a long way.**

So being unrepayable may not have been a defect. Built to be repaid it would have
stopped there. Built so it cannot be, it kept going. Setting it down like that
feels a touch too neat, and I have not found another way to account for it.

## Passing It On Makes You Lighter

A person who has only received is heavy. The regret from the last chapter is that
weight. And once they have given to somebody, the weight drops.

I do not know why it drops. It is not that the account balanced. What is owed
to A does not disappear by giving to B. And people really do get lighter. That
is probably why those who have raised a child start to see their own parents a
little differently, and doing it is what shows you the size, and once the size
is visible the regret turns partly into gratitude. Not entirely. A little.

## And the People Who Received Nothing

Here is the place I cannot solve.

What becomes of someone who received little. Someone with nothing to pass on. If
what was received is what flows, then someone who received nothing is naturally
unable to give. Written out like that it becomes a piece that gives up far too
easily.

And in fact there are people who received nothing and give. I have seen people
who give more. Then the flowing model is wrong, and I have not found what is
more nearly right. The question comes back in the second book. I do not expect to
solve it there either.

## Someone Comes to Mind

There was a time you passed on what you had received.

Does it come to you who it went to. That person had never given you anything.
You gave anyway. You probably still cannot explain why.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside.

There is likely research on what is handed down across generations. I did not use
it. Bring a model in and people become variables, and what this chapter is
looking at is the behavior people keep performing without any explanation for it.
