## How Many Times Was It

Have you ever thought about how many repetitions it takes for a person to learn
to speak.

A single word takes dozens. Heard and heard again, copied wrong, corrected,
wrong again, corrected again. And there are thousands of words. Multiply it out
and the number gets so large that counting stops meaning anything. One thing is
certain though. **Somebody absorbed every one of those tens of thousands.**

And that person does not remember it. Which attempt produced the first *mama*,
which word took longest, what got corrected on which evening. One or two scenes
survive and the rest is gone.

## Neither Side Remembers

The second chapter said the receiving side does not remember. This chapter is
the other side of it. **The giving side does not remember either.**

So the tens of thousands of repetitions that made a person are missing from
both memories. It plainly happened and nobody holds the scene. An event with
no witness, and the result is inside the head of whoever is reading this
sentence, and that you know what these letters mean is the result of those
tens of thousands.

## Repetition Does Not Stay

There is a guess available for why it does not stay. Repeated things do not
stay in memory.

What happened once stays, and what happens daily becomes background. So a
person's memory holds the special days and omits the ordinary ones that actually
made them. Memory summarizes, and the first thing a summary throws out is
repetition.

That is a little unfair. The thing done most is the thing that survives least.
The thousands of meals cooked do not stay and one meal out somewhere does. Of
those two, the one that kept the person alive is the first.

## So Gratitude Lands Wrong

Something slips out of line here.

Gratitude goes to what can be remembered. The special thing, the visible thing,
the thing that happened once. And what was actually received in quantity sits on
the opposite side. So a person's gratitude always lands on **the smaller
portion of what they were given.**

This is not a failing of character, it is how memory is built. There is nowhere
to lay the blame, which makes it the more frustrating kind of error.

## What Do You Do With Something That Will Not Total

Here is the unsolved place.

There is no way to learn the size of what you received. Counting fails for
want of records. Asking fails because the giver does not remember. Estimating
fails because the figure will not hold still, and so you go on not knowing,
and going on not knowing, a day arrives when it feels like you were given
nothing.

Keeping the sentence *I received a great deal* and leaving the amount blank
seems to be all there is. We are back at the same place as the fifth chapter.
Second time here and it still does not open.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone taught you to speak. That person does not remember doing it. Ask them
and they will say they just did it. Inside that *just* are tens of thousands of
times.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside.

How many exposures a child needs to learn one word has been studied, as far as I
know. **I have not read the original, so the chapter says only dozens, and the
thousands and the tens of thousands are estimates at that same level.** The
numbers are not the frame of the chapter, but it would be worth propping them up
before publication.

The same goes for repetition staying less well in memory. It is written at the
range anyone would recognize, with no source behind it.
