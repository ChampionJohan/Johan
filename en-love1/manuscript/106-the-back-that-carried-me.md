## A Back Nobody Chose

Nobody chooses the back that carries them, and nobody knows whether it was broad
or narrow, young or already bent, sweating or not. You were up there and you
went somewhere, and on the way you fell asleep, and when you woke you were
somewhere else, and the road is missing from the memory of the one carried while
the weight stays in the body of the one who carried.

## The Weight Is All on One Side

That is the shape of carrying. The weight goes on one side only.

The one carried is light. Their feet are off the ground, so there is nowhere
to put any effort, and with no effort to make they fall asleep, and the one
carrying is heavier by exactly that much. Two people are going somewhere and
one of them is producing all of the force, and the carrier does not
propose splitting it, partly because it cannot be split, and partly because
proposing it would make the thing stop being carrying.

What is strange is that the one carried feels no guilt about it. A four-year-old
on a father's back is not apologizing. There is an age at which that is not
strange, and after that age it becomes strange. Where the line sits is the odd
part. Nobody was ever taught it and everybody knows.

## The Carried Place Comes Back

Being carried does not end with childhood.

Too ill to walk and someone holds you up. Fallen and someone gets you to your
feet. Work collapses and someone props you for months. Each time the weight
goes to one side only, and the side it goes to tends not to talk about it, and
people who carry generally do not make much of it out loud.

So there is a separate moment, later, when the one carried learns the size of
what happened. It arrives after they have carried someone themselves. Carrying
makes your back hurt, and only once it hurts does the thought come: **that back
must have hurt too.** The thought nearly always comes too late.

## You Cannot See a Back's Face

Being carried has one particular feature. You cannot see the face of the one
carrying you.

So the one carried does not know whether this is hard. There is no expression to
read. There is only a voice, and a voice can be arranged. Told that it is fine,
you understand it to be fine.

There are many other places built the same way, where the receiver cannot see
the giver's face. Most of what this book is looking at sits in places like that.
Places where the one who received never finds out.

## Should the Carrying Be Mentioned

Here is the unresolved place.

What happens if the one who carried brings it up. *I carried you like that for
years* is true, and it is true, and the instant it is said the weight transfers
to the one who was carried. It can become gratitude and it can become debt, and
which it will become is not known even to the person saying it.

So a great many people never bring it up. Not bringing it up means the other
never knows. Bringing it up moves the weight. Both are a loss and I cannot pick
the better one.

## Someone Comes to Mind

There was a back that carried you.

Do you know whose it was. You do not have to. What is certain is that you did
not walk here on your own. For the first few years you could not take a single
step.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside.

I considered writing about how carrying differs between countries, or how far
back the practice goes, and cut it. That direction turns the chapter into a
piece about custom, and what it is looking at is the structure where the weight
lands on one side only.

The blank in the middle is for the author to fill.
