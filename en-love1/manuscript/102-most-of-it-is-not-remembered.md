## Before Three

There is a point at which a person starts to remember their own life, usually
somewhere around three or four, and before that point the person was already
living. Eating, crying, sleeping, being held. Which means everyone has **several
years of being alive with no memory of it.** Try counting what happened in those
years and you run out of breath. Fed six or seven times a day, changed about as
often, somebody up several times a night. A year is thousands of times. A few
years is well past ten thousand.

Every one of those ten thousand is gone.

## They Did Not Vanish

There is an easy thought to fall into here: that if it is not remembered it
might as well not have happened. It is not so. What was received in those
years stays in the body, and a person who was held is different from a person
who was not, and a person whose crying brought someone is different from a
person whose crying brought no one. **The one who received cannot remember,
and the result remains.**

That is the strangest thing in this chapter. A large share of what made a person
sits outside that person's memory. They do not know how they came to be the way
they are.

## So There Is No Way to Be Grateful

With no memory there is no gratitude. Gratitude runs on memory. You have to
know what somebody did for you before you can thank them. And the stretch in
which you received the most is blank from end to end, and so people live out
their lives **unable to be grateful for the largest thing they were ever
given.**

It is an odd arrangement and nobody says so, because everyone is living inside
it. The ones who gave know it too. Lifting a child at night, they know the child
will not remember this. They know and they do it. That is the seventh chapter.

## What Does Get Remembered Is Small

What does stay is stranger still. The childhood people remember is mostly small
things. The light on some particular afternoon. One sentence somebody said. A
road walked holding a hand. The big things do not stay and the small ones do. I
do not know why, though I can guess at part of it. The big things were
continuous and the small ones happened once. What is continuous becomes
background, and background is not remembered. What is always there is the
hardest thing to see.

## What Do You Do With an Empty Place

Here is where I get stuck. If you received it and have no memory of it, what
are you supposed to do with that. Treating it as nothing will not work,
because the result is still here. Being grateful will not work either, because
no one comes to mind, and you could ask, except it is usually too late, and
when it is not, the person you ask does not remember much either.

Leaving it empty seems to be the only option. Though leaving it empty while
knowing it is empty is probably not the same as simply not knowing. What the
difference amounts to I cannot write down.

## Someone Comes to Mind

Someone lifted you up at an hour you cannot remember. Have you ever asked who it
was. If there is still someone alive to ask, they will not remember well either.
Ask anyway. Something surfaces on their side.

## Where This Chapter Came From

Nothing in this chapter was brought in from outside.

When childhood memory begins, and how what came before it persists afterward,
have certainly been studied. The three-or-four figure comes from that direction
as far as I know. I have not read the original, so the chapter says *usually* and
nothing more. To use a number I would have to read the source, and even then I
am not sure this chapter would need it.

The feedings and changes per day were not counted either. They are the range
anyone would recognize, which is why the chapter says six or seven, and
thousands.
